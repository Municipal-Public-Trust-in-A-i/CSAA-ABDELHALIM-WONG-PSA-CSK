## Waiver: personal dialogue protected under the First Amendment

This document is my **personal dialogue** and public commentary. It is my speech.

It is protected expression under the **First Amendment** to the United States Constitution and **article I, section 2** of the California Constitution. I am commenting on public court proceedings, insurance claim-handling, and the conduct of litigation in which I am a party. That is speech on a matter of public concern. It is not converted into a pleading by being addressed to counsel, posted on a public docket-mirror, or disliked by the people it describes.

**This is not a court paper.** It is not a pleading, motion, memorandum, declaration, proof of service, request for judicial notice, Evidence Code section 1152 settlement offer, or Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 safe-harbor notice. I am not offering it for filing in any court. I do not ask any tribunal to take judicial notice of it. I do not ask any tribunal to treat it as evidence of any claim or defense.

**Formal positions stay in the file.** Positions I direct to CSAA Insurance Exchange, to any other party, or to any tribunal remain solely those stated in papers already on file in CGC-25-631801, CGC-25-631802, A173827, and CGC-21-594102. This dialogue is not a substitute for those papers. It is not a judicial admission. It is not a concession. It is not an amendment of any filed theory.

**Do not conflate the two.** Counsel has repeatedly placed routine correspondence before the judge as if personal exchange were a formal litigation position. That is the error this waiver exists to prevent. If any party later attaches this commentary as an exhibit, it remains what it is on its face: personal dialogue. Attaching it does not make it a filing by me, and it does not erase the filed papers that actually state my positions.

**Vexatious is a statutory finding, not a speech code.** A careful critique of motion practice, including in personal dialogue, does not make the speaker "vexatious." Code of Civil Procedure section 391 is not a prior restraint. It is not a license to punish commentary because an unrepresented claimant declined to be quiet.

Mr. O'Connell:

Let us state a simple fact: Tyler took a monumental risk. If anyone in this matter is engaging in gamesmanship, it is you: a reality made obvious by your ongoing motion practice. I suggest you stop digging your own trench. It was your improper inclusion of newly minted arguments and late-submitted evidence that obligated my response. As an officer of the court, you really ought to know better.

Black-letter practice dictates that a party cannot introduce novel arguments or evidence in a reply brief. Doing so merely invites the court to grant me leave to address whatever imaginative fables you have recently cooked up. Furthermore, my severe critique of your practice does not render me "vexatious." A careful reading of the supplemental filing makes it abundantly clear why I will prevail in the long run. You may have landed a few speculative long shots, but you persistently conflate our personal correspondence with the formal positions I direct to CSAA through you as their conduit.

No insurer welcomes a trial, least of all when facing potential eight-figure exposure, if not more. You surely realize by now that I am fully capable of deploying every weapon in the civil practice arsenal. My recent reply alone confirms that you are not dealing with your average *pro per* litigant: a fact made glaringly evident by the ambitious yet utterly failed filings you have presented to the court. You continuously put routine correspondence before the judge, confusing foundational litigation processes with personal attacks. When I previously urged you to display candor to the court rather than conceal controlling authority, you inexplicably interpreted it as an assault on your ego and self-perceived brilliance: a vanity no doubt nurtured by a lifetime of unearned praise.

I can, to an extent, sympathize. In the heat of a dispute, one can lose track of their words. Since sustaining severe brain trauma and a concussion in the collision, tracking every communication channel requires extra effort from me, yet rest assured: my logic, strategy, and tactical capabilities remain entirely uncompromised.

It must be a bit embarrassing that a self-represented litigant can wield the legal arsenal with such precision. How startling it must be to discover that someone from the supposed "underclass", those you presume lack the intellect to understand procedural law, can effectively adjudicate claims in our court system. What a jarring revelation that must be for you all.

Naturally, members of the bar possess such breathtaking superiority, not only in their elevated societal stature, but in their boundless moral flexibility. Isn't that right, Mr. Giuliani? Attorneys, after all, are infallible entities incapable of error, while the unsophisticated public is deemed too intellectually vacant to comprehend statutory schemes or procedure. We are told we cannot possibly master the tools of the trade. Yet, the moment we execute those exact tools to devastating effect, you reflexively brand us "vexatious." Why? Because you never anticipated a fight. You expected an easy mark.

Consider an analogy: a man routinely wanders into a posted private park to gather fallen wood, ignoring the explicit warning signs because it saves him the trouble of sourcing his own timber. For a time, his trespasses go unchecked. He grows comfortable, believing he is simply collecting harmless sticks, right up until the day he reaches down, scoops up what he assumes is dead wood, and realizes too late that he has grasped a pit viper.

Perhaps that is precisely your predicament, Mr. Tyler. You envisioned a quick resolution and a swift, painless insurance payout. You saw an unrepresented litigant and assumed an easy target: someone easily brushed aside, entirely ignorant of procedure. So you grew sloppy and neglected your diligence. You failed to realize that the litigant who filed those two seemingly contradictory documents across two cases was playing chess moves ahead of you. You swallowed the bait whole. By asserting litigation privilege over a routine business communication and adopting an immediately hostile, zero-dollar settlement posture with an unrepresented claimant, you walked directly into an explicit trap. You failed to consider how the industry views such conduct, effectively placing CSAA directly in the crosshairs of a bad-faith investigation.

Of course, the fault is not entirely yours. Ample credit belongs to Priya and Alberta: the brilliant architects who coerced Judge Wong (may he enjoy his peaceful retirement) into usurping the authority of the jury and violating the United States Constitution. How Judge Wong presumed he could execute such a maneuver without consequence remains a mystery, though desperate motives tend to yield desperate rulings. Perhaps our mutual friends at CSAA and PSA Law could enlighten us on what transpired off the record. Isn't that right, Justice Clarence? Whatever drove Judge Wong's actions, it ultimately cost him his seat on the bench.

And let us not lose sight of the central irony: your entire argument that this matter was previously adjudicated rests upon that late judge's unfortunate misstep on the bench.

So, by all means, proceed with whatever imaginative maneuver you believe will save you. Rest assured that every scenario you might improvise has already been fully plotted, stress-tested, and neutralized on my board long before your office even thought to outline its strategy. As we make our inevitable return to the Superior Court, take solace in knowing that you have permanently bound yourself to your co-defendant, trapping your posture in a corner from which there is no graceful exit.

When the upcoming phase of discovery commences, I trust you will spare the court your usual performative shock and manufactured outrage. You invited this level of scrutiny through your own procedural arrogance. You were fully noticed, you took the bait, and now you must live with the record you so carelessly created. I am thoroughly prepared for your next move. I suggest you prepare for mine.
