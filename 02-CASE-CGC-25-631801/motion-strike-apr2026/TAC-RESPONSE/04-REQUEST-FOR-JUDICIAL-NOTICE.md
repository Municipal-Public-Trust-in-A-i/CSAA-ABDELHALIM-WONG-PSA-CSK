## V. LEGAL BASIS

### A. Court Records Are Proper Subjects of Judicial Notice

Evidence Code § 452(d) permits judicial notice of "[r]ecords of (1) any court of this state or (2) any court of record of the United States or of any state of the United States."

The documents listed as Exhibits A through K are records of the Superior Court of California, County of San Francisco, or the California Court of Appeal, and are therefore proper subjects of judicial notice. The Court may take judicial notice of the existence and contents of court records but not of the truth of factual matters asserted therein. *Sosinsky v. Grant* (1992) 6 Cal.App.4th 1548, 1564.

### B. Facts Not Reasonably Subject to Dispute Are Proper Subjects

Evidence Code § 452(h) permits judicial notice of "[f]acts and propositions that are not reasonably subject to dispute and are capable of immediate and accurate determination by resort to sources of reasonably indisputable accuracy."

The CSAA denial letter (Exhibit L) is a document whose existence and contents are not reasonably subject to dispute.

### C. Statutes Are Proper Subjects of Judicial Notice

Evidence Code § 452(a) permits judicial notice of "[t]he decisional, constitutional, and public statutory law of this state and of the United States."

The statutes listed as Exhibits M through W are California statutes and are therefore proper subjects of mandatory judicial notice.

### D. Judicial Notice Is Mandatory Upon Proper Request

Evidence Code § 453 provides: "The trial court shall take judicial notice of any matter specified in Section 452 if a party requests it and... [g]ives each adverse party sufficient notice of the request... to enable such adverse party to prepare to meet the request."

Plaintiff has provided this request to all parties and has attached true and correct copies of the documents (or excerpts thereof) as exhibits.

### E. Cal. Supreme Court Opinions Are Mandatorily Noticed Under Evid. Code § 451(a)

Evidence Code § 451(a) makes judicial notice of "[t]he decisional … law of this state" mandatory and does not require formal request. The California Supreme Court opinions listed in Part IV — *Westphal*, *Kulchar*, *Modnick*, and *Sanders* — are published decisional law of the California Supreme Court and are therefore subject to mandatory judicial notice under § 451(a) without any further request. Plaintiff nevertheless lodges certified copies of these opinions with pincite tabs for the convenience of the Court and to memorialize the pincites on which the TAC and Memorandum rely (TAC §I.4.5, ¶ 7.5, ¶ 15.5, ¶ 26.5, ¶ 27.5, ¶ 33, ¶¶ 34.5–34.6, §IV.5.5, Eighth Cause of Action; Memo §§ I.A–I.F, V.G).
