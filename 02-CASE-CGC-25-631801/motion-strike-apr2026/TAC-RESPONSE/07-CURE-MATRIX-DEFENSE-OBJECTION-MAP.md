## Detailed Cure Analysis

### Objection 1: Kulchar Extrinsic-Fraud Framework Not Pleaded

**Defense Argument:** The complaint fails to plead the elements of an independent action in equity under *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 1:** Identifies action as independent action in equity under *Kougasian/Olivera/Stevenot*
- **¶ 2:** Distinguishes from appeal, motion for new trial, or collateral attack
- **¶ 3:** Four-channel *Kulchar* framework pleaded element-by-element:
  - (a) Concealment of facts (production history)
  - (b) False promises/representations of non-receipt
  - (c) Keeping Plaintiff in ignorance (893-day silence)
  - (d) Misleading the tribunal (categorical denials)
- **¶¶ 4–6:** Judicial-reliance quote and seven consequential rulings

**Authority:** *Kougasian v. TMSL, Inc.* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1032–35

**Cal. Supreme Court Anchor:** *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471 ("fair adversary hearing" primary right); *In re Marriage of Modnick* (1983) 33 Cal.3d 897, 905 (broad-concept extrinsic fraud); *Estate of Sanders* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 607, 614 (fiduciary-breach conversion to extrinsic). TAC installs these anchors at new §I.4.5 (¶¶ 3.5–3.7) and new ¶ 7.5.

**Back-Reference:** Bench Brief §§ I.A-I.C (judicial reliance sequence)

---

### Objection 2: Appeal A173827 Is the Remedy

**Defense Argument:** Plaintiff's remedy is the pending appeal; no independent action is cognizable.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶¶ 28–29:** Distinguishes appeal (instructional error) from this action (institutional fraud)
- **¶ 30:** Cites *Kougasian* and *Stevenot* for parallel-remedy principle
- **¶ 31:** Pre-rebuts *Throckmorton* as non-binding federal authority

**Authority:** *In re Marriage of Stevenot* (1984) 154 Cal.App.3d 1051; *Kougasian*, 118 Cal.App.4th at 1034–36

---

### Objection 3: Litigation Privilege Bars Claims

**Defense Argument:** All claims are barred by the absolute litigation privilege under Civil Code § 47(b).

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 8:** Identifies conduct as non-communicative (silence, file-transfer, failure to correct)
- **¶ 9:** Nine-Point Evidentiary Chain documenting 893 days of defense possession
- **¶¶ 10–11:** *Flatley* illegality exception (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a))
- **¶ 12:** *Action Apartment* extrinsic-fraud carveout
- **¶ 13:** *Throckmorton* pre-rebuttal

**Authority:**
- *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048 (non-communicative conduct)
- *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 320–325 (illegality exception)
- *Action Apartment Ass'n v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1248–49 (extrinsic-fraud carveout)

**Cal. Supreme Court Anchor:** *Estate of Sanders* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 607, 614 (equity exception for officer-of-court fraud); *In re Marriage of Modnick* (1983) 33 Cal.3d 897, 907 (denial of existence as extrinsic concealment).

**Back-Reference:** SAC-06 §IV.D.0 (Nine-Point Chain); Bench Brief §IV.E

### Objection 4: Lack of Standing (Shaolian/Norton)

**Defense Argument:** Plaintiff lacks standing to sue defense attorneys due to attorney-client privity requirements.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 15:** *Shaolian* distinction (policy benefits vs. fraud-on-court)
- **¶ 15(c):** *Estate of Sanders* (fraud-on-court parties)
- **¶¶ 16–17:** Civ. Code § 2338 agency/ratification
- **¶ 18:** *Doctors' Company* aiding-and-abetting framework
- **¶ 19:** § 6128(a) independent tort under *Rickley/Shaffer*
- **¶¶ 20–21:** Civ. Code § 1573 constructive fraud

**Authority:**
- *Estate of Sanders* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 607, 615–616
- *Doctors' Company v. Superior Court* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 39
- *Rickley v. Goodfriend* (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 1136

**Cal. Supreme Court Anchor:** *Estate of Sanders* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 607, 615 (officer-of-court fiduciary duty runs to adjudicative integrity; opposing party has equity standing regardless of privity). TAC installs this anchor at new ¶ 15.5 and at new Eighth Cause of Action.

**Back-Reference:** Bench Brief §II.F; SAC-06 ¶¶ 7–14 (party block)

### Objection 5: Collateral Estoppel / Res Judicata

**Defense Argument:** Action is a collateral attack on a valid judgment barred by res judicata.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 22:** Distinct primary right: freedom from fraud on the court
- **¶ 24:** Five-issues-not-adjudicated block
- **¶ 25:** Primary-rights comparison table
- **¶¶ 26–27:** *Mycogen/Crowley/Lucido* recital

**Authority:**
- *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335
- *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888, 904
- *Boeken v. Philip Morris USA, Inc.* (2010) 48 Cal.4th 788, 798

**Cal. Supreme Court Anchor:** *Mycogen*, 28 Cal.4th at 904 (primary right = injury suffered); *Boeken*, 48 Cal.4th at 798 (primary right independent of forum); *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666, 681–682 (separate injuries → separate primary rights); *Lucido*, 51 Cal.3d at 341 (actually-litigated + necessarily-decided). TAC installs these at new ¶ 26.5.

**Back-Reference:** SAC-06 §IV.B (Issues Not Adjudicated)

### Objection 9: Ultimate Admission Cures Fraud ("It Got In On Day 7")

**Defense Argument (Anticipated):** The Day-7 admission of the 911 audio, after Defendant Abdelhalim authenticated it as his own voice, retroactively cures any prior concealment and moots the extrinsic-fraud injury.

**TAC Cure:**

- **§IV.5.5, ¶ 34.7:** Installs the Supreme Court deprivation rule — *Westphal*, *Kulchar*, *Modnick*, *Sanders* — as a four-test foreclosure of the Day-7 defense.
- **¶ 34.5:** Applies *Modnick* 907 "fairly before the court for adjudication" standard to the seven rulings issued within the corrupted record.
- **¶ 34.6:** Applies *Sanders* 616 / *Larrabee* 650–651 "effectively prevented" standard to the Court's decision-making matrix.
- **¶ 33 (edited):** Embeds *Westphal* 397 "opportunity to present" and *Kulchar* 471 "fair adversary hearing" into the partial-presentation rebuttal.
- **Eighth Cause of Action (¶¶ 81–87):** Officer-of-the-court fiduciary-duty theory anchored in *Sanders* 615.
- **Memo § V.G:** Parallel argument in the memo.

**Authority (Cal. Supreme Court Only):**

- *Westphal v. Westphal* (1942) 20 Cal.2d 393, 397 (extrinsic = "prevented from exhibiting fully his case")
- *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471 (primary right = "fair adversary hearing")
- *In re Marriage of Modnick* (1983) 33 Cal.3d 897, 907 ("never fairly before the court for adjudication")
- *Estate of Sanders* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 607, 616 (conduct that "effectively prevented … a fair adversary hearing"), quoting *Larrabee v. Tracy* (1943) 21 Cal.2d 645, 650–651

**Cal. Supreme Court Anchor:** All four holdings above. The Supreme Court has located the operative injury at the point of deprivation of opportunity, not at the point of eventual admission. The Day-7 leak-through of a single exhibit does not retroactively cure the seven structural rulings issued within the corrupted *Modnick* record.

**Back-Reference:** Memo § I.F; Memo § V.G; TAC Eighth Cause of Action.
