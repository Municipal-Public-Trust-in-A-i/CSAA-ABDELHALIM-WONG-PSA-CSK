I.   INTRODUCTION
Plaintiff’s First Amended Complaint (“FAC”) improperly seeks damages that are simply not
recoverable as a matter of law, including monetary relief, restitution, prior litigation court costs, lost
earnings, and even the underlying medical costs from his prior, underlying personal injury trial—which
Plaintiff lost by defense verdict.
All these items of alleged damages arise from Plaintiff’s underlying third-party litigation against
a CSAA insured, where Plaintiff lost by defense verdict; hence, these alleged damages are unsupported
by any statutory, contractual, or factual basis. Further, the FAC targets Defendants for their litigation
conduct in that underlying jury trial; conduct which is absolutely privileged as occurring during a judicial
proceeding. Under Code of Civil Procedure § 436, the Court is authorized to strike any irrelevant,
improper, or nonconforming matter from a pleading. Because the deficiencies in Plaintiff’s damages
claims appear on the face of the FAC and cannot be cured by amendment, the challenged portions should
be stricken in their entirety.

III.      LEGAL STANDARD FOR AN ORDER STRIKING ALL, OR PART, OF PLAINTIFF’S
FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT
“Any party, within the time allowed to respond to a pleading may serve and file a notice of motion
to strike the whole or any part thereof….” (Code Civ. Proc. § 435(B)(1).) Furthermore, “[t]he court may,
upon a motion made pursuant to section 435, or at any time in its discretion, and upon terms it deems
proper:
             MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES IN SUPPORT OF MOTION TO STRIKE PLEADING
---
            (a)     strike out any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading.
            (b)     strike out all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of
this state, a court rule, or an order of the court.” (Code Civ. Proc. § 436)
Where pleadings are defective, “the defect may be raised by a demurrer or motion to strike or by
motion for judgment on the pleadings.” (Coyne v. Krempels (1950) 35 Cal.2d 257, 263.)
Here, the entirety of Plaintiff’s FAC, its principal legal theory, and each of Plaintiff’s alleged
damages fall squarely within this rule. First, Plaintiff’s proper legal remedy for his loss of his underlying
jury trial is an appeal. Second, Plaintiff’s legal contention of “extrinsic fraud” is not legally supportable.
And third, Plaintiff cannot recover any of his claimed items of damages, including: costs of litigation,
emotional distress damages.
A. THERE IS NO BASIS FOR PLAINTIFF’S FRAUD BASED EQUITABLE CLAIMS
The entire legal theory of the FAC is flawed. Plaintiff cannot establish the requisite “extrinsic
fraud” necessary for the relief he seeks. And even if he could, his proper legal recourse is an appeal—not
this subsequent lawsuit.
In the context of litigation “fraud usually arises when a party is denied a fair adversary hearing
because he has been ‘deliberately kept in ignorance of the action or proceeding, or in some other way
fraudulently prevented from presenting his claim or defense.” (Kulchar v. Kulchar (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467,
471.) The type of extraordinary circumstances which can give rise to a claim for extrinsic fraud include:
                    Where the unsuccessful party has been prevented from exhibiting fully his case, by
fraud or deception practiced on him by his opponent, as by keeping him away from court,
a false promise of compromise; or where the defendant never had knowledge of the suit,
            being kept in ignorance by the acts of the plaintiff; or where an attorney fraudulently or
without authority assumes to represent a party and connives at his defeat; or where the
            attorney regularly employed corruptly sells out his client’s interest to the other side, --these,
and similar circumstances show that there has never been a real contest in the trial or
hearing of the case, are reasons for which a new suit may be sustained to set aside and
            annul the former judgment or decree, and open the case for a new and a fair hearing.
(Kulchar, supra, 1Cal.3d at 471.)
Absent such extreme circumstances, “[r]elief is denied. . . if a party has been given notice of an
action and has not been prevented from participating therein. He has had an opportunity to present his
case to the court…” (Kulchar, supra, 1Cal.3d at 471.)
Here, Plaintiff had every opportunity to present his case to the Court and the jury, in his underlying
jury trial. Still, inexplicably, the FAC alleges extrinsic fraud. (See FAC pg. 20, line 26-pg. 21, line 14.)

B. THE FAC’S CLAIMED DAMAGES ARE IRRELEVANT, FALSE, IMPROPER AND
DO NOT CONFORM WITH THE LAWS OF THIS STATE
The entire factual basis contained in the FAC is legally untenable because it targets Defendants
for their privileged conduct and communications under Civ. Code § 47(b)—amidst the judicial proceeding
of an underlying jury trial. Further, Plaintiff lacks standing to sue CSAA or its retained insurance defense
attorneys involved in the defense of the underlying lawsuit for the claims contained herein, including his
claims for compensatory damages. Furthermore, the specific items of damage listed in the FAC are simply

1. The Absolute Litigation Privilege Bars All the FAC’s Claims for Damages
            Civ. Code § 47(b) provides in relevant part: A privileged publication or broadcast is one made. .
            . (b) In any (1) legislative proceeding, (2) judicial proceeding, (3) in any other official proceeding
authorized by law, or (4) in the initiation or course of any other proceeding authorized by law and
            reviewable pursuant to Chapter 2…

“The only exception to the application of 47(2) to tort suits has been for malicious prosecution
actions.” (Ribas v. Clark (1985) 38 Cal. 3d 355, 364.) The privilege also “poses a clear bar to [an] action
insofar as it is based on. . . the tort of intentional infliction of emotional distress.” Ibid.
Here, all the conduct alleged in Plaintiff’s FAC arises from statements, filings, or other
communicative acts undertaken in connection with prior judicial proceedings.3 While Plaintiff attempts
to reframe the issue as sounding solely in equity, at its core the FAC is a rehashed, and more elaborate
rendition of his claims in his Amended Complaint For Damages and Injunctive Relief, Case No. CGC25-631802.4
Plaintiff has not alleged in the FAC any cause of action for malicious prosecution, nor could he do
so under these circumstances and facts alleged. As the party initiating both this lawsuit, and the failed one
which he seeks to relitigate here, Plaintiff is unable to assert malicious prosecution. Therefore, the
litigation privilege “poses a clear bar to [this] action insofar as it is based on . . . infliction of emotional
distress.” (Ribas, supra 38 Cal.3d at 364.) Accordingly, all portions of Plaintiff’s FAC related to damages
alleged to have been caused by Defendants’ underlying litigation conduct should be stricken pursuant to
Code Civ. Proc. § 436.

Damages

3. Plaintiff is Collaterally Estopped from Seeking Recovery on the Same Damages
Sought in his Underlying Jury Trial—Which he Lost.
Plaintiff is estopped from simply attempting to relitigate all the same damages that he failed to
obtain at his underlying jury trial.
“A collateral attack is an attempt to avoid the effect of a judgment or order made in some other
proceeding.” (F.E.V. v. City of Anaheim (2017) 15 Cal.App.5th 463, 471 (quoting Rico v. Nasser Bros.
Realty Co. (1943) 58 Cal.App.2d 878, 882.) “An attack in a second action on an earlier judgment is
collateral.” (Ibid.) (citing Wouldridge v. Burns (1968) 265 Cal.App.2d 82, 84.) “A judgment of a court of
general jurisdiction can only be set aside on collateral attack if the judgment is void on the face of the
record.” (Wouldridge, supra, 265 Cal.App.2d at 84.) Further, “collateral estoppel precludes relitigation
of issues argued and decided in prior proceedings.” (Singh v. Lipworth (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 813, 826.)
Finally, “[t]here are no maxims of the law more firmly established, or of more value in the administration
of justice, than [those]…designed to prevent repeated litigation between the same parties in regard to the
same subject of controversy.” (U.S. v. Throckmorton (1878) 98 U.S. 61, 65 (emphasis added).)
The entire purpose of the present lawsuit is to relitigate the same issues presented in the underlying
case—including seeking recovery of all the same damages Plaintiff was denied in that case. Despite
repeatedly and duplicitously disclaiming “general personal-injury damages, …or attorney’s fees” (See
FAC, pg. 19, lines 4-5, pg. 26, lines 3-4,) Plaintiff now seeks those same damages but somehow transforms
them by describing them as “Equitable Monetary Relief (Ancillary and Restitutionary)”, and “allowable
costs of suit.” (See FAC pg. 27, lines 19-25.) This does not change the nature of the relief sought or the
fact that Plaintiff seeks compensation for the same harms alleged in his failed previous litigation.
Plaintiff also seeks additional benefit by way of this lawsuit in the form of vacatur. This is nothing

4. Plaintiff Was Not the Prevailing Party at the Underlying Jury Trial and Cannot
Therefore Seek Costs
In order to be entitled to compensatory damage or prevailing party costs, Plaintiff must have
prevailed at trial.
He did not.
Under Code Civ. Proc. § 1032, the term “Prevailing party” includes the party with a net monetary
recovery…the “prevailing party” shall be as determined by the court, and under those circumstances, the
court, in its discretion, may allow costs or not and, if allowed, may apportion costs between the parties.
Furthermore, “a prevailing party is entitled as a matter of right to recover costs in any action or
proceeding.”
Here, the Plaintiff’s alleged damages that are simply his costs in litigating the underlying jury trial
cannot be recovered and should be stricken. Plaintiff was not “the prevailing party” in his underlying
lawsuit. To the contrary, he lost, and carries significant liability for attorney’s fee’s and costs borne by
CSAA in the defense of Plaintiff’s meritless, prior lawsuit.
