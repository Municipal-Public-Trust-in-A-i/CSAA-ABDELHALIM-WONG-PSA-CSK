 1                                           TENTATIVE RULINGS
 2           A.      The San Francisco Superior Court adopts CRC 3.1308 as the tentative ruling
 3   procedure in civil law and motion and discovery matters. For Real Property Court, compliance with
 4   8.10(B) is required.
 5           B.      Parties may obtain a tentative ruling issued by the Law and Motion and Discovery
 6   Departments      by    telephoning     (415)    551-4000      or   visiting    the   court’s    website     at
 7   https://sf.courts.ca.gov/ and clicking the online services link. Changes in telephone numbers will
 8   appear in the official newspapers.
 9           C.      A party who fails to appear at the hearing is deemed to submit to the tentative ruling.
10   However, no party may submit to a tentative ruling that specifies that a hearing is required.
11           D.      Parties who intend to appear at the hearing must give notice to opposing parties and
12   the court promptly, but no later than 4:00 p.m. the day before the hearing unless the tentative ruling
13   has specified that a hearing is required. Notice of contesting a tentative ruling must be provided by
14   sending an email to the court to contestdept301tr@sftc.org or contestdept302tr@sftc.org with a copy
15   to all other parties stating, without argument, the portion(s) of the tentative ruling that the party
16   contests. A party may not argue at the hearing if the opposing party is not so notified and the
17   opposing party does not appear.
18           E.      If no party appears, or if a party does not appear because the opposing party failed to
19   give sufficient notice of intent to argue, then the tentative ruling will be adopted.
20           F.      Tentative rulings are generally available by 3:00 p.m. the day before the hearing. A
21   tentative ruling that does not become available until after 3:00 p.m. is a late tentative ruling. A late
22   tentative ruling will indicate that the ruling is late. If a tentative ruling is late, the parties must appear
23   unless all parties agree to submit to a late tentative ruling in which case the Court will adopt the late
24   tentative ruling pursuant to subsection E above.
25           G.      The prevailing party on a tentative ruling is required to prepare a proposed order
26   repeating verbatim the substantive portion of the tentative ruling and must bring the proposed order
27   to the hearing even if the motion is not opposed or the tentative ruling is not contested. If the
28   prevailing party is appearing at the hearing remotely, the proposed order may be sent to the court
