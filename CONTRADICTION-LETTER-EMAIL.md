## Settlement communication — Cal. Evid. Code § 1152

This letter is sent for purposes of compromise and settlement discussions under California Evidence Code § 1152. It is not an admission of any fact and is not offered to establish liability. It is offered only as a structured presentation of the documentary record that already exists in the three live matters between the undersigned and your client(s).

---

Dear Counsel and Claims Representatives,

I am writing in my capacity as plaintiff / appellant in the following three related matters, all arising from the same underlying pedestrian-versus-vehicle collision in San Francisco:

1. **Appeal A173827** (First District Court of Appeal) — appeal from the judgment in CGC-21-594102.
2. **CGC-25-631801** (San Francisco Superior Court) — independent action in equity to vacate for extrinsic fraud.
3. **CGC-25-631802** (San Francisco Superior Court) — fraud, concealment, and Business & Professions Code § 17200 action against CSAA.

The purpose of this letter is narrow. I am not asking you, at this stage, to concede any element in any of the three cases. I am asking you to look at two statements that your side has already placed on paper, to see why they cannot coexist, and to understand the legal consequence that contradiction produces in each of the three forums. That consequence is the basis on which a negotiated resolution is materially less expensive than continued litigation.

---

## 1. The two statements your side has placed on paper

Two statements have been made in this matter that cannot both be accurate at the same time. Both are documentary. Neither is in dispute as to its existence.

**Statement A — February 25, 2021 (CSAA denial letter).** CSAA informed the claimant, in writing, that its investigation was **"concluded"** after **"careful review,"** and denied the claim on that basis.

**Statement B — January 2025 motions in limine and February 18, 2025 hearing (defense counsel, CGC-21-594102).** Defense counsel represented to the trial court that the 911 audio, the body-worn camera footage, and the unredacted materials were **"not produced,"** and characterized those materials as **"news to me."**

A third statement, from the underlying trial itself, ties the first two together and forecloses any attempt to reconcile them:

**Statement C — April 16, 2025, certified trial transcript (CGC-21-594102, Day 7).**

> **MR. ROSARIO:** And the Dolan Law Firm provided the digital copies of the 911 calls, the body-worn camera footage, and all of the unredacted documents that —
>
> **MS. NAVARATNASINGHAM:** Sure. They were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure.

Statement C is not my characterization. It is on a certified court reporter's transcript, under the signatures of officers of the court. It establishes that the defense's own file chain — from Dolan Law Firm, to successor counsel, to present counsel — held the materials throughout.

---

## 2. Why Statements A and B cannot coexist

Statement A represents to a claimant that a regulated investigative process was performed. Under Title 10, California Code of Regulations §§ 2695.4 and 2695.7, a "concluded" investigation is required to be a "thorough, fair, objective" one. A thorough investigation of a pedestrian-versus-vehicle claim — starting from a police report that references a CAD number on its face — includes pulling the 911 audio, the body-worn camera footage, and the CAD record. Those are not exotic or optional steps. They are the obvious next steps that any reasonable claims investigator would take, and they are the steps a regulator would expect to see in the file.

Statement B represents that those same materials were unknown to the defense as of early 2025.

- If Statement A is accurate, then the materials existed and were collected in 2021 — which contradicts Statement B's "news to me" framing in 2025.
- If Statement B is accurate, then the materials were not collected — which contradicts Statement A's representation of a completed investigation.

Statement C eliminates any remaining ambiguity: the materials were in the defense's own file chain from the earliest stages. That means Statement A was either (i) accurate as to collection but misrepresented the investigation's scope in its written conclusion, or (ii) false as to the investigation having been completed. It also means Statement B cannot be accurate in either scenario.

There is no third reading of the record that avoids this result.

---

## 3. The legal consequence in each of the three cases

The contradiction is not rhetorical. It drives elements in each of the three live matters, and it does so regardless of which of Statements A or B your side ultimately chooses to stand on.

- **Appeal A173827 — prejudice (CCP § 614; Cal. Evid. Code § 451(a); *Soule v. GM Corp.*).** If the materials were available and known to the defense by trial, the concealment of the 911 audio and the refusal to instruct the jury on Vehicle Code §§ 22107 and 21804(a) were prejudicial under a 9–3 liability verdict, which is the bare minimum civil threshold.

- **CGC-25-631801 — extrinsic fraud (independent action in equity; the *Kulchar* line; CCP § 473(d)).** Elements 1 (false representation) and 2 (prior possession / chain of custody) are independently proven by whichever of Statements A or B your side disavows. Civil Code § 2338 (agency) and ratification principles carry those findings to CSAA. Business & Professions Code § 6128(a) attaches personal attorney exposure.

- **CGC-25-631802 — fraud, concealment, and UCL against CSAA.** If CSAA stands on Statement A, the 2021 investigation was incomplete (the materials were in the file chain but not used), and the "concluded / careful review" representation is a material half-truth under Civil Code §§ 1709–1710 and Business & Professions Code § 17200. If CSAA stands on Statement B, CSAA's retained and funded counsel misrepresented the record to a trial court in 2025, and CSAA — having retained, paid, and supervised that counsel — ratified the misrepresentation under Civil Code § 2338. Either path satisfies the "liability reasonably clear" trigger of Insurance Code § 790.03(h)(5), with the attendant DOI-regulatory exposure.

Any attempt to reconcile Statements A and B without abandoning one requires impeaching the February 25, 2021 denial letter, the January 2025 motion papers, or the certified April 16, 2025 trial transcript. None of those documents can be withdrawn.

---

## 4. Why this matters for valuation

The three cases are anchored in the same documentary record. That record is shared across the three forums. Under California judicial estoppel and issue-preclusion doctrine, positions taken in one forum are available to the plaintiff in the others. A ruling in any one of them — an interlocutory fraud finding in CGC-25-631801, a reversal and remand in A173827, a denial of summary judgment in CGC-25-631802 — produces findings that propagate.

That dynamic is asymmetric by design. It raises the settlement floor over time; it does not lower it. Each week of continued litigation adds to the shared record without removing anything from it. The risk-surface materials already circulated with the cross-case documents place the worst-case CSAA-only exposure in the mid-nine-figure range at a 5:1 punitive ratio, with institutional second-order costs (DOI regulatory exposure, reinsurance pricing, UCL injunction compliance) above that figure. Those numbers are provided for framing, not as a demand; the demand is set out in § 6 below.

## 5. The narrow question I am putting to you

I am not asking you to concede that Statement A or Statement B is false. I am asking you to recognize three things:

1. Both statements exist on paper, in records maintained by neutral officers of the court and by CSAA itself.
2. Statement C, on a certified trial transcript, prevents the file-chain facts from being walked back.
3. The three live cases are structured so that whichever of Statements A or B your side eventually stands on will complete at least one element in at least one case.

That recognition, and only that recognition, is the premise of this letter.
