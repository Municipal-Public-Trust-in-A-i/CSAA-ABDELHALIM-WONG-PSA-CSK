## C. Authority to amend (analysis)

### C.1 Distinctions the court and statutes invite

| Concept | Role here |
| --- | --- |
| **Amendment once without leave (CCP § 472(a))** | Timing tied to answer, demurrer, or motion to strike, and opposition deadlines **when section 472 applies**. |
| **Carve-out for anti-SLAPP (CCP § 472(b))** | “This section shall not apply to a special motion brought pursuant to Section 425.16.” So the **automatic** one-amendment-without-leave framework in subdivision (a) **does not govern** the pleading cycle once a section 425.16 motion is treated as the operative pleading challenge. |
| **Amendment by leave (CCP §§ 473, 576)** | Discretionary judicial authority to allow pleading changes “in the furtherance of justice,” with terms; section 576 is explicit that a judge may allow amendment of a pleading before or after commencement of trial. |
| **Stipulation** | Parties may agree to amended pleadings or timelines; record here does not show a stipulation path. |
| **Court order after motion for leave** | Typical vehicle after an adverse strike: noticed motion for leave to file a further amended complaint, with a proposed pleading attached, addressing prejudice, delay, diligence, and merits threshold issues defendants will raise again under section 425.16 if the new pleading revives the same theory stack. |
| **Relation back** | Primarily a **limitations** and **party-naming** tool chain in California practice; **CCP § 474** in this repo addresses **fictitious-name** amendment mechanics, not general “new theory” relation back. See `CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CCP-474.md`. |

### C.2 Map to what the minutes say happened

The court **struck the FAC**, it did **not** enter language in the minutes excerpted here that simultaneously **grants leave** to file a replacement complaint. Post-order, a plaintiff who wishes to continue in the same action therefore needs a **new procedural step**: typically a **motion for leave to amend** under sections **473** and/or **576**, joined with a **proposed** second amended complaint (or differently framed pleading), with service and compliance with any **case management** or **local e-filing** rules.

**Interaction with anti-SLAPP:** If the proposed pleading still “arises from” the same protected litigation activity, defendants may file another special motion under section 425.16. Section 425.16, subdivision (f), sets default timing for filing the special motion from service of the complaint (and analogous treatment of amended complaints in practice); subdivision (g) addresses **discovery stay** while the motion is pending. Section 425.16, subdivision (b)(2), lists what the court shall consider in making its prong-two determination. Subdivision (b)(3) limits later **evidentiary** use of a determination when the **plaintiff** has established a probability of prevailing (that is, when the special motion is **denied**), so read it carefully before citing it in the **defense prevailed** posture here. See `CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CCP-425.16.md`.

## E. Motion and appellate menu (pros, cons, timing placeholders)

**Universal caveat:** Confirm **notice of entry** dates, **CRC** deadlines, **San Francisco** department rules, and any **fee motion** schedule. Placeholders below use generic California litigation memory; they are **not** substituted for calendaring.

### E.1 Motion for reconsideration (Code of Civil Procedure section 1008 and non-statutory motions)

- **Statutory motion (CCP § 1008):** Typically requires **new or different** facts, **new** law, or a **different** application of law to facts that could not have been presented with **reasonable diligence** before the ruling (subdivision (a)), or shows the order rests on an **incorrect legal** basis or **overlooked** a dispositive statutory or factual matter (subdivision (b), “intrinsic” mistake in a narrow sense). **Con:** Same evidence and same arguments re-packaged are vulnerable to denial as an **impermissible** motion for reconsideration. **Repo:** Full text in `CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CCP-1008.md` (synced May 13, 2026); still confirm current operative text on leginfo before filing.
- **Non-statutory “reconsideration”:** Some trial courts treat informal requests as **successive motions** under local rules. **Con:** High risk of denial and of **waiver** if used instead of appeal.

### E.2 Motion for new trial (CCP § 657)

- **Availability:** Grounded in **trial** irregularities, misconduct, surprise, new evidence, excessive or inadequate damages, insufficiency of evidence, or error in law at trial. See `CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CCP-657.md` opening subdivisions.
- **Fit here:** **Poor fit** for a pretrial order striking a complaint under section 425.16, **unless** the case had already proceeded to verdict in **this** action (it has not, per the minutes’ reference to a **prior** trial and a separate appeal). **State clearly N/A** unless unusual procedural facts emerge.

### E.3 JNOV and other post-verdict vehicles

- **JNOV** and directed verdict analogies appear in the minutes’ **prong two** discussion (*Computer Xpress* language), but that is **analogy within** the anti-SLAPP test, not a post-verdict motion in this branch.
- **Conclusion:** **JNOV is not a standard vehicle** for responding to an order granting a special motion to strike absent a final judgment after trial in the same case.

### E.4 Appeal from the anti-SLAPP order

- **Appealability:** Code of Civil Procedure section 904.1, subdivision (a)(13), expressly lists orders **granting or denying** a special motion to strike under sections 425.16 and 425.19. Section 425.16, subdivision (i), cross-references section 904.1.
- **Pros:** Direct review of the **order**, consolidated record on the motion, established body of case law on **prong one** and **prong two** standards.
- **Cons:** **Delay**; standards for prong two are **demanding**; potential **fee** exposure under section 425.16, subdivision (c)(1), if not reversed.
- **Standard of review (high level, for research confirmation):** California decisions often treat **pure questions of law** on anti-SLAPP motions as reviewed **de novo**, while **factual** findings supporting prong two may attract **substantial evidence** or analogous deferential review depending on the issue’s character. Map each minutes paragraph to **legal** versus **factual** boxes in the draft brief (the minutes blend both).
- **Preservation tied to section 425.16, subdivision (b)(3):** If a later ruling **denies** a special motion because the plaintiff showed a probability of prevailing, subdivision (b)(3) limits use of that determination in later proceedings. After a **grant** of the motion here, ordinary **appeal** and **record** rules govern prejudice arguments.

### E.5 Writ strategies (extraordinary relief)

- **Certiorari or mandate** may be mentioned in aggressive briefing, but the **bar is high**: petitioner must show **no adequate remedy at law** (appeal is usually adequate for interlocutory anti-SLAPP orders made appealable by section 904.1(a)(13)) and meet **strict** writ practice requirements.
- **Con:** Writs are **discretionary**, often denied where appeal exists.

### E.6 Preservation tactics tied to this record

- **Unauthorized papers:** If a motion for reconsideration or a parallel procedural motion is considered, attach the **register of actions**, **proofs of service**, and any **leave to exceed page limits** or **motion to file** documents to show whether “unauthorized” was **technically** correct.
- **RFJN chain:** The minutes cite RFJN exhibits (MILs and scheduling order). Preserve objections to **judicial notice** scope, **authenticity**, and **materiality** if not already in the motion record.
- **CRC 3.1312 order compare:** When the signed order lodges, compare **verbatim** tentative requirement against the **minutes** and the **oral** ruling if a reporter’s transcript exists (minutes list reporter as N/A, so **official transcript** may be limited).
- **Statement of decision:** Generally tied to **bench trials**; **not** the default here unless the case proceeds on a non-jury trial track.

### E.7 “De novo” in this context

Use the phrase **carefully**: **de novo** appellate review commonly attaches to **legal** elements of anti-SLAPP rulings, while **mixed** questions may split. A separate concept is **trial de novo** after certain appeals, which is **not** triggered by the May 12 order alone. Your checklist should separate **appellate** de novo from **trial** de novo.

## I. RFJN and record incorporation (realistic framing)

### I.1 What judicial notice does **not** do

California **judicial notice** under **Evidence Code section 452**, especially subdivision **(d)** (**records of any court of this state**), supports **existence** and **content** of **court records** for **procedural** context and **pinpoint** citation. It does **not** force the trial court to treat every assertion inside a noticed **MIL** as **true** for Anti-SLAPP **prong two**, or convert **MIL outcomes** into omnibus **fraud merits** findings. See `CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/EVID-452.md` (subdivisions (c) official acts, (d) court records). **Evidence Code section 352** remains relevant to **prejudicial** or **misleading** notice requests.

### I.2 Legitimate leverage (internal strategy)

1. **Pin** the exact **clerk’s transcript** and **register** pages the May 12 minutes rely on (RFJN **Exs. E through H** path) so **appeal** or **1008** arguments can attack **mischaracterization** or **incompleteness**.
2. **LRSF 8.6** (Evidence at Hearing and Judicial Notice; ties to **CRC 3.1306** in the local digest): follow **San Francisco** attachment and presentation norms for noticed materials; non-SF file sources may need **certified** copies per local rule digest discussion in `CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/rules-of-court/sf-superior/SF-Local-Rules-effective-2026-01-01.md` (see heading **8.6**).
3. **CRC 3.1113(j)** incorporation by reference: use where the rule’s **specificity** and **explanation** requirements are met for heavy exhibits, per [03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/PROCEDURAL-AUTHORITIES-OPERATIVE-OPPOSITIONS-NESTED-LIST.md](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/PROCEDURAL-AUTHORITIES-OPERATIVE-OPPOSITIONS-NESTED-LIST.md).
4. **Lodgment** of supplemental authority is department-dependent; **do not assume** Dept 301 uses the same lodging path as other departments without confirmation.

### I.3 Overclaim warning

Avoid internal drafts that imply RFJN **precludes** insurer Anti-SLAPP defenses or **locks** the court into merits admissions. Use **issue estoppel** or **judicial admission** doctrines only where elements are validated against the **actual** appellate record (outside this memo’s scope).

## J. Record crystallization packet (internal strategy label)

Replace informal “lock the judge” phrasing with a **record crystallization packet**: a single matrix (spreadsheet or appendix) listing each **document**, **noticed for what purpose**, **disputed versus undisputed** facets, and the **minute or order quote** that depends on it.

| Column | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Document** | RFJN exhibit, MIL PDF, order, CT or RT cite, docket printout. |
| **noticed for** | Procedural existence, authentication, impeachment context, or **not** for truth of nonparty statements. |
| **disputed vs undisputed** | Separates **historical** facts from **procedural** facts the court may treat as **indisputable** under **452(h)** only when actually indisputable. |
| **minute/order quote** | Ties the exhibit to a **specific** sentence in the May 12 minutes or proposed order for appeal preservation. |
