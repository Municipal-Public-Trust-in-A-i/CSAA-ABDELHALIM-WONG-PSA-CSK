## Abstract

This article examines the litigation architecture in *Rosario v. Abdelhalim et al.* (CGC-21-594102), the companion independent equity action (CGC-25-631801), the companion insurance fraud and UCL action (CGC-25-631802), and the pending appeal (A173827, First Appellate District, Division Three) as a case study in post-verdict multi-front litigation design. The revised edition incorporates the April 2026 procedural developments: the narrowed Appellant's Opening Brief (now anchored in a single dispositive CCP § 614 instructional error); the Second Amended Complaints in both superior court actions (adding constructive fraud under Civ. Code § 1573, attorney deceit under Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a), and unifying a "Two-Phase Lullaby" theory of extrinsic fraud); and the April 2026 demurrer / Anti-SLAPP volley filed by CSAA Insurance Exchange. The article is written for an audience of senior civil litigators, appellate practitioners, and insurance bad-faith specialists. It addresses, in technical depth: the strategic architecture of the three-proceeding structure; the narrowing of the appeal to a single categorical legal question reviewed de novo; the Two-Phase Lullaby (out-of-court silence plus court-facing reversal) as a structural theory of extrinsic fraud; the pleading mechanics of constructive fraud and attorney deceit as scienter-calibrated back-ups to common-law fraud counts; the 883-day silence window as an estoppel-by-silence anchor; the four-hearing institutional-reliance chain; the privilege and anti-SLAPP preemption sequence (including the *Flatley* / *Park* / *Bonni* / *Baral* / *Action Apartment* channel); the binary deposition trap as settlement leverage; the *Prentice* / *Mai* damages architecture; and the strategic significance of a deliberating jury's written request for Vehicle Code section 22107 as both an appellate prejudice anchor and an equity reliance marker. The article's central thesis is unchanged: the most operationally significant feature of this litigation is not any single legal theory but the structure that makes each theory self-reinforcing and each defense posture self-defeating -- a closed logical web assembled almost entirely from the defense's own statements, filings, and conduct. The April 2026 amendments have hardened that structure. We examine it here, technically and in full.

## I. Introduction: The Strategic Problem and Why It Matters

The typical post-adverse-verdict litigation pathway is a single appeal. A litigant loses at trial, identifies one or two preserved errors, and files an opening brief. The structural weakness of this approach is its dependency: one shot, one decision-maker, one standard of review. If the Court of Appeal does not find prejudicial error, the proceeding ends. There is no secondary pressure. The defense has only one front to defend.

The *Rosario* cohort presents a structurally different approach. Following an adverse 9-3 verdict in a San Francisco Superior Court pedestrian-vehicle trial, the plaintiff did not file a single appeal and wait. The plaintiff constructed three independent proceedings, each with its own legal theory, its own decision-maker, its own standard of review, and its own independent remedy lane -- while engineering each proceeding so that a successful defense posture in one case automatically generates a concession or adverse inference in the others. The result is a closed logical system from which the defense cannot exit without incurring liability in at least two proceedings simultaneously.

The April 2026 amendments tightened that system further. In the equity action, the Second Amended Complaint (SAC) expanded the causes of action from three to seven, adding intentional misrepresentation against the individual attorneys, attorney deceit under Business & Professions Code § 6128(a), constructive fraud under Civil Code § 1573 as a scienter-free alternative, and aiding-and-abetting / ratification / civil conspiracy liability against CSAA as institutional principal. In the insurance fraud action, the proposed SAC narrowed the theory of liability to a "non-communicative institutional failure to investigate" actively concealed by a pre-litigation denial letter, with the denial letter pleaded as evidentiary corroboration of the underlying omission -- a structural choice designed to defeat CSAA's April 13, 2026 demurrer and April 13, 2026 Special Motion to Strike under Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16. On the appellate side, the Opening Brief was revised to isolate a single dispositive instructional error reviewed de novo, dropping secondary arguments to reinforcing-only status.

For the senior litigator, the architectural question is not merely "how do you appeal a bad verdict" but "how do you structure the post-trial landscape so that the defense's rational strategies become self-defeating." The answer in this cohort is: by building the architecture around the defense's own record. Every element of the plaintiff's proof is drawn from the defense's own filings, the court's own transcripts, and the defense client's own sworn testimony. The plaintiff did not need to speculate, infer, or reconstruct. The plaintiff needed only to align.

This article examines the mechanics of that alignment at a level of technical granularity appropriate for practitioners who will face similar strategic choices.

## II. The Predicate Tort Architecture: Why the Denial Letter Is Not a Coverage Dispute

The first strategic decision -- and the one that determines everything that follows -- is the threshold framing choice: is the insurer's early denial a coverage dispute, or is it a predicate tort?

Most practitioners treat an insurer's denial letter as the starting point for a coverage lawsuit or bad-faith claim. That framing accepts the insurer's position as a liability dispute and contests it on its merits. The *Rosario* plaintiff made a different choice, and the April 2026 SAC in CGC-25-631802 further refined it: the injury-producing act is not the letter itself. The letter is **evidentiary corroboration of a non-communicative institutional failure to investigate**. That reframing is structurally important because it removes the letter from the reach of the litigation privilege (Civ. Code § 47(b)) and the anti-SLAPP statute (CCP § 425.16) by locating the gravamen in the **omission** -- the failure to obtain the 911 audio, body-worn camera footage, and CAD dispatch records -- rather than in any communicative act.

The insurer's denial, issued on February 25, 2021 (21 days after the collision) over the signature of Claims Representative Sarah Rash for Claim No. 1004-09-1054 under policy CAAS100310857, stated that CSAA had "concluded our investigation" and had "carefully review[ed] the facts." The word "concluded" carries an implied factual predicate: the investigation is finished because the investigative steps have been performed. A reasonable recipient of a letter from a multi-billion-dollar institutional insurer understands "concluded investigation" to mean that the basic steps of an automobile liability investigation -- obtaining 911 recordings, bodycam footage, computer-aided dispatch records -- have been performed and reviewed. The statement conveys investigative finality, not a preliminary opinion.

The plaintiff's former counsel had obtained the 911 recording through a routine public records request within three months of the collision. That recording contained the defendant's own words: "I barely hit him with anything." It also contained the operator's direct question ("you hit a pedestrian?") and the defendant's affirmative answer ("yeah"). The insurer had not obtained this recording before issuing the denial. The insurer had not obtained body-worn camera footage. The insurer had not obtained dispatch records. The "concluded investigation" representation was factually false when made.

What makes this framing so powerful as a litigation foundation is that it transforms the insurer's early denial from a disagreement about fault into a documented instance of institutional misrepresentation. The denial letter becomes a timestamp: a fixed moment in the record against which every subsequent event is measured. Everything the defense does from that point forward -- every motion, every representation to the court, every trial maneuver -- is now evaluable as either consistent with or contradictory to the claim that the investigation had been "concluded." This is the first lie. Its function is to set the anchor from which the rest of the web is constructed.

This framing has several tactical advantages that a practitioner should internalize:

**It defeats the "opinion" demurrer.** If the denial letter says "it is our opinion that our insured is not liable," that is harder to characterize as a false statement of fact. The word "concluded" is not opinion language. It is completion language. It asserts that a process has been performed. The process either was or was not completed. That is a provable fact, not a judgment call.

**It creates a fixed timestamp.** The denial letter has a date -- February 25, 2021. It creates a closed evidentiary question: what did the insurer do and not do in the twenty-one days between the collision and that date? The insurer's own claim file will answer that question. If the file shows no record of a 911 request, no bodycam request, no dispatch request, the falsity is self-evident from the insurer's own records. The plaintiff does not need to prove subjective intent. The gap between the representation and the file is the fraud.

**It avoids the litigation privilege.** Civil Code section 47(b) protects communications made in the course of judicial proceedings. A denial letter mailed before any litigation is filed is not a communication in a judicial proceeding; it is a routine business communication in the ordinary course of claims handling. *Silberg v. Anderson* (1990) 50 Cal.3d 205, 212-214 requires a judicial proceeding as a predicate. *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1058 limits the privilege to "communications" and to conduct "necessarily related" to privileged communications. The February 2021 denial letter is neither. The April 2026 SAC further insulates the theory by pleading the non-communicative failure to investigate as the gravamen and the letter as only evidentiary corroboration of that omission.

**It avoids the anti-SLAPP statute.** Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 applies to claims "arising from" protected petitioning activity. A pre-litigation denial letter does not arise from petitioning activity. The fraud complaint is structured to plead the denial letter as the operative wrong, with post-litigation conduct pleaded only as circumstantial corroboration of falsity and scienter -- not as the basis for liability. This structure quarantines the fraud claim from anti-SLAPP challenge by placing the gravamen of the action before any protected activity occurred. *Park v. Bd. of Trustees* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057, 1062-1064 is the controlling authority: the court must identify the injury-producing act before applying the protected-activity label; *Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (2021) 11 Cal.5th 995, 1010-1012 further requires claim-by-claim parsing. A generic "litigation conduct" categorization is error.

The technical lesson for practitioners is this: before treating an insurer's early denial as a coverage or bad-faith dispute, ask whether the denial contains factual representations about process. If it does, treat it as a representational event, fix the timestamp, and build a chronology that tests the implied facts. The gap between the representation and the claim file is the fraud anchor. The anchor does not require proving intent. It requires only that the representation was false when made and that the falsity is provable from the insurer's own records. If the action is then pleaded with the non-communicative omission as the gravamen and the letter as evidentiary corroboration, the standard defense arsenal of litigation privilege and anti-SLAPP is preempted on the face of the pleading.

## III. The Two-Phase "Lullaby" Deception: How the Fraud Web Folded Back on Itself

To understand the *Rosario* architecture, one must first understand its generative mechanism: not one lie but two, separated by years of silence, each demanding the other, until the second lie created a contradiction that could be proved with the defense's own record. The April 2026 SAC in CGC-25-631801 formalized this generative pattern as the "Two-Phase Lullaby" theory: a structural fraud built from **Phase One out-of-court lulling** (August 2022 through May 2023) followed by **Phase Two court-facing reversal** (January 2025 through April 2025).

The first lie was institutional and pre-litigation. The insurer declared its investigation complete before performing the foundational steps of any automobile liability inquiry. That declaration was not a reckless estimate. It was a representational event designed to carry coercive force -- to tell the injured claimant that a multi-billion-dollar institution had already examined the facts and reached a final determination. The practical effect was to shift the entire burden of evidence procurement onto the plaintiff and to force litigation as the only means of obtaining what the insurer should have gathered before issuing the denial. The plaintiff had to go to court, at significant cost, simply to obtain the evidence that the insurer had claimed to have already reviewed.

**Phase One -- Out-of-Court Lulling (August 2022 -- May 2023).** During Phase One, defense counsel (Michael R. Chambers of Carbone, Smith & Koyama, LLP) received the 911 audio, body-worn camera footage, and CAD logs from the plaintiff's counsel (Dolan Law Firm) during discovery. The operative date anchors are specific: the 911 audio was served on or about August 18, 2022; Chambers appeared at the plaintiff's August 18, 2022 deposition; Chambers again appeared at the defendant's September 30, 2022 deposition where the defendant gave sworn testimony about placing the 911 call. During the 883 days that followed, defense counsel filed no motion to compel, sent no meet-and-confer letter, served no written discovery objection, and raised no authenticity challenge. The silence was continuous, total, and -- as the SAC now pleads -- designed to lull the plaintiff into treating authentication and provenance as resolved. Meanwhile, on February 1, 2024, successor counsel (Alberto Reyna of Phillips, Spallas & Angstadt LLP) issued a Compex subpoena (Order No. CA1147102-021) to the San Francisco Police Department requesting "all documents, records stored in any format" relating to the collision. One does not subpoena records one has never heard of.

**Phase Two -- Court-Facing Reversal (January 2025 -- April 2025).** The second lie arose four years after the denial letter, at the threshold of trial. By then, the defense file had been transferred to successor counsel (PSA) through a Substitution of Attorney filed May 4, 2023, bearing San Francisco Superior Court Transaction ID 69953800. Twenty months after the substitution and 883 days after the initial production, successor counsel (Priya D. Navaratnasingham of PSA) filed Motions in Limine Nos. 6 and 17 on January 17, 2025. MIL No. 6 sought to exclude the 911 audio and related materials as "not produced in discovery." MIL No. 17 represented that "Defendant has no idea where this 911 call log or audio recording came from" -- a pleaded fact on which the subsequent exclusionary rulings would rest. At the February 18, 2025 hearing, Navaratnasingham restated the posture orally: "this is news to me." The tribunal responded, "I see. Okay," and denied the plaintiff's continuance request. On February 28, 2025, the tribunal articulated its reliance explicitly: "I'm not going to question a lawyer's remarks regarding what they have and don't have. They are an officer of the Court, and they are supposed to conduct themselves appropriately before the Court." (Hr'g Tr. Feb. 28, 2025, pp. 291:26-292:7.)

These two lies were not independent events. They were structurally linked, and the record showed them to be structurally linked. The second lie was the cover story required by the first. If the defense had acknowledged that the 911 recording existed, had been produced in discovery, and had been in their file for nearly two years, the natural question would have followed: if the insurer and its counsel had possessed this recording since discovery, how could the insurer possibly have "concluded" a proper investigation twenty-one days after the collision, before the recording even existed as a recoverable artifact? The first lie required the second. The second lie protected the first. The Phase One silence set up the Phase Two reversal; the Phase Two reversal completed the structural deprivation Phase One had begun.

This is where the architecture becomes extraordinary -- because it was the defense's own doubling down, in the weeks before trial, that created the contradiction that the plaintiff then exploited. Had the defense simply admitted possession of the recording when the subject arose at the pretrial hearing, the second lie would not have entered the record. But the defense chose to maintain the denial posture, to tell the court that the materials were unknown and were appearing at the eleventh hour, and to seek exclusion of evidence that had been in their own files for nearly two years. That choice was the critical error. It committed the defense to a position that the record would contradict -- not through the plaintiff's arguments, but through the sworn testimony of a witness the defense could not control, followed by an unguarded admission by the defense's own lead attorney.

That witness was the plaintiff's former counsel, Jeremy Jessup of Dolan Law Firm. He was an attorney. He was an officer of the court. On February 18, 2025, he took the stand and testified under oath that the 911 audio, body-worn camera footage, and dispatch records had been served on the defense during discovery and used during depositions. (Hr'g Tr. Feb. 18, 2025, pp. 139-140.) "We had 911 emergency services send those to you and Dolan had a copy since 2021." (*Id.* at p. 139, l. 11.) He did not testify to something he had reconstructed from memory or inference. He testified to what he had done: he had served the materials, he had used them in depositions, and he had passed them along when his representation ended. His testimony placed the materials squarely in the defense's possession for nearly two years before trial counsel stood before the judge and said the evidence was "news to me."

The payoff came on April 16, 2025 (Trial Day 7). Navaratnasingham -- still pressing the Position-A denial ("I don't know what recording he is referring to," RT 7, p. 162, ll. 4-12; "we have no idea who this person is," *id.* p. 207, ll. 19-21) -- pivoted mid-argument and made the Position-B admission: "Sure. They were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure." (RT 7, p. 213, ll. 13-15.) This single sentence, spoken in open court on the certified reporter's transcript, is the operational linchpin of the equity action. It converts the Phase One silence into demonstrable falsity. It converts the Phase Two "unknown provenance" representation into a documented misstatement by an officer of the court. And it does so through the admission of the very attorney who had constructed the deception.

Think of this as a pinwheel. On one side, the defense had made representations to the trial court about the materials -- unknown, unproduced, appearing from nowhere. On the other side, the prior counsel's testimony fixed the chain of custody with the precision of a filed document, and the defense's own lead attorney's casual admission ("for whatever, sure") confirmed that the Dolan origin was a known, settled fact within the defense team. The pinwheel spun when the record -- the defense's own record -- collapsed the Position-A framing from both ends simultaneously.

At that moment, the plaintiff possessed something rare: a factual contradiction that was entirely of the defense's own making, proven by the defense's own procedural record, confirmed by opposing counsel's own former attorney, admitted by lead defense counsel in open court, and memorialized in the official trial transcript. The plaintiff had not been trying a case about the insurer's denial letter. The plaintiff had been trying a personal injury case about a collision. But the defense's decision to double down on the second lie -- to maintain the "unknown provenance" posture directly before trial, and then to reverse it under pressure in the middle of trial -- had inadvertently created a documented instance of fraud on the court that now existed as a matter of public record.

The architecture the plaintiff built after trial was not invented. It was observed. The two lies, their structural interdependency, and the 883-day silence that bridged them were already in the record. The plaintiff's task was to recognize the structure, understand its legal implications across three independent proceedings, and deploy those proceedings in a configuration from which the defense could not exit.

## IV. The Appeal: Categorical Isolation After the April 2026 Narrowing

The Opening Brief in A173827, in its April 2026 form, addresses a single dispositive legal error. Secondary preserved errors -- evidentiary rulings, juror misconduct grounds, due-process arguments -- remain in the record but are reframed as reinforcing only. The dispositive argument is categorical and reviewed de novo.

During deliberations, the jury sent a written note requesting the governing turning-duty statute: "In considering the question of whether the defendant was negligent, should we consider the provisions of Vehicle Code Sections 22107 and 21804(A)?" The trial court responded with a written prohibition: "You may not consider VC Sections 22107 and or [sic] 21804 subdivision A, nor will the Court instruct the Jury on those provisions." (RT 11, p. 7, ll. 23-27.) The court explained the prohibition orally: "neither Vehicle Codes Section 22107 nor 21804 subdivision A were part of the evidence. They simply are not permitted to consider that as evidence in the case, period." (RT 11, p. 8, ll. 4-9.) Plaintiff objected ("Of course we have an objection," RT 11, p. 10, ll. 8-9); the court overruled ("They are overruled and the Court believes that this is within its discretion to issue these responses," RT 11, p. 13, ll. 1-2).

This is not a close appellate question. Code of Civil Procedure section 614 provides that when a deliberating jury "desires to be informed on any point of law arising in the case," the information "required must be given." The statute imposes a mandatory duty. The court had two lawful options: answer the question directly or refer to an existing instruction that answered it. The court chose neither. It prohibited consideration. Under *Flores v. Liu* (2021) 60 Cal.App.5th 278, 289, a trial court's discretion over the manner of response does not extend to responses based on legal error. Treating statutes as evidence subject to the rules of admissibility is a fundamental legal category error. Statutes are law. Evidence Code section 451(a) imposes mandatory judicial notice of California statutory law. There is no admissibility question for a statute. Statutes are not admitted into evidence; they are the framework within which evidence is evaluated.

For the appellate practitioner, the strategic significance of this error is in its categorical purity. There is no discretionary gray area, no factual dispute to resolve, and no deference owed to the trial court. The question -- whether statutes are evidence -- is reviewed de novo. The answer is not debatable. And the prejudice analysis under *Soule v. General Motors Corp.* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 548, 574 is structurally compelling: the jury explicitly asked for the governing duty standard; the court refused to provide it; the verdict was 9-3, the minimum civil majority; the defendant's own authenticated statement on the 911 recording constituted direct evidence of fault; and the error occurred at the moment of verdict formation. The Court of Appeal's recent decision in *Drury v. Ryan* (2025) (4th Dist., Div. 3, No. G063080) reinforces the reversibility of refusing a specific vehicle-code duty instruction in favor of general negligence language; *Sesler v. Ghumman* (1990) 219 Cal.App.3d 218 establishes the same principle for turning-statute cases. *Halada v. Venice Lake Park, Inc.* (1955) 132 Cal.App.2d 788, 792 and *Smith v. Shankman* (1962) 208 Cal.App.2d 177, 183 establish that deliberation-stage refusals violating CCP § 614's strict compliance requirement are prejudicial as a matter of law.

The practitioner's lesson from the appellate strategy is the principle of isolation. The revised Opening Brief does not mention fraud. It does not mention the denial letter. It does not mention any misrepresentation. It relies on four facts: the jury asked a written question; the court's written response was based on legal error (statutes are not "evidence"); the verdict was 9-3; the defendant authenticated a recording containing his own admission ("Yes. That is my voice." RT 8, p. 108, l. 28). That is a clean appellate issue that requires the Court of Appeal to decide a legal question, not weigh evidence or evaluate credibility. Isolated appellate issues are more persuasive and less vulnerable to "harmless error" analysis than complex multi-issue briefs where weak arguments contaminate strong ones. The court granted augmentation of the record on February 17, 2026 to include the jury questions and court responses (doc. 09577565) and the filed jury instructions (doc. 09573595), converting what might have been a disputed record question into a certified appellate anchor.

One additional technical point for the advanced practitioner, sharpened by the April 2026 revisions: the defense's own trial exhibit contained the very Vehicle Code sections the jury later asked about. The defense introduced the plaintiff's original complaint as Defense Exhibit 500 -- an exhibit the defense used and argued from during closing. That complaint pleaded the turning-movement duty statutes (VC §§ 22107, 21804(a)) as the governing standards. The defense, by introducing and arguing from an exhibit containing those statutory references, created an irreconcilable paradox: if the statutes were "not evidence" as the court instructed, the defense's own exhibit should not have been admitted on that basis; if the exhibit was properly before the jury, the court's prohibition was without logical foundation. The respondent cannot argue both that the trial was fair and that the statutes were properly excluded, when the defense itself placed them in the jury's hands. The revised Opening Brief flags this "record-wrong" observation in § D.1 and preserves a failsafe paragraph establishing that reversal does not depend on treating the complaint as proof of the statute; the dispositive principle remains Evidence Code § 451(a) mandatory judicial notice.

This paradox mirrors, at the trial level, the same doubling-down pattern that characterizes the second lie. The defense introduced statutory references while simultaneously supporting a framework under which those same statutes were excluded from the jury's consideration. The asymmetry was not accidental. It was the courtroom expression of the same institutional posture: narrow what the jury can consider, exclude the plaintiff's governing framework, and protect the "not liable" conclusion from scrutiny. The appellate record captures this asymmetry. The Court of Appeal will see it.

## V. The Equity Action: Seven Causes of Action Built on the Second Lie

The independent action in equity to set aside the judgment for extrinsic fraud is the proceeding most directly constructed from the Phase Two reversal. The April 2026 Second Amended Complaint in CGC-25-631801 expanded the original three-count structure to seven counts, each calibrated to a different element and each supported by a different subset of the record.

The foundational doctrinal distinction is between intrinsic and extrinsic fraud. Intrinsic fraud occurs during the trial itself: perjury, forged documents, false testimony weighed by the jury. Courts have held that intrinsic fraud cannot support an independent action to vacate because the opposing party had the opportunity to expose it during trial through cross-examination and impeachment. *Westphal v. Westphal* (1942) 20 Cal.2d 393, 397. Extrinsic fraud refers to conduct outside the trial's adversarial process that deprives a party of the opportunity to have a fair hearing at all -- not fraud that the adversary could have challenged, but fraud that operated to shut down the party's access to a fair process before the challenge could be made. *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471 defines extrinsic fraud as conduct that "deliberately kept [a party] in ignorance of the action or proceeding, or in some other way fraudulently prevented [him] from presenting his claim or defense."

The distinction is precisely where the Two-Phase Lullaby lands. The Phase One silence (a non-communicative institutional act of omission) kept the plaintiff in ignorance of the defense's eventual authenticity posture and prevented him from structuring his pretrial evidentiary work around an anticipated provenance challenge. The Phase Two representations to the trial court -- that the materials were of "unknown provenance," that they had not been produced in discovery, that they were appearing at the "eleventh hour," and that the 911 call was "news to me" -- were not testimony offered to the jury for evaluation. They were representations made to the judge as an officer presiding over the pretrial and trial process, representations on which the court relied in making evidentiary and procedural rulings. The court accepted those representations without requiring verification. No evidentiary hearing was conducted. No file inventory was ordered. The court simply responded -- "I see. Okay." -- and proceeded to make rulings predicated on the false premise. The court's February 28, 2025 articulation of its reliance ("I'm not going to question a lawyer's remarks regarding what they have and don't have. They are an officer of the Court") is now the central documentary anchor of that reliance: it memorializes, in the judge's own words at the moment of ruling, the institutional candor assumption that the April 16, 2025 "Dolan" admission retrospectively falsified.

The seminal California cases remain *Kulchar* and *Olivera v. Grace* (1942) 19 Cal.2d 570, 575-576 (equity jurisdiction for independent actions); *Kougasian v. TMSL, Inc.* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1034-1036 holds that an independent equity action is not barred by res judicata and is governed by equitable accrual rather than CCP § 473(b)'s six-month deadline. The April 2026 SAC adds a *Kerns v. CSE Insurance Group* (2003) 106 Cal.App.4th 368, 387-388 / CCP § 473(d) overlay -- "where counsel perpetrates a fraud on the court in obtaining a judgment, the judgment is void ab initio" (*In re Marriage of Melton* (1994) 28 Cal.App.4th 931, 937) -- providing a statutory vacatur vehicle in parallel to the inherent equity power.

**The Three-Leg Framework of Extrinsic Fraud.** The equity action is built on a three-leg framework that maps directly onto the *Kulchar* standard.

The first leg establishes that the defense had the materials. This leg rests on two independent sources that are mutually reinforcing and individually unassailable, and now on seven additional record artifacts catalogued in the SAC's Nine-Point Evidentiary Chain of Defense Receipt: (1) service of the 911 audio on Chambers via Dolan on August 18, 2022; (2) Chambers' presence at the plaintiff's August 18, 2022 deposition; (3) Chambers' presence at the defendant's September 30, 2022 deposition with 911-related sworn testimony; (4) the May 4, 2023 file transfer by Substitution of Attorney (Transaction ID 69953800) from CSK to PSA; (5) the Reyna Compex subpoena to SFPD on February 1, 2024; (6) the February 26, 2024 SFPD production response; (7) the September 9, 2024 service of CAD identifiers on Reyna through Further Responses to Form Interrogatories and Supplemental Responses to Special Interrogatories; (8) Jessup's February 18, 2025 sworn testimony that Dolan had a copy since 2021; and (9) Navaratnasingham's April 16, 2025 "provided by Dolan" admission.

The second leg establishes that the defense told the court the opposite. This leg is established by the court's own official records: MIL No. 6 (CT Vol. 1, pp. 149-151) asserting materials were "not produced in discovery"; MIL No. 17 (CT Vol. 1, p. 238) representing that "Defendant has no idea where this 911 call log or audio recording came from"; the January 27, 2025 hearing transcript in which defense counsel said "we never received these CDs, certainly" (Hr'g Tr. Jan. 27, 2025, p. 34, ll. 1-10); the February 18, 2025 "news to me" representation (Hr'g Tr. Feb. 18, 2025, p. 141, ll. 4-11); the April 16, 2025 trial representation that the supplemental police report was "not anything that we received as part of the subpoena" (RT 7, p. 156, ll. 5-16); and the stacked trial-day denials ("I don't know what recording he is referring to," RT 7, p. 162; "we have no idea who this person is," RT 7, p. 207). These statements are verbatim in the record. They cannot be recharacterized, softened, or qualified. And the Reyna subpoena of February 1, 2024, in PSA's own files, logically forecloses any defense counsel's subsequent claim of ignorance about materials the subpoena itself sought.

The third leg establishes that the trial court acted on the defense's representations to the plaintiff's detriment. The SAC now pleads a Four-Hearing Institutional-Reliance Chain: (1) January 27, 2025 -- implied reliance on the "not produced / lacks foundation" framing produced MIL exclusion of the 911 CDs and transcripts; (2) February 18, 2025 -- expressed reliance ("I see. Okay"; "given their representations") produced denial of continuance and rejection of the § 473 "surprise" motion; (3) February 28, 2025 -- explicitly articulated reliance on officer-of-the-court candor produced the burden-shift ruling requiring plaintiff to re-produce materials already in defense possession; and (4) April 16, 2025 -- continued inference from defense authentication posture produced exclusion of the supplemental police report and deferral of the 911 admission. Each tier of reliance produced a ruling. Each ruling narrowed the plaintiff's evidentiary presentation at a liability-only trial. The court was deceived, and the rulings that followed denied the plaintiff a fair opportunity to present his case.

**The Seven Causes of Action.** The April 2026 SAC in CGC-25-631801 names seven causes of action, each with a distinct evidentiary predicate and a distinct roster of defendants.

*First Cause of Action: Independent Action in Equity for Vacatur; Extrinsic Fraud; Alternatively, Extrinsic Mistake* (Against All Defendants). This is the core *Kulchar* / *Kougasian* / *Olivera* theory: the four-factor test (concealment/deception, structural prevention, reliance, meritorious underlying case) supported by the Two-Phase Lullaby record. Alternative extrinsic-mistake pleading preserves the claim if any defendant defeats fraudulent intent.

*Second Cause of Action: Fraudulent Concealment* (Against Chambers, CSK, PSA, Navaratnasingham). The *OCM Principal Opportunities Fund v. CIBC World Markets Corp.* (2007) 157 Cal.App.4th 835, 859 elements: concealment, duty to disclose, intent to defraud, reliance, damages. The duty to disclose is pleaded as (a) the duty of successor-counsel substitution candor (Chambers and CSK upon transferring the file), and (b) the continuing duty under California Rules of Professional Conduct rule 3.3 (PSA and Navaratnasingham upon discovering the Dolan origin). The claim is pleaded as *noncommunicative* institutional conduct -- file handling, production-history omission -- to defeat the litigation privilege under *Kimmel v. Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202, 211-212 and *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1058.

*Third Cause of Action: Intentional Misrepresentation* (Against Chambers and Navaratnasingham). Civil Code §§ 1709-1710 elements, pleaded against Chambers for his Phase One silence-and-acquiescence in the face of a duty to object, and against Navaratnasingham for her Phase Two tribunal-directed misrepresentations (MIL No. 17 pleadings, February 18, 2025 "news to me" statement, January 27, 2025 witness characterization). The fact that the claim is against individual attorneys, not the firm, is tactically significant: it creates personal exposure that triggers malpractice-carrier involvement.

*Fourth Cause of Action: Aiding and Abetting, Ratification, and Civil Conspiracy* (Against CSAA). *Doctors' Company v. Superior Court* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 39 preserves aiding-and-abetting claims against insurers acting outside the normal insurer role. The three-prong agency framework is pleaded: (1) funding and operational control of the defense; (2) approval and direction of the specific litigation positions that became the court-facing misrepresentations; (3) ratification of the May 4, 2023 file transfer and the attendant production-history omission. Civ. Code §§ 2307, 2310, 2338 supply the ratification and principal-liability framework; *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 54 Cal.4th 826 forecloses any categorical insurer-immunity defense; *Applied Equipment Corp. v. Litton Saudi Arabia Ltd.* (1994) 7 Cal.4th 503 confirms civil conspiracy operates as a joint-liability theory attaching to underlying torts rather than as a freestanding claim.

*Fifth Cause of Action: Declaratory Relief* (Against All Defendants). Standard declaratory vehicle that consolidates the vacatur and estoppel issues into a single judicial declaration.

*Sixth Cause of Action: Attorney Deceit -- Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a)* (Against Chambers, Navaratnasingham, Reyna). This is the most underutilized cause of action in the California practitioner's arsenal. Section 6128(a) provides: "Every attorney is guilty of a misdemeanor who . . . (a) Is guilty of any deceit or collusion, or consents to any deceit or collusion, with intent to deceive the court or any party. . . . In addition to the punishment prescribed . . ., any person is liable to a party who is injured, and damaged thereby, for the amount of any damages sustained, treble damages and reasonable attorney's fees." The statute provides a direct civil remedy against attorneys for deceit of the court, with treble damages and fees as the default recovery. *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 320-325 establishes the illegal-conduct exception to anti-SLAPP; *Lefebvre v. Lefebvre* (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 696, 704-705 applies it to knowing false out-of-court conduct inducing tribunal reliance. Together they create a structural anti-SLAPP defeat channel for the § 6128(a) count that neutralizes the defense's most reflexive motion. The SAC pleads each individual attorney's specific deceit acts: Chambers' failure-to-convey production history; Navaratnasingham's MIL and hearing misrepresentations plus her categorical hearsay misstatement (omitting controlling exceptions under Evid. Code §§ 1220, 1240, 1241, 1271, 1280, and 1414(b)); and Reyna's "not received as part of the subpoena" representation while the subpoena response was in PSA's files.

*Seventh Cause of Action: Constructive Fraud -- Civ. Code § 1573* (Against Chambers, CSK, Navaratnasingham, Reyna, PSA, in the alternative to Counts 2 and 3). Constructive fraud is the breach of a legal or equitable duty flowing from a confidential or fiduciary relationship, *without proof of intent to deceive*. *Assilzadeh v. California Federal Bank* (2000) 82 Cal.App.4th 399, 415. The duty of candor to the tribunal arises from California Rules of Professional Conduct rule 3.3 and from the institutional role of defense counsel as officer of the court. This count preserves the fraud-on-the-court recovery if any defendant defeats scienter on Counts 2 and 3; it covers the same nucleus of conduct without requiring intent. As a structural matter, Count 7 closes the last scienter-escape hatch: if the individual attorneys' depositions produce denials of knowing falsity, Count 7 converts the denials into admissions of reckless disregard, which is the equitable equivalent of fraud.

For the advanced practitioner, the critical drafting insight in an extrinsic fraud complaint is to maintain a clean separation between what happened at trial and why it happened. The defense will argue -- reflexively and predictably -- that the plaintiff is merely relitigating evidentiary disputes that occurred during trial and that the proper remedies were a new trial motion or an appeal. The response is precise: extrinsic fraud is not about the correctness of the court's rulings. It is about the corrupted informational basis on which those rulings were made. When a court relies on representations that were demonstrably false, the rulings are not ordinary evidentiary calls subject to deferential review. They are the product of a decision-making process that was compromised from the outside. The party damaged by that compromise is entitled to an independent remedy in equity -- a remedy that is distinct from and not foreclosed by the appellate process. The April 2026 SAC's gravamen disclaimers (§§ V.A -- V.D) operationalize this principle by explicitly disclaiming reliance on any in-court statement as an injury-producing act and anchoring every count to out-of-court lulling, file-custody conduct, or tribunal-directed misrepresentations within the *Action Apartment Ass'n v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1242-1247 extrinsic-fraud exception.

A well-constructed equity action of this kind serves a function that no single appeal can perform: it asks the court to adjudicate not whether the trial court was wrong as a matter of law, but whether the trial court was deceived as a matter of fact. That question requires its own proceeding, its own evidentiary record, and its own judicial finding.

## VI. The April 2026 Demurrer / Anti-SLAPP Volley and the Consolidated Opposition Architecture

On April 13, 2026, CSAA Insurance Exchange filed a general demurrer to the First Amended Complaint in CGC-25-631802 on eight statutory grounds, together with a Special Motion to Strike under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16. The combined hearing is set for May 13, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. in Department 302. Plaintiff's opposition was filed on April 16, 2026; the proposed Second Amended Complaint was concurrently lodged on April 14, 2026. The consolidated opposition package comprises six core instruments: (1) Opposition to Demurrer (MPA); (2) Opposition to Anti-SLAPP (MPA); (3) Combined Evidentiary Objections; (4) Plaintiff Declaration and Request for Judicial Notice; (5) Consolidated Fraud / Estoppel Brief; and (6) Bench Binder -- Hearing Packet.

The strategic design of the opposition package is worth technical attention because it illustrates the principle of pleading-stage doctrinal preemption.

**Anti-SLAPP step one is parsed claim-by-claim, not collapsed under a "litigation conduct" label.** *Park v. Bd. of Trustees* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057, 1062-1064 requires identification of the injury-producing act underlying each claim. *Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (2021) 11 Cal.5th 995, 1010-1012 requires parsing each claim and each alleged act. *Wilson v. Cable News Network, Inc.* (2019) 7 Cal.5th 871, 884 requires that protected activity be tied to an **element** of the claim, not merely evidentiary support. *Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376, 396 governs mixed-conduct complaints. The SAC includes an element-level *Bonni* matrix (Exhibit R) identifying, for each of the seven causes of action, the element at issue, the act alleged to satisfy the element, whether that act is communicative or noncommunicative, and whether it is protected petitioning activity or falls within a recognized exception.

**Extrinsic fraud is a recognized exception to both the litigation privilege and the anti-SLAPP statute.** *Action Apartment Ass'n v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1242-1247 recognizes the extrinsic-fraud exception. *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 320-325 creates the illegal-conduct exception: "section 425.16 cannot be invoked by a defendant whose assertedly protected activity is illegal as a matter of law." *Lefebvre v. Lefebvre* (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 696, 704-705 applies *Flatley* to knowing false out-of-court conduct inducing tribunal reliance. *Kimmel v. Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202, 211-212 confirms that noncommunicative conduct falls outside the privilege. *Jacob B. v. County of Shasta* (2007) 40 Cal.4th 948, 960 forbids extending § 47(b) by analogy where the communicative core is absent. Together these authorities create a four-channel escape sequence for the SAC: (1) noncommunicative institutional conduct (file-custody transfer, production-history omission) under *Kimmel*; (2) extrinsic fraud on the tribunal under *Action Apartment*; (3) illegal conduct as a matter of law under *Flatley*; and (4) pre-litigation conduct outside the communicative core under *Silberg*.

**Collateral estoppel and res judicata are defeated by the primary-right mismatch.** *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666 and *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335, 341-342 require that the identical issue was "necessarily decided" in the prior action. *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888, 904 and *Boeken v. Philip Morris USA, Inc.* (2010) 48 Cal.4th 788, 798 require "same primary right." The SAC explicitly enumerates the issues *not* adjudicated in the underlying trial (Paragraph 23): the production-history concealment, the out-of-court positions of prior counsel, CSAA's direction and ratification, the inconsistency between out-of-court and tribunal-facing positions, and whether plaintiff was deprived of a fair hearing by extrinsic fraud. The primary-right analysis in the SAC's Paragraph 24 matrix establishes that the primary right in the underlying action (freedom from negligent bodily harm) is categorically different from the primary right in the equity action (freedom from a judgment procured by extrinsic fraud and right to a fair adversary hearing).

The tactical significance of pleading-stage doctrinal preemption is that it forces the demurrer and anti-SLAPP to engage the substance of the fraud allegations, rather than disposing of the claim through a procedural doctrine. Every standard insurer defense has been pleaded around in advance. The demurrer cannot win on litigation privilege, anti-SLAPP, collateral estoppel, or § 473(b) without confronting the extrinsic-fraud, illegal-conduct, and primary-right exceptions. Once those exceptions are squarely before the court, the demurrer becomes a merits fight on a record that the defense's own conduct has already locked down.

## VII. Pre-Service Sequencing: Shaping the Court's Understanding Before the Defense Can Respond

One of the most technically disciplined elements of the plaintiff's strategy is the sequencing of filings before service of the summons. The standard approach to a new lawsuit is file, serve, and wait for responsive pleadings. The approach here is materially different.

Before any defendant received a summons in either new action, the plaintiff had already filed with the court: a notice of related cases identifying all four related proceedings to the court; a request for judicial notice attaching the court records from the underlying trial; an ex parte application for a document preservation order; requests for admission; a memorandum presenting the fraud-on-the-court theory; and a structured demand for judicial summary of facts presenting the factual predicate of the claims with attached supporting records. Each filing served a distinct strategic purpose, and together they shaped the court's understanding of the case before the defense had any opportunity to frame the narrative.

The notice of related cases is more than a procedural formality. Filed before service, it tells the court that this is not an isolated new lawsuit. It is one of four related proceedings -- including a pending appeal -- all arising from the same nucleus of operative fact. A court that understands this context before any defendant appears will approach case management, scheduling, and coordination with an entirely different perspective than a court confronting what appears to be a standalone complaint. The case management conference in CGC-25-631801 is scheduled for May 27, 2026 in Department 610, with the 631802 demurrer/anti-SLAPP hearing on May 13, 2026 in Department 302. The calendar is now coordinated across both actions.

The pre-service application for a document preservation order is the most time-sensitive element. In fraud cases involving institutional defendants with substantial document management infrastructure, the window between service and potential evidence alteration is real. An insurer that has spent four years managing a litigation posture predicated on the first and second lies has institutional incentive to "lose" or restructure internal communications once it learns it faces three independent proceedings. By obtaining a preservation order before serving the summons, the plaintiff creates a legal obligation to preserve before any incentive to destroy exists. More importantly, the existence of the order signals to every defendant, from the moment of service, that the plaintiff anticipated their documentary management tendencies and that any post-service loss of materials will be treated as willful violation of a court order. That signal has behavioral effect independent of any subsequent enforcement motion.

Think of this sequencing as staking out the terrain before the battle begins. By the time the defense's lawyers open the summons, the court already has context; the preservation obligation already exists; the factual framework is already in the court file in a form that cannot be removed; and the requests for admission are running their thirty-day statutory clock. The defense enters the litigation having already lost the first move.

## VIII. Requests for Admission as Binary Compulsion Architecture

Requests for admission are a discovery weapon chronically underused in civil litigation. Practitioners deploy them as a rote procedural step rather than as a designed pressure system. The *Rosario* fraud actions deploy them as exactly that: a structured binary-choice mechanism in which every possible response advances the plaintiff's theory.

The drafting principle is binary compulsion. Each request is constructed so that either admission or specific denial creates a problem for the responding party. If the insurer admits that the denial letter stated the investigation was concluded, the representation is established. If the insurer denies it, the document itself contradicts the denial. If the insurer admits that it had not obtained the 911 recording before issuing the denial, the falsity element is established. If the insurer denies it, discovery into the claim file tests the denial -- and if the file contains no record of a 911 request before February 25, 2021, the denial creates a false statement in discovery. If the insurer admits that its claim file contains no record of any request for law enforcement records prior to the denial, the investigation was a sham. If the insurer denies it, the file must produce the request records.

The structural elegance of this approach is that the insurer occupies an internally inconsistent position: it must simultaneously maintain that its investigation was "concluded" while its agents later claimed to have "no idea" where the 911 recording came from. These two positions cannot both be true. Any set of admissions or denials that tries to reconcile them will produce an internal contradiction in the discovery record. That contradiction becomes impeachment material and potentially a basis for sanctions under Code of Civil Procedure § 2023.030.

Served simultaneously with the complaint, the requests create early pressure before the defendant has fully retained and briefed outside counsel, before it has reviewed the claim file systematically, and before it has coordinated its response with co-defendants. The thirty-day statutory deadline runs from the date of service. The pressure is immediate. The defendant must choose positions before it fully understands the landscape. And those early positions, once taken, become commitments that constrain every subsequent strategic choice.

## IX. The Deposition Trap: Binary Compulsion at the Individual Level

The most technically sophisticated element of the post-filing strategy is the deposition architecture. Because the plaintiff locked the production timeline into the court record through uncontradicted sworn testimony (Jessup, February 18, 2025), the public substitution filing (May 4, 2023, Transaction ID 69953800), the Reyna subpoena (February 1, 2024), and Navaratnasingham's own April 16, 2025 "Dolan" admission, each individual defendant attorney faces a forced binary at deposition in which both possible answers lead to independent liability. The binary compulsion is mathematical, not merely rhetorical.

Imagine the trap as a vise with two faces. On the front face: did the successor defense counsel review the file when it was transferred to them? On the back face: did they review it before representing to the court that the materials were "unknown"? Either face, when pressed, generates crushing liability.

If Navaratnasingham reviewed the file at transfer -- which is the professional responsibility standard for any attorney taking over an active litigation -- then she possessed the 911 recording and all served discovery materials for nearly two years before standing before the trial judge and declaring them to be "news to me." That declaration was not an oversight. It was a knowing misrepresentation to the court about a material fact, made for the purpose of obtaining a procedural advantage, with actual judicial reliance. That is fraud on the court in its most direct form -- and now, under the April 2026 SAC, the direct predicate for a Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a) deceit claim with treble damages and attorney's fees.

If Navaratnasingham did not review the file at transfer, then the formal pretrial representations in MIL Nos. 6 and 17 asserting that materials had not been produced in discovery were made without any review of the file on which those representations purported to be based. A lawyer who signs motions asserting specific facts about what is or is not in the discovery record, without reviewing the record to verify those assertions, is operating with recklessness of a degree that courts treat as the legal equivalent of knowing falsity -- and is professional negligence so severe that it triggers malpractice liability independently of any fraud theory.

Reyna faces a variant of the same vise: either he received the SFPD subpoena response (which is a matter of record, as SFPD produced materials responsive to Order No. CA1147102-021 on February 26, 2024) and denied knowing about them when he represented at trial that the supplemental police report was "not anything that we received as part of the subpoena" (RT 7, p. 156, ll. 5-16), or he made formal representations about the evidentiary record without following up on his own discovery requests. Neither answer absolves him. The April 2026 SAC names him individually in the § 6128(a) deceit count.

Chambers faces a different binary: either the transferred file contained the served discovery materials, in which case successor counsel possessed them for twenty months; or the transferred file did not contain them, in which case Chambers bears responsibility for a deficient file transfer that stripped the successor of materials that had been served and used in depositions -- a deficiency that enabled the subsequent fraud. Notably, defendants' own pleading in this action (Case No. CGC-25-631801, Clerk File No. 10157174, p. 6, ll. 15-18) has stated that Chambers transferred the file "without ensuring proper documentation and conveyance of the production history to successor counsel" -- a judicial admission the SAC now treats as evidentiary corroboration of the Phase One concealment channel.

CSAA faces the widest binary: either it directed the litigation strategy, including the decision to maintain the "unknown provenance" posture before trial, in which case the institutional connection between the first lie and the courtroom fraud is direct and the pattern establishes punitive liability under Civil Code § 3294 and § 2338 agency principal liability; or it exercised no oversight over the litigation it funded, in which case it was willfully blind to fraud committed in its name -- and willful blindness courts treat as functional direction for corporate ratification purposes under Civ. Code §§ 2307 and 2310. The SAC documents CSAA's institutional absence from all four pretrial hearings at which the misrepresentations were made, and treats that absence plus continued funding as ratification under the *Doctors' Company v. Superior Court* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 39 framework.

The vise design is important because it creates structural fracturing among co-defendants. No single defendant can give the answer that minimizes its own exposure without simultaneously generating devastating liability for another defendant. The only way for Navaratnasingham to avoid the "knowing lie" answer is to say she never reviewed the file -- and that answer destroys itself against the professional responsibility standard and implicates Chambers' file transfer. The only way for CSAA to avoid the "directed the fraud" answer is to say it had no oversight -- and that answer establishes willful blindness while pointing fingers at its own retained attorneys. The only way for Chambers to avoid the "deficient transfer" answer is to say the file was complete -- and that answer proves Navaratnasingham had the materials for twenty months. The only way for Reyna to avoid the "ignored my own subpoena" answer is to say he relied on Navaratnasingham's framing -- and that answer implicates Navaratnasingham's knowing falsity.

In multi-defendant litigation, when the optimal answers for each defendant are mutually incompatible, team defense dissolves before depositions begin. The fracturing produces its own settlement pressure: once each defendant's outside counsel advises the client about its independent exposure and the impossibility of coordinated defense, the path to global settlement becomes the only rational strategy.

## X. The Crime-Fraud Exception and § 6128(a) as Privilege Demolition Mechanisms

In fraud-on-the-court litigation, the most damaging evidence will typically reside behind attorney-client privilege and work product protection: internal communications between the insurer and its attorneys about litigation strategy, billing records showing what was reviewed and when, internal memoranda about specific evidence, and written communications about the pretrial posture. The defense will assert privilege over all of it.

The crime-fraud exception to the attorney-client privilege (Evidence Code § 956(a)) provides that the privilege does not apply when the attorney's services were sought or obtained to enable or aid anyone to commit a crime or fraud. In the *Rosario* context, the exception operates as a privilege demolition mechanism: if the attorneys' communications with the insurer were in furtherance of making false representations to the court -- and fraud on the court is a crime under Penal Code § 182 -- the privilege that would ordinarily protect those communications evaporates.

The April 2026 SAC's Sixth Cause of Action further calibrates this demolition by invoking specific Penal Code predicates in Paragraph 153: Penal Code §§ 115 (filing false or forged instrument), 118 (perjury), 132 (offering false evidence), 134 (preparing false evidence), 127 (subornation of perjury by analogy), and 182(a)(5) (conspiracy to obstruct justice). Each statutory predicate independently triggers the *Flatley* illegal-conduct exception to anti-SLAPP and the crime-fraud exception to the privilege. The privilege demolition is therefore not derivative of a single theory; it is redundant across multiple statutory channels.

The practical execution follows a logical sequence. First, the pre-service preservation order creates an evidentiary baseline -- all communications must be preserved before any "housekeeping" can eliminate them. Second, targeted discovery demands reach the specific chain of custody for the defense file, internal communications about the contested evidence, and billing records that would show what the attorneys reviewed and when. Third, when the defense asserts privilege -- as it certainly will -- the motion to compel invoking the crime-fraud exception rests not on allegations but on the public court record: the pretrial hearing transcripts showing the "news to me" representation and the February 28, 2025 officer-of-court reliance statement, juxtaposed against the prior counsel's uncontradicted sworn testimony that the materials were served two years earlier, Navaratnasingham's April 16, 2025 "provided by Dolan" admission, and the Reyna subpoena itself. That juxtaposition is a prima facie showing that the representations to the court were false and were made in the context of an ongoing professional relationship with the insurer. Fourth, and most consequentially: if the billing records show that the defense attorneys billed time for reviewing the defense file in the weeks before the January 17, 2025 MIL filings -- standard trial preparation -- those records would demonstrate that the attorneys had contemporaneous knowledge of the file's contents at the very moment they were telling the court the evidence was unknown.

Even if a court ultimately denies the motion to compel, the process has generated something that settlement negotiators on the defense side understand very clearly: there is now a public record of the defense asserting privilege over communications directly related to a fraud allegation, and the risk that a court orders in camera review -- and finds billing records showing file review before the pretrial denials -- hangs over the entire litigation. That sword, even if never swung, has coercive value.

## XI. The *Prentice* / *Mai* Architecture: Converting Institutional Victory Into Consequential Liability

The most structurally elegant element of the fraud action's damages theory is the deployment of *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1964) 225 Cal.App.2d 245 to convert the insurer's trial victory into consequential liability.

The American Rule requires each party to bear its own attorney fees absent a statutory exception or contractual provision. *Prentice* is one of the recognized exceptions: when a party is forced into litigation with a third party as the direct, foreseeable consequence of another's tortious conduct, the attorney fees and costs of that third-party litigation are recoverable as consequential damages of the original tort. The rationale is straightforward: the tortfeasor who forces the plaintiff into litigation should not benefit from the rule that makes each party pay its own way.

Applied to this case, the theory is devastating in its simplicity. The insurer's fraudulent denial letter -- a pre-litigation misrepresentation asserting investigative finality before any meaningful investigation was performed -- foreseeably forced the plaintiff into four years of litigation to obtain and authenticate evidence the insurer had represented it had already reviewed. The entire cost of the underlying litigation -- attorney fees, court costs, deposition expenses, expert fees, transcript costs, and the appeal costs -- becomes the insurer's consequential damages bill in the fraud action. The insurer's "victory" at trial is, under *Prentice*, the very measure of its liability in the fraud action.

The conversion is intellectually satisfying precisely because it turns the logic of institutional denial against itself. The insurer denied early to force the claimant to give up or settle cheaply. When that strategy failed and the plaintiff litigated to completion, the insurer won a jury verdict. But the litigation the insurer forced through its fraudulent denial is now the damages pool in the fraud action. The longer and more expensive that litigation, the greater the exposure. The institutional strategy of exhaustion through attrition becomes, under *Prentice*, an engine for generating its own consequential damages liability.

The *Mai v. HKT Cal Inc.* (2021) 66 Cal.App.5th 504 limitations on *Prentice* recovery require careful pleading discipline. *Mai* requires that the fees be actually incurred, reasonably necessary, proximately caused by the tort, and segregable from fees that would have been incurred regardless of the fraud. A complaint that explicitly acknowledges these requirements, commits to proof by billing records and testimony, and provides a category-by-category causal analysis linking each fee type to the denial letter's misrepresentation -- rather than to the plaintiff's independent litigation choices -- positions the theory to survive a motion to strike for speculative damages. The April 2026 SAC in CGC-25-631802 segregates damages into itemized lanes: transcript and court-reporter costs; subpoena, process-service, and certification fees; document-preparation, authentication, and verification costs; and professional and logistical costs of reconstructing the claim history. Each lane is supported by specific exemplars and is traceable to the denial-letter misrepresentation.

In the CGC-25-631801 equity action, the damages architecture layers additional recovery channels: Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a) treble damages against the individual attorneys; Civ. Code § 3294 punitive damages against the firms and the insurer; Civ. Code § 1573 constructive fraud compensatory recovery without requiring scienter proof; CCP § 2023.030 discovery sanctions; and restitution of the $76,308.90 memorandum of costs entered on August 8, 2025 in the underlying action. Multiple independent damages lanes, segregated and independently supported, ensure that the defeat of any single lane does not collapse the damages architecture. Each lane has its own evidentiary foundation, its own causation theory, and its own legal basis. The aggregate exposure across all lanes against a multi-billion-dollar institutional defendant is calibrated to exceed the cost of global settlement -- not as an accident of pleading, but as a design choice.

## XII. The Cross-Front Contradiction Web: Why Every Defense Posture Fails

The most distinctive operational feature of the entire architecture -- and the one most likely to produce settlement pressure before any proceeding concludes -- is the cross-front contradiction web. Each of the three proceedings creates a factual and legal posture that contradicts the optimal defense posture in the other two. There is no single set of positions that satisfies all three fronts simultaneously.

Think of it as a logical triangle with the two lies at its foundation. The first lie (CSAA's February 25, 2021 "concluded investigation" denial) and the second lie (the January 17, 2025 MIL denials of receipt, culminating in Navaratnasingham's April 16, 2025 "provided by Dolan" admission) are structurally linked, and every defense position that tries to maintain one collapses the other.

Consider the appeal. To defeat the appeal, the defense must argue that the trial was fair and the verdict reliable. That argument requires the defense to endorse the evidentiary and procedural landscape at trial as legitimate. But the trial's evidentiary landscape was shaped by the second lie -- by defense representations about unknown evidence that the prior counsel's testimony proved were false and that the defense's own lead counsel admitted were false in open court on April 16, 2025. Defending the appeal as a legitimate proceeding implicitly validates the factual record showing the court relied on false representations, and that validated factual record is the proof spine of the equity action. The CCP § 614 error at the core of the appeal was itself produced in an evidentiary landscape where the defense had suppressed key materials through the "unknown provenance" misrepresentations; the instructional error and the extrinsic fraud are therefore not alternative theories but overlapping aspects of the same procedural corruption.

Consider the equity action. To defeat it, the defense will characterize the attorneys' courtroom representations as "mere advocacy" -- zealous lawyering on behalf of a client, not fraud on the court. But the appeal record proves the court actually relied on those representations to make adverse procedural and evidentiary rulings, and the February 28, 2025 transcript memorializes that reliance in the court's own words. If the representations were "mere advocacy" with no operative judicial effect, the appeal fails on prejudice grounds. If the court did rely on them, the advocacy was not "mere" -- it was operative misrepresentation with documented judicial effect. Either the trial was affected by the statements or it was not. The defense cannot maintain both answers simultaneously. Furthermore, Navaratnasingham's April 16, 2025 "provided by Dolan" admission forecloses any "ignorance or mistake" defense to the judicial estoppel analysis under *MW Erectors, Inc. v. Niederhauser Ornamental & Metal Works Co.* (2005) 36 Cal.4th 412, 422.

Consider the fraud action. To defeat it, the insurer must argue that the denial letter's "concluded investigation" representation was accurate. But if the investigation was truly concluded after twenty-one days, the insurer must have obtained the 911 recording in which its own insured admitted, "I barely hit him with anything." If the insurer had that recording and still denied liability, it suppressed its own insured's admission -- an aggravated form of fraud. If the insurer did not have the recording, the "concluded investigation" representation was false. The defense attorneys' claim that the recording was "unknown" to them years after the denial corroborates that the insurer never obtained it, making the denial letter's falsity self-evident. There is no position that simultaneously validates the denial letter, explains the attorneys' ignorance, and defeats the fraud claim.

The logical structure is captured in a simple binary tree. If the investigation was complete, the lawyers lied (equity action wins). If the investigation was not complete, the denial letter was false (fraud action wins). If the lawyers directed their posture independently, they are personally liable (equity action, § 6128(a)) and the insurer ratified their conduct (fraud action and equity action Count 4, punitive exposure). If the insurer directed the lawyers, the institutional connection is proven for agency principal liability under Civ. Code § 2338 and *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 54 Cal.4th 826. Every branch of the logical tree leads to liability in at least two proceedings. There is no branch where the defense escapes unscathed.

This is the architecture of interlocking constraint. It is not a clever litigation strategy in the conventional sense. It is a logical trap constructed from the defense's own documentary and testimonial record, deployed simultaneously across three proceedings so that every strategic choice becomes a concession.

## XIII. Procedural Precision as Strategic Foundation

A point that experienced practitioners sometimes undervalue: in complex multi-front litigation, meticulous procedural compliance is not administrative housekeeping. It is a strategic foundation that prevents the defense from obtaining early technical victories that would disrupt the pressure system before substantive engagement begins.

Defense counsel in institutional litigation are trained to scan opposing filings for technical deficiencies as a first-line defense: improper formatting, untimely service, missing required tables, defective notice. Each deficiency is an invitation for a motion that, if granted, creates delay without any substantive engagement on the merits. A motion to strike for a formatting error, a demurrer for lack of required content, a quash motion for defective service -- none of these engage the substance of the fraud claims. All of them create procedural disruption that benefits the defense by consuming time and resources and delaying the pressure that the architecture is designed to generate.

The strategic response is to eliminate the entire category of technical attack through meticulous compliance. Every filing that meets every applicable rule requirement is a filing the defense must engage on the merits. When the defense cannot find a technical deficiency, it must confront the substance. And on the substance, as this analysis has demonstrated, the defense has no viable position that does not concede a damaging element in at least one of the three proceedings. The April 2026 consolidated opposition package (six core instruments plus consolidated bundles, master packet, exhibit appendices, and bench binder) exemplifies this principle: every California Rule of Court compliance item -- including electronic bookmarks (Rule 3.1110(f)(4)), exhibit separator sheets (Rule 3.1110(f)), and record citation standards -- is pre-addressed, leaving no procedural surface for the demurrer reply.

## XIV. The Settlement Calculus and the Architecture of Global Pressure

For the defense-side risk analyst, this architecture resolves to a single question: at what premium does settlement become rational? The answer requires analyzing each defendant's independent exposure across each proceeding, the compounding effect of simultaneous losses, and the institutional risks that transcend formal damages quantification.

The insurer faces compensatory damages in the fraud action -- consequential litigation costs under *Prentice* spanning the entire underlying proceeding, lost earnings, emotional distress, and fraud-delta physical harm; punitive damages under Civil Code § 3294 based on institutional fraud, officer ratification, and conscious disregard; UCL restitution and injunctive relief potentially affecting statewide claims-handling operations; aiding-and-abetting exposure in the equity action under *Doctors' Company*; monetary exposure in the equity action for ancillary relief and for the $76,308.90 costs taxed against the plaintiff; and full personal injury compensatory damages upon retrial after reversal or vacatur. Institutional discovery in the fraud action -- claim file contents, denial-letter templates, adjuster training materials, claim-closure metrics, and the specifics of CSAA's panel-counsel arrangements -- creates regulatory, legislative, and media exposure that cannot be quantified in damages terms but that rational institutional actors price heavily in settlement analysis.

The defense attorneys face personal liability for fraud-on-the-court damages; Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a) treble damages and attorney's fees as the default recovery on the deceit count; Civ. Code § 1573 constructive fraud compensatory damages without a scienter defense; malpractice exposure that triggers their professional liability carriers; and the career-ending consequence of a judicial finding of fraud on the court, which provides the foundation for State Bar disciplinary proceedings. Malpractice carrier involvement introduces an external settlement driver: professional liability insurers have independent financial incentives to resolve cases that expose their insured to findings of intentional wrongdoing, because such findings may void coverage and increase future premiums. That external pressure operates independently of the civil defendants' litigation strategies and is not controllable by any coordination agreement between co-defendants.

The compounding effect is geometric rather than additive. A judicial fraud finding in the equity action is the highest possible punitive damages multiplier in the fraud action. An appellate reversal returns the personal injury claim to retrial in conditions where the defendant's own authenticated admission is already in evidence, the fraud-on-the-court theory has been publicly aired, and the CCP § 614 instructional error that produced the 9-3 verdict will not recur. A fraud action verdict with substantial punitive damages strengthens the equity action's case for ancillary monetary relief and provides an institutional fraud finding that the appeal can deploy to demonstrate the corrupted character of the original proceeding. A judicial ruling granting the motion to compel privileged communications under the crime-fraud exception -- even if limited to in camera review -- exposes the claim file and internal litigation-strategy communications to institutional scrutiny that no amount of settlement discipline can undo.

The only rational exit is a global settlement across all three fronts at a premium reflecting the full scope of multi-front exposure, the compounding effect of simultaneous losses, and the institutional risks that exceed formal damages estimates. The architecture was designed to produce that conclusion before any proceeding reaches judgment -- and the fracturing among co-defendants, produced by the binary deposition structure, ensures that the defense cannot hold together long enough to fight all three fronts simultaneously.

## XV. Lessons for Advanced Practice

The *Rosario* cohort offers transferable principles for senior practitioners across a range of civil litigation contexts.

**Treat early institutional communications as representational events.** When a corporate or institutional opponent makes a written statement implying process, diligence, or completed action, identify the factual predicates that statement implies. Test whether those predicates are true. The gap between the representation and the underlying record is the fraud anchor. Fix the timestamp. The anchor does not require proving intent. It requires only that the representation was false when made.

**Plead the injury-producing act as non-communicative institutional conduct where possible.** The April 2026 SAC in CGC-25-631802 rebuilt the theory of liability around CSAA's non-communicative institutional failure to investigate, with the denial letter as evidentiary corroboration rather than the operative wrong. This structural choice defeats the litigation privilege and anti-SLAPP statute on the face of the pleading. Practitioners confronting institutional defendants should ask whether the actionable conduct can be characterized as an omission or institutional practice, with any communicative artifacts pleaded as proof of that omission rather than as the wrong itself.

**Recognize when the opponent has doubled down.** The second lie -- the defense's pretrial denial of receipt for materials they had possessed for nearly two years, followed by the reversal on Trial Day 7 -- was the critical strategic error that transformed an adverse trial verdict into a three-front institutional accountability case. The doubling down was not forced; it was a choice. The defense chose to maintain the denial posture at the most dangerous moment -- immediately before trial, in open court, in the presence of a former attorney who could and did contradict it under oath, and ultimately reversed it mid-trial through an unguarded admission. When an opponent doubles down on a provably false position in circumstances where a witness exists who can lock in the timeline, the plaintiff's strategic task is to recognize what has been handed to them and to build the architecture that makes the contradiction undeniable.

**Use the former attorney as the pinwheel.** When prior counsel has direct, personal knowledge of events that contradict the current defense's representations -- particularly events within that attorney's own professional conduct, such as serving discovery or using materials in depositions -- that attorney is an officer of the court whose testimony carries an authority that no ordinary witness can match. Structure the evidentiary timeline to create the conditions for that testimony to enter the record. Once it does, the contradiction is locked. The defense cannot attack it without attacking a fellow officer of the court, and no court will receive that attack without significant skepticism.

**Build the three-leg framework for process fraud.** Knowledge and access; false representations to the court; court reliance on those representations. Establish each leg through a different type of evidence -- testimony for the first, official filings and transcripts for the second, the court's own ruling record for the third. The framework is self-reinforcing because attacking any single leg requires the defense to confront the others. A defense that denies the first leg must call the former attorney a perjurer. A defense that minimizes the second leg must explain away verbatim transcript language. A defense that argues no reliance must contend with the court's own written rulings -- including, as in *Rosario*, an articulated officer-of-court reliance statement in the record.

**Calibrate causes of action to scienter gradients.** The April 2026 SAC's seven-count structure reflects a scienter-gradient discipline: Count 3 (intentional misrepresentation) requires proof of intent; Count 7 (constructive fraud, Civ. Code § 1573) requires only breach of a duty of candor without intent; Count 6 (§ 6128(a) attorney deceit) requires intent to deceive the tribunal but triples the damages; Count 4 (aiding-and-abetting and ratification) reaches the institutional principal without requiring that the principal directly committed the underlying wrong. A well-constructed SAC includes at least one count that survives if the defendant defeats scienter, at least one count that triples the damages if the defendant fails to defeat scienter, and at least one count that reaches the institutional principal regardless of which individual actor proves the scienter point.

**Shape the court's understanding before the defense can.** In fraud litigation against institutional defendants, file related-case notices, judicial notices, and preservation applications before service. By the time the defense opens the summons, the court already has context, the preservation obligation already exists, and the plaintiff's factual framework is already in the court file. The defense enters the litigation having already lost the first move.

**Design binary deposition questions.** For each individual defendant, identify the core factual question where both possible answers lead to liability. Structure the sequence so that the questions are posed in the order that maximizes fracturing among co-defendants. The fracturing is not a byproduct of aggressive questioning. It is the mathematical consequence of a closed evidentiary system in which no single defendant's optimal answer is compatible with any other defendant's optimal answer.

**Isolate appellate issues.** A focused brief on a categorical legal error reviewed de novo is more powerful than a multi-issue brief where marginal arguments dilute strong ones. The April 2026 Opening Brief revision -- narrowing the appeal to the single CCP § 614 / "statutes are not evidence" error -- illustrates the principle. The jury's own written question during deliberations is the strongest possible prejudice evidence because it documents, in the jury's own words, exactly what the withheld law was material to. That question, combined with a bare-minimum verdict, requires no speculative prejudice analysis. The harm is on the record.

**Preempt defense motions with binding authority.** File memoranda addressing the defense's most likely arguments before those arguments are made. Cite controlling precedent. A preemptive memorandum converts the anticipated defense motion into an opportunity to lock in the plaintiff's legal framework before the defense has set the framing. When the court first encounters the legal issues through the plaintiff's authorities and analysis rather than the defense's demurrer, the burden of persuasion effectively shifts. The SAC's element-level *Bonni* matrix (Exhibit R) operationalizes this principle by parsing each cause of action against the anti-SLAPP and litigation-privilege doctrines before the defense has filed its moving papers.

**Never underestimate the opponent's own witnesses.** The defendant's five-word answer on the stand -- "Yes. That is my voice" (RT 8, p. 108, l. 28) -- authenticated the recording his lawyers had spent months characterizing as unknown and unverifiable. Navaratnasingham's six-word mid-trial admission -- "They were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure" (RT 7, p. 213, ll. 13-15) -- reversed four years of denials in a single sentence. Both moments were worth more than volumes of extrinsic evidence. Structure trial and discovery to create the conditions for the opposing party's own principals to authenticate or admit what the opposition has denied. When the opposing party's own witness collapses the denial, the fraud theory requires no argument. The record completes the proof.

## XVI. Conclusion: The Two Lies, the Silence, and the Web That Held

The *Rosario* cohort will be studied not because it involves an unusual accident or an exceptional legal theory. It will be studied because it demonstrates, with unusual clarity, how an adversary's own lies become the architecture of their defeat.

The first lie was institutional and early: a February 25, 2021 declaration of investigative finality issued before any investigation of consequence had been performed. That lie forced the plaintiff into litigation and set the fraud anchor. The second lie was tactical and late: the January 17, 2025 pretrial denial of receipt for materials the defense had possessed for nearly two years, repeated orally at the February 18, 2025 hearing as "news to me," reinforced at the February 28, 2025 hearing through the tribunal's articulated officer-of-court reliance, and finally reversed on April 16, 2025 when Navaratnasingham admitted in open court that the materials had been "provided by Dolan." Between the first lie and the second lay 883 days of silence -- silence during which defense counsel received the materials, attended depositions at which they were used, transferred the file, subpoenaed SFPD for the same materials, received discovery responses identifying them, and never once raised an authenticity challenge.

But the second lie was made in the presence of the one witness who could undo it: the prior attorney who had served those materials, used them in depositions, and transferred them with the file. When Jeremy Jessup, as an officer of the court, testified under oath that the defense had the materials since 2021, the second lie became a documented instance of fraud on the court. The pinwheel turned. The contradiction that had existed privately in the defense's own file was now a matter of public record, established by testimony no one had contradicted and no one has since attempted to impeach -- and reinforced by the defense's own lead counsel's unguarded admission two months later.

At that moment, the two lies, the 883 days of silence between them, and their logical interdependency were visible in the court record. The plaintiff recognized the structure and built three independent proceedings from it. The April 2026 amendments -- the narrowed Opening Brief, the seven-count equity SAC, the three-count fraud SAC with its non-communicative-omission gravamen, and the six-instrument consolidated opposition package -- hardened each proceeding's legal foundations while preserving the architectural interdependency. The appeal stands on instructional error alone and requires no fraud theory. The equity action stands on the Two-Phase Lullaby alone and requires no appeal. The fraud action stands on the first lie alone and requires neither.

Together, they create a closed logical web from which there is no exit. A defense position that survives one proceeding concedes an element in another. A factual admission in one case feeds the evidentiary record in the others. A win on any single front cascades into the remaining two in ways that compound the exposure geometrically. And the April 2026 inclusion of the § 6128(a) attorney deceit count and the § 1573 constructive fraud count closed the last scienter-escape hatches by ensuring that personal attorney liability persists regardless of which intent theory the defense attacks.

The insurance company wrote the first lie. The defense attorneys committed the second -- and through 883 days of silence, made the second lie structurally inevitable. The defendant authenticated the recording his lawyers had denied knowing about. The defense's lead attorney admitted in open court that the materials had been "provided by Dolan." The jury asked the question. The judge gave the wrong answer. The plaintiff's contribution was to see what the record had produced, understand its legal implications, and build the architecture that turned the defense's own conduct into a trap from which there is no rational exit.

For the advanced practitioner, this is the lesson: the most powerful litigation architecture is one built not from your own arguments but from your opponent's own contradictions. The lawyer's job is not to be smarter than the defense. The lawyer's job is to be disciplined enough to recognize when the defense has built the trap for itself -- and structured enough to ensure that every element of the trap is properly documented, properly framed, and deployed simultaneously across proceedings designed so that a win on any front cascades into the others.

The two lies held the web together. The 883 days of silence between them turned the web into something irreversible. Once the prior attorney testified and the defense's own lead counsel admitted the Dolan origin, the web began to collapse inward. What remained was not the plaintiff's theory. It was the defense's own record, turned back on itself.
