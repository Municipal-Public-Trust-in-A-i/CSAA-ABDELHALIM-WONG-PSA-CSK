\section{IV. LEGAL BASIS}\label{iv.-legal-basis}

\subsection{A. Court Records Are Proper Subjects of Judicial Notice}\label{a.-court-records-are-proper-subjects-of-judicial-notice}

Evidence Code \textsection{} 452(d) permits judicial notice of court records. The documents listed as Exhibits A through H are records of the Superior Court of California, County of San Francisco.

\subsection{B. Facts Not Reasonably Subject to Dispute}\label{b.-facts-not-reasonably-subject-to-dispute}

Evidence Code \textsection{} 452(h) permits judicial notice of facts capable of immediate and accurate determination by resort to sources of reasonably indisputable accuracy. The CSAA denial letter (Exhibit I) is a document whose existence and contents are not reasonably subject to dispute.

\subsection{C. Statutes and Regulations}\label{c.-statutes-and-regulations}

Evidence Code \textsection{}\textsection{} 452(a) and (b) permit judicial notice of statutes and regulations. The statutes and regulations listed as Exhibits J through P are proper subjects of mandatory judicial notice.

\subsection{D. Judicial Notice Is Mandatory Upon Proper Request}\label{d.-judicial-notice-is-mandatory-upon-proper-request}

Evidence Code \textsection{} 453 provides that the trial court shall take judicial notice of any matter specified in Section 452 if a party requests it and gives adverse parties sufficient notice.
