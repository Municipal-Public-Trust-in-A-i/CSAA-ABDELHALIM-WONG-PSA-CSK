\section{3. Underlying Facts --- The Collision and the Denial}\label{underlying-facts-the-collision-and-the-denial}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{4}
\item
  On \textbf{February 4, 2021}, I was involved in a motor vehicle collision in San Francisco with CSAA's insured, Subhi Abdelhalim.
\item
  On \textbf{February 25, 2021}---just 21 days after the collision---CSAA sent me a denial letter for \textbf{Claim No.~1004-09-1054}. The letter was signed by \textbf{Sarah Rash, Claims Representative}.
\item
  The denial letter stated, in pertinent part:
\end{enumerate}

\begin{quote}
``We have concluded our investigation of your claim. Based on our investigation, and after carefully reviewing the facts and circumstances of your claim, we have concluded that our insured is not liable\ldots{}''
\end{quote}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{7}
\tightlist
\item
  At the time I received this denial, I was an unrepresented claimant with no independent means to verify the scope of CSAA's investigation. I accepted the denial as the product of a legitimate investigative process because the letter expressly represented that CSAA had ``concluded'' its investigation and ``carefully reviewed'' the facts.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\section{4. The Evidence CSAA Failed to Obtain}\label{the-evidence-csaa-failed-to-obtain}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{8}
\item
  As I later learned through the underlying litigation, at the time of the February 25, 2021 denial, CSAA had not obtained:

  \begin{enumerate}
  \def\labelenumii{(\alph{enumii})}
  \item
    The \textbf{911 audio recording} from the date of the collision---which contains the insured's own statements acknowledging contact with me;
  \item
    My \textbf{contemporaneous 911 report} stating that the driver struck me;
  \item
    \textbf{Body-worn camera footage} from responding officers---which contains a third-party eyewitness (Justin Rosemond) independently corroborating that the driver struck me.
  \end{enumerate}
\item
  Taken together, these three sources constituted virtually uncontested evidence of liability: confession, victim report, and eyewitness corroboration. CSAA denied my claim without obtaining any of them.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\section{5. The Three-Anchor Delayed-Discovery Sequence}\label{the-three-anchor-delayed-discovery-sequence}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{10}
\tightlist
\item
  I did not discover the facts constituting the fraud until the following sequence of events:
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{(\alph{enumi})}
\item
  \textbf{January 17, 2025:} Defense counsel in the underlying action filed Motion in Limine No.~17, asserting that the 911 materials had ``not been produced in discovery'' and were of ``unknown provenance.'' This revealed that CSAA had never obtained them during its 2021 ``investigation.''
\item
  \textbf{February 18, 2025:} At a pretrial hearing, Jeremy Jessup (Dolan Law Firm) testified that these materials had been produced to defense counsel in August-September 2022. Defense counsel simultaneously stated the 911 call was ``news to me.'' This direct contradiction established that the materials existed and had been in defense possession, but the non-investigation had been concealed.
\item
  \textbf{April 16, 2025:} Defense counsel admitted on the trial record: ``They were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure.'' (RT 7, p.~213, ll. 13--15.)
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{11}
\tightlist
\item
  These three events, taken together, constitute the first point at which I could reasonably have determined that CSAA's 2021 representation of investigative completeness was false.
\end{enumerate}
