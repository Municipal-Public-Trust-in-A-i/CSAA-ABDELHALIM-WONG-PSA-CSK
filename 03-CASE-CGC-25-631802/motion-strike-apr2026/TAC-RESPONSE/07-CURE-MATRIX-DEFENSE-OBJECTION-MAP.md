## Detailed Cure Analysis

### Objection 1: *Trope* — Pro-Per Fees Barred

**Defense Argument:** Pro se litigant cannot recover attorney's fees under *Trope v. Katz*.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 22:** Express deletion of pro-per fee demands
- **¶¶ 23–24:** *Prentice* fees pleaded as tort damages element (three-element test):
  - Element 1: Fraud
  - Element 2: Induced litigation
  - Element 3: Fees recoverable as damages, not fee award
- **¶ 24A:** Four-line-item *Prentice* enumeration — (1) professional fees/costs incurred in CGC-21-594102; (2) the $75,000 cost judgment as induced-litigation damages (re-framing MPA2 footnote 2 rhetoric into Plaintiff's damages element); (3) transcript/record/appeal preparation costs; (4) investigative/records-acquisition costs (911, CAD, BWC) that CSAA itself should have obtained.

**Authority:** *Trope v. Katz* (1995) 11 Cal.4th 274; *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618, 620

---

### Objection 2: Oppression / Malice Not Pleaded

**Defense Argument:** Punitive damages allegations fail to meet § 3294 standard.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶¶ 25–27:** *Lazar* particulars block with § 3294(b) managing-agent ratification
- **¶ 25A:** Pleading-stage vs. trial-stage bright line — *G.D. Searle & Co. v. Superior Court* (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 22, 29: at pleading, plaintiff need only allege facts which, **if proven by clear and convincing evidence at trial**, would authorize punitive recovery. Forecloses the anticipated defense misuse of *In re Angelia P.* (1981) 28 Cal.3d 908 as a pleading standard.
- **¶¶ 28–30:** Deny-delay-defend institutional pattern; "despicable conduct"
- **¶ 29:** Confession/victim report/eyewitness corroboration triple — claim denied despite all three (refined with SFPD Incident Report No. 210083458 (conformed to proof), named eyewitness Justin Rosemond, and public-records/subpoena availability within days of February 4, 2021).

**Authority:** Civ. Code § 3294; *Lazar*; *American Airlines, Inc. v. Sheppard, Mullin* (2018) 28 Cal.App.5th 1077; *G.D. Searle & Co. v. Superior Court* (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 22, 29

### Objection 3: Fraud Lacks Particularity

**Defense Argument:** Complaint fails *Lazar* who/what/when/where/how requirements.

**TAC Cure:**

- **WHO:** Sarah Rash, Claims Representative (§I, ¶ 3; §IV, ¶ 25(a))
- **WHAT:** False representation of investigative completeness (§I, ¶ 4)
- **WHEN:** February 25, 2021 — 21 days after collision (§I, ¶ 3; §IV, ¶ 25(c))
- **WHERE:** CSAA claims office; communication to Plaintiff in San Francisco (§IV, ¶ 25(d))
- **HOW:** Written denial letter without investigation (§IV, ¶ 25(e))
- **BY WHAT MEANS:** U.S. Mail (§IV, ¶ 25(f))
- **Claim No.:** 1004-09-1054 (§I, ¶ 3)

**Authority:** *Lazar v. Superior Court* (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631

### Objection 4: Litigation Privilege / *Ribas*

**Defense Argument:** Claims barred by Civil Code § 47(b) and *Ribas v. Clark*.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶¶ 14–16:** Pre-litigation carveout — denial issued before any litigation
- **¶ 16A:** Three-factor *Action Apartment* walk-through — affirmative pleading that, as of February 25, 2021, **none** of the three contemplation factors was present in CSAA's favor: (a) no litigation pending or contemplated; (b) no counsel retained, no demand exchanged, no tolling agreement; (c) no litigation-hold / preservation letter and no litigation reserve.
- **¶¶ 17–19:** *Ribas* distinction — *Ribas* addresses IIED, not fraud
- **¶¶ 20–21:** Non-communicative conduct (failure to investigate) outside privilege
- **¶ 21A:** *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 317–21, illegality carve-out — categorical removal of § 47(b) for conduct that is illegal as a matter of law, including fraud under Civ. Code §§ 1709, 1710.

**Authority:**
- *Action Apartment Ass'n v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1248–49
- *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 317–21
- *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048
- *Ribas v. Clark* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 355 (distinguished)

### Objection 5: Standing / *Shaolian*

**Defense Argument:** No direct duty to third-party claimant under *Shaolian v. Safeco*.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 5:** Direct-addressee theory — Plaintiff is the person to whom the representation was made
- **¶ 6:** CSAA voluntarily undertook direct communicative relationship with unrepresented claimant
- **¶ 6A:** Civil Code § 2338 agency/ratification spine — CSAA is directly liable for wrongful acts and willful omissions of Sarah Rash, claims department personnel, and retained claims professionals acting within scope; ratification pleaded through failure to correct after the January 17, 2025 / February 18, 2025 / April 16, 2025 record events. Anchored in *Doctors' Company v. Superior Court* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 39 (insurer directly liable when it acts outside ordinary insurer role).

**Distinction:** *Shaolian* addresses policy benefits claims; this is a common law fraud claim by the direct addressee of the misrepresentation, with CSAA directly liable under Civ. Code § 2338 for the agent-acts by which the misrepresentation was made.

### Objection 6: Collateral Estoppel / § 1032

**Defense Argument:** Damages duplicate those sought or awarded in CGC-21-594102.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 41:** Distinct primary right — freedom from misrepresentation about investigative completeness
- **¶ 41A:** *Lucido* five-factor walk-through against CGC-21-594102 record: (i) identical issue — no (negligence vs. fraud); (ii) actually litigated — no (CSAA not a party; no fraud tendered); (iii) necessarily decided — no; (iv) final judgment — no (pending on appeal A173827); (v) same party or privy — no. Primary-rights framework under *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888, 904, and *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666.
- **¶ 43:** Express disclaimer of PI damages
- **¶ 44:** 2021-forward damages anchor — all damages arise from Feb. 25, 2021 denial onward
- **¶ 44A:** Express 801/802 double-recovery reservation — no overlap between legal-damages relief here and equitable-vacatur relief in CGC-25-631801; pre-judgment election of remedies preserved.

**Authority:** *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335, 341; *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888, 904; *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666, 681–82.

### Objection 7: Investigation Standard Not Pleaded

**Defense Argument:** Complaint fails to allege what investigation was required.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 45:** What thorough investigation requires under 10 CCR § 2695.7(b)
- **¶ 46:** Itemized failures — CSAA did not obtain 911, BWC, CAD records
- **¶ 47:** *Moradi-Shalal* carve-out — not a private § 790.03 claim; UCL predicate only
- **¶ 47A:** Ins. Code § 790.03(h)(5) "liability reasonably clear" pleaded **solely** as a UCL unlawful-prong predicate consistent with the *Moradi-Shalal* carve-out; liability was reasonably clear to CSAA on the face of SFPD Incident Report No. 210083458 (conformed to proof) and through routine public-records availability of 911/CAD/BWC within days of the February 4, 2021 collision.

**Authority:** 10 CCR §§ 2695.4, 2695.7(b); Ins. Code § 790.03(h)(5); *Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos.* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287; *State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Superior Court* (1996) 45 Cal.App.4th 1093, 1103–04.

### Objection 8: Anti-SLAPP Overlap / CCP § 425.16

**Defense Argument (anticipated):** Claims are predicated on protected litigation conduct within § 425.16(e).

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶ 52A:** Express wrongful-conduct-scope disavowal — no liability predicated on filing/prosecution of CGC-21-594102, in-limine practice, or statements made in connection with a judicial proceeding. All wrongful conduct anchored in 2021 pre-litigation claim handling, already placed outside § 47(b) by ¶¶ 16A and 21A. Collapses § 436 Cluster B overlap with § 425.16 step-1.

**Authority:** CCP § 425.16(e); *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 317–21.

---

### Objection 9: Reasonable-Consumer / UCL Fraudulent-Prong Sufficiency

**Defense Argument (anticipated):** UCL fraudulent-prong claim fails absent allegations of likely deception of a reasonable consumer.

**TAC Cure:**

- **¶¶ 66, 66A:** Reasonable-consumer test pleaded — a business practice is "fraudulent" under § 17200 if members of the public are likely to be deceived. Representation of "concluded investigation" to unrepresented third-party claimants who lack means to audit an insurer's claim file is plausibly likely to deceive a reasonable claimant.

**Authority:** Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200; *Williams v. Gerber Products Co.* (9th Cir. 2008) 552 F.3d 934, 938; *Chapman v. Skype, Inc.* (2013) 220 Cal.App.4th 217, 226.
