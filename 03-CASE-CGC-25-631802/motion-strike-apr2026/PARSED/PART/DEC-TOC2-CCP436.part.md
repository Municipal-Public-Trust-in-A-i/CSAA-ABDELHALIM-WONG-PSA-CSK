INTRODUCTION AND LIMITED THEORY OF THE CASE
1. Plaintiff brings this action on a narrow, document-anchored theory: a pre-litigation business
misrepresentation by Defendant CSAA Insurance Exchange in a liability-claim denial letter, made
---
knowingly or recklessly and used as institutional leverage to discourage or curtail an injured
claimant's pursuit of a valid claim (a deny-d~lay-defend claim-suppression tactic).
2. The core alleged misrepresentation is in a letter dated February 25, 2021, in which CSAA stated,
in substance, that it had "concluded" its investigation and "concluded" its insured was not liable
(the "Denial Letter"). A true and correct copy of the Denial Letter, on CSAA letterhead regarding
Claim No. 1004-09-1054 and signed by Sarah Rash, Claims Representative, is attached hereto as
Exhibit A and incorporated by this reference.
3. Plaintiff alleges that, in context and ordinary meaning, the Denial Letter represented a com-
·9   pleted investigative fact, namely that CSAA had already performed and completed an investigation sufficient to justify a categorical liability denial as of the letter date. A reasonable claimant
would understand "concluded our investigation" to mean CSAA obtained and reviewed the basic objective incident records it would normally collect to evaluate liability (including 911/CAD
and police-generated incident records), before issuing a categorical denial. The representation of
investigative finality was intended and reasonably understood to signal futility-i.e., that further pursuit would be pointless unless Plaintiff undertook burdens CSAA represented had
already been completed.
4. Plaintiff alleges that the Denial Letter representation of a "concluded" investigation was materially false when made because, at that time, CSAA had not obtained or reviewed basic, readily
obtainable, and foundational objective liability evidence for the incident, including:
• 911 audio;
• body-worn camera footage; and
• computer aided dispatch records.
As alleged below, these categories of foundational objective evidence were generated contemporaneously with the February 4, 2021 incident and were identifiable and obtainable during the period
CSAA represented its investigation had already "concluded."
5. Plaintiff further alleges that the falsity of the Denial Letter is corroborated by CSAA's later
claim activity reflecting post-denial pursuits of the same foundational objective liability evidence.
6. Plaintiff alleges reliance and resulting injury in connected categories that include (a) discrete
---
out-of-pocket procurement and investigation expenses incurred to obtain foundational objective
evidence that CSAA represented had already been investigated; (b) fees and costs incurred in
related third-party litigation that Plaintiff alleges was reasonably and proximately forced by the
Denial Letter misrepresentation, because the misrepresentation shifted the burden of foundational evidence collection and authentication onto Plaintiff as the price of continuing, sought
here as damages under the tort-of-another doctrine described in Prentice v. North Am~ Title Guar.
Corp. (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618; (c) incremental medical, therapeutic, and health-related expenses
incurred as a result of the fraud-induced delay and forced proof burden described herein; and (d)
incremental non-economic harm, including physical exacerbation, pain and suffering, emotional
distress, humiliation, and loss of enjoyment of life, proximately caused by the fraud-induced delay
and burdens created by CSAA's misrepresentation.
7. Plaintiff pleads attorney-fee damages with segregation and proof discipline, and does not seek
to recover unrecoverable or unsegregated fees. Plaintiff seeks third-party litigation fees and costs,
if any, only to the extent they are proximately caused by CSAA's tortious conduct and are proven
with competent evidence, segregation, and reasonableness.
8. Plaintiff further alleges that he incurred additional economic loss in the form of lost earnings,
diminished earning capacity, and lost productive time attributable to the incremental procurement,
review, authentication, and litigation-support burdens created by CSAA's Denial Letter misrepresentation. Plaintiff pleads these categories separately from attorney-fee damages and will prove
them with competent, non-speculative evidence, including documentation of time expended, economic value of that time, and the causal connection to the fraud-induced burdens alleged herein.
