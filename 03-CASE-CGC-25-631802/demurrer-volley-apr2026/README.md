## What Happened, In One Paragraph

After CSAA Insurance Exchange drew an Anti-SLAPP opposition from Plaintiff that systematically cited the controlling California Supreme Court authority the motion had omitted, CSAA filed a concurrent general demurrer attacking the First Amended Complaint on eight statutory grounds. The demurrer repeated the same structural error as the Anti-SLAPP motion: it tested the pleading against an evidentiary standard instead of the four-corners standard the statute requires, and it relied on categorical labels such as "collateral attack," "appeal in disguise," and "protected litigation conduct" as if those labels were admitted facts. Plaintiff responded with a full memorandum in opposition, a supporting declaration, a proposed order overruling the demurrer, a narrow notice and request for judicial notice, and, most importantly, a contemporaneously lodged Proposed Second Amended Complaint that moots every futility argument the demurrer could have raised. The result is a record on which the Court has no lawful discretion to sustain the demurrer without leave, and in which each of the eight grounds fails as a matter of law on the face of CSAA's own filings.

## What the Defense Attempted, and Why It Is Wrong

CSAA's demurrer asserts eight statutory grounds under Code of Civil Procedure sections 430.10 and 430.50. Each ground fails independently. The catalog below pairs the defense theory with the controlling California authority the motion did not apply.

| # | Ground Asserted by CSAA | Why It Fails on the Four Corners |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **§ 430.10(a) -- "lack of jurisdiction" / "appeal in disguise"** | Section 904.1 defines appealability; it does not bar later torts on new duties against different defendants. The operative pleading disclaims appellate relief and pleads new duties, new wrongs, and new remedies against CSAA. |
| 2 | **§ 430.10(c) -- "another action pending"** | California uses Primary Rights Theory under *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666. The underlying negligence case adjudicated bodily-injury liability between an insured driver and a claimant; this case concerns the carrier's pre-denial institutional fraud. Different parties, different primary rights. |
| 3 | **§ 430.10(e) -- "failure to state a cause of action"** | The pleading pleads every element of common-law fraud and tethers the UCL claim to the unlawful prong. CSAA's reliance on *Service by Medallion* and *Orcilla* asks the court to resolve factual disputes against the pleader, which is improper at demurrer under *Rannard v. Lockheed Aircraft Corp.* (1945) 26 Cal.2d 149. |
| 4 | **§ 430.10(f) -- "uncertainty"** | Uncertainty demurrers are disfavored and lie only where the pleading is unintelligible. *Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp.* (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135. The defense has already filed a paragraph-by-paragraph demurrer and a paragraph-specific Anti-SLAPP motion against the same pleading. The ground is self-refuting on the face of the defense's own filings. |
| 5 | **Civ. Code § 47(b) -- litigation privilege** | Privilege protects qualifying communications, not noncommunicative institutional conduct. *Kimmel v. Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202. The denial letter is pleaded as evidence of the omission under *Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310, and *Action Apartment Assn., Inc. v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232 recognizes the extrinsic-fraud exception. |
| 6 | **Collateral attack / preclusion** | Issue preclusion requires the same issue actually litigated, necessarily decided, against the same party. *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335. The underlying trial never adjudicated fraud or UCL theories against CSAA; CSAA was not a party. Federal rhetoric from *U.S. v. Throckmorton* does not supplant California law. |
| 7 | **Statute of limitations** | Delayed discovery, concealment, and diligence are well-pleaded and accepted as true on demurrer. *Cansino v. Bank of America* (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1462. Nothing on the face of the pleading establishes earlier accrual as a matter of law. |
| 8 | **Standing / misjoinder** | Plaintiff is the direct addressee of the denial letter and the direct relier on its "concluded investigation" representation. *Shaolian v. Safeco Ins. Co.* (1999) 71 Cal.App.4th 268 addresses contract-standing patterns, not the direct-addressee fraud standing pleaded here. *Norton v. Hines* and *Omega Video* concern suits against opposing counsel; the operative pleading targets the carrier's institutional claim-handling, not counsel's courtroom conduct. |
| 9 | **Insurance-channel bar -- *Moradi-Shalal* and *Zhang*** | *Moradi-Shalal* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287, at page 305, **expressly preserves** "traditional theories as fraud." *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364 is narrow. Neither eliminates a UCL theory grounded in independent common-law fraud. |

Three structural errors run across every ground and across the companion Anti-SLAPP motion: **standard bleed** (importing probability and summary-judgment language into a demurrer), **labels treated as admitted facts**, and **wall-of-authority citation volume** substituted for the three mandatory California analytical filters (gravamen under *Park*, conduct-versus-communication under *Kimmel*, evidence-versus-gravamen under *Kwikset*). Without those filters, no demurrer ground can reach the pleading on its legally tested surface, and no Anti-SLAPP step-one burden can shift.

## What the Plaintiff Filed, and Why Each Filing Matters

Plaintiff's opposition is not a single brief. It is a six-instrument packet designed so that, if the Court sustains any ground, the record still supports mandatory leave to amend under *Price v. Dames & Moore* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 355, *Coffman Specialties, Inc. v. Department of Transportation* (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 1135, and *Dalton v. East Bay Mun. Utility Dist.* (1993) 18 Cal.App.4th 1566. Every component is keyed to a specific defect in the demurrer and to a specific consequence that attaches the moment the Court enters a denial order.

| Role | Instrument | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| Filed core | **Memorandum in Opposition to Demurrer** | Applies the four-corners standard; runs the three analytical filters the defense omitted; answers each of the eight grounds in turn; preserves the mandatory-leave argument under *Price* / *Coffman* / *Dalton*. |
| Filed core | **Declaration of Plaintiff** | Authenticates the operative pleading, the proposed SAC, and the chronology of meet-and-confer; narrows judicial notice to existence, not truth, under Evidence Code sections 452 and 453. |
| Filed core | **Proposed Order Overruling Demurrer** | Primary (overrule); alternative (overrule with leave to amend per § 472 / *Price*); narrow leave-to-amend form for any single-paragraph defect. |
| Optional filed | **Notice of Opposition Filing** | Document list for departments that require one; records that the opposition packet was filed as a single unit. |
| Optional filed | **Request for Judicial Notice** | Narrow RJN of operative pleadings and demurrer as filed; expressly invokes Code Civ. Proc. § 430.70 existence-versus-truth limitation; separate document under CRC rule 3.1113(l). |
| Filed core | **Proof of Service** | Demonstrates timely electronic service on CSAA's counsel of record under CRC rule 2.251. |

**Leave-to-amend companion packet.** Contemporaneously lodged with the opposition, Plaintiff's leave-to-amend motion placed the Proposed Second Amended Complaint on the docket so that the Court can test the operative pleading against the very objections the defense asserts. That lodging forecloses any argument of futility under *Price*, *Coffman*, and *Dalton* and defeats any terminating disposition even on the most favorable reading of any arguable defect. See [apr-2026-sac-leave folder](../apr-2026-sac-leave/).

**Anti-SLAPP opposition companion packet.** Filed the same week, Plaintiff's Anti-SLAPP opposition runs the *Park v. Board of Trustees of California State University* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057 gravamen analysis the defense omitted; preserves the *Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376 step-two argument if ever reached; and objects to CSAA's Request for Judicial Notice on the same existence-versus-truth ground that defeats the demurrer RJN. See [plaintiff-opposition-apr2026 folder](../plaintiff-opposition-apr2026/).

## What the Plaintiff's Response Highlights About the Demurrer

The opposition does not merely disagree. It identifies **thirty-two independent structural defects** (nineteen in the demurrer; thirteen in the companion Anti-SLAPP motion), any one of which is sufficient for denial and which together foreclose the motions entirely. Four of those defects are particularly consequential because they appear on the face of CSAA's own filings and cannot be cured by reply briefing.

1. **Standard bleed.** CSAA imports Anti-SLAPP probability rhetoric and summary-judgment evidentiary reasoning into a demurrer. Demurrer tests legal sufficiency, not evidence. *Serrano v. Priest* (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584; *ABF Capital Corp. v. Berglass* (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 825. A motion that applies the wrong statutory standard cannot be granted.
2. **Labels treated as admitted facts.** "Collateral attack," "appeal in disguise," and "protected litigation conduct" are characterizations, not pleaded facts. Under *Serrano*, *ABF Capital*, and *Young v. Gannon* (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 209, the Court reads the complaint, not the defense's gloss.
3. **Wall-of-authority citation volume.** CSAA stacks cases in a table of authorities without performing the three mandatory filters California law requires before any privilege or preclusion doctrine attaches: gravamen (*Park*), conduct-versus-communication (*Kimmel*), evidence-versus-gravamen (*Kwikset*). Stacking cases is not analysis; *Park* expressly forbids the shortcut.
4. **RFJN misuse.** CSAA asks the Court to take judicial notice of the truth of disputed contents within court records to contradict the operative pleading. Judicial notice establishes existence of a record, not truth. Evid. Code §§ 452, 453; Code Civ. Proc. § 430.70.

The opposition pairs each defect with the pinpoint California authority that forecloses it, then establishes that the cumulative weight of the defects is dispositive even if any individual defect could be excused. This is the structural architecture behind the assessment that the Court has no lawful discretion to sustain the demurrer on any of the eight grounds.

## Consequences to the Defense

The question is not whether the demurrer will be overruled. The record supports overruling on structural grounds. The question is what collateral exposure CSAA and Porter Scott now carry because these motions are filed, served, and time-stamped on the Department 302 docket. A fully briefed demurrer is a judicial admission of the positions taken, a fixed procedural reference point, and a permanent part of the appellate record. The consequences below flow from the defects identified in the opposition, each by operation of California statute or decisional authority rather than by any single judge's discretion.

| Consequence | Triggering Conduct | Statutory or Case-Law Hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Pleading-stage closure** | Demurring on all eight grounds | Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(b) restricts subsequent demurrers to grounds that could have been raised in the first demurrer |
| **Mandatory leave to amend** | Ignoring the lodged Proposed SAC and the futility standard | *Price v. Dames & Moore*; *Coffman Specialties*; *Dalton v. East Bay Mun. Utility Dist.* |
| **Judicial admissions binding the defense** | Paragraph-specific attack acknowledging addressee, claim file, and fact allegations arguendo | *Minish v. Hanuman Fellowship* (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 437; *Valerio v. Andrew Youngquist Construction* (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 1264 |
| **Law of the case adverse to CSAA** | Placing gravamen, section 47(b), *Crowley*, limitations, and answerability in issue | *Morohoshi v. Pacific Home* (2004) 34 Cal.4th 482; *Clemente v. State of California* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 202 |
| **Loss of Anti-SLAPP discovery stay** | Filing § 425.16 motion that will be denied | Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(g) terminates the stay on denial |
| **Mandatory fee-shifting against CSAA** | Anti-SLAPP motion filed without *Park* step-one gravamen analysis | Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(c)(1); *Moore v. Shaw* (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 182; *Decker v. U.D. Registry, Inc.* (2003) 105 Cal.App.4th 1382 |
| **Admissibility as bad-faith evidence** | Using litigation privilege as shield over pre-litigation claim handling | *White v. Western Title Ins. Co.* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 870 |
| **Cross-case admissibility (631801)** | Paragraph-specific characterizations overlapping with the companion equity action | Evid. Code § 1220 admissions of a party; *White* institutional-conduct evidence |
| **§§ 128.7 and 128.5 sanctions exposure** | Legal contentions not warranted by existing law; bad-faith tactics | *Peake v. Underwood* (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 428; *Levy v. Blum* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 625 |
| **State Bar referral exposure** | Omission of directly adverse controlling authority | Cal. Rules Prof. Conduct 3.1, 3.3; Bus. & Prof. Code § 6086.7(a)(2) |
| **Regulatory exposure** | Framing non-investigation as petitioning | Ins. Code §§ 790.03, 790.035; Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, §§ 2695.1 et seq. |
| **Malicious-prosecution vulnerability** | Asserting categorical bars foreclosed by cited authority | *Paiva v. Nichols* (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1007 |

O'Connell of Porter Scott, cataloging these defects and consequences and demanding withdrawal of both the demurrer and the Special Motion to Strike. The letter gives counsel twenty-one days of notice for a Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 safe-harbor motion and identifies a withdrawal window closing April 30, 2026, after which the section 425.16(c)(1), section 128.5, section 128.7, and section 6086.7 mechanisms become live. ## Why the Record Is Unusually Bad for the Defense

Two case-specific factors intensify the consequences. **First**, Plaintiff appears in pro per against institutional defense counsel. Pro per plaintiffs are often discounted, but when the opposition correctly cites *Park*, *Kimmel*, *Kwikset*, *Crowley*, *Baral*, *Action Apartment*, *Lucido*, *Cansino*, and *Aryeh* against motions that skipped those foundational cases, the asymmetry reverses on the face of the briefs. **Second**, Plaintiff lodged the Proposed Second Amended Complaint concurrently with the opposition. That lodging forecloses any argument of futility under *Price*, *Coffman*, and *Dalton*. Even if the Court found a single defect on any of the eight grounds, it must grant leave. The defense gets the worst of both worlds: the motions fail on the merits, and even if they had not, leave to amend would be mandatory. The SAC's lodging is a procedural checkmate on the demurrer's only escape route.
