## Part 4. CGC-25-631801, the equity action

**The theory.** An independent action in equity to set aside the judgment for extrinsic fraud, plus intentional misrepresentation, concealment, constructive fraud (Civ. Code sections 1709, 1710(1), 1710(3), 1573), and attorney deceit (Bus. and Prof. Code section 6128(a)).

The claim is not that the Rules of Professional Conduct create a private right of action. It is narrower and older than that. By appearing of record and making affirmative tribunal-directed representations, counsel undertook a duty of candor whose breach is actionable under *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, *In re Marriage of Modnick* (1983) 33 Cal.3d 897, and *Shafer v. Berger, Kahn* (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 54. Extrinsic fraud is the doctrine for exactly this situation: not an error inside a fair contest, but conduct that kept a fair contest from happening.

## Part 5. CGC-25-631802, the denial-letter action

**The theory.** Negligent misrepresentation under Civil Code section 1710(2). It is deliberately **not** a third-party bad-faith claim, which *Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos.* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287 forecloses. The distinction is the whole case.

An insurer owes no duty to a third-party claimant to settle. That is *Moradi-Shalal*. But an insurer that chooses to put a factual assertion in writing to that claimant has done something different from declining to settle. By issuing a written denial predicated on the positive assertion of a concluded investigation, CSAA undertook a duty under *Biakanja v. Irving* (1958) 49 Cal.2d 647 to ensure the accuracy of that representation. Insurance Code section 790.03 and 10 CCR section 2695.7 supply the standard of care, not the cause of action. The pleaded wrong is the non-performance of the investigation the letter described as already complete, and the letter is the artifact through which that non-performance reached the claimant.

**Where it stands.** The court granted the special motion to strike on **July 1, 2026** (DocID `10297081`). The demurrer was mooted and leave to file a third amended complaint was denied. Plaintiff filed a notice of appeal on **August 14, 2026** (Tx `80347004`, register sequence 327 to 329) with a same-day stay notice (Tx `80347745`).

The court-sent face text already on this site is: **Off calendar. Case stayed.** Seq 354 continues the motion to October 6, 2026, 9:00 a.m., Dept. 302. Seq 349 (August 21, no View) takes the CCP 425.16(c) fee motion off calendar at the moving party's request. After the empty-file / anti-SLAPP contradiction, the access ask remains an escape from merits adjudication, not a separate case. It is also pressed while the July 1 order is on appeal.

**Why the (b)(2) label fails while appeals are pending.** Code of Civil Procedure section 391(b)(2) requires a litigation that "has been finally determined" before repeated-relitigation designation. *First Western Development Corp. v. Superior Court* (1989) 212 Cal.App.3d 860, 864: "When, as here, all avenues for direct review have been exhausted, the judgment is final for all purposes." Pending appeals are not exhausted review. ## Part 6. The one error that runs through both 2025 cases

The two 2025 actions plead different torts against partly different defendants. The defense answered both the same way: special motions to strike that required characterizing the February 2021 denial letter as protected petitioning in anticipation of litigation. Four results follow from that filed choice.

1. **It concedes the purpose the regulation forbids.** Under 10 CCR section 2695.7(d) a claims investigation must be thorough, fair, and objective. Saying the denial was adversarial in purpose is not a defense to a concealment claim. It is the claim. See [IMMUNITY-MAPPING-TO-BAD-FAITH-SIGNAL.md](IMMUNITY-MAPPING-TO-BAD-FAITH-SIGNAL.md).
2. **It overclaims the privilege.** The denial letter predates the litigation it is now said to anticipate, which puts it outside the *Action Apartment* predicate and makes a litigation-privilege theory a misfit for an ordinary-course claims communication.
3. **It walked into the section 425.17(c) gateway.** Commercial and insurer factual representations are carved out of section 425.16 altogether under *Simpson Strong-Tie Co. v. Gore* (2010) 49 Cal.4th 12, a threshold bar that operates before either prong. See [ANTI-SLAPP-OFF-RAMPS.md](ANTI-SLAPP-OFF-RAMPS.md) · [ANTI-SLAPP-CONSEQUENCE-RADIUS.md](ANTI-SLAPP-CONSEQUENCE-RADIUS.md).
4. **It manufactured the appeal it now has to answer.** An order granting a special motion to strike is immediately appealable under section 904.1(a)(13). The two orders the defense won on July 1 and August 6 are the same two orders that created the appellate lane, and on plaintiff's filed section 916 and *Varian* position they suspend the trial-court proceedings embraced by the appeals.

The defense asked a trial court for $70,038.73 and an access bar on the strength of two rulings now on appeal, in a posture whose central premise concedes that the claims investigation was adversarial rather than objective. On August 26, 2026, Department 302's court-sent face ruling on the section 391 access motion stated "Off calendar. Case stayed." and ordered jurisdiction / *Pittman* briefing.
