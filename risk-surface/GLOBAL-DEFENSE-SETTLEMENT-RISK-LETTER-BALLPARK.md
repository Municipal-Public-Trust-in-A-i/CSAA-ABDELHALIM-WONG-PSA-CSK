## Short comparables (standards, not verdict headlines)

**Insurer investigation and punitive framework.** California appellate authority in **Major** (reported as *Major v. Western Home Insurance Co.*, Cal. Ct. App.) and **Amerigraphics, Inc. v. Mercury Casualty Co.** (Cal. Ct. App.) illustrates how courts evaluate **biased or inadequate investigation**, **withholding**, **multiple acts of misconduct**, **managing-agent** theories for **punitive** exposure under Civil Code section 3294, and **constitutional ratio** discipline when **compensatory** is small. Those doctrines support **why** Track C must be modeled with **ratio sensitivity** and **institutional** focus — not as a guarantee of any particular verdict.

**Tribunal and attorney channel.** Equitable vacatur doctrines and **extrinsic-fraud** boundaries (for example, *Action Apartment Association v. City of Santa Monica* and related California authorities cited in the operative **CGC-25-631801** pleading) frame **why** Track B is not interchangeable with Track C. **Business and Professions Code section 6128**, subdivision (a), supplies a **legislative** damages multiplier and **fee** hook for **attorney deceit** theories pleaded against named individuals — separate from the **UCL** channel against the carrier alone.
