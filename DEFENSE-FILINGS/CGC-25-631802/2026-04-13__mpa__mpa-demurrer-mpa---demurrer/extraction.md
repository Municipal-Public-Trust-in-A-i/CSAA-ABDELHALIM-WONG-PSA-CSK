IV.

LEGAL STANDARD

2

Generally, “[a] demurrer to a complaint or cross-complaint may be taken on the whole

3

complaint…or any of the causes of actions stated therein.” (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. (“C.C.P.”), §

4

430.50.) A pleading may be objected to by a demurrer if the pleading does not state facts sufficient

5

to constitute a cause of action or if it is uncertain, or the person filing the pleading does not have the

6

legal capacity to sue. (C.C.P. §430.10.) A pleading is uncertain if it is ambiguous or unintelligible.

7

(Id.) A demurrer challenges the sufficiency of a complaint by raising questions of law. (ABF Capital

8

Corp. v. Berglass (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 825, 833.) This means that a complaint must state facts

9

sufficient to constitute a cause of action upon which relief may be based. (Young v. Gannon (2002)

10

97 Cal.App.4th 209, 220.)

11

Moreover, a complaint must contain a statement of the facts constituting the cause of action

12

in ordinary and concise language. (C.C.P. § 425.10(a)(1).) It must “set forth the essential facts of

13

the case with reasonable precision and with sufficient particularity to acquaint the defendant with

14

the nature, source, and extent of the cause of action.” (Rannard v. Lockheed Aircraft Corp. (1945)

15

26 Cal.2d 149, 156-157.) As such, a plaintiff must show the complaint alleges facts sufficient to

16

establish every element of each cause of action. (Rakestraw v. California Physician’s Services

17

(2000) 81 Cal.App.4th 39, 43.) A demurrer for uncertainty is intended to be directed at the

18

uncertainty existing in the allegations actually made, not the failure to incorporate sufficient facts in

19

the pleading. (Butler v. Sequeira (1950) 100 Cal.App.2d 143, 146.)

20

When considering a demurrer, a court must accept as true all material facts pled in the

21

complaint and those arising by reasonable implication. (ABF Capital Corp. v. Berglass (2005) 130

22

Cal.App.4th 825, 833.) However, while all material facts pled must be treated as true, contentions,

23

deductions, or conclusions of fact or law are not. (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591.)

24

Finally, the burden of proving a reasonable possibility of curing the defect in a complaint

25

dismissed on demurrer is squarely on the plaintiff. (Price v. Dames & Moore (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th

26

355, 359.) A demurrer looks only to the face of the pleadings and to matters judicially noticeable,

27

and not to the evidence or other extrinsic matters. (Id.) A trial court does not abuse its discretion by

28

sustaining a demurrer without leave to amend if it appears that under applicable substantive law
