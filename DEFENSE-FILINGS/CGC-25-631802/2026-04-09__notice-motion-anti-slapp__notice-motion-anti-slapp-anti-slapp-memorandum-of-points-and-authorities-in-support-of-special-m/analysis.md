## Summary of Deficiency Patterns

### Pattern 1: Systematic conflation of regulated insurer conduct with protected speech

The MPA repeatedly treats mandatory insurance regulatory acts (claim denial under 10 CCR 2695.7(b)(3), investigation obligations, CDI notice requirements) as if they were volitional communicative acts protected under CCP 425.16(e). This conflation runs through headings II, IV.A, IV.A.1, IV.A.3, and IV.B.1. The controlling distinction -- between communicative acts and non-communicative institutional conduct -- is established by Rusheen, Wallace, and Old Republic but never addressed.

### Pattern 2: Omission of primary-rights doctrine

The "collateral attack" theory (headings II and IV.B.2) depends entirely on treating the UCL action as a relitigation of the prior tort verdict. Plant Insulation's primary-rights analysis -- which would establish that the UCL claim rests on a different primary right (unlawful business practice) than the tort claim (bodily injury) -- is omitted from the MPA and reply.

### Pattern 3: Omission of authority unfavorable to defense position

The MPA omits Flatley (illegality exception), Zhang (UCL not barred by Moradi-Shalal), Kwikset (UCL standing), Lazar/Stansfield (relaxed fraud pleading for institutional defendants), and Bonni (claim-splitting analysis). Each of these authorities directly addresses a defense argument and would require the MPA to confront unfavorable law.

### Pattern 4: Procedural deficiency in sanctions request

The reply embeds a CCP 128.7 sanctions request without compliance with the mandatory 21-day safe-harbor requirement of 128.7(c)(1), rendering the sanctions request procedurally void.
