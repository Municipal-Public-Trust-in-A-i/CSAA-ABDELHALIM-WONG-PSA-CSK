## Heading Summaries -- MPA

### II. Summary (2:9-17)

Defense frames the entire UCL/fraud action as a "collateral attack" on the prior personal-injury judgment, invoking Park v. Board of Trustees for the proposition that anti-SLAPP protection extends broadly. The summary characterizes plaintiff's claims as arising from protected petitioning activity and argues the motion should be granted at step one. No engagement with UCL standing or Insurance Code obligations at the summary level.

### IV.A Step 1: Protected Activity (6:23-7:25)

Defense contends that all causes of action "arise from" protected petitioning activity under CCP 425.16(e). Relies on Park and City of Cotati for the proposition that claims targeting communicative acts in connection with litigation satisfy step one. References FAC paragraphs 1, 4, and 22F as the operative allegations. Does not specify which subdivision of 425.16(e) applies.

### IV.A.1 Denial Letter Protected (8:9-9:14)

Argues the claim-denial letter constitutes pre-litigation protected speech under subdivisions (e)(1) and (e)(2). Cites Aronson (lawyer demand letter) and Kashian (privilege opinion) for the proposition that communications anticipating litigation are within the anti-SLAPP statute. Treats the regulatory denial as equivalent to a lawyer's demand letter.

### IV.A.2 "Materially false" still protected (9:15-10:14)

Asserts that allegations of material falsity do not strip anti-SLAPP protection, relying on Park. Contends that the truth or falsity of a statement is irrelevant at step one, and that the court should not examine the merits of the fraud allegation until step two. No discussion of the Flatley illegality exception.

### IV.A.3 Counsel statements at trial (10:15-11:11)

Extends the protected-activity argument to defense counsel's statements during the prior personal-injury trial, citing City of Colton and Graham-Sult for the proposition that statements made during judicial proceedings are petitioning activity. Targets FAC paragraph 22F (referencing counsel representations at trial).

### IV.B.1 Civ. Code 47(b) bars all claims (11:13-12:5)

Argues the litigation privilege under Civil Code section 47(b) provides an absolute bar to all claims. Cites Silberg v. Anderson for the breadth of the privilege and Rusheen v. Cohen for its application. Contends privilege applies to the denial letter, trial conduct, and all communications connected to the prior litigation.

### IV.B.2 Collateral attack doctrine (12:6-13:6)

Characterizes the UCL action as an impermissible collateral attack on the prior judgment, arguing plaintiff cannot relitigate issues resolved in the personal-injury trial through a new cause of action. Does not cite specific authority for the collateral-attack doctrine in this section.

### IV.B.3.i Fraud cannot survive (13:7-14:8)

Argues the fraud cause of action is insufficiently pleaded under CCP 430.10(e) demurrer standards. Contends the fraud allegations lack the required specificity (who, what, when, where, how). Does not address Lazar or Stansfield superior-knowledge relaxation for institutional defendants.

### IV.B.3.ii UCL no standing / Moradi-Shalal (14:9-15:end)

Invokes Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund for the proposition that Insurance Code section 790.03(h) does not create a private right of action, and argues plaintiff lacks UCL standing. Contends the UCL claim is merely a repackaged Insurance Code violation. No discussion of Zhang v. Superior Court or Kwikset injury-in-fact analysis.

## Heading Summaries -- Reply (May 6, 2026)

### I. Introduction (Reply 1-2)

Reply characterizes plaintiff's opposition as procedurally deficient, noting multiple filings and overlength concerns. Reasserts Park framework and argues opposition fails to engage the two-step anti-SLAPP analysis properly.

### III.A Conduct vs Communication framework (Reply 3-4)

Argues plaintiff misstates controlling law by conflating "conduct" with "communication." Reply contends Park and City of Cotati establish that the gravamen of the complaint targets communicative acts, not mere business conduct. Targets FAC paragraph 22F.

### III.B Clustered authority (Reply 4)

Dismisses plaintiff's grouping of authorities into thematic clusters as an improper analytical framework, arguing it does not alter the step-one analysis under Park.

### III.C Institutional conduct carve-out fails (Reply 4-5)

Contends there is no recognized "institutional conduct" exception to the anti-SLAPP statute. Cites Rusheen for the communicative/non-communicative distinction but argues all challenged conduct is communicative. Invokes Civ. Code 47(b) as additional support.

### IV. Step Two failure (Reply 5-6)

Argues plaintiff has failed to produce admissible evidence sufficient to establish a probability of prevailing on any claim. Cites Soukup v. Law Offices of Herbert Hafif for the evidentiary burden at step two.

### V. Conclusion + fee request (Reply 6)

Requests attorney fees under CCP 425.16(c) and embeds a sanctions request under CCP 128.7, contending the opposition was filed for an improper purpose. Does not address 128.7(c)(1) safe-harbor procedural requirements.
