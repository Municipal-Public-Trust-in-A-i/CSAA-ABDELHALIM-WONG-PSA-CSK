I.

INTRODUCTION

2

Plaintiff’s Amended Complaint (“FAC”) constitutes an impermissible collateral attack on a final

3

judgment entered after a full jury trial and extensive pre- and post-trial proceedings. In the underlying

4

action, a jury returned a defense verdict on liability following the presentation of all evidence Plaintiff

5

claims was “wrongfully obstructed.” The Court thereafter denied Plaintiff’s multiple post-trial motions

6

challenging the verdict and judgment. Plaintiff has since filed a notice of appeal, which remains pending.1

7

Rather than awaiting appellate review, Plaintiff now asserts a single equitable claim styled as an

8

“Independent Action to Set Aside Judgment for Extrinsic Fraud,” naming not only the original defendant

9

but also the insurance carrier and all underlying defense attorneys for their “conduct” in obtaining a

10

defense verdict. The FAC seeks to impose liability based entirely on Defendants’ litigation conduct

11

in defending the underlying case—including discovery practices, motions in limine, and

12

representations to the Court. These allegations do not state a cognizable claim as a matter of law and

13

instead fall squarely within the scope of conduct protected by California’s Anti-SLAPP statute and the

14

absolute litigation privilege.

15

California courts have long recognized that subsequent lawsuits premised on a party’s or counsel’s

16

participation in judicial proceedings—whether during discovery, motion practice, or trial—are barred as

17

a matter of law. (Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16, subd. (e); Civ. Code § 47(b).) Representing a client in litigation,

18

filing and opposing motions, managing discovery, and making arguments regarding the admissibility or

19

weight of evidence are the epitome of protected petitioning activity. Mere disagreement with the jury’s

20

assessment of evidence, or with the Court’s discretionary rulings, does not constitute extrinsic fraud and

21

cannot justify setting aside a judgment. At most, Plaintiff alleges matters that were intrinsic to the

22

underlying case insofar as they were all raised, litigated, and resolved in the underlying action—precisely

23

the type of claims that are barred as a matter of law and must be addressed, if at all, through appellate

24

review.

25

Accordingly, the FAC is subject to a special motion to strike under Code of Civil Procedure section

26

425.16 because (1) each allegation targets protected litigation conduct, and (2) Plaintiff cannot establish

27

a probability of prevailing on the merits of his sole cause of action. The absolute litigation privilege

SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT

6

Plaintiff seeks to make an improper collateral attack on a properly rendered jury verdict and this

The Legislature passed Code of Civil Procedure (“Code Civ. Proc.”) § 425.16 to protect against
strategic lawsuits used to attack individuals who exercise their rights under the First Amendment—
including affording protection for litigation activity. The Legislature adopted Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16 in
response to the disturbing increase in litigation “brought primarily to chill the valid exercise of the
constitutional rights of freedom of speech and petition.” Commonly referred to as the Anti-SLAPP Statute,
it “provides for the early dismissal of unmeritorious claims filed to interfere with the valid exercise of the
constitutional rights of freedom of speech and petition for the redress of grievances.” (Club Members v.
Sierra Club (2008) 45 Cal.4th 309, 312.) The Legislature has declared that the statute must be “construed
broadly” to that end. (Fremont Reorganizing Corp. v. Faigin (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 1153, 1165.)
