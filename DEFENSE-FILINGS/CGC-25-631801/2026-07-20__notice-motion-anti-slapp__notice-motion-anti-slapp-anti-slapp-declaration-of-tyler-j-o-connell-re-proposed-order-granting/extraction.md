CRC 3.1312(a) still governs approval or objection to any proposed order you serve. A request that I
“stipulate” does not shorten that rule, cure the fifty-nine-day default, or waive objections to
caption defects, clerical errors, or any attempt to use the order form to scrub positions you
advanced to obtain the anti-SLAPP grant.

III. No omission of the positions you forwarded to win the
strike

You asked the Court to strike an independent equitable action framed around extrinsic fraud and
fraud on the Court. To do that, you forwarded (and the May 12 minutes adopted) a package that
includes, among other things:

1. Prong-one litigation-conduct framing under CCP § 425.16(e)(2);
2. Rejection of the commercial exemption because the suit was cast as litigation conduct, not
commercial activity, from the outset. That was the price of your anti-SLAPP. You needed a
litigation-conduct frame to get the strike, so you took it. What you appear not to have
considered (or preferred not to write down) is that the same frame carries statutory and
regulatory consequences you cannot later pretend were accidental.
I am not going to catalog those codes, laws, and regulations for you; you will have to do your
homework on this one, Chad and Tyler. If you meant to understand what you were doing when
you told the Court you entered immediately into a litigation posture, you would already have
done it. As it stands, the tactic looks less like strategy than like a short-term win purchased
with long-term admissions. Either you did not see that, you did not care about what you
committed CSAA too, or you hoped no one would notice. Stupid, ignorant, or unethical: choose
your label. The arithmetic is the same.

1. A prong-two characterization of the claim as intrinsic rather than extrinsic fraud, resting on
the assertion that the issues “were fully litigated in the trial court,” citing your MIL /
scheduling RFJN; and
2. Civil Code § 47(b) litigation-privilege analysis, including the Court’s
own Rubenstein acknowledgment that the privilege does not eliminate the equitable remedy
of vacating a fraudulently obtained judgment, while barring ancillary monetary relief.

I will push back, in this Court and on any later review, on any stipulation, proposed order, reply, or
oral presentation that omits, softens, or walks away from those positions after they were used to
5

obtain the strike. The written order, if entered, must carry the Court’s actual holdings, including
the Rubenstein / § 47(b) path the minutes already recite. It will not be used as a vehicle to delete
the extrinsic-fraud / privilege architecture you asked the Court to adopt.

IV. “Fully litigated” and what the Court itself already said

Separately, and so there is no later claim of surprise: any assertion that the underlying issues were
“fully litigated” in a sense that closes extrinsic-fraud analysis, or that treats motions in limine as a
final adjudication of statutory negligence duty, is technically and literally inaccurate on the record
now before Department 301.

The May 12 minutes themselves preserve that Rubenstein vacatur space even while striking
monetary relief. The Wong RT 11 record (already noticed: Tx 79843794 / 79830359 / 79843899)
shows that after the jury asked for Vehicle Code §§ 22107 and 21804(a), those statutes were
withheld. That record is inconsistent with any slogan that statutory negligence duty was fully
litigated and necessarily decided. Exhibit D and the CCP § 1008 proposed order ask the Court to
qualify the minutes accordingly.

Anything said, or left unsaid, in connection with your July 10 draft that fails to carry forward the
litigation-privilege / Rubenstein positions you forwarded, or that continues to treat “fully litigated”
as if Wong never happened, will be met as incomplete and inaccurate. The Court will be required to
address that conflict on July 31, 2026.

V. Bottom line on stipulation

I will not stipulate to a sanitized or incomplete form of order. I will not stipulate away CRC 3.1312
non-compliance. I will not stipulate to any reading of “fully litigated” that contradicts the Court’s
own Rubenstein language or the Wong instruction record. If you wish to lodge a corrected
proposed order that (a) cures caption and clerical defects, (b) repeats the May 12 substantive
holdings without omission of the privilege / extrinsic-fraud path you invoked, and (c) does not
foreclose Exhibit D / CCP § 1008 qualifications, serve it under CRC 3.1312 and I will respond within
the rule.

Until then, this weeks cohort and my competing lodging stand.
