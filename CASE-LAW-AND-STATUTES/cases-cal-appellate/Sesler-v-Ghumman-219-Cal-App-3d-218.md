# Sesler v. Ghumman

**Citation:** 219 Cal.App.3d 218, 268 Cal.Rptr. 70 (1990)

**Court:** California Court of Appeal, Fourth District, Division Three

**Date:** March 28, 1990

**Docket:** G006819

---

## Facts / Holding

Negligence action arising from a traffic collision at an uncontrolled intersection in Santa Ana. Plaintiff Sesler was making a left turn on his motorcycle when struck by defendant Ghumman's northbound car. Sesler had stopped in the left-turn pocket; cars in lanes 1 and 2 stopped and motioned for him to proceed. Seeing no hazard in lane 3, Sesler began his turn. Ghumman had moved from lane 1 into lane 3 to avoid stopped traffic and collided with Sesler.

**Key holding:** Under Vehicle Code section 21801, a left-turning driver cannot proceed beyond the first lane of traffic based on signals from vehicles in that lane if approaching vehicles in other lanes constitute a hazard. While a motorist may waive his or her own right-of-way, that waiver does not apply to any other motorist. A left-turning driver must yield to all oncoming vehicles "close enough to constitute a hazard at any time during the turning movement" and must check each successive lane before proceeding.
