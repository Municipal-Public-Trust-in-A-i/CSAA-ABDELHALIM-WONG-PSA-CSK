## 1. The California Two-Step (and Its Threshold Filters)

California's anti-SLAPP regime, CCP § 425.16, is a burden-shifting screen, not a merits adjudication:

- **Step one — "arises from" protected activity.** Defendant must show each challenged claim is *based on* an act in furtherance of petitioning or speech in connection with a public issue (§ 425.16(e)(1)–(4)). *Equilon Enterprises v. Consumer Cause, Inc.* (2002) 29 Cal.4th 53, 66; *City of Cotati v. Cashman* (2002) 29 Cal.4th 69, 76–78.
- **The "injury-producing act."** *Park v. Board of Trustees* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057, 1062–1065 holds that the speech itself must supply an *element* of the claim — speech that merely evidences, communicates, or surrounds the wrong is not enough.
- **Mixed-conduct parsing.** *Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376, 393–395 and *Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (2021) 11 Cal.5th 995, 1010–1012 require *claim-by-claim, allegation-by-allegation* analysis. *Wilson v. CNN, Inc.* (2019) 7 Cal.5th 871, 884.
- **Step two — minimal merit.** *Navellier v. Sletten* (2002) 29 Cal.4th 82, 88–89; *Oasis West Realty, LLC v. Goldman* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811, 820. Plaintiff need only show a "probability" of prevailing; *Sweetwater Union High School Dist. v. Gilbane Building Co.* (2019) 6 Cal.5th 931, 949.

Three layers sit *above* this two-step and can bypass it entirely:

- **§ 425.17(c) commercial-speech exemption** — *Simpson Strong-Tie Co. v. Gore* (2010) 49 Cal.4th 12; *JAMS, Inc. v. Superior Court* (2016) 1 Cal.App.5th 984; *Stewart v. Rolling Stone LLC* (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 664.
- **§ 425.17(b) public-interest exemption** — *Club Members for an Honest Election v. Sierra Club* (2008) 45 Cal.4th 309, 316–318.
- **§ 425.17(e)** — denial on either exemption defeats the *interlocutory appeal* under § 425.16(i) and the automatic stay under CCP § 904.1(a)(13).

A fourth, *Flatley* illegality, sits inside step one but operates as a categorical bar:

- **Illegal-as-a-matter-of-law** activity is unprotected. *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 317–325; reaffirmed in *Lefebvre v. Lefebvre* (2011) 199 Cal.App.4th 696. *City of Montebello v. Vasquez* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 409 cabins it: a mere allegation of illegality is not enough.

A fifth structural limit: **litigation privilege ≠ § 425.16(e).** *Kimmel v. Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202, 211 and *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1056 hold the privilege reaches only communicative acts; non-communicative tortious conduct is outside both the privilege and § 425.16. *Action Apartment Ass'n v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232 is the boundary case.

A sixth: **timeliness on amended pleadings.** *Newport Harbor Ventures, LLC v. Morris Cerullo World Evangelism* (2018) 4 Cal.5th 637, 645–650 — an amended pleading does not reopen § 425.16 as to claims already present in earlier pleadings.

A seventh: **public-interest content/context analysis** for (e)(3)/(e)(4). *FilmOn.com Inc. v. DoubleVerify Inc.* (2019) 7 Cal.5th 133, 149–150.

## 2. Off-Ramps Stacked in Filings

The **bench insert** drafted for May 13 (Dept. 302) sets the order of analysis the Court is urged to use — threshold → *Cashman* → *Flatley* → prong two — so a denial can rest on the *narrowest* available ground. Draft / lodgment-aligned materials mirror on this site under [Apr. 24 supplemental — 802](03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/anti-slapp-supplemental-apr24/README.md), [court courtesy / bench brief lanes](03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/clerk-filing-2026-04-28/11-COURTESY-JD-302/README.md), and [LEGAL-AUTHORITIES.md](LEGAL-AUTHORITIES.md).

### Off-Ramp 1 — § 425.17(c) (Commercial / Insurance Services)

CSAA is an insurer named on the face of (c). The misrepresentations are characterized as *factual representations about business operations and services* delivered in the commercial channel — discovery custody, file production, substitution, denial-letter content. Under *Simpson Strong-Tie* the Court can deny without reaching *Cashman*; under § 425.17(e) there is then **no automatic stay** for those counts.

### Off-Ramp 2 — § 425.17(b) (Public Interest)

Pleaded in the alternative for **Count 5 (declaratory relief)** and **Count 6 (Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a) attorney deceit)** as private enforcement of a legislative public-regarding norm. *Club Members*, 45 Cal.4th at 316–318. Individualized damage counts weaken (b)(1) for those counts alone; the lead avenue is § 425.17(c) for the commercial stack.

### Off-Ramp 3 — *Cashman* Prong One (Gravamen)

This is the analytic heart of the *prong-one* materials lodged toward the May hearings; see **[40-PRONG-ONE-RECORD](03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/clerk-filing-2026-04-28/40-PRONG-ONE-RECORD/)** and the merged checklist PDF in that cohort. Shared defects argued on the motions include:

- The motion attacks *imagined* theories rather than the operative counts (*Baral* 393–395; *Bonni* 1010–1012).
- It treats *ordinary underlying conduct* described in writings as "protected" (*Wilson* 884; *Park* 1062–1063).
- It conflates "triggered by" with "arises from" (*Cashman* 76–78; *Park* 1063–1064).
- It collapses *gravamen* across mixed claims and across communicative/non-communicative acts (*Rusheen* 1056).
- It targets background allegations and evidentiary references rather than a stricken-able claim for relief (*Baral* 393–394).
- It mis-applies prelitigation-communication doctrine where there is no "serious and good-faith contemplation of litigation" (*Neville v. Chudacoff* (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th 1255, 1268–1269; *Briggs v. Eden Council for Hope & Opportunity* (1999) 19 Cal.4th 1106, 1115).
- It ignores *FilmOn.com*'s content/context filter for "public issue."
- It misuses Civ. Code § 47(b) as a substitute for step one — see **[DEFENSE-FATAL-FLAWS.md § 5](DEFENSE-FATAL-FLAWS.md#5-anti-slapp-gravamen-mismatch-under-prong-one)**.

### Off-Ramp 4 — *Flatley* Illegality

Your case positions *Flatley* well because predicates rest on **statutes** in each action, not only common-law tort hooks.

- **801 illegality predicates:** Bus. & Prof. Code § 6128(a) attorney deceit (criminal misdemeanor, with reckless-disregard scienter under *Shafer v. Berger, Kahn, Shafton, Moss, Figler, Simon & Gladstone* (2003) 107 Cal.App.4th 54, 79–80), Cal. Rule of Prof. Conduct 3.3(a)(1) candor-to-tribunal, and CCP § 128.7(b)'s reasonable-inquiry certification floor (*Kim v. Westmoore Partners, Inc.* (2011) 201 Cal.App.4th 267, 286–287). The "knowingly" element is satisfied by deliberate avoidance under *People v. Smith* (2014) 60 Cal.4th 603, 616.
- **802 illegality predicates:** 10 CCR § 2695.7(d) ("decision must be based on all information reasonably available"); Insurance Code § 790.03(h)(1), (h)(3), (h)(5); and the *Egan v. Mutual of Omaha Ins. Co.* (1979) 24 Cal.3d 809, 818–819 / *Wilson v. 21st Century Ins. Co.* (2007) 42 Cal.4th 713, 720–721 duty to investigate thoroughly.

### Off-Ramp 5 — Prong-Two Minimum Merit

The *Sweetwater* record + verified declarations + lodged exhibits supply the minimal-merit spine if the Court reaches prong two. *Navellier*; *Oasis West*. For Count 1 you also have the **independent action in equity** channel: *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471 and *Kougasian v. TMSL, Inc.* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1032–1036 — extrinsic-fraud actions to set aside a judgment are categorically not SLAPPs.

## 3. Why Each Case Specifically Falls Outside § 425.16

### CGC-25-631801 (panel counsel + CSAA)

Building on Parts I–II of the prong-one checklist lodged with coordinated packets toward the Anti-SLAPP hearings:

- **801-A.** The Jan. 17, 2025 MIL Nos. 6 and 17, the Feb. 18, 2025 colloquy (RT 141:4–11), and the Apr. 16, 2025 trial admission (RT 7, 213:13–15) are the *injury-producing acts* of extrinsic fraud, § 6128(a) deceit, constructive fraud, and officer-of-the-court fiduciary breach — not incidental speech. *Estate of Sanders* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 607, 614–616; *In re Marriage of Modnick* (1983) 33 Cal.3d 897, 905–907; *Kougasian*, 118 Cal.App.4th at 1032–1035.
- **801-B.** The May 4, 2023 file-transfer omission (Carbone, Smith & Koyama → Phillips, Spallas & Angstadt) is **non-communicative**: it is the structural foundation of the constructive-fraud and aiding-and-abetting counts. *Park* 1064–1065; *Rusheen* 1056; *Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310.
- **801-C.** The disclaimers are illegal as a matter of law under § 6128(a) + Rule 3.3(a)(1) + § 128.7(b) — *Flatley* 317–320.
- **801-D.** Position B (2025 panel-counsel disclaimers) and Position A (CSAA's Feb. 25, 2021 "concluded investigation") are **structurally inconsistent** statements of CSAA itself under Civ. Code §§ 2330, 2338 and Evid. Code § 1222 — *Engalla v. Permanente Medical Group, Inc.* (1997) 15 Cal.4th 951, 974; *Wilson v. 21st Century Ins. Co.* 42 Cal.4th at 720–721; *Mock v. Michigan Millers Mutual Ins. Co.* (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 306, 328.
- **801-E.** Litigation privilege does not reach the file-transfer omission — *Kimmel* 211; *Rusheen* 1056, 1058.

### CGC-25-631802 (CSAA, regulatory failure)

Building on Parts I and III of the same checklist:

- **802-A.** The Feb. 25, 2021 denial letter (Claim No. 1004-09-1054, signed by Sarah Rash) is *evidence* of the regulatory failure, not the wrong itself. The wrong is the 21-day premature claim-file closure without retrieving the four "Silent Witnesses" — 911 audio + SFDEM CAD; SFPD BWC; SFPD CAD log; and any supplemental SFPD report — each reasonably available by CCP § 1985 + Evid. Code § 1560 subpoena, Gov. Code § 7920 PRA, Penal Code § 832.7(b) request, or insured-authorization channel. *Park* 1060, 1064–1065; *Rusheen* 1056; *Kwikset* 51 Cal.4th 310.
- **802-B.** A 21-day denial letter to an unrepresented direct third-party claimant is **not** a "prelitigation communication" in serious contemplation of litigation; it is regulatory correspondence governed by 10 CCR §§ 2695.4(a), 2695.7(b)–(d) and Insurance Code § 790.03(h). *Neville* 1268–1269; *Briggs* 1115.
- **802-C.** Same conduct violates 10 CCR § 2695.7(d), Ins. Code § 790.03(h)(1)/(3)/(5), and the *Egan/Wilson* duty — *Flatley* 317–320.
- **802-D.** Position A vs. Position B contradiction = *Engalla* reckless disregard; forecloses the *Wilson v. 21st Century* "genuine dispute" defense at 723.
- **802-E.** Litigation privilege does not reach premature file-closure — *Kimmel* 211; *Rusheen* 1056, 1058.
- **802-F.** CSAA's "funding and directing of defense counsel" is **commercial/contractual indemnity**, not CSAA's *own* petitioning. *Park* 1062–1065.

## TL;DR

Your cases fall outside § 425.16 along **five independent off-ramps**, any one of which is argued as sufficient:

1. **§ 425.17(c)** — CSAA is an insurer; the speech is commercial-channel representation of fact.
2. **§ 425.17(b)** — public-interest enforcement of § 6128(a) and declaratory relief.
3. **Park / Cashman gravamen** — the injury-producing acts are non-communicative regulatory failure (802) and non-communicative file-transfer concealment (801); the disclaimers and the denial letter are *evidence*, not *the wrong*.
4. **Flatley illegality** — § 6128(a) + Rule 3.3(a)(1) + § 128.7(b) (801); 10 CCR § 2695.7(d) + Ins. Code § 790.03(h) + *Egan/Wilson* (802).
5. **Prong-two minimal merit** — *Sweetwater* admissibility floor with the Position-A-vs-Position-B contradiction triggering *Engalla* reckless disregard.

Plus structural backstops: litigation privilege does not reach non-communicative acts (*Kimmel/Rusheen*), an amended pleading does not reopen § 425.16 for already-pleaded claims (*Newport Harbor Ventures*), and an independent action in equity to set aside a judgment is categorically not a SLAPP (*Kougasian*).
