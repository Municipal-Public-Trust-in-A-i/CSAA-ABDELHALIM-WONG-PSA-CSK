## I. Application to Third-Party Bodily-Injury Claims (Insured Driver Strikes Pedestrian)

### A. Legal framework

The tort claim here is brought by a **third-party pedestrian** against CSAA's insured driver. California law applies the same investigation and fair-claims duties to CSAA's handling of that third-party claim.

**1. Implied covenant and third-party claims**

The **implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing** runs between the insurer and its insured, but it governs **how the insurer investigates and defends third-party claims**. *Gruenberg v. Aetna Ins. Co.* (1973) 9 Cal.3d 566, 573–574; *Comunale v. Traders & General Ins. Co.* (1958) 50 Cal.2d 654, 658–661; *Crisci v. Security Ins. Co.* (1967) 66 Cal.2d 425, 430–433. Mishandling the pedestrian's liability claim exposes the insured to excess judgments, punitive exposure, and findings of fraud on the court. The insurer therefore must investigate the pedestrian's claim thoroughly and fairly.

**2. Fair Claims Settlement Practices and Insurance Code**

The **Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations** (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, § 2695 et seq.) and **Insurance Code section 790.03(h)** expressly regulate the handling of **third-party liability claims**, including prompt investigation, honest communication of facts and policy provisions, and good-faith efforts to settle when liability is reasonably clear. These rules bind the insurer whether the claimant is the insured or an injured third party.

**3. Regulatory and civil consequences**

A third-party claimant ordinarily cannot sue for bad faith in tort absent an assignment or other vehicle. The **California Department of Insurance** may nevertheless investigate and sanction the insurer for regulatory violations in its treatment of the third-party claim, and courts may treat those violations as **evidence of the insurer's bad faith toward its own insured**. *Gruenberg*, supra, 9 Cal.3d at 573–574; *Yacullo v. AIG Property Casualty Company* (S.D. Cal. 2024) 2024 WL 104758 (regulatory violations admissible as evidence of bad faith).

**4. Concrete duties in the insured-driver / pedestrian posture**

In the posture of an **insured CSAA driver striking a pedestrian in a crosswalk**, CSAA's duties to:

1. Obtain and evaluate 911/BWC/CAD and supplemental law-enforcement records;  
2. Act as a neutral, truth-seeking investigator rather than an adversary;  
3. Avoid strategic delay once liability is reasonably clear; and  
4. Reconsider its position when contrary evidence emerges  

are all fully engaged. CSAA cannot relax or avoid those duties because the injured party is a third-party pedestrian; the authorities provide the legal backbone for both **regulatory relief for the pedestrian** and **bad-faith / excess-exposure consequences vis-à-vis CSAA's insured**.

## II. Failure to Investigate and Misrepresentation of a "Concluded" Investigation (Egan / Bartel / Ins. Code 790.03 / CCR 2695.7)

### A. Legal rule

**1. Egan v. Mutual of Omaha Ins. Co.** (1979) 24 Cal.3d 809

The California Supreme Court held that an insurer may not reasonably deny a claim **without first thoroughly investigating the foundation for its denial**. A denial supported only by selective or incomplete investigation is itself evidence of bad faith. The duty exists independently of the ultimate validity of the claim; an insurer can be held liable for bad faith when the investigation was inadequate. *Egan*, supra, 24 Cal.3d 809.

**2. Bartel v. Chicago Title Insurance Co.** (2025) 99 Cal.App.5th ___ [H052083]

The Court of Appeal held that an insurer's investigation duties are **not cabined by the initial pleadings or the narrow way a claim is first presented**. When facts or circumstances suggest other potential bases for coverage, the insurer must explore them. An insurer cannot shield itself behind a cramped reading of the claim if surrounding circumstances alert it to broader coverage implications. Failure to investigate potential bases for coverage outside the pleadings can constitute bad faith. *Bartel*, supra.

**3. Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, § 2695.7**

The Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations require insurers to diligently investigate claims **before** denying coverage and to base coverage decisions on **all information reasonably available**. An insurer may not persist in seeking information not reasonably required to resolve the claim while ignoring obvious, readily available sources such as audio recordings, law-enforcement reports, and contemporaneous documentation. Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, § 2695.7(b), (d).

**4. Misrepresentation of a "Concluded" Investigation (Ins. Code § 790.03(h)(1))**

The Unfair Claims Practices Act expressly prohibits "[m]isrepresenting to claimants pertinent facts or insurance policy provisions relating to any coverages at issue." When an insurer affirmatively represents that its investigation is "concluded" or "thorough" to justify a denial, but has failed to obtain key, standard law-enforcement evidence, that statement constitutes an actionable misrepresentation of a pertinent fact. Under the amended complaint, this transforms a mere failure to investigate into an affirmative fraud or deceit.

### B. Corollary for CSAA in the Abdelhalim matter

Once CSAA had notice that the collision involved **pedestrian claims, disputed fault, and contemporaneous law-enforcement and 911 evidence**, it had an affirmative obligation to:

- Request, obtain, and review the **911 recording** documenting the insured driver's description of the incident.  
- Obtain and evaluate **BWC, CAD, and supplemental reports** reflecting officers' contemporaneous observations and statements.  
- Reconcile that objective evidence with any later self-serving statements by the insured or a preferred reconstruction narrative.

CSAA could not lawfully limit itself to the narrowest, most self-favorable parts of the record while **declining to secure the 911/BWC/CAD** that would obviously illuminate liability and severity. If CSAA's internal files show that these materials were not requested, requested but not followed up, or obtained but not meaningfully considered in coverage and settlement decisions, the investigation falls within the **Egan / Bartel** category of perfunctory, outcome-driven claim handling.

The collision involved a pedestrian in a crosswalk, a 911 caller who allegedly stated he "barely hit" the pedestrian, and officers who created BWC and CAD entries. These are textbook materials a reasonable, good-faith insurer would treat as core to liability and coverage evaluation. 

Crucially, as set forth in the amended complaint, CSAA did not merely fail to investigate—it affirmatively **misrepresented the status of its investigation**. In its February 25, 2021 denial letter, just 21 days after the incident, CSAA falsely represented that its investigation was "concluded." Because CSAA had not obtained or reviewed the 911, BWC, or CAD records at that time, this "concluded" representation was mathematically and logically false. The failure to investigate thus transformed into actionable fraud/deceit and an unfair claims practice (Ins. Code § 790.03(h)(1)). CSAA's failure to obtain and scrutinize core liability evidence, while simultaneously using a falsely "concluded" investigation to justify denial, is completely incompatible with its duty to search for and fairly evaluate coverage-related evidence.

## III. Use of authorities in briefing and discovery

- **Third-party BI application:** Cite *Gruenberg*, *Comunale*, *Crisci*, Insurance Code section 790.03(h), and Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, § 2695 et seq. to establish that the same investigation and fair-claims duties apply to third-party bodily-injury claims and that regulatory violations are relevant to bad faith.
- **Failure to Investigate and Misrepresentation:** Cite *Egan* and *Bartel* for the duty to investigate thoroughly before denial and to investigate potential bases for coverage beyond the narrow framing of the claim; cite Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, § 2695.7(b) and (d) for the regulatory duty to investigate before denying and not to ignore readily available sources; and cite Insurance Code section 790.03(h)(1) for the proposition that an affirmative representation of a "concluded" investigation when such basic investigation steps were omitted constitutes an actionable misrepresentation of a pertinent fact.

RFAs and interrogatories above are drafted so that: (1) admissions lock in facts that support the plaintiff's theory; (2) denials that contradict documents (e.g., claim file, denial letter) create impeachment and cost-of-proof exposure under Code of Civil Procedure section 2033.420; and (3) interrogatory answers force disclosure of the scope of CSAA's investigation and internal standards.
