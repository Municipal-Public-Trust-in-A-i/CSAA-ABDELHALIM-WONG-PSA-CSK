## Executive Summary

On April 13, 2026, Porter Scott, counsel of record for CSAA Insurance Exchange, filed a general demurrer to the First Amended Complaint on eight statutory grounds under Code of Civil Procedure sections 430.10 and 430.50. The demurrer rode in convoy with a previously filed Special Motion to Strike under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16. On April 16, 2026, Plaintiff filed a six-instrument opposition packet. On April 14 and April 16, 2026, Plaintiff concurrently lodged a Proposed Second Amended Complaint under the leave-to-amend motion and filed a full Anti-SLAPP opposition, both directed at the same operative pleading.

The demurrer presents three structural problems, any one of which is sufficient for denial:

1. **Standard bleed.** The motion tests the pleading against an evidentiary standard instead of the four-corners standard the statute requires. On demurrer, well-pleaded facts are accepted as true, and contentions, deductions, and conclusions of law are not. *Serrano v. Priest* (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, 591; *ABF Capital Corp. v. Berglass* (2005) 130 Cal.App.4th 825, 832; *Rannard v. Lockheed Aircraft Corp.* (1945) 26 Cal.2d 149, 156-157.
2. **Labels treated as admitted facts.** The demurrer asks the court to accept defense characterizations, such as "collateral attack," "appeal in disguise," "protected litigation conduct," and "no legal channel for this grievance," as if they were pleaded facts. They are not. The court reads the pleading, not the defense's gloss on the pleading. *Young v. Gannon* (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 209.
3. **Wall-of-authority substitution for analysis.** The motion stacks cases in its table of authorities without running the three analytical filters California law requires before any privilege, preclusion, or channel doctrine attaches: gravamen under *Park v. Board of Trustees of California State University* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057; conduct-versus-communication under *Kimmel v. Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202; and evidence-versus-gravamen under *Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310.

Cumulatively, these three structural errors carry every ground-specific defect in the motion. Each of the eight grounds below fails as a matter of law on the pleading. Leave to amend is mandatory notwithstanding any defect. A Proposed Second Amended Complaint is already lodged in the file, which defeats any futility argument under *Price v. Dames & Moore* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 355, *Coffman Specialties, Inc. v. Department of Transportation* (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 1135, and *Dalton v. East Bay Mun. Utility Dist.* (1993) 18 Cal.App.4th 1566.

## Ground-by-Ground Review

The subsections below pair each asserted demurrer ground with the California authority that forecloses it and with the point of the filed pleading that disposes of it. The review is written for a reader who wants to check each theory against the pinpoint source. Each ground is legally independent. The demurrer must stand or fall on each.

### Ground 1. Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (a): "lack of jurisdiction" or "appeal in disguise"

This action is a new fraud and Unfair Competition Law action against the **carrier**, CSAA, for pre-denial institutional conduct; it is not an appeal of the **insured's** underlying negligence judgment. Code of Civil Procedure section 904.1 defines appealability. It does not categorically bar later civil actions on new duties against different defendants. *Rochin v. Pat Johnson Mfg. Co.* (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1228 addresses trial court power in specific settings, not categorical preclusion of later civil actions on distinct theories. The operative pleading disclaims appellate relief at paragraphs 3 and 10 and pleads new duties, new wrongs, and new remedies directed at CSAA itself.

**Disposition:** Ground 1 fails because it confuses appellate-review preclusion with the permissible scope of a later civil tort action on different duties against a non-party to the earlier case.

### Ground 2. Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (c): "another action pending"

Section 430.10, subdivision (c), requires the **same parties on the same cause of action**. California applies Primary Rights Theory. *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666, 681-682. Overlap of background facts does not merge fraud and UCL theories with a negligence primary right. The underlying negligence case adjudicated bodily-injury liability between an insured driver and a claimant. This case concerns the carrier's pre-denial institutional fraud against the claimant. The defendants are different. The primary rights are different. The duties are different. The relief is different. The later proceedings are pleaded only as corroborative context, not as the source of the new cause of action.

**Disposition:** *Crowley* controls. The SAC operationalizes the primary-rights wall at paragraphs 3 through 4, 6 through 9, and 24 through 27.

### Ground 3. Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (e): "failure to state a cause of action"

The operative pleading pleads each element of common-law fraud: misrepresentation and concealment, scienter, reliance, causation, and damages. The UCL claim rests on the **unlawful prong**, tethered to the independently pleaded common-law fraud, not on private enforcement of Insurance Code section 790.03. Economic damages are segregated from any bodily-injury recovery and include investigative costs, transcript and subpoena fees, lost business opportunities, and out-of-pocket expenses that no negligence jury was ever asked to consider.

CSAA's reliance on *Service by Medallion, Inc. v. Clorox Co.* (1996) 44 Cal.App.4th 1807 and *Orcilla v. Big Sur, Inc.* (2016) 244 Cal.App.4th 982 asks the court to resolve merits-stage factual disputes against the pleader. That is improper at demurrer. *Rannard v. Lockheed Aircraft Corp.* (1945) 26 Cal.2d 149, 156-157.

**Disposition:** Ground 3 confuses defense disagreement with the theory for facial insufficiency. The SAC states causes of action for fraud and UCL.

### Ground 4. Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10, subdivision (f): "uncertainty"

Uncertainty demurrers are disfavored and lie only where the pleading is so unintelligible that the responding party cannot reasonably answer. *Williams v. Beechnut Nutrition Corp.* (1986) 185 Cal.App.3d 135, 139, footnote 2; *A.J. Fistes Corp. v. GDL Best Contractors, Inc.* (2019) 38 Cal.App.5th 677, 695; *Mahan v. Charles W. Chan Ins. Agency, Inc.* (2017) 14 Cal.App.5th 841, 848. The bar is high.

The operative pleading identifies the defendant by name, the denial letter by date, the theory of non-investigation, the delayed-discovery chronology, the damages categories, and the causes of action in numbered paragraphs. CSAA has already filed a paragraph-specific demurrer and a paragraph-specific Anti-SLAPP motion against the same complaint. A party that briefs a complaint paragraph by paragraph cannot credibly argue that the same complaint is too uncertain to answer.

**Disposition:** Ground 4 is self-refuting on the face of CSAA's own filings.

### Ground 5. Civil Code section 47, subdivision (b): litigation privilege

The gravamen is the carrier's noncommunicative institutional failure to investigate before denying the claim. Privilege protects qualifying communications. It does not swallow noncommunicative conduct. *Kimmel v. Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202, 211-212. The February 25, 2021 denial letter is pleaded as evidence of the omission and as misrepresented investigative finality under *Kwikset Corp. v. Superior Court* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 310, not as the gravamen.

Where concealment deprives the claimant of a fair opportunity to know and respond to the true factual record, the extrinsic-fraud exception to privilege applies. *Action Apartment Assn., Inc. v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1246-1247.

**Disposition:** Privilege cannot swallow the conduct pleaded. The SAC distinguishes the noncommunicative omission from the corroborating communication and pleads the concealment as extrinsic, not intrinsic.

### Ground 6. Collateral attack and preclusion

Issue preclusion requires that the same issue have been actually litigated, necessarily decided, against the same party or one in privity, and that application of preclusion be fair. *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335, 341. The underlying negligence trial did not adjudicate fraud or UCL theories against CSAA. CSAA was not a party. The carrier's pre-denial institutional conduct was never actually litigated.

CSAA's generic collateral-attack authorities, *F.E.V. v. City of Anaheim* (2017) 15 Cal.App.5th 462, *Singh v. Lipworth* (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 813, and *Wouldridge v. Burns* (1968) 265 Cal.App.2d 82, state doctrine but do not perform *Crowley* primary-rights mapping. *U.S. v. Throckmorton* (1878) 98 U.S. 61 is federal rhetoric, not California preclusion law. California Primary Rights Theory and *Lucido* issue preclusion elements control.

**Disposition:** Definitions do not replace analysis. Neither *Crowley* nor *Lucido* is performed in the defense papers.

### Ground 7. Statute of limitations

The operative pleading pleads concealment, delayed discovery on or about January 17, 2025, and diligence thereafter. On demurrer, well-pleaded discovery and diligence allegations are accepted as true. *Cansino v. Bank of America* (2014) 224 Cal.App.4th 1462, 1472-1473; Code Civ. Proc. § 338, subd. (d); *Aryeh v. Canon Business Solutions, Inc.* (2013) 55 Cal.4th 1185. Nothing on the face of the pleading establishes earlier accrual as a matter of law.

**Disposition:** Limitations is a fact-bound merits defense. It cannot be resolved on the pleading where delayed discovery is coherent and non-self-contradictory.

### Ground 8. Standing and misjoinder

Plaintiff is the **direct addressee** of the February 25, 2021 denial letter. He relied on its asserted investigative finality and suffered separate economic harm. *Shaolian v. Safeco Ins. Co.* (1999) 71 Cal.App.4th 268 addresses contract-standing patterns; it does not bar direct fraud claims by the recipient of an insurer's misrepresentation. *Norton v. Hines* (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 917 and *Omega Video v. Superior Court* (1983) 146 Cal.App.3d 470 concern suits against opposing counsel for courtroom advocacy; the operative pleading targets CSAA's institutional claim-handling, not counsel's courtroom conduct.

**Disposition:** Direct communication plus direct reliance plus direct economic harm equals direct standing. The "suing counsel" frame is a category error.

### Ground 9 (offered as an umbrella). *Moradi-Shalal* and *Zhang* channel bars

*Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos.* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287, 305, **expressly preserves** "traditional theories as fraud" in appropriate common-law actions. *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364 forbids using the UCL to plead around an absolute statutory bar; it does not eliminate UCL theories whose unlawful prong is grounded in independent common-law fraud. Because the SAC disclaims any private Insurance Code section 790.03 enforcement theory and grounds the UCL claim in independent common-law fraud, neither categorical bar applies.

**Disposition:** Both opinions are narrow holdings. Reading them as total immunity is an overreach that the Supreme Court has not sanctioned and has specifically refused to adopt. The preservation text at *Moradi-Shalal*, page 305, is directly adverse authority that the defense papers do not address.

## Procedural Defects Independent of the Eight Grounds

Independent of the ground-by-ground analysis, the defense filings carry five procedural defects that, standing alone, support denial or warrant the court's criticism.

1. **Misuse of judicial notice.** The Request for Judicial Notice seeks notice of the **truth** of disputed factual contents within court records to contradict well-pleaded fraud allegations. Judicial notice establishes the **existence** of a record, not the truth of hearsay or disputed findings within it. Evidence Code sections 452 and 453; Code of Civil Procedure section 430.70. See [defense RFJN](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/demurrer-volley-apr2026/defense-demurrer/04-Request-for-Judicial-Notice-ISO-Demurrer.pdf).
2. **Importation of section 437c(g) language into a demurrer.** Citing summary-judgment language inside a demurrer notice is textbook standard bleed. Demurrer tests legal sufficiency, not evidence.
3. **California Rules of Court, rule 3.1308 misuse.** Tentative-ruling procedure is procedural; it does not alter the demurrer merits standard.
4. **Table-of-contents and table-of-authorities artifacts.** The memorandum contains formatting artifacts, including a TOC line reading "Civ. Code § 47(b)3" that does not correspond to a substantive subdivision (b)(3) argument in the body. That suggests an auto-generated authority list compiled without verification against the filed PDF and the official reporters. The court is entitled to test each citation against the source.
5. **Meet-and-confer performed but unresolved.** Conferral under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.41 occurred on April 6 and April 8, 2026. It did not resolve the issues. Plaintiff therefore lodged the Proposed SAC to narrow and clarify the pleading in direct response to the objections. That act alone defeats any claim that amendment would be futile.

## Mandatory Leave to Amend

California law sets one of the most protective standards in the nation for leave to amend. A demurrer may not be sustained without leave unless there is **no reasonable possibility** of cure. *Price v. Dames & Moore* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 355, 360-361; *Coffman Specialties, Inc. v. Department of Transportation* (2009) 176 Cal.App.4th 1135, 1144-1145; *Dalton v. East Bay Mun. Utility Dist.* (1993) 18 Cal.App.4th 1566, 1572. Plaintiff has lodged a Proposed Second Amended Complaint that addresses the very objections CSAA raises. That lodging defeats any futility argument. Leave to amend is not discretionary on this record; it is mandatory.

See the leave-to-amend packet at [apr-2026-sac-leave](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/apr-2026-sac-leave/).

## Consequences That Attach to the Demurrer as Filed

The question is not whether the demurrer will be overruled. The record supports overruling on structural grounds. The question is what collateral exposure CSAA and Porter Scott now carry because this demurrer is filed, served, and time-stamped on the Department 302 docket. The consequences below flow from the defects identified above, each by operation of California statute or decisional authority rather than by any single judge's discretion.

| Consequence | Triggering Conduct in the Filed Papers | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Pleading-stage closure** | Demurring on all eight grounds at once | Code Civ. Proc. § 430.41(b) |
| **Mandatory leave to amend** | Ignoring the contemporaneously lodged Proposed SAC | *Price*; *Coffman*; *Dalton* |
| **Judicial admissions binding the defense** | Paragraph-specific attack acknowledging addressee, claim file, and fact allegations arguendo | *Minish v. Hanuman Fellowship* (2013) 214 Cal.App.4th 437; *Valerio v. Andrew Youngquist Construction* (2002) 103 Cal.App.4th 1264 |
| **Law of the case adverse to CSAA** | Placing gravamen, section 47(b), *Crowley*, limitations, and answerability in issue | *Morohoshi v. Pacific Home* (2004) 34 Cal.4th 482; *Clemente v. State of California* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 202 |
| **Loss of the Anti-SLAPP discovery stay on denial of the companion motion** | Filing § 425.16 motion that will be denied | Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(g) |
| **Mandatory fee-shifting against CSAA on the Anti-SLAPP side** | Anti-SLAPP motion filed without *Park* step-one gravamen analysis | Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16(c)(1); *Moore v. Shaw* (2004) 116 Cal.App.4th 182; *Decker v. U.D. Registry, Inc.* (2003) 105 Cal.App.4th 1382 |
| **Admissibility as bad-faith evidence** | Using litigation privilege as shield over pre-litigation claim handling | *White v. Western Title Ins. Co.* (1985) 40 Cal.3d 870 |
| **Cross-case admissibility (CGC-25-631801)** | Paragraph-specific characterizations overlapping with the companion equity action | Evid. Code § 1220; *White* |
| **Code of Civil Procedure sections 128.7 and 128.5 sanctions exposure** | Legal contentions not warranted by existing law; bad-faith tactics | *Peake v. Underwood* (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 428; *Levy v. Blum* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 625 |
| **State Bar referral exposure** | Omission of directly adverse controlling authority (*Park*, *Kimmel*, *Kwikset*, *Baral*, *Action Apartment*, *Moradi-Shalal* p. 305) | Cal. Rules Prof. Conduct 3.1, 3.3; Bus. & Prof. Code § 6086.7(a)(2) |
| **Regulatory exposure** | Framing non-investigation as petitioning | Ins. Code §§ 790.03, 790.035; Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, §§ 2695.1 et seq. |
| **Malicious-prosecution vulnerability upon favorable termination** | Asserting categorical bars foreclosed by the text of the cited authority | *Paiva v. Nichols* (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 1007 |

No single item on this list is catastrophic in isolation. The compounding effect is the problem. A carrier that files a procedurally defective demurrer plus a procedurally defective Special Motion to Strike in a case where the plaintiff has lodged a clarifying SAC has created an asymmetric downside stack. Every denial becomes a foundation for the next plaintiff-favorable ruling. Every motion characterization becomes a judicial admission. Every missed authority becomes evidence of either institutional carelessness or deliberate strategic overreach. Neither reading helps the defendant.

On April 17, 2026, Plaintiff served a written notice on Tyler J. O'Connell, Esq., of Porter Scott, counsel of record for CSAA. The notice catalogs the defects and consequences summarized above, identifies the controlling authority omitted from the defense motion, and demands withdrawal of both the demurrer and the Special Motion to Strike in full and without condition. The notice operates as a good-faith identification under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7, subdivision (c), with a safe-harbor clock to be triggered by separate service of the draft motion; sets a withdrawal window closing April 30, 2026; and identifies the specific rules-based actions Plaintiff is prepared to pursue if the motions are not withdrawn, including section 425.16, subdivision (c)(1), fee-shifting on denial, section 128.5 bad-faith sanctions, section 128.7 sanctions, and a request for judicial referral to the State Bar under Business and Professions Code section 6086.7, subdivision (a)(2).

The notice is not a substitute for the opposition papers. It is a professional-courtesy notification before the written denial order issues. It is referenced here because it is a public litigation communication addressing the same defects the opposition briefs address, and because it catalogs the compounding collateral consequences that flow from leaving the motions on the docket. The notice itself is not a court filing; the operative briefs are the ones on the Department 302 docket and are linked above.
