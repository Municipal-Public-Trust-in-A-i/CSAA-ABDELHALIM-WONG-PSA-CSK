## What the declaration tries to do

The declaration supports CSAA’s special motion to strike under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16. It authenticates exhibits, summarizes the underlying trial and appeal, characterizes Plaintiff’s claims as targeting protected activity, and describes discovery and service. In the Anti-SLAPP posture, it is offered to frame facts and documents for step one and step two.

---

## Plaintiff’s critique (evidentiary and legal-conclusion defects)

Consistent with the longer narrative and Plaintiff’s concurrent evidentiary objections:

1. **Ultimate legal conclusions as facts.** Characterizations of what claims “arise from” and what is “protected” are legal issues, not lay facts from personal knowledge.
2. **Opinion and personal knowledge limits.** Intent of discovery conduct and similar topics exceed typical personal-knowledge foundation when offered as factual proof.
3. **Service facts and improper substantive use.** Service stipulations and procedural history may be narrow in purpose; using them for broad substantive conclusions can overreach.

These gaps matter because **Anti-SLAPP step one** still requires correct identification of the gravamen (see *Park v. Board of Trustees of California State University* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057), and **step two** requires minimal merit on a properly limited record (see *Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376). A declaration that mixes legal conclusions with fact also interacts with **judicial notice** boundaries on the companion RJN: notice can establish existence and authenticity of court records, not the truth of every assertion inside them.
