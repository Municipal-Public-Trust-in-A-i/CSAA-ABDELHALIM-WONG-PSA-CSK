### *Club Members for an Honest Election v. Sierra Club* (2008) 45 Cal.4th 309

#### Memorandum citations

**Memorandum citations** ([10152120.pdf](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/defense-anti-slapp-clerk/10152120.pdf)): TOA, p. 3, l. 23; argument, Part IV opening, p. 10, ll. 10-23.

#### Overview

**Defendant’s use and analytical defect.** **Overbroad Statutory Abstraction.** Treating broad Anti-SLAPP construction as a per se step-one win and bypassing *Park* gravamen isolation. Defendant invokes the broad remedial purpose of the Anti-SLAPP statute and the early dismissal of claims that interfere with speech and petitioning as rhetorical *dicta* and policy atmospherics favoring an early strike. That is an **Overbroad Statutory Abstraction** (*Park*): while the opinion supports broad construction *in the abstract*, Defendant overreads the case to imply that a broad statute means the defense automatically prevails at step one. That reasoning fails because it substitutes a broad-construction mandate for the mandatory “arising from” inquiry, incorrectly implying that a statute’s remedial purpose creates a *per se* presumption of protection that overrides gravamen isolation.

#### Plaintiff’s rebuttal

**Plaintiff’s rebuttal.** Plaintiff overcomes the defense’s misapplication of *Club Members for an Honest Election v. Sierra Club* because broad construction is not a substitute for the mandatory step-one requirement to isolate the specific injurious act causing the injury. Under *Park*, the remedial purpose of the statute cannot be used to collapse the gravamen inquiry into a generic “litigation” bucket. Because the operative wrong here is a noncommunicative institutional omission, the defense’s framing fails to satisfy the statutory “arising from” *nexus* as a question of law reviewed *de novo*.

Subdivision (e) also includes clause **(4)**, “any other conduct in furtherance of” petition or free speech in connection with a public issue or interest, which does not erase *Park*’s requirement to separate protected **petitioning** from the **specific injurious act** that is the gist of the wrong. Subdivision (b) itself separates the motion to strike from the **probability** showing; blanket rhetorical equations of “all allegations” with protected activity **collapse** those stages.

#### Plaintiff’s rebuttal

**Plaintiff’s rebuttal.** Plaintiff overcomes Defendant’s statutory framing by applying **section 425.16 as a whole**: broad construction in subdivision (a) does not create a *per se* step-one win, and subdivisions (e)(1) and (e)(2) do not **define away** a gravamen that rests on **noncommunicative** institutional conduct or omissions. Under *Park*, the court still isolates whether the claim **arises from** protected activity or from conduct that is **merely incidental** to it. Where Defendant’s theory depends on treating a denial letter or investigation as the **only** act from which injury flows, *Park* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057, 1064, read with *City of Cotati v. Cashman* (2002) 29 Cal.4th 69, 78, supports treating protected speech as **evidence** of a different injurious act when the speech is **not** itself the wrong complained of, without conceding that the **gist** is petitioning. *Laker*’s own **“absent illegality”** framing limits how far “official proceeding” analogies extend when Plaintiff alleges **fraud, concealment, or systemic** practices, not good-faith claim handling alone.

### Civil Code section 47 (subdivision (b))

#### Memorandum citations

**Memorandum citations** ([10152120.pdf](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/defense-anti-slapp-clerk/10152120.pdf)): TOA, p. 4, l. 39; argument examples: Part IV.A introductory block, p. 10, l. 24 through p. 12, l. 5; Part IV.B.1, p. 15, l. 23 through p. 16, l. 23; *Notice*, pp. 2-3, ll. 5-12.

#### Overview

**Defendant’s use and analytical defect.** **Truncated statute; Conflation of Conduct and Communication.** Quoting subdivision (b) branch while ignoring exceptions and treating privilege as absolute against fraud and conduct theories. Defendant quotes the opening branch of subdivision (b), describing privileged publications in legislative, judicial, or other official proceedings, and then treats **section 47(b)** as an **absolute** bar to fraud and to both Anti-SLAPP steps, including through *Briggs* (“equally entitled to the benefits of section 425.16”) and *Pollock* (“doubts” favor privilege). That presentation is structurally **incomplete** relative to the **same subdivision**: section 47(b) contains **numbered exceptions**, including communications in furtherance of **intentional destruction or alteration of physical evidence** (subdivision (b)(2)) and communications **knowingly concealing the existence of insurance** in a judicial proceeding (subdivision (b)(3)). The memorandum does not integrate those exceptions into its “all statements are privileged” thesis. Even aside from exceptions, Defendant’s move is a **Conflation of Conduct and Communication**: it assumes that every alleged wrong is a **communication** subject to privilege, rather than **conduct** that may be evidenced by communications, a line *Kimmel* and the case clusters above already stress.

#### Plaintiff’s rebuttal

**Plaintiff’s rebuttal.** Plaintiff overcomes Defendant’s use of section 47(b) by insisting on **full subdivision context** and **conduct-versus-communication** separation. Privilege attaches to **qualifying communications**, not to every **institutional** practice or omission that the complaint targets. *Action Apartment* and *Flatley* (Cluster C) supply limits on extending the privilege where **unlawful conduct** or fraud on the court is alleged, so that section 47(b) cannot operate as a **blanket immunity** to all tort theories. Where the complaint’s gravamen is **noncommunicative** wrongdoing, or falls within statutory **exceptions** Plaintiff is prepared to prove, Defendant cannot rely on a **truncated** quotation of (b) to strike the action as a matter of law at step one.

### Business and Professions Code section 17200

#### Memorandum citations

**Memorandum citations** ([10152120.pdf](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/defense-anti-slapp-clerk/10152120.pdf)): TOA, p. 4, l. 38; argument: Part II (Summary of Argument), p. 6, ll. 1-28; Part IV.B.3, pp. 17-19, l. 10 on p. 17 through l. 28 on p. 19.

#### Overview

**Defendant’s use and analytical defect.** **Policy gloss; Channel error.** Adding non-text UCL policy to narrow prongs and overreading *Zhang* / *Moradi-Shalal* as total immunity. Defendant quotes the **definitional** sentence of section 17200 accurately: unfair competition includes **unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent** business acts or practices and related advertising categories. **Plaintiff’s rebuttal.** Plaintiff overcomes Defendant’s use of section 17200 by **separating accurate black-letter definition from Defendant’s policy narrative**. Defendant’s insistence that a jury verdict on **underlying tort** liability ends all **UCL** analysis confuses **merits** outcomes in one action with **elements** and **channel** questions in this action.

### Insurance Code section 790.03 (with 10 Cal. Code Regs. section 2695.7)

#### Memorandum citations

**Memorandum citations** ([10152120.pdf](../../03-CASE-CGC-25-631802/defense-anti-slapp-clerk/10152120.pdf)): TOA: Insurance Code section 790.03 at p. 4, l. 41; 10 Cal. Code Regs. section 2695.7 at p. 4, l. 44; argument, Part IV.A.1, pp. 12-13, ll. 6-12.

#### Overview

**Defendant’s use and analytical defect.** **Compliance-to-immunity leap; Overbroad Statutory Abstraction.** Inferring Anti-SLAPP immunity and fraud disproof from regulatory timing and standards without *Park* or *Baral* analysis. Defendant invokes section 790.03 together with **10 Cal. Code Regs. section 2695.7** to argue that CSAA satisfied a **duty to investigate promptly** and issue a timely denial, and that this compliance supports characterization of the denial as **protected** pre-litigation activity (including under *Aronson*). The **verbatim** timing language quoted in the memorandum tracks the **regulation**, while **790.03** supplies the broader **unfair claims settlement practices** framework, particularly subdivision (h). The inferential leap is the problem: **compliance** with claim-handling norms (even if true) does **not** establish that Plaintiff’s causes of action **arise from** protected **petitioning** under section 425.16, nor does it prove the **absence** of **fraud** or **unfair** practices as a matter of law at the Anti-SLAPP stage. Defendant also cites *Zhang* for the proposition that **790.03(h)** violations are **not** independently actionable in certain **UCL** configurations; that limitation **does not** convert statutory claim standards into a **complete** immunity shield against all theories Plaintiff pleads.

#### Plaintiff’s rebuttal

**Plaintiff’s rebuttal.** Plaintiff overcomes Defendant’s use of section 790.03 by treating it as **what it is**: a **source of public policy and standards** for insurer conduct in the business of insurance, not a **substitute** for *Park* gravamen analysis or for *Baral*’s **probability** standard. Allegations that go to **misrepresentation, concealment, or systematic mishandling** are not **refuted** by pointing to **any single** regulatory timeline. At step two, Defendant still cannot **judicially notice** away disputed facts; at step one, **790.03** cannot **collapse** noncommunicative wrongs into privileged **communications**. The memorandum’s blend of **790.03**, **CCR 2695.7**, and Anti-SLAPP **immunity** is an **Overbroad Statutory Abstraction** that moves from **regulatory compliance** to **civil immunity** without the intermediate statutory and precedential steps California law requires.
