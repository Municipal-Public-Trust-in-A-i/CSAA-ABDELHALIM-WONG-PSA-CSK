## 2. Short answer to your question

**Yes — structurally, the April 22 Motions to Strike are a re-skin of the same defensive theory already filed on April 9–10 in the Anti-SLAPP and Demurrer packages.** The doctrinal spine is identical. What changed is the **procedural vehicle** (from § 425.16 and § 430.10 to §§ 435/436), the **hearing date** (pushed out ~7–10 days past the prior-set May 12/13 hearings to **May 19** / **May 22**), and a **narrowing of relief** to damages-level strike rather than dismissal. The authorities, facts, and narrative ("9–3 verdict → appeal A173827 is the only lane → privilege + standing + collateral estoppel bar everything") are recycled verbatim.

## 4. Delta Analysis — What actually changed on April 22

### 4.1 Procedural vehicle
- **Old (A):** CCP § 425.16 — two-prong test; fee-shifting to movant if granted.
- **Old (B):** CCP § 430.10 — facial demurrer; sustained → leave or dismissal.
- **NEW (C):** **CCP §§ 435, 436** — motion to strike "irrelevant, false, or improper" matter; discretionary; *Coyne v. Krempels* 35 Cal.2d 257 standard.

Why this matters: § 436 is a **lower-visibility, lower-stakes vehicle**. It lets them **re-litigate the same doctrines a third time** if Rounds A and B lose, while preserving appellate issues. It also **targets damages line-by-line** rather than seeking dismissal, so even a partial grant narrows your recovery prayer without disposing of liability.

### 4.2 Relief requested (narrowed)
- Rounds A and B sought **dismissal** of the FAC.
- Round C seeks **either dismissal OR line-item strike of damages buckets** (litigation costs, lost earnings / productive time, medical/therapy, ancillary equitable $, fee prayer, punitive). The **Notice of Motion itself enumerates each damages category by FAC page/line** — a mechanical, redundant surgical strike designed to survive even if the collateral-attack theory fails.

### 4.3 Hearing-date push (effectively a trial-timeline stall)
| Vehicle | 801 hearing | 802 hearing |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Anti-SLAPP (A) | **May 12, 2026**, Dept. 301 | **May 13, 2026**, Dept. 302 |
| Demurrer (B) | **May 12, 2026**, Dept. 301 | **May 13, 2026** |
| § 436 MTS (C — NEW) | **May 22, 2026**, Dept. 301 | **May 19, 2026**, Dept. 302 |

- Filing a third substantive attack on the FAC **10 days after the first two**, noticed for hearings **10 days later** than A/B, is a classic stacking maneuver: if A or B is granted with leave, the § 436 motion can be folded into the amended pleading; if A/B is denied, the § 436 motion takes a third bite at the apple. Either way, the **operative pleading cannot reach answer/discovery until at least late May 2026**, and any ruling on Round C compresses or displaces the case-management / trial-setting conference. The effect, whether or not it is the stated purpose, is to **move the trial date**.

### 4.4 Authorities delta (net new vs. recycled)

**Net new authorities in Round C (not present in A/B defense packages):**
- *Coyne v. Krempels* (1950) 35 Cal.2d 257 — § 436 standard (cited as "36 Cal.2d 257" in the 802 MPA — **typo / citation defect** in their own brief; canonical is **35 Cal.2d 257**, which 801 MPA gets right).
- *Trope v. Katz* (1995) 11 Cal.4th 274 — pro per cannot recover attorney's fees. (802 MPA only; new emphasis.)
- *Food Pro Int'l v. Farmers Ins. Exch.* (2008) 169 Cal.App.4th 976 — punitive pleading standard. (802 MPA only.)
- *TODAY'S IV, Inc. v. Los Angeles County MTA* (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 1137 — "oppression" under § 3294.
- *McNeal v. Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc.* (2022) 80 Cal.App.5th 841 — "despicable" standard.
- *Stewart v. Truck Ins. Exchange* (1993) 17 Cal.App.4th 468; *Angelica* 28 Cal.3d 908 — punitive reinforcement (802).
- C.C.P. § 435, § 436, § 1032, § 1021 — re-cited as relief predicates.

**Recycled verbatim from A/B:** *Ribas*, *Kulchar*, *Shaolian*, *Norton*, *Omega*, *F.E.V.*, *Rico*, *Wouldridge*, *Singh*, *Throckmorton*, Civ. § 47(b), Evid. §§ 452/453, C.C.P. § 430.70 (RFJN hook).

**Bottom line on authorities:** ~80% of the case citations are the **same universe** used in Rounds A and B. The new citations cluster **entirely around the fee/punitive damages sub-argument**, which is what a § 436 motion is procedurally designed to reach.

This is the **only substantive difference** between the two new Round C packages.

## 5. Defects / exploitable seams in the new Round C package

1. **Citation defect in 802 MPA:** *Coyne v. Krempels* cited as "(1950) 36 Cal.2d 257" — canonical reporter is **35 Cal.2d 257** (801 MPA gets it right). Points to shared template with uncorrected copy-paste. (See `802-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/PARSED/BREAKDOWN/MPA2-CCP436.md`.)
2. **Pincite drift:** *F.E.V.* cited as **15 Cal.App.5th 462** in 802 vs **15 Cal.App.5th 463** in 801. Same opinion; inconsistent pincite.
3. **Improper procedural ladder:** Rounds A and B remain **pending and unruled** as of the Apr. 22 filing. § 436 is not a permitted vehicle to re-argue matter already placed at issue under § 425.16 or § 430.10 **when no amended pleading has been filed in between**. The FAC has not been amended since Feb. 25, 2026. The "operative pleading attacked" is **identical** across A, B, and C — this is stacking, not sequencing.
4. **Substantive redundancy:** A § 436 motion tests **irrelevant/false/improper matter on the face of the pleading**. The defense's fact section (594102 trial, verdict, appeal) is **extrinsic** — it requires the RFJN to reach. If the RFJN is narrowed or denied, the § 436 theory collapses to pure facial review of the FAC.
5. **Damages-only strike as backdoor MSJ:** Striking damages line items without answer/discovery converts § 436 into a *de facto* partial summary judgment — arguably improper under *PH II v. Superior Court* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 1680 and the general rule that § 436 is not a substitute for demurrer or MSJ.
6. **Privilege argument is not "face of the pleading":** § 47(b) requires evaluating communications **in context**; FAC alleges fraud-on-the-court and extrinsic conduct (911/CAD/BWC suppression) — not a § 436 subject.
7. **Trial-date displacement:** The effective postponement of trial is **not supported by any declaration of diligence** and is not tied to a proper § 435(b)(3) timing showing — preserves a CMC/continuance objection.

- **Treat Rounds A + B + C as a single coordinated attack** and file a **consolidated opposition / notice of related / stay request** pointing out the procedural ladder abuse (pillar 3 above).
- **Hit the citation defects** (Coyne 35 vs 36; F.E.V. 462 vs 463) in your opposition caption page to undermine template credibility.
- **Preserve the trial-date objection** on the record at the next CMC — the objective effect of the three stacked defensive rounds is to vacate trial setting without a noticed motion.
