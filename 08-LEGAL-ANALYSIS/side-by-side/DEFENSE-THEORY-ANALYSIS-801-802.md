## I. Executive summary

The defense — Porter Scott for CSAA, Carbone Smith & Koyama, and Michael R. Chambers — has now filed, in both 801 and 802, a pair of motions to strike under Code of Civil Procedure §§ 435 and 436. Stripped of ornamentation, their theory across both cases reduces to a single, five-part argument:

1. **Everything is litigation conduct**, so everything is absolutely privileged under Civil Code § 47(b) and *Ribas v. Clark* (1985) 38 Cal.3d 355.
2. **Plaintiff has no standing** to sue an insurer for handling the defense of its insured, or to sue defense counsel for representing that insured, under *Shaolian v. Safeco Ins. Co.* (1999) 71 Cal.App.4th 268, *Norton v. Hines* (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 917, and *Omega Video v. Superior Court* (1983) 146 Cal.App.3d 470.
3. **Plaintiff is collaterally estopped** because he lost the underlying 21-594102 jury trial 9–3; the new suits are just collateral attacks on a final judgment under *F.E.V. v. City of Anaheim* (2017) 15 Cal.App.5th 462/463, *Wouldridge v. Burns* (1968) 265 Cal.App.2d 82, *Singh v. Lipworth* (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 813, and *U.S. v. Throckmorton* (1878) 98 U.S. 61.
4. **Plaintiff is not a prevailing party**, so all "costs" are barred under CCP § 1032; and as a pro per, Plaintiff cannot recover attorney fees under *Trope v. Katz* (1995) 11 Cal.4th 274.
5. **Punitive damages fail** because the denial-letter-plus-defense-verdict record cannot meet the "clear and convincing evidence" threshold of Civil Code § 3294, *Food Pro Internat., Inc. v. Farmers Ins. Exchange* (2008) 169 Cal.App.4th 976, *Today's IV v. Los Angeles Metro. Transp. Auth.* (2022) 83 Cal.App.5th 1137, and *McNeal v. Whittaker, Clark & Daniels, Inc.* (2022) 80 Cal.App.5th 841.

In 801 the defense additionally argues that the entire "independent action in equity for extrinsic fraud" is "not legally supportable" under *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, because Plaintiff actually participated in the underlying trial and presented the 911, BWC, and CAD evidence to the jury. In 802 the defense additionally argues that the fraud claim fails the heightened particularity of *Lazar v. Superior Court* (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631, and that the "denial letter" narrative is not fraud, oppression, or malice under Civ. Code § 3294.

Every one of these arguments has a recognizable fatal flaw — either a misstatement of the governing doctrine, an overstatement of a holding, a conclusory assertion masquerading as law, or a procedural error in using a CCP § 436 motion as a demurrer substitute. Plaintiff's SAC (FAC in defense's nomenclature) already anticipated most of these objections; the proposed TACs in both cases (filed April 22, 2026, the same day as the motions to strike) expressly cure each objection on an element-by-element basis under the Cure Matrix (TAC-RESPONSE 07 in each folder). What follows is a comprehensive dissection of each defense theory, each flaw, and each corresponding cure.

## II. The statutory frame the defense chose — and why it limits them

Both motions are brought under Code of Civil Procedure §§ 435 and 436. Section 435 is the procedural notice statute. Section 436 is the substantive strike statute and it authorizes the court to strike only:

> "(a) any irrelevant, false, or improper matter inserted in any pleading" or
> "(b) all or any part of any pleading not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state, a court rule, or an order of the court."

Both MPAs quote *Coyne v. Krempels* for the proposition that "[w]here pleadings are defective, the defect may be raised by a demurrer or motion to strike or by motion for judgment on the pleadings." The 801 MPA cites *Coyne* at 35 Cal.2d 257, 263; the 802 MPA cites *Coyne* at **36 Cal.2d 257**, which is a typographical or OCR error — the canonical reporter pincite for *Coyne* is 35 Cal.2d 257. This is flagged in `SIDE-BY-SIDE/RECYCLED-AUTHORITIES.md` and `SIDE-BY-SIDE/801-vs-802-AUTHORITY-CROSSREF.md`. That is a small thing; but it sits at the start of a pattern of carelessness that recurs across both briefs.

The statutory limitation matters here because a § 436 motion is not a general-purpose sufficiency attack. That is what a demurrer does. Section 436 is narrower: it targets matter that is "irrelevant, false, or improper" on the face of the pleading, or matter that does not conform to law, a rule, or a court order. A § 436 motion cannot be used to re-litigate disputed fact allegations, and it cannot be used to impose a heightened pleading standard on a well-pleaded allegation. The defense's briefs repeatedly ask the court to accept factual conclusions — "Plaintiff was able to present all the evidence to the jury," "CSAA conducted a routine investigation," "the jury's 9–3 verdict confirms CSAA's denial was proper" — that the face of the pleading explicitly contests. On a § 436 motion, the court must accept the pleading's well-pleaded allegations as true (as it would on demurrer), not the movant's competing narrative. That is the first structural error.

The second structural error is that the defense treats a § 436 motion as a claim-dispositive vehicle: "strike the complaint in its entirety." A court's discretion under § 436 is broad, but an order striking an entire complaint in lieu of ruling on a demurrer or a motion for judgment on the pleadings is disfavored and reversible when it deprives the plaintiff of leave to amend where amendment is possible. Our TAC filings make that impossibility argument unavailable: the TAC is already drafted, already lodged, and already cures the objections identified in the motion. California's pro-amendment policy — *Howard v. County of San Diego* (2010) 184 Cal.App.4th 1422, 1428; *Morgan v. Superior Court* (1959) 172 Cal.App.2d 527, 530; *Blank v. Kirwan* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318; *Kolani v. Gluska* (1998) 64 Cal.App.4th 402, 412 — makes striking "in its entirety" legally available only if no reasonable possibility of cure exists. The TAC, by design, shows the opposite.

The third structural error is the defense's reliance on their own RFJN exhibits to bootstrap factual contentions. Both RFJNs ask the court to judicially notice (1) the FAC itself, (2) the Register of Actions in 594102, and (3) the Notice of Entry of Judgment in 594102. Under Evidence Code §§ 452(d), 453, and CCP § 430.70, a court may take judicial notice of the existence of those records, but not of the truth of the factual matters asserted within them. The defense's MPAs repeatedly use the ROA and NOE for a stronger, prohibited purpose — as affirmative proof that Plaintiff "presented all the evidence he sought" at trial, that CSAA's denial was "confirmed" by the jury, and that "the same issues were … ruled upon" pre-trial. None of those propositions are properly noticed from the existence of the ROA or NOE. A dual-purpose objection to the RFJN — accepting existence but contesting the truth-of-contents use — is straightforward, and it undermines the defense's factual predicate across both motions.

Taken together, the defense has over-scoped a narrow statute, imported disputed facts through an improperly aggressive RFJN, and asked the court to pre-judge credibility disputes that are not suitable for § 436 resolution.

## IV. Defense theory #1 — "Absolute litigation privilege under Civ. Code § 47(b) bars everything"

**What the defense says.** Both MPAs build their § 47(b) argument around *Ribas v. Clark*'s statement that "[t]he only exception to the application of 47(2) to tort suits has been for malicious prosecution actions," and that the privilege "poses a clear bar to [an] action insofar as it is based on … the tort of intentional infliction of emotional distress." In 801 the argument is that all the FAC's allegations "arise from statements, filings, or other communicative acts undertaken in connection with prior judicial proceedings." In 802 the argument is that the 2021 denial letter, the 2024 in-limine practice, and the entire trial presentation are all within § 47(b). Both MPAs then treat that privilege as fatal because Plaintiff has not pleaded malicious prosecution.

**Why this fails on the law.** *Ribas* is a narrow holding. It applies to communications made in a judicial proceeding, and it does not reach non-communicative conduct. The California Supreme Court expressly recognized that limit in *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1058: "[T]he litigation privilege applies to communicative acts and not tortious courses of conduct." Custodial possession of evidence, chain-of-custody control, failure to produce documents in discovery, and failure to correct a false record are not "publications" or "broadcasts" within § 47(b). They are non-communicative acts. *Rusheen* is the dispositive authority the defense never cites.

The *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299 exception is equally fatal. *Flatley* holds that conduct that is criminal or illegal as a matter of law is categorically outside § 47(b). Business and Professions Code § 6128(a) criminalizes attorney "deceit or collusion … with intent to deceive the court or any party." Our factual spine — the February 18, 2025 "news to me" statements, the October 29, 2024 Day-7 "provided by Dolan" transcript, the concealment of the Dolan production set — alleges § 6128(a) conduct. Under *Flatley*, § 47(b) does not protect alleged § 6128(a) deceit.

For 802, there is a second, independently fatal limitation: **pre-litigation claim handling is not privileged**. *Action Apartment Ass'n, Inc. v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1248–49 holds the privilege requires that the communication be made "in connection with a judicial proceeding" that is "contemplated in good faith and under serious consideration." The February 25, 2021 denial letter was issued before any litigation was filed, contemplated, or threatened by CSAA. It was a claim-handling communication governed by Title 10 CCR §§ 2695.4 and 2695.7 and Insurance Code § 790.03(h), not a litigation privilege event. The defense's § 47(b) argument completely collapses the temporal distinction between (a) 2021 claims-handling conduct, which sits squarely outside the privilege, and (b) 2024–2025 trial conduct, which may or may not be privileged depending on whether it is communicative.

**Where the TAC cures this.** The 801 TAC has a dedicated Section II "Non-Communicative Conduct Block" (¶¶ 5–7) that identifies, with specificity, the custodial possession of the Dolan production set (CARBONE00001–CARBONE00789), the non-disclosure, the chain-of-custody break, CSAA's direction/supervision, and the failure to correct the record after the Day-7 admission. It then anchors the *Flatley* illegality carve-out by pleading § 6128(a) as an independent tort (¶¶ 6–7). The 802 TAC has a parallel Section II "Pre-Litigation Privilege Carveout" (¶¶ 13–19) identifying the 2021 denial as a pre-litigation communication under *Action Apartment*, temporally separating 2021 conduct from 2024 trial conduct, and pleading non-communicative failure-to-investigate (no 911, no BWC, no CAD) as a separate basis.

**Overstatement / conclusory rhetoric the defense uses.** Both MPAs repeatedly describe § 47(b) as "absolute." The word "absolute" is the defense's tell. The privilege is absolute *only within its scope*. Outside its scope — non-communicative conduct, non-litigation communications, illegal conduct — it is not a privilege at all. When the defense writes "all the conduct alleged in Plaintiff's FAC arises from statements, filings, or other communicative acts undertaken in connection with prior judicial proceedings" (MPA at 5–6; MPA2 at 7–8), that is not a legal argument. It is an ipse dixit. The face of the FAC alleges non-communicative custodial possession, non-disclosure, and failure-to-investigate, all of which the defense simply ignores rather than distinguishes.

## V. Defense theory #2 — "No standing under *Shaolian* / *Norton* / *Omega*"

**What the defense says.** Both MPAs recite *Shaolian v. Safeco* for "[a]s a general rule, a third party may directly sue an insurer only when there has been an assignment of rights by, or a final judgment against, the insured." Then, paired with *Norton v. Hines* and *Omega Video v. Superior Court*, the defense asserts that "an attorney cannot be sued by an adverse party for that attorneys' actions in representing their client, except for an action for malicious prosecution." The defense applies this rule to strike the entirety of both FACs, or at minimum all compensatory damages.

**Why this fails on the law.** The *Shaolian* rule is not universal. It is a rule about *direct-action suits* to collect insurance benefits from the insurer of a third-party wrongdoer. It does not bar: (a) an independent action in equity to vacate a judgment procured by fraud, where the insurer's agents committed the fraud (801); (b) a first-party bad-faith, fraud, and UCL claim against one's own insurer or, through the UCL predicate of Insurance Code § 790.03(h), against any insurer whose claim-handling practices injured the plaintiff (802 via *State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Superior Court* (1996) 45 Cal.App.4th 1093, 1103–04 — the *Moradi-Shalal* carve-out); or (c) ratification liability under Civil Code § 2338, where the principal insurer is liable for wrongful acts of its retained agent-attorneys.

*Norton* and *Omega* likewise do not bar this action. Both stand for the narrow proposition that an adverse party cannot sue opposing counsel for litigation conduct *absent an independent tort.* An independent action in equity for extrinsic fraud, pleaded under *Kougasian v. TMSL, Inc.* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1032–35 and *Olivera v. Grace* (1942) 19 Cal.2d 570, 575–76, is the independent equitable action the California Supreme Court has expressly preserved. And § 6128(a) is, by statute, an independent cause of action against attorneys *specifically* brought by adverse parties — the Legislature passed a statute aimed at attorney-on-court-on-adversary deceit.

**Where the TACs cure this.** The 801 TAC's Section III "Standing Block" (¶¶ 8–13) plants three independent standing flags: (1) *Kougasian* / *Olivera* equity-action standing; (2) agency / ratification liability for CSAA under Civ. Code § 2338; and (3) § 6128(a) as independent tort, reinforced by the *Rickley v. Goodfriend* (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 1136 and *Shaffer v. Superior Court* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 993 line recognizing statutory deceit by attorneys as actionable by the deceived party.

The 802 TAC's Section I "Direct-Duty Gravamen" (¶¶ 1–12) plants Title 10 CCR §§ 2695.4 and 2695.7(b)/(d) as regulatory duties that run directly to claimants, Business & Professions Code § 17200 as the UCL vehicle with *State Farm Fire & Casualty*'s *Moradi-Shalal* carve-out, and the February 25, 2021 denial letter as an actionable pre-litigation representation. That is an entirely different standing rationale than the defendant's-insurer-direct-action theory *Shaolian* addresses. The defense's standing brief does not even attempt to distinguish *Kougasian*, *Olivera*, or the UCL carve-out — those cases simply do not appear in the MPAs, which is itself a tell.

**Conclusory rhetoric.** The 801 MPA concludes "Plaintiff lacks standing against all Defendant's in their capacity as either the insurance carrier for a third party, or as defense counsel for a third party insured — to whom Plaintiff lost at trial." That sentence assumes the conclusion. Whether Plaintiff "lost at trial" is not responsive to whether an extrinsic-fraud action exists; whether the defendants are "insurance carrier" or "defense counsel" does not answer whether agency / ratification or § 6128(a) standing applies. The defense repeats a one-sentence *Shaolian* syllogism and calls it dispositive.

## VI. Defense theory #3 — "Collateral attack / issue preclusion"

**What the defense says.** Both MPAs deploy the *F.E.V. / Rico / Wouldridge / Singh / Throckmorton* cluster. The substantive position is that the 9–3 defense verdict in 21-594102, plus the Notice of Entry of Judgment, plus Plaintiff's pending appeal in A173827, preclude any relitigation of damages or liability issues in 801 or 802. The MPAs characterize both new suits as "collateral attacks" on a "valid judgment." Both MPAs also cite *Wouldridge* for the proposition that "[a] judgment of a court of general jurisdiction can only be set aside on collateral attack if the judgment is void on the face of the record."

**Why this fails on the law.** The defense's entire collateral-attack argument collapses on one distinction: **an independent action in equity for extrinsic fraud is not a collateral attack**. It is a direct, separately-authorized action that the California Supreme Court has specifically protected. *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467 — the very case the defense relies on in 801 — explicitly preserves the independent equitable action where extrinsic fraud prevented a fair hearing. *Kougasian v. TMSL* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028, 1032–35, reaffirms that independent actions for extrinsic fraud remain available even post-judgment, and it explicitly distinguishes them from "collateral attacks" on a facially valid judgment. *Olivera v. Grace* (1942) 19 Cal.2d 570 is the foundational case, dating back 80+ years.

*Wouldridge*'s "void on the face of the record" formulation is for *collateral* attacks, not *direct* equitable actions. The defense's use of *Wouldridge* here — implying that unless the 21-594102 judgment is void on its face, this suit cannot proceed — is a straightforward doctrinal error. The *Kougasian* / *Olivera* framework is a separate track.

The *Singh v. Lipworth* citation is even weaker. *Singh* states that "collateral estoppel precludes relitigation of issues argued and decided in prior proceedings." But issue preclusion has five elements under *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335 — identical issue, actually litigated, necessarily decided, final judgment, and same party or privy — and the defense makes no attempt to walk through any of those elements against either the 801 or 802 theories. The issue in 21-594102 was *negligence* — whether Abdelhalim breached a Vehicle Code duty toward Plaintiff. The issues in 801 are whether defense counsel fraudulently concealed evidence and whether that fraud procured the judgment. The issues in 802 are whether CSAA committed fraud and bad faith in its 2021 claim handling and whether its denial practices violate the UCL. Those are three different primary rights under *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888, *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666, and *Lucido* itself — a claim-preclusion spine the defense does not engage.

*Throckmorton* (1878), 98 U.S. 61, is federal and ancient; it is sometimes cited in California for the general principle against repeated litigation. But *Throckmorton* famously distinguishes **extrinsic** from **intrinsic** fraud, and permits equitable vacatur in extrinsic-fraud cases. The defense cites *Throckmorton* for the part they like ("prevent repeated litigation between the same parties in regard to the same subject of controversy") while ignoring the extrinsic-fraud carve-out that same opinion preserves. That is a selective reading of the very case they put in their brief.

**Where the TACs cure this.** The 801 TAC's Section IV "Primary Rights Statement" (¶¶ 14–16) expressly walks through the *Lucido* factors and asserts the distinct primary right — freedom from fraud on the court — that is not the same primary right as the personal-injury right litigated in 21-594102. The TAC also expressly distinguishes appeal (A173827, which concerns instructional error and is a different remedial vehicle) from this action (which concerns evidence fraudulently concealed from both Plaintiff and the trier of fact, discovered post-trial). Section VIII (¶¶ 26–27) is specifically the "Appeal Non-Preclusion" paragraph.

The 802 TAC's Section VI "Primary Rights / Reservation" (¶¶ 43–45) anchors a distinct primary right — freedom from fraudulent claim handling — and anchors all damages to February 25, 2021 and forward, so there is no temporal overlap with the personal-injury damages tried in 21-594102.

**Overstatement.** The defense claims Plaintiff is "simply attempting to relitigate all the same damages that he failed to obtain at his underlying jury trial." That is a recharacterization, not a doctrinal point. Plaintiff's reservation paragraphs in both TACs (801 TAC ¶ 17 and 802 TAC ¶¶ 43–45) expressly disclaim personal-injury damages from 21-594102, expressly disclaim prevailing-party costs from 21-594102 (except derivatively upon vacatur), and expressly disclaim duplicative damages between 801 and 802. There is no overlap the defense can colorably identify once the TACs are in front of the court. Defense's insistence on the "relitigation" framing ignores the reservation on the face of the TAC.

## VII. Defense theory #4 — "No prevailing party, no costs" and "Pro per cannot recover fees"

**What the defense says.** Both MPAs argue that Plaintiff was not a prevailing party in 21-594102, so he cannot recover costs as a matter of CCP § 1032; and that as a self-represented litigant he cannot recover attorney fees under *Trope v. Katz* (1995) 11 Cal.4th 274. Both arguments are used to strike the damages buckets labeled "costs of suit," "litigation costs and economic injury," "forced third-party litigation," and any attorney-fee-like item.

**Why this fails on the law.** *Trope* bars fee recovery by a pro se attorney for his or her own time in a suit where the plaintiff represents themselves. *Trope* does not bar: (a) *Prentice* damages — attorney fees and litigation expenses incurred in a **separate, prior** action caused by the defendant's tortious conduct, which are a measure of tort damages rather than a "fee award" under *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618; (b) conditional fees under CCP § 1021.5 (Private Attorney General), if Plaintiff prevails in a case conferring significant public benefit; (c) § 6128(a) treble-damage recovery, which is a statutory damage measure, not a *Trope* fee award; (d) costs of suit in **this** action under CCP §§ 1032, 1033.5.

The CCP § 1032 argument is even narrower than the defense uses it. Section 1032 addresses who gets costs as a "prevailing party" in an action. It does not follow that because Plaintiff lost 21-594102, he may not sue, in a separate tort action, to recover the attorney fees and litigation costs he incurred there **as a measure of tort damages** against a defendant whose wrongdoing caused the second-action costs. That is exactly what *Prentice* authorizes.

**Where the TACs cure this.** The 802 TAC's Section III "Attorney's Fees Cure" (¶¶ 20–24) (a) expressly deletes any pro per fee demand, (b) re-pleads *Prentice* fees as tort damages with a three-element test, and (c) reserves CCP § 1021.5 fees conditionally. Section V ¶ 41 limits costs of suit to "this action only" under CCP §§ 1032 and 1033.5. The 801 TAC's Section V (¶ 17) contains parallel reservation language, and its Tier 3 statutory-damages prayer (¶ 20) anchors § 6128(a) treble damages as a statutory tort-damage measure independent of *Trope*.

The defense briefs, quite notably, do not acknowledge *Prentice* — a 1963 California Supreme Court decision — anywhere in the MPA. Its absence is another tell; the defense is aware the *Trope* argument has a *Prentice* exception, and it chose not to address it.

**Conclusory rhetoric.** The 802 MPA footnotes: "The FAC duplicitously claims that 'Plaintiff does not seek attorney fees in this action as a prevailing-party fee award.' (FAC ¶ 28). Well, naturally, given that Plaintiff lost his jury trial and owes approximately $75,000.00 in costs." That footnote is doing two improper things: (1) it is sarcastic editorializing in a merits brief, which is not argument; and (2) it imports the $75,000 cost judgment from 21-594102 as a fact the court should credit on a § 436 motion, which is exactly the impermissible use of judicial notice addressed in Section II above. The defense cannot convert a cost-judgment *existence* fact into a merits-weighing *character* fact through rhetoric.

## VIII. Defense theory #5 (802 only) — "Punitive damages fail under § 3294 and *Lazar*"

**What the defense says.** The 802 MPA argues that the FAC cannot plead fraud with *Lazar* particularity, cannot plead malice under Civ. Code § 3294(c)(1), cannot plead oppression under *Today's IV*, and cannot show "despicable conduct" under *McNeal*. The defense reads *In re Angelia P.* (1981) 28 Cal.3d 908, 919 as imposing a "clear and convincing" standard at the pleading stage — that the evidence must be "so clear as to leave no substantial doubt" and "sufficiently strong to command the unhesitating assent of every reasonable mind."

**Why this fails on the law.** The defense conflates the *evidentiary* standard at trial with the *pleading* standard at the demurrer / strike stage. *In re Angelia P.* describes the trial-stage burden of proof; it does not purport to govern the face of a complaint. At the pleading stage, a plaintiff pleads facts which, if proven by clear and convincing evidence at trial, would support the punitive damages claim. The distinction is elementary, and the defense elides it.

Fraud must be pleaded with particularity under *Lazar* — yes. The 802 TAC's Section IV "Punitive Damages Specificity" (¶¶ 25–30) is literally a *Lazar* particulars block: WHO (named claims representative), WHAT (false representations in the denial letter), WHEN (February 25, 2021), WHERE (CSAA's claims office), HOW (written denial without investigation), and BY WHAT MEANS (U.S. Mail / electronic). The TAC also pleads the § 3294(b) managing-agent / ratification element (¶¶ 26–27), a continuing-fraud 2021–2025 timeline (¶ 28), and "despicable conduct" using the *American Airlines* vocabulary (¶¶ 29–30).

Malice under § 3294(c)(1) includes "conduct which is intended by the defendant to cause injury to the plaintiff **or despicable conduct which is carried on by the defendant with a willful and conscious disregard of the rights or safety of others**." The 802 theory is the second clause: willful and conscious disregard. Issuing a categorical denial letter claiming a "concluded" investigation without pulling the 911, BWC, or CAD that the police report referenced by number is, at minimum, a pleaded allegation of conscious disregard for the claimant's rights. Whether a jury will find clear and convincing evidence of that at trial is a trial question. Whether it is pleaded is a pleading question, and the TAC pleads it.

*Today's IV*'s oppression definition — "subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of their rights" — is also pleaded: a categorical denial, with no investigation of available materials, to a severely-injured pedestrian claimant with known physical vulnerability (see 802 TAC ¶¶ 33–35, the eggshell-plaintiff block), plausibly meets that pleading threshold.

**Where the TAC cures this.** Section IV, as described above. The Cure Matrix for 802 at Objection 4 maps each particularity requirement to a specific paragraph.

**Conclusory rhetoric and overstatement.** The defense writes: "Plaintiff's FAC cannot possibly support even an inference of malice. CSAA performed an investigation, determined that its insured was not liable, and informed Plaintiff of their decision." That sentence is a factual argument, not a pleading argument. On a § 436 motion the court accepts the pleading's allegation that CSAA did not perform an investigation — did not pull the 911, BWC, or CAD — and that the "concluded investigation" representation was therefore false. The defense's sentence inverts the pleading standard: it asks the court to credit *the defense's* factual narrative and reject the FAC's. That is the reverse of what § 436 allows.

Similarly: "The same facts were presented to a jury, and the jury agreed with CSAA's denial position, rendering a '9-3 defense verdict in the Underlying Action.'" The jury's 9–3 finding was on **negligence** by the insured driver — a different issue from whether the insurer committed fraud in its claim handling two-and-a-half years earlier. Conflating the two is a conceptual error, not an argument.

## IX. Defense theory #6 (801 only) — "*Kulchar* extrinsic fraud is not supportable"

**What the defense says.** The 801 MPA centers the argument that Plaintiff "had every opportunity to present his case to the Court and the jury, in his underlying jury trial" — therefore no extrinsic fraud under *Kulchar*. The defense reads *Kulchar*'s statement that "[r]elief is denied … if a party has been given notice of an action and has not been prevented from participating therein" as categorically closing out Plaintiff's equity action. The defense also claims "Plaintiff was ultimately permitted to present this evidence to a jury" — citing the denial of Defendant's MIL #17 as proof that the 911, BWC, and CAD came in.

**Why this fails on the law and the record.** *Kulchar* identifies four non-exhaustive channels of extrinsic fraud, including concealment that "prevents the unsuccessful party from exhibiting fully his case." Our TAC pleads the third and fourth *Kulchar* channels — fraudulent concealment of material evidence (the Dolan production set CARBONE00001–CARBONE00789) and non-communicative procurement of judgment — and the deprivation-of-fair-hearing channel through the fabricated/provenance-obscured Dolan expert testimony. The defense's "Plaintiff presented all the evidence" argument is a factual argument about what happened at trial; on the face of the FAC, Plaintiff alleges he did *not* have the Dolan production set in time to use it effectively. Whether the denial of MIL #17 made the 911, BWC, and CAD formally admissible does not answer the deeper extrinsic-fraud question: did the defense's concealment of the underlying Dolan provenance materials deprive Plaintiff of the ability to effectively impeach Dolan and present a fair case? That is a factual dispute the court cannot resolve on a § 436 motion.

The April 16, 2025 Day-7 transcript "provided by Dolan" admission — quoted verbatim in the 801 TAC ¶ 28 — is the evidentiary anchor that makes this more than a pleading dispute. On that date, in open court, defense counsel admitted on the record that Dolan "provided" the very materials (911, BWC, unredacted documents) the defense had earlier represented it did not have. That certified admission is a "fraud on the court" anchor under *Flatley* and § 6128(a), and it is the kind of post-trial evidence that *Kougasian* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028 expressly treats as supporting an independent action in equity.

**Where the TAC cures this.** The 801 TAC's Section I "Character of the Action" (¶¶ 1–4) anchors the action as an independent action in equity under *Kougasian* / *Olivera*, not a collateral attack. Section I's "Four-Channel Kulchar Framework" (¶ 3(a)–(d)) pleads each of the *Kulchar* channels element-by-element. Section IX "Day-7 Admission Foundation" (¶¶ 28–30) quotes the certified trial-transcript colloquy verbatim and pleads the failure to correct.

**Conclusory rhetoric / *record* overstatement.** The 801 MPA says: "despite Plaintiff's strained recapitulation of the facts of his underlying case, the reality is he presented all the evidence he sought and simply failed to persuade the jury." (MPA p. 6.) That "reality" is exactly what the FAC's factual allegations deny. On a § 436 motion the court accepts the FAC's version, not the defense's. And the Day-7 transcript — a neutral, certified record — shows the defense's "reality" is contradicted by the defense's own sworn statements in open court. The defense cannot introduce a contrary factual narrative through rhetoric at the pleading stage.

There is a second overstatement: the 801 MPA claims Plaintiff's "proper legal recourse is an appeal — and indeed, Plaintiff's appeal is pending. (See *Rosario v. Abdelhalim*, CA 1st Dist. Ct. of Appeal, Case No. A173827.)" This is a familiar move: "you have a remedy; you can't have two." But California law is clear that an appeal on instructional error and an independent equity action for extrinsic fraud concern different issues, different evidentiary records, and different remedies. *Kougasian* expressly holds that the existence of an appellate remedy on a direct-review ground does not foreclose an independent equitable action on an extrinsic-fraud ground. The pending A173827 appeal concerns the trial court's refusal to give the Vehicle Code §§ 22107, 21804(a), 360 jury instructions after the jury requested them by number. That is not the same issue as the post-trial-discovered fraudulent concealment of the Dolan production set.

## X. Procedural and doctrinal missteps common to both motions

Beyond the individual theory flaws above, a set of common procedural/doctrinal errors runs through both briefs. Cataloguing them matters because, on a § 436 motion, procedural posture is often dispositive.

**1. Using § 436 as a demurrer.** Both MPAs argue sufficiency of fraud, sufficiency of standing, and sufficiency of the § 3294 allegations. Those are classic demurrer arguments under CCP § 430.10(e) (failure to state a cause of action) or § 430.10(f) (uncertainty). The defense has been down this road — both cases have already seen demurrer briefing, and the defense's April 22 package also includes a concurrent 425.16 anti-SLAPP motion (footnoted in both MPAs). Using § 436 to retread demurrer arguments is both improper and inefficient, and the court has a well-recognized discretion to treat a § 436 motion as a demurrer surrogate only to the extent the "irrelevant / false / improper / nonconforming" criteria are actually met on the face of the pleading. They are not, here.

**2. Selective / truth-of-contents RFJN use.** As noted in Section II, both RFJNs request judicial notice of existence of the FAC, ROA, and NOE in 21-594102. The defense then uses those exhibits to prove factual propositions — e.g., that "Plaintiff presented all the evidence" or that "the Court denied Defendant's motions in limine" — that are not properly noticed for their truth. A standard RFJN objection limits the RFJN to the existence and legal effect of the records, not the truth of contested factual narratives derived from them.

**3. Mis-citation drift.** The 802 MPA cites *Coyne v. Krempels* at 36 Cal.2d 257, which is incorrect — the canonical cite is 35 Cal.2d 257. The two MPAs also split on the *F.E.V. v. City of Anaheim* pincite, citing 15 Cal.App.5th 463 in 801 and 15 Cal.App.5th 462 in 802. These are small errors that raise a larger point: the packets are recycled templates; they have not been carefully checked against the current record in each case. Not fatal, but noteworthy.

**4. Failure to cite dispositive authority.** The defense does not cite:
- *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048 — the leading California Supreme Court case on the non-communicative-conduct exception to § 47(b).
- *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299 — the leading illegality-exception case.
- *Action Apartment Ass'n* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232 — the leading pre-litigation timing case.
- *Kougasian v. TMSL* (2004) 118 Cal.App.4th 1028 — the leading modern independent-equity-action case, specifically addressing the *Kulchar* framework and the attorney-defendant standing issue.
- *Olivera v. Grace* (1942) 19 Cal.2d 570 — the foundational California Supreme Court case on independent actions in equity.
- *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618 — the foundational case on tort-measure attorney-fee damages.
- *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335 — the governing issue-preclusion framework.
- *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888 and *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666 — primary-rights doctrine.
- *State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Superior Court* (1996) 45 Cal.App.4th 1093 — *Moradi-Shalal* carve-out for UCL.
- *Rickley v. Goodfriend* (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 1136 and *Shaffer v. Superior Court* (1995) 33 Cal.App.4th 993 — § 6128(a) as independent tort.
- Business & Professions Code § 6128(a) and (b) — the operative attorney-deceit statute.
- Title 10 CCR §§ 2695.4 and 2695.7 — the Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations.
- Insurance Code § 790.03(h)(5) — the "liability reasonably clear" standard.
- Civil Code § 2338 — principal-agent liability, including for wrongful acts within scope of agency.

A brief that does not confront the authority governing the carve-outs and exceptions on which the opposing pleading expressly relies has a credibility problem. The defense's motions read as though they were written against a first draft of the pleading, not against a pleading that has been refined through a prior demurrer and now through a TAC.

**5. Meet-and-confer in 802.** The 802 packet includes a Declaration of Tyler J. O'Connell with a table of contents and a meet-and-confer narrative (DEC-TOC2). CCP § 435.5 requires meet-and-confer before filing a motion to strike, and specifically requires a declaration demonstrating a good-faith effort to meet. That procedural compliance is present for 802 but appears less emphasized for 801 (no DEC-TOC in that folder in the parsed corpus). Any deficiency in the § 435.5 process is a potential independent ground to deny the motion without prejudice.

## XI. The "completed investigation vs. no knowledge" self-defeating pair

The strategic spine described in `GITHUB-PAGE/CROSS-CASE-NARRATIVE-LEGAL-THEORY-AND-LEVERAGE.md` — the "completed investigation vs. no knowledge" self-defeating pair — is what makes every one of the defense's arguments across both motions structurally unstable.

On February 25, 2021, CSAA wrote to Plaintiff that its investigation was "concluded" after "careful review." That written representation was issued before the 911, BWC, or CAD had been pulled. It is the 802 fraud predicate.

On February 18, 2025, and again throughout the trial of 21-594102, defense counsel represented to the trial court that the 911, BWC, and CAD were "news to me," "not produced," or similar — the defense's affirmative in-limine position.

On April 16, 2025 (Trial Day 7), defense counsel stated on the record, captured by the certified court reporter, that those same materials "were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure."

The 2021 denial letter and the 2025 "news to me" framing cannot both be true: either the 2021 investigation was not "concluded" (in which case the denial letter is the fraud), or the defense did have the 2021 materials all along (in which case the 2025 "no knowledge" framing is the fraud). The certified Day-7 transcript proves the defense had the materials. Which means:

- In 802, the 2021 denial-letter representation of "concluded investigation" was a misrepresentation, and the TAC pleads it with *Lazar* specificity.
- In 801, the 2025 in-limine representations that the materials were "not produced" were § 6128(a) deceit on the court, and the TAC pleads them with *Kulchar* channel specificity and *Flatley* illegality.

This is the lever the defense cannot escape. Any argument they make in one forum that shores up the 2021-side of the contradiction damages them in the 2025-side, and vice versa. The motions to strike do not address this lever anywhere, because any attempt to address it would require the defense to pick a side of the contradiction and commit to it on paper. They have not, and they cannot.

## XIII. Tactical implications at the May 2026 hearings

**Most probable defense fallback.** Given the fatal flaws above, the defense's most realistic fallback at the hearings is (a) narrow the strike to specific damages items — attorney fees for self-representation (already conceded/deleted in the 802 TAC), and perhaps specific "future damages" absent CACI 3903A foundation (already cured) — and (b) attempt to convert a granted strike into a denial of leave to amend, on the theory that amendment has been "repeatedly" attempted. The *Howard* / *Morgan* / *Blank* / *Kolani* / *Atkinson* line makes that fallback legally weak as long as the TAC cures the defects on its face, which it does. Plaintiff's MPA ISO Leave to File TAC in both cases walks the court through each curing paragraph-for-paragraph.

**Judicial notice posture.** At hearing, the RFJN objection should be limited: judicial notice of existence of the FAC, ROA, and NOE is unobjectionable; judicial notice of the *truth* of what those records say about trial-court rulings or jury findings is objectionable under *Arce v. Kaiser Foundation Health Plan, Inc.* (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 471 and the general Evid. Code § 452(d) limit. Preserving this objection keeps the defense from importing 21-594102 merits into the 801/802 § 436 analysis.

**Anti-SLAPP interaction.** Both MPAs footnote that Defendants have "concurrently filed a Special Motion to Strike under Code Civ. Proc. § 425.16." The § 436 and § 425.16 motions are separate vehicles, and a § 436 denial does not moot § 425.16, nor does a § 425.16 denial moot § 436. But the defense's § 47(b) / standing / collateral-attack arguments in § 436 will overlap substantially with the § 425.16 protected-activity / probability-of-prevailing framework. If § 425.16 is argued or decided first, and the *Flatley* illegality prong is sustained (as it should be under the 801 TAC's pleading), that ruling carries directly over into the § 436 disposition, because a court cannot find the same conduct "irrelevant or improper" under § 436 that it has already found actionable under § 425.16 step 2.

**Timing cross-pressure.** 802 is heard May 19; 801 is heard May 22. The 802 order enters first. If the 802 court denies the motion or narrows it to specific damages buckets, the 801 court sees that as persuasive (not binding) authority three days later. Conversely, if the 802 court grants broadly, the 801 court may feel pressure to conform. Because the strongest arguments (pre-litigation privilege carve-out, UCL standing, Title 10 regulatory duties) are in 802, the preferred tactical posture is to lead with the 802 cure package and let it anchor the 801 hearing.

## XIV. Bottom line

Across both 801 and 802, the defense's April 22, 2026 CCP §§ 435/436 motions to strike do five distinct kinds of damage to their own position.

First, they **use a narrow procedural vehicle** (§ 436) to re-argue sufficiency issues that belong to the demurrer track, which they have already exhausted. That is a doctrinal category error.

Second, they **overstate the reach of the litigation privilege** by ignoring *Rusheen*'s non-communicative-conduct limit, *Flatley*'s illegality limit, and *Action Apartment*'s pre-litigation-timing limit. That is three dispositive California Supreme Court authorities uncited.

Third, they **overstate the *Shaolian* / *Norton* / *Omega* standing rule** by ignoring *Kougasian* / *Olivera* equity-action standing, § 6128(a)-independent-tort standing, Civ. Code § 2338 agency/ratification, and the UCL's *Moradi-Shalal* carve-out under *State Farm Fire & Casualty*. Again, a family of dispositive authorities is uncited.

Fourth, they **misuse the collateral-attack framework** by conflating it with independent actions in equity, ignoring *Kulchar*'s own four-channel framework, mis-applying *Wouldridge*'s void-on-face test to non-collateral actions, and reading *Throckmorton* selectively. The preclusion framework they invoke (issue preclusion under *Singh*) is not walked through element-by-element under *Lucido*, and the primary-rights framework under *Mycogen* and *Crowley* is missing entirely.

Fifth, they **conflate the pleading standard with the trial evidentiary standard** for punitive damages under § 3294 and *Lazar*, asking the court to credit the defense's factual narrative against the face of the pleading. That is the reverse of what a § 436 motion permits.

Against these errors, Plaintiff's TAC packets in both cases provide a cure. Each defense objection is mapped to at least one TAC paragraph that pleads around it using California-Supreme-Court-level authority the defense failed to address. The 801 TAC anchors an independent equitable action under *Kougasian* / *Olivera* / *Kulchar*'s four-channel framework, non-communicative conduct under *Rusheen*, illegality under *Flatley* and § 6128(a), agency under § 2338, and a distinct primary right under *Lucido* / *Mycogen* / *Crowley*. The 802 TAC anchors direct regulatory duties under Title 10 CCR, UCL standing under *State Farm Fire & Casualty*, pre-litigation carve-out under *Action Apartment*, *Prentice* fees as tort damages rather than a *Trope*-barred fee award, and § 3294 / *Lazar* particularity with a full WHO/WHAT/WHEN/WHERE/HOW block and § 3294(b) managing-agent pleading.

The motions to strike were filed against the SAC/FAC. They will, by the time of hearing, be applied to the TAC if leave is granted (and leave should be granted under *Howard* / *Morgan* / *Blank* / *Kolani*). The TAC, as drafted, leaves nothing the § 436 standard can reach — no irrelevant matter (each paragraph ties to an element of a pleaded cause of action), no false matter (everything is consistent with the certified record and the 2021 denial letter), no improper matter (no pro per fee demand, no prevailing-party cost demand from 21-594102, no duplicative damages between 801 and 802), and nothing "not drawn or filed in conformity with the laws of this state" (every paragraph is anchored to statute, regulation, or controlling California authority).

The defense's spine is not illegitimate. It is simply incomplete. They have built a case theory that works only against a strawman version of the Plaintiff's pleading — a FAC that does not plead *Kougasian* / *Olivera* equity standing, does not plead *Rusheen* non-communicative conduct, does not plead *Action Apartment* pre-litigation carve-out, does not plead Title 10 CCR direct duties, does not plead the *Lazar* particularity block, does not plead the *Lucido* distinct-primary-right analysis, and does not plead the § 6128(a) illegality anchor. The actual pleading — especially as cured in the TAC — pleads all of it. When the court overlays the defense's spine on the real pleading, the spine misses on every count.

That is why, on the merits, these motions to strike are not a threat to the Plaintiff's cases. They are, if anything, a gift — because they have now committed the defense to a theory of the case that the TACs, the Day-7 admission, the 2021 denial letter, and the Title 10 regulatory framework each independently dismantle. The defense has drawn their lines in the sand on paper. At hearing, they will be asked to hold those lines against authority they did not cite, facts they did not acknowledge, and a cured pleading they did not address. On the current record, that is not a hearing the defense can win without conceding something they cannot afford to concede in one of the other forums.
