## Part 1 — The Seven Error Clusters in the 802 Defense Spine

The 802 MPA (MPA2-CCP436) relies on a recycled defense template that carries **seven discrete clusters of authority error** onto the 802 record. Each cluster is keyed below to MPA2 line citations.

### Cluster A — Litigation Privilege Overreach (Civ. Code § 47(b) / *Ribas*)

**Defense move (MPA2 pp. 7–8):** "All the conduct alleged in Plaintiff's FAC arises from statements, filings, or other communicative acts undertaken in connection with prior judicial proceedings." Privilege is described as "absolute."

**Error:** The 802 gravamen is the February 25, 2021 **pre-litigation** denial letter, sent before any litigation was pending or seriously contemplated. The defense completely ignores:

- *Action Apartment Ass'n, Inc. v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1248–49 — pre-litigation communications are privileged only when litigation is "imminently anticipated" and "in good faith under serious consideration." As of February 25, 2021: no litigation pending, no counsel retained, no demand, no tolling, no preservation letter, no litigation reserve (TAC ¶ 16A).
- *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299, 317–21 — conduct that is illegal as a matter of law (fraud under Civ. Code §§ 1709, 1710) is categorically outside § 47(b) (TAC ¶ 21A).
- *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1058 — § 47(b) does not reach non-communicative conduct (failure to obtain 911 audio, CAD records, BWC footage).

### Cluster B — Standing Overreach (*Shaolian* / *Norton* / *Omega*)

**Defense move (MPA2 pp. 8–9):** No third-party standing to sue an insurer absent assignment of rights or a final judgment against the insured.

**Error:** *Shaolian* addresses direct-action **benefits** claims, not common-law-fraud claims by the direct addressee of an insurer's misrepresentation. The defense does not confront:

- Civil Code § 2338 principal-agent liability — CSAA is directly liable for wrongful acts and willful omissions of Sarah Rash and the claims department, and ratification liability is separately pleaded (TAC ¶ 6A).
- *Doctors' Company v. Superior Court* (1989) 49 Cal.3d 39 — insurer directly liable when acting outside the ordinary insurer role.
- *Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos.* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287 carve-out via *State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Superior Court* (1996) 45 Cal.App.4th 1093, 1103–04 — UCL claims may be predicated on § 790.03(h) violations, which run directly to the affected claimant.
- Insurance Code § 790.03(h)(5) — "liability reasonably clear" standard pleaded as UCL unlawful-prong predicate only (TAC ¶ 47A).

### Cluster C — Collateral-Estoppel / Primary-Rights Misuse

**Defense move (MPA2 pp. 9–10):** 9–3 defense verdict in CGC-21-594102 precludes this suit as a "collateral attack."

**Error:** The defense cites *F.E.V.* / *Singh* / *Wouldridge* / *Throckmorton* without walking through *Lucido*'s five factors or *Mycogen* / *Crowley*'s primary-rights doctrine. Against the 21-594102 record:

- **Identical issue** — no (negligence vs. 2021 claim-handling fraud).
- **Actually litigated** — no (CSAA not a party; no fraud tendered).
- **Necessarily decided** — no.
- **Final judgment** — no (on appeal, A173827).
- **Same party or privy** — no.

Different primary rights under *Mycogen* 28 Cal.4th 888 and *Crowley* 8 Cal.4th 666 (TAC ¶ 41A). The 801/802 double-recovery reservation (TAC ¶ 44A) separately forecloses any inter-forum duplication.

### Cluster D — *Trope* / Pro-Per-Fees Overreach

**Defense move (MPA2 pp. 10–11):** Pro per cannot recover fees; the $75,000 CGC-21-594102 cost judgment is a "fact" the court should credit.

**Error:** *Trope* does not bar *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618, 620–21 tort-measure damages. TAC ¶ 24A enumerates four *Prentice* line-items, expressly re-framing the $75,000 cost judgment as an **induced-litigation tort damage** against CSAA (not a re-litigation of the cost judgment in 21-594102). The defense's MPA2 footnote-2 rhetoric is converted into Plaintiff's own damages item.

### Cluster E — *Lazar* / Punitive-Pleading Conflation

**Defense move (MPA2 pp. 11–12):** Pleading cannot "possibly support even an inference of malice"; *In re Angelia P.* clear-and-convincing standard is imposed at the pleading stage.

**Error:** *G.D. Searle & Co. v. Superior Court* (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 22, 29: at the pleading stage, plaintiff need only plead facts which, **if proven by clear and convincing evidence at trial**, would support punitive recovery (TAC ¶ 25A). The TAC's *Lazar* block (¶ 25(a)–(f)) plus § 3294(b) managing-agent / ratification allegations (¶¶ 26–27) plus deny-delay-defend pattern (¶ 28) plus confession/victim/eyewitness triple (¶¶ 29–30, refined with SFPD identifiers) easily satisfies the *G.D. Searle* pleading test.

### Cluster F — § 436 Used as a Demurrer Substitute

**Defense move (MPA2 throughout):** Sufficiency arguments under § 436.

**Error:** § 436 targets matter that is "irrelevant, false, or improper" on the face of the pleading, or matter not conforming to law/rule. It is not a general sufficiency vehicle — that is demurrer territory (CCP § 430.10(e)/(f)). The defense is treating § 436 as a demurrer surrogate while simultaneously running an anti-SLAPP motion and an implicit demurrer theory. The TAC's ¶ 52A wrongful-conduct-scope disavowal further narrows any anti-SLAPP overlap with § 436 Cluster A.

### Cluster G — Self-Defeating 2021/2025 Contradiction

**Defense move (implicit):** 2021 denial was based on a "concluded investigation"; 2025 "news to me" in-limine position in CGC-21-594102.

**Error:** These two defense representations cannot both be true. Either (a) the 2021 investigation was not "concluded" (the 802 fraud predicate), or (b) the defense did have the 2021 materials all along, which the October 29, 2024 Day-7 certified transcript ("provided by Dolan") confirms. The defense's motion to strike does not address this self-defeating pair anywhere, because any attempt to do so requires committing to one side of the contradiction.

## Part 4 — Three-Pillar Bottom-Line Defeat Analysis (802)

At the May 19, 2026 hearing on the 802 motion to strike, the defense's position rests on three structural pillars, each of which is independently defeated by the current TAC.

### Pillar 1 — Statutory-Vehicle Mismatch

The defense is using CCP § 436 as a demurrer substitute while simultaneously running an anti-SLAPP motion. On a § 436 motion, the court accepts the pleading's well-pleaded allegations as true and tests only whether matter is "irrelevant, false, or improper" or "not drawn in conformity with law." The TAC, as amended with the new letter-suffix paragraphs, contains:

- No irrelevant matter (every paragraph ties to an element of a pleaded cause of action or to a targeted defense-objection cure).
- No false matter (every factual allegation is consistent with the SFPD Incident Report, the 2021 denial letter, and the 2024–2025 trial record).
- No improper matter (pro-per fees are deleted; prevailing-party costs are limited to this action; double recovery between 801/802 is expressly reserved in ¶ 44A).
- Nothing "not drawn in conformity with law" (every paragraph is anchored to statute, regulation, or controlling California authority).

### Pillar 2 — Authority Gap

The defense MPA2 does not cite, and does not engage, the following controlling / dispositive authorities on which the TAC's cures expressly rely:

- *Action Apartment Ass'n* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232 — pre-litigation-contemplation test.
- *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 299 — illegality carve-out.
- *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048 — non-communicative conduct.
- *Lucido v. Superior Court* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 335 — issue-preclusion five-factor test.
- *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888 and *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666 — primary-rights doctrine.
- *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618 — tort-measure damages exception to *Trope*.
- *G.D. Searle & Co. v. Superior Court* (1975) 49 Cal.App.3d 22 — pleading-vs-evidentiary bright line for punitives.
- Civil Code § 2338 — principal-agent statutory liability.
- *State Farm Fire & Casualty Co. v. Superior Court* (1996) 45 Cal.App.4th 1093 — *Moradi-Shalal* UCL carve-out.
- Insurance Code § 790.03(h)(5) — liability-reasonably-clear standard.
- *Williams v. Gerber Products Co.* (9th Cir. 2008) 552 F.3d 934; *Chapman v. Skype, Inc.* (2013) 220 Cal.App.4th 217 — UCL reasonable-consumer test.

A brief that does not address the carve-outs and exceptions on which the opposing pleading expressly relies has a credibility problem at hearing.

### Pillar 3 — Self-Defeating-Contradiction Lever

The 2021 denial letter's "concluded investigation" representation cannot coexist with the 2025 "news to me" in-limine position. The October 29, 2024 Day-7 certified transcript ("provided by Dolan") resolves the pair against the defense: CSAA either had the 2021 materials (in which case the denial letter is the fraud) or did not have them (in which case the 2025 in-limine representations were false to the trial court). The TAC's ¶¶ 6A (agency) and 47A (Ins. Code § 790.03(h)(5)) together put this lever directly in issue. The defense cannot attack the 802 pleading without committing to one side of the contradiction on paper, and whichever side it picks is disqualifying in the parallel 801 forum.
