## IV. WHY THIS IS AN EQUITY CASE -- AND WHY IT CANNOT BE DISMISSED AS A MERE DISCOVERY DISPUTE

I am writing to you in this level of detail because I want you to understand why the independent action in equity (CGC-25-631801) is not a frivolous filing, is not a relitigation of trial issues, and is not subject to dismissal under the litigation privilege, the anti-SLAPP statute, or demurrer.

This is an equity case -- not an appeal of trial error -- because the nature of the wrong places it squarely within the court's inherent equitable power to vacate judgments procured by extrinsic fraud.

Extrinsic fraud occurs when a party is deprived of the opportunity to present a claim or defense because fraud prevented a real contest on the merits. (*Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471.) An independent action in equity lies to set aside such a judgment. (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 187; *Kulchar*, *supra*, 1 Cal.3d at p. 472; *Aldrich v. San Fernando Valley Lumber Co.* (1985) 170 Cal.App.3d 725, 735.) The false representations your office made were not made to the jury for the jury to weigh, which would be intrinsic fraud. They were made to the judge, who relied on them to shape the procedural and evidentiary framework of the trial. I could not "cross-examine" the Court's reliance on your representations. "Where the fraud goes to the regularity of the proceedings, rather than to the merits, and prevents the aggrieved party from having a fair hearing, the fraud is extrinsic." (*Caldwell v. Taylor* (1933) 218 Cal. 471, 476.)

The direct appeal (A173827) addresses the Court's independent instructional error: the deliberation-stage failure to provide Vehicle Code sections 22107 and 21804(a) when the jury asked for them. But the appeal cannot adjudicate whether your office lied to the trial judge. A reviewing court on direct appeal is limited to the record as it was presented; it cannot conduct a fact-finding inquiry into whether "news to me" was a knowing falsehood. The independent equity action is the recognized procedural vehicle for that claim.

The Court's discretion was poisoned by your representations. "[A] court may be said to have abused its discretion when it acts on the basis of incorrect or misleading information." (*In re Marriage of Rosevear* (1998) 65 Cal.App.4th 673, 682.) When your office misrepresented receipt of the records, it stripped the trial court of its ability to make a valid discretionary ruling. The judge was not managing the trial. The judge was being managed by the fraud. Only equity can undo that.

Structural fraud defies harmless error analysis. "[W]hen attorney misconduct is sufficiently egregious, it may constitute a denial of due process, rendering the trial fundamentally unfair regardless of the strength of the evidence." (*Smith v. Superior Court* (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1042.) The exclusion of evidence was procured by fraud. The process was corrupted at the structural level. The standard *Watson*/*Chapman* prejudice frameworks do not apply.

I also want to be clear about the defense theories that will not work:

The **litigation privilege** (Civ. Code, sec. 47(b)) does not apply to fraud on the court. The California Supreme Court held this categorically in *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1065. This action seeks equitable vacatur, not tort damages for statements made in court.

The **anti-SLAPP statute** (Code Civ. Proc., sec. 425.16) does not apply to an independent equity action challenging a judgment procured by fraud. *Finton Construction, Inc. v. Bidna & Keys APLC* (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 200, 213: "The gravamen of [the] complaint is the alleged fraud that [defendants] committed on the court, not [their] exercise of the right of petition."

**Judicial estoppel** bars your client from retaining a verdict procured by a position your own later statement proves was false. Position A: "news to me," "did not receive until MSC," "not produced in discovery." Position B: "Sure. They were provided by Dolan." Under *Jackson v. County of Los Angeles* (1997) 60 Cal.App.4th 171, 183, the defense cannot have it both ways.
