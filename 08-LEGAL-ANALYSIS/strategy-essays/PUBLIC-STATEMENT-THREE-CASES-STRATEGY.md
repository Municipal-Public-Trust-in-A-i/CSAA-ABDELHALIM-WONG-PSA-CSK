# THREE CASES, ONE STRATEGY: HOW THE PLAINTIFF BUILT A THREE-WAY TRAP

## A strategic review of the record-based moves that corner the insurer and its retained counsel

This document is not a re-telling of the underlying collision. It is a high-level, record-anchored review of the **tactical and strategic structure** the plaintiff contends was used to corner the insurance carrier and the defense lawyers through their **own written statements and on-the-record representations**.

The insurer at issue is **CSAA Insurance Exchange**, affiliated with the **American Automobile Association (AAA)**.

The strategy is simple in design and severe in consequence:

- **Lock in a provable falsehood early** (the insurer’s “concluded investigation” denial).
- **Force the litigation posture to collide with that falsehood** (discovery, authenticity, and production history).
- **Convert the collision into a three-front legal pressure system** that the insurer cannot satisfy without either (a) admissions that support fraud and unfair-practice exposure or (b) a reversal or set-aside of the prior judgment as a product of fraud on the court.

This is the “three-way trap” as the plaintiff frames it: three proceedings that support one another, each aimed at a different decision-maker, each using the same record anchors.

---

## I. THE CORE STRATEGY: USING THE DENIAL LETTER AS THE FRAUD ANCHOR

The strategic foundation is the insurer’s denial letter issued **21 days** after the incident, declaring its investigation “concluded” while, as the plaintiff contends, the insurer had not obtained or reviewed the most basic contemporaneous evidence categories such as:

- the 911 call recording
- body-worn camera footage
- computer-aided dispatch records

The plaintiff’s position is that this is not merely a coverage position or a disputed liability view. It is a **representational event**. The phrase “concluded its investigation” is treated as a factual assertion about process and diligence, not just an opinion about fault.

As the plaintiff frames it, the trap begins here: evidence that was available through routine requests was later obtained early by the plaintiff’s then-counsel (including the Dolan Law Firm), underscoring the contention that the denial was not the product of a completed investigation, but a decision issued ahead of the record.

### Why the timing matters tactically

The strategy leverages timing to convert “denial” into “proof structure”:

- **Early denial fixes the timestamp** for what the insurer did and did not do at the moment it claimed completion.
- That timestamp becomes the backbone for a fraud and institutional-practice theory, because it frames the denial as a decision made **before** the inconvenient evidence was gathered.
- Once that anchor is fixed, later litigation conduct is evaluated as an extension of the same posture: deny, disclaim, and exhaust.

The plaintiff’s narrative goal is to show continuity: a denial that is “concluded” before foundational evidence is reviewed is not an isolated error. It is presented as the first visible step in an institutional pipeline.

---

## II. THE SECOND LAYER: TURNING DISCOVERY AND AUTHENTICATION INTO A CREDIBILITY COLLAPSE

The next move is to make the dispute about evidence (production, possession, provenance) become a dispute about **truthfulness to the court**.

The plaintiff contends the record contains three critical elements that, when aligned, create an unavoidable credibility collapse:

- **Service history**: the materials were served during discovery in 2022.
- **Custody history**: defense counsel substitutions occurred, with the defense file transferred and held for many months before pretrial and trial.
- **On-the-record denials**: motions and statements asserting the materials were not produced, were of unknown provenance, or were “news.”

The tactic is not only to prove the evidence exists. It is to prove that **denying knowledge of it was itself a litigation instrument** that shaped rulings, scheduling, and admissibility.

In the plaintiff’s strategy, sworn testimony from attorneys, as officers of the court, functions as a pin that locks the production timeline in place. Once the record contains uncontradicted testimony and documentary markers establishing service and custody, later disclaimers of knowledge become, in the plaintiff’s framing, the evidentiary bridge from “hard-fought litigation” to “fraud exposure.”

### The “officer of the court” gateway

The plaintiff’s strategic framing is that once counsel, as officers of the court, make representations that are materially untrue (as alleged), the case stops being only about the accident and becomes about **fraud on the court** and the integrity of adjudication.

That shift matters because it changes the remedy landscape:

- It is no longer only “who was negligent.”
- It becomes “whether the judgment is reliable at all.”

---

## III. THE TRIAL INFLECTION POINT: WHEN DENIAL OF DISCOVERY AND RULINGS OPENED THE FRAUD THEORY

The plaintiff’s strategy treats the trial phase as the moment the trap closed. The argument is not that the court made ordinary discretionary calls. It is that the court’s rulings were induced by a record built on misrepresentations and on a one-sided evidentiary posture.

Two tactical objectives were pursued at trial:

- **Create a clear reliance chain**: defense representations to the court, followed by court rulings that accepted or operationalized those representations.
- **Preserve appellate-grade error**: ensure the record contains clean, reviewable decision points (jury questions, instruction responses, evidentiary rulings, and continuance denials).

The plaintiff’s contention is that the denial of discovery pathways and the narrowing of testimony, combined with counsel’s denials of possession or knowledge, effectively opened the entire case to a fraud analysis that reaches beyond the personal injury posture.

---

## IV. WHY “BAD FAITH” MATTERS HERE, EVEN THROUGH A CORPORATE FRAUD GATEWAY

This strategy is not presented as a conventional “bad faith refusal to settle” case inside the personal injury action. The plaintiff’s framing is different: the insurer’s conduct is treated as part of a **corporate fraud gateway** where “bad faith” functions as a conduct lens and a proof engine.

### The plaintiff’s bad faith frame (as applied here)

The plaintiff’s position is that “bad faith” is relevant because it describes the insurer’s institutional duty posture and the predictability of the denial pipeline. In this framing, bad faith is used to support and illuminate:

- **fraud**: a “concluded investigation” representation made without the underlying investigative acts
- **unfair business practices**: a practice of categorical denials before basic evidence acquisition, followed by deny-delay-defend mechanics
- **consequential damages theory**: the claim that forced litigation costs were a foreseeable result of an investigatory denial that was never real

In short, the plaintiff’s strategic stance is that the case is not limited to a tort dispute between pedestrian and driver. It is positioned as a case study in how claims-handling representations, when false, can function as a corporate fraud instrument that then propagates into litigation conduct.

---

## V. THE THREE-WAY TRAP: HOW THE THREE PROCEEDINGS SUPPORT EACH OTHER

The three-case structure is the operational core. Each case is designed to create pressure on a different axis, while borrowing proof from the same record anchors.

### A. The appeal (instructional and legal-error reversal track)

The appeal targets the legal framework given to the jury. The plaintiff’s theory is that when jurors asked for governing statutory duties and were told they could not consider them, the court created a reversible legal error with high prejudice value, especially with a 9-3 verdict margin.

Strategic function:

- forces a de novo review pathway
- makes prejudice legible through the jury’s own question and the closeness of the verdict
- creates a straightforward reversal lane that does not require proving subjective intent

### B. The equity action (set-aside track for extrinsic fraud on the court)

The equity action targets the reliability of the judgment itself. The plaintiff’s theory is that misrepresentations to the court about evidence service and provenance corrupted the process and deprived the plaintiff of a fair opportunity to present the case.

Strategic function:

- reframes the fight as institutional integrity and reliance on counsel representations
- seeks to vacate the judgment through an independent remedy lane
- uses the production history and on-the-record denials as the proof spine

### C. The insurance fraud and unfair-practices action (claims-handling exposure track)

This case targets the denial letter and the insurer’s investigation posture at the outset. The plaintiff’s theory is that the denial’s timing and “concluded investigation” language supply a provable, corporate-level misrepresentation that can open discovery into claim file contents, templates, training, metrics, and institutional practices.

Strategic function:

- forces the carrier to defend its claims process as a truth-based practice, not a litigation tactic
- expands exposure beyond the underlying verdict through fraud and business-practice remedies
- creates a pathway to institutional discovery that, in turn, supports the fraud-on-the-court narrative

## VI. HOW THE TRAP CORNERS THE INSURER AND COUNSEL USING THEIR OWN STATEMENTS

The plaintiff’s thesis is that the defense is cornered because the system is built on statements that cannot all be true at once:

- If the denial letter’s “concluded investigation” meant what it said, then the insurer’s claim process must show a real investigation completed in 21 days, including review of foundational evidence or documented efforts to obtain it.
- If the foundational evidence was served in discovery (as the plaintiff contends), then later “unknown provenance” and “news” representations become explainable only as (a) file breakdown and institutional negligence or (b) intentional misrepresentation.
- If the court’s rulings were shaped by those representations, then the judgment becomes vulnerable to a fraud-on-the-court challenge.

This is why the three proceedings reinforce one another. Each case pushes the insurer and counsel toward positions that either:

- create admissions that feed the fraud and unfair-practice claims, or
- strengthen the reversal or set-aside record by demonstrating that the trial result was the product of a corrupted decision framework.

---

## VII. WHAT THE PLAINTIFF CLAIMS THIS STRATEGY ACHIEVES

As the plaintiff frames it, the three-way trap is designed to force a binary:

- **Admission track**: acknowledgments about investigation timing, claim file contents, evidence possession, and the truth-value of on-the-record representations, which support fraud and unfair-practice exposure.
- **Reversal track**: an appellate reversal or an equitable set-aside because the jury was denied governing law and the court was induced to rule on a false evidentiary premise.

Under either outcome, the plaintiff contends the institutional story becomes unavoidable: the “concluded investigation” denial, the later litigation denials of evidence service or provenance, and the court’s reliance form a continuous chain that converts an ordinary liability case into an institutional accountability case.
