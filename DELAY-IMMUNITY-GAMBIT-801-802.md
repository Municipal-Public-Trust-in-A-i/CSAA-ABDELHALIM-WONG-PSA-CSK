## What the defense filings did

**Actor.** CSAA (and aligned defendants in 801).

**Act.** Deny the merits of the pleaded wrongs, and in the same papers claim categorical immunity so those merits never run.

**Object.** The February 25, 2021 denial letter and in-litigation representations about the same claim-handling record, recast as protected petitioning or privileged litigation speech.

The two steps cooperate in the filed papers. Step one preserves a merits posture. Step two, if accepted, prevents that posture from being tested. The operative prayer in those special motions to strike is that the ordinary civil docket stop while the immunity question is briefed, decided, and appealed.

Registry row: [Defend](COMMENTARY/CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md#privilege-choice).

## Layer A: pre-litigation denial as petitioning

**Actor.** CSAA.

**Act.** In the April 9, 2026 special motions to strike, CSAA treated the February 25, 2021 written denial (investigation concluded; insured not liable) as protected prelitigation petitioning under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 and as within Civil Code section 47, subdivision (b).

**Object.** Customer-facing claim-handling speech delivered years before these complaints.

Filed plaintiff papers assign that letter as regulated commercial claim-handling under Insurance Code section 790.03 and 10 CCR section 2695.7, and invoke Code of Civil Procedure section 425.17, subdivision (c), as the commercial-speech carve-out that removes the special-motion vehicle before either anti-SLAPP prong (*Simpson Strong-Tie Co. v. Gore* (2010) 49 Cal.4th 12; *Club Members for an Honest Election v. Sierra Club* (2008) 45 Cal.4th 309).

**Consequence already assigned.** Purpose admission under section 2695.7(d); privilege overclaim; section 425.17(c) gateway; immediate appeals from the July 1 and August 6, 2026 strike orders under section 904.1(a)(13). See homepage [Part 6](README.md#part-6) and [ANTI-SLAPP-CONSEQUENCE-RADIUS.md](ANTI-SLAPP-CONSEQUENCE-RADIUS.md).

## Layer B: in-litigation privilege for tribunal-directed representations

**Actor.** CSAA and panel counsel named in CGC-25-631801.

**Act.** The same immunity rhetoric covers Motions in Limine Nos. 6 and 17 (January 17, 2025 declarations that 911 materials were of unknown origin and not produced), the February 18, 2025 colloquy, and related tribunal-directed statements about the same evidence; April 16, 2025 includes the "provided by Dolan" line.

**Object.** Extrinsic-to-the-tribunal representations about investigation and evidence custody, filed as court papers and then defended as petitioning.

Filed equity papers in CGC-25-631801 assign those representations as the extrinsic-fraud spine. Registry **Delay** row: [CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md](COMMENTARY/CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md).

## Section 425.17(c) as the gateway already briefed

Filed cross-hearing and opposition papers already on this site state that when subdivision (c) applies, section 425.16 does not apply, and that the carve-out is decided before prong analysis. Insurance is a named commercial channel in the statute. The audience for the 2021 denial is the claimant and the regulated claim-handling channel.

Section 425.17, subdivision (e), limits interlocutory appeal from a denial grounded on subdivision (c). That limit is the Legislature's answer to using the special motion as a delay vehicle. The consequence-radius narrative collects the maneuver-to-consequence ledger already published: [ANTI-SLAPP-CONSEQUENCE-RADIUS.md](ANTI-SLAPP-CONSEQUENCE-RADIUS.md).
