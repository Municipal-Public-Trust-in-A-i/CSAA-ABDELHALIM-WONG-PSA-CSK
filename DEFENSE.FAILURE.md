**Date:** November 17, 2026  
**Cases:** *Rosario v. Abdelhalim*, Court of Appeal No. A173827; Supreme Court No. S299144

Filed papers (hub): [S299144 supreme-court hub](01-APPEAL/s299144-supreme-court/README.md). Registry diction: [Deny / Delay / Defend / Oppose](COMMENTARY/CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md).

## What the respondent papers did

Four acts appear in the Respondent's Brief and the Supreme Court Answer. Each act has an actor, an act, and an object already named in the corrected petition and the filed overlength reply.

1. **Reorder chronology.** The respondent placed later colloquy before earlier lines. (RB p. 8; Answer p. 6; pins 11 RT 4:8-25.)
2. **Omit the immediate qualification.** The respondent cited 11 RT 4:8-14 while reproducing only 11 RT 4:8-10 and dropping 11 RT 4:11-14.
3. **Swap CACI numbers for a section 614 duty.** The respondent treated the absence of a named CACI form as if it answered a deliberating jury's written request for Vehicle Code sections 22107 and 21804(a).
4. **Expand objections into consent.** The respondent assigned Rosario's objections about distinct judicial acts to consent to Refusal 1 (the completed refusal to instruct).

The Court of Appeal affirmed on consent-based forfeiture and invited error without adjudicating instructional correctness or *Soule* prejudice. Those four acts are the gateway the filed S299144 papers attack.

## Act 1. Chronology reorder

Certified sequence on April 23, 2025:

1. Rosario acknowledged that jurors could not consider material outside the existing instructions. (11 RT 4:8-10.)
2. He immediately qualified that acknowledgment by complaining that the judge had added material and describing its effect. (11 RT 4:11-14.)
3. The judge asked whether Rosario objected to the response to Jury Request No. 1. (11 RT 4:15-17.)
4. Rosario answered and identified his objection concerning what the jury had been accessing or requesting. (11 RT 4:18-25.)

The Respondent's Brief at page 8 presented steps 3 and 4 before step 1, then introduced the earlier acknowledgment as occurring "Further on." The Answer repeated that arrangement at page 6.

**Object of the act.** Make the earlier acknowledgment appear to follow the judge's focused question, as if Rosario later confirmed agreement. The transcript places the acknowledgment before the question and pairs it with the immediate qualification.

## Act 2. Omit 11 RT 4:11-14

Both the Respondent's Brief and the Answer cited 11 RT 4:8-14 while reproducing only lines 8-10. Lines 11-14 are the qualification that accompanies the acknowledgment.

**Object of the act.** Present an unqualified assent where the record shows a qualified statement.

## Act 3. CACI-number swap

The respondent emphasized that Rosario had not specifically requested CACI No. 705 and identified CACI No. 707 as a speed-limit instruction.

Code of Civil Procedure section 614 imposes a separate duty when a deliberating jury requests information on a point of law. The jury named Vehicle Code sections 22107 and 21804(a) and asked to be instructed if those sections applied. The defendant testified that he was making a right turn at approximately five miles per hour. (8 RT 120:17-26.)

**Object of the act.** Substitute a pre-charge form-number dispute for the deliberation-stage duty the jury's written request triggered.

## Act 4. Objection expansion into consent

Rosario's statements addressed several matters: suspected unauthorized juror research, the restriction on considering provisions outside the instructions, the court's refusal to supply instructions under Jury Request Form No. 1, and the additional vehicle and highway definitions supplied under Form No. 2.

Those subjects are not interchangeable.

He objected to the trial judge's failure to instruct the jury before deliberations, which, in his understanding, left jurors needing to seek legal guidance concerning the evidence. His objection to that resulting predicament is not an objection to supplying the instructions, and it is not consent to refusing them.

The completed Form No. 1 response contained two commands: a restriction on consideration and an express refusal to instruct. The respondent treated agreement with the former as approval of the latter.

Form No. 2 raised a separate object: the court's additional highway-and-street explanation and its transmission during deliberations. That dispute concerns scope, disclosure, timing, and effect of the addition, not consent to Refusal 1.

## The judge's stated reason the respondent denied

The Respondent's Brief at pages 11-12 maintained that the court's refusal was not based on the statutes' absence from evidence.

The transcript records the judge's explanation:

> "neither Vehicle Codes Section 22107 nor 21804 subdivision A were part of the evidence." (11 RT 7:5-7.)

The judge immediately stated that jurors were not permitted to consider those provisions as evidence. (11 RT 7:8-9.)

California public statutory law is subject to judicial notice under Evidence Code section 451(a). A statute is law the court declares, not an exhibit that must first be admitted.

## Invited error after transmission

Under *Norgart v. Upjohn Co.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 383, 403, invited error requires conduct that induced the challenged ruling. A statement made after transmission cannot itself have caused the earlier transmission.

The judge later stated that Rosario had submitted arguments clarifying objections to the responses to both Jury Request Forms Nos. 1 and 2, and that the court had considered and overruled those objections. (11 RT 12:27-13:7; 11 RT 11:28-12:4.)

Those rulings place opposition to the completed responses on the record. The Opinion's consent-based disposition did not reconcile that sequence with the broader agreement premise.
