# JKC3H8 v. Colton (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468

## 1. Citation block

**JKC3H8 et al. v. James Colton et al.** (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468, 164 Cal.Rptr.3d 450 (Third Appellate District; Hoch, J.), filed November 13, 2013, review denied. Appeal from the Superior Court of San Joaquin County (Roger Ross, J.). Disposition: judgment **vacated as moot**.

## 2. Verbatim issue-and-holding statement (opinion, opening passage)

The Court of Appeal stated the issue and the holding in a single passage:

> "Where a plaintiff who has filed a complaint containing causes of action that are based, in part, on protected conduct and therefore potentially subject to a special motion to strike under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16 (the anti-SLAPP statute) — subsequently files an amended complaint removing the allegations of protected conduct as a basis for liability, does the filing of the amended complaint render moot a later-filed anti-SLAPP motion based on the original complaint? **We conclude that it does.**"
>
> *(JKC3H8 v. Colton (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468, 471–472 (opinion of Hoch, J.).)*

## 3. Verbatim limit / "subvert or avoid" passage (pp. 477–478)

The Court of Appeal also drew the express limit on its holding:

> "[A] plaintiff or cross-complainant **may not seek to subvert or avoid a ruling on an anti-SLAPP motion by amending the challenged complaint or cross-complaint in response to the motion**."
>
> *(JKC3H8 v. Colton, supra, 221 Cal.App.4th at pp. 477–478, 164 Cal.Rptr.3d 450.)*

This passage is the "subvert-or-avoid" rule that draws the dividing line between (a) amendments that **narrow or moot** issues raised by an anti-SLAPP motion, which the Court of Appeal expressly recognized as a legitimate procedural feature of California law, and (b) amendments designed to **subvert or avoid** an anti-SLAPP ruling, which are not permitted.

## 4. Procedural posture (pp. 472–473, 479)

As subsequent published and unpublished California opinions have summarized the procedural posture of *JKC3H8*:

> "[T]he plaintiff filed an amended complaint **less than two hours prior to** the defendant filing an anti-SLAPP motion directed at the original complaint. (Id. at pp. 472–473.) The amendments eliminated from the complaint **all allegations of activity purportedly protected by the anti-SLAPP statute**. (Id. at p. 479.) As a result, the appellate court concluded **there were no causes of action arising from protected activity to strike at the time the defendant's motion was filed, meaning the motion was moot.** (Ibid.)"
>
> *(Truong v. Tran (Cal. Ct. App., 4th Dist., Div. 3, Aug. 29, 2023, No. G061703) [nonpub. opn.] [recapping JKC3H8 v. Colton].)*

The "point at which a plaintiff is prohibited from amending the complaint in response to an anti-SLAPP motion is **no earlier than the filing of the anti-SLAPP motion**." (Cf. *Newport Harbor Ventures, LLC v. Morris Cerullo World Evangelism* (2017) and *JKC3H8*, supra.)

## 5. Three propositions of California law established or recognized by *JKC3H8* that are directly adverse to the MFL-SAC opposition's "no such authority exists" representation

The MFL-SAC opposition filed by defendants on April 23, 2026 represents to the tribunal, in its Introduction:

> "Plaintiff cites no authority to support the proposition that he can file his Motion for Leave to Amend — as **no such authority exists** to support his motion."

That representation is incorrect as a matter of California law. *JKC3H8* establishes or recognizes the following three propositions, each directly adverse to that representation:

**Proposition 1 — Authority on the leave-to-amend / anti-SLAPP intersection plainly exists.**
*JKC3H8* is itself a published, controlling California Court of Appeal decision squarely addressing whether a plaintiff may amend a complaint in the face of a contemplated or pending anti-SLAPP motion. The mere existence of *JKC3H8* (and its cited progeny — *Salma v. Capon* (2008) 161 Cal.App.4th 1275; *Sylmar Air Conditioning v. Pueblo Contracting Services, Inc.* (2004) 122 Cal.App.4th 1049; *Schaffer v. City and County of San Francisco* (2008) 168 Cal.App.4th 992; *Simmons v. Allstate Ins. Co.* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 1068) defeats any representation that "no such authority exists" on the question.

**Proposition 2 — Amendments that "narrow or moot" protected-activity issues are recognized as lawful and effective in California.**
The express holding of *JKC3H8* is that an amendment **removing the protected-activity allegations** from the pleading is effective and renders an anti-SLAPP motion directed at the prior pleading moot. (*JKC3H8*, supra, 221 Cal.App.4th at pp. 471–472, 479.) That is precisely the "narrowing or mooting" pattern California recognizes. It is not "pleading-around-the-SLAPP" trickery; it is a procedural feature the Court of Appeal expressly endorsed for judicial economy.

**Proposition 3 — Anti-SLAPP filing does not displace the trial court's ordinary pleading-management authority.**
*JKC3H8* does not displace the trial court's ordinary pleading-management authority under Code of Civil Procedure sections 472, 473(a)(1), 576, and 425.16(f). The "subvert or avoid" rule (221 Cal.App.4th at pp. 477–478) is a **limit** on amendment to defeat an anti-SLAPP ruling — it is not a wholesale grant of jurisdictional immunity to the anti-SLAPP movant from amendments offered for legitimate purposes (narrowing, clarification, alignment with the gravamen analysis required by *Park v. Board of Trustees* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057). The trial court therefore retains discretion to manage the pleadings under §§ 473(a)(1) and 576 even when an anti-SLAPP motion is on calendar.

## 6. Why this case is "directly adverse, controlling" within the meaning of CRPC 3.3(a)(2)

California Rule of Professional Conduct 3.3(a)(2) provides:

> "A lawyer shall not: ... (2) fail to disclose to the tribunal legal authority in the controlling jurisdiction known* to the lawyer to be directly adverse to the position of the client and not disclosed by opposing counsel ...."

*JKC3H8* satisfies every element of that disclosure obligation as applied to the MFL-SAC opposition's "no such authority exists" representation:

1. **Legal authority** — *JKC3H8* is a published California Court of Appeal opinion (review denied), citable in California superior courts.
2. **In the controlling jurisdiction** — California; the Third District is a California Court of Appeal whose published opinions bind every California superior court under *Auto Equity Sales, Inc. v. Superior Court* (1962) 57 Cal.2d 450.
3. **Directly adverse** — the opinion expressly addresses the leave-to-amend / § 425.16 intersection that defendants represented "no such authority exists" on.
4. **Not disclosed by opposing counsel** — the MFL-SAC opposition does not cite *JKC3H8* anywhere, and instead represents that no authority exists.

## 7. Distinguishing characteristics of plaintiff's proposed Second Amended Complaint

Plaintiff's MFL-SAC, in CGC-25-631801 and CGC-25-631802 respectively, is offered to **narrow or moot** protected-activity allegations and to **clarify** the gravamen-defining facts so the case is decided on the unprotected, non-communicative core of plaintiff's claims (claim-handling failures, primary-rights segregation, and UCL alignment with independent fraud channels). In substance, plaintiff's amendment falls on the *narrowing/mooting* side of the *JKC3H8* line, **not** the "subvert or avoid" side. Plaintiff's amendment:

- Removes or limits protected-activity allegations (the *JKC3H8* fact pattern).
- Reinforces the express anti-SLAPP disavowal that the gravamen is **non-communicative pre-litigation claim handling** under 10 California Code of Regulations section 2695.7(b) (CGC-25-631802) and primary-rights segregation under *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666 (CGC-25-631801).
- Aligns the Unfair Competition Law allegations with their **independent-fraud channel** under *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364 and *Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Cos.* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287 (CGC-25-631802).
- Sharpens the *Action Apartment Assn. v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232 communicative-act boundary.
- Is offered for **judicial economy**, not to defeat the merits of the anti-SLAPP motion.
