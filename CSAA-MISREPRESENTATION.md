# CSAA MISREPRESENTATIONS

**Authority links:** Where a statute or opinion is mirrored in this repository, the citation links to [`CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/`](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/). All other citations link to official or commonly used public sources and are labeled **(external)** in the link text.

When a California liability carrier issues a letter announcing that "liability is denied" and then closes the claim file without ever retrieving the 911 audio, the body-worn camera footage, the CAD log, or the supplemental police report, it has not completed an investigation in any sense the law recognizes. It has prematurely terminated one. That distinction is doing all of the work in this analysis, because under the doctrinal stack now culminating in [Bartel v. Chicago Title Insurance Co. (2025) 111 Cal.App.5th 655 — external, courts.ca.gov published opinion](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083), and reaching back through [Wilson v. 21st Century Ins. Co. (2007) 42 Cal.4th 713 — external](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/439609/wilson-v-twenty-first-century-insurance-company/), [Egan v. Mutual of Omaha (1979) 24 Cal.3d 809](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/opinions-pdf/Egan-v-Mutual-of-Omaha-Ins-Co-24-Cal-3d-809.pdf), [Mariscal v. Old Republic Life Ins. Co. (1996) 42 Cal.App.4th 1617 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/42/1617.html), [Maslo v. Ameriprise Auto & Home Ins. (2014) 227 Cal.App.4th 626 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2014/b249271.html), [Jordan v. Allstate Ins. Co. (2007) 148 Cal.App.4th 1062 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2007/b187706m.html), and [10 CCR § 2695.7 — external, official CCR (OAL / Westlaw)](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29), an "investigation complete" representation is not something the carrier gets to declare into existence by its own subjective belief. It is a regulated factual assertion that the carrier has retrieved and weighed all evidence reasonably available to it. A denial issued without those silent witnesses is therefore objectively unreasonable on its face the moment it is sent, and from the day the carrier is given formal notice that those silent witnesses were never retrieved, every subsequent day of continued denial migrates the conduct from negligence into willful, oppressive, malicious bad faith, the territory of [Civil Code § 3294](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md).

The legal "situs" of that denial is not a single tort. It sits at the intersection of at least five independent breaches, any one of which would by itself be actionable. The first is the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, where California has been rejecting passive-review insurance practice for nearly fifty years. [Egan](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/opinions-pdf/Egan-v-Mutual-of-Omaha-Ins-Co-24-Cal-3d-809.pdf) held in 1979 that an insurer cannot reasonably and in good faith deny payments to its insured without thoroughly investigating the foundation for the denial, the duty is affirmative, not reactive. [Frommoethelydo v. Fire Ins. Exchange (1986) 42 Cal.3d 208 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/42/208.html) made plain that the manner of investigation is independently actionable even where the claim might ultimately fail on the merits. And [Wilson](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/439609/wilson-v-twenty-first-century-insurance-company/) in 2007 became the modern locus classicus, holding that an insurer may not deny a claim based on speculation, may not fail to fully investigate the grounds for its denial, may not "fail to objectively and fairly evaluate the claim," and may not "selectively credit one piece of evidence over another." The second breach is the [Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083) objective-baseline failure: in 2025 the Court of Appeal sharpened the duty by holding that the test is not whether the adjuster subjectively thought the file was complete, but whether an objectively reasonable insurer in the same position, applying industry custom and the regulatory minima, would treat the record as adequate to support a denial. Bartel exposes two distinct failure modes, an existence-of-evidence failure (the insurer did not even ask whether silent witnesses existed) and a retrieval failure (the insurer knew they existed but chose not to retrieve them), and the present fact pattern is, embarrassingly for the carrier, a double failure, because any police report on a motor-vehicle/pedestrian incident invariably indicates BWC activation and 911 receipt under [SFPD body-worn camera policy (DGO 10.11; briefing may cite superseded GO 5.07) — external](https://www.sanfranciscopolice.org/your-sfpd/policies/general-orders/10-11) and [Penal Code § 832.18 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=832.18.). The third breach is [Mariscal](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/42/1617.html)'s neutral truth-seeking duty: an insurer cannot reasonably reject a claim where it has not fully inquired into possible bases that might support the claimant's position. Mariscal rejects the asymmetric "investigate to defeat" model. And every silent witness here, Rosemond's eyewitness statement on BWC, the insured's "yeah" 911 admission, the CAD timing, the supplemental report, supports the claim, not the denial. The fourth breach is the claim-file duty announced in [Maslo](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2014/b249271.html) and reinforced by [Hamilton v. Maryland Cas. Co. (2002) 27 Cal.4th 718 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/27/718.html): the carrier must build a complete claim file sufficient to support its determination, the duty to investigate runs continuously through the life of the claim, and an incomplete file is itself evidence of bad faith. The fifth breach is statutory and is what gives this analysis its real load-bearing strength: [Insurance Code § 790.03(h)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/INS-790.03.md) and the Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations at [10 CCR § 2695.7 — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29) lay down a regulatory floor that, once breached, leaves the carrier with no ground to stand on at all.

That regulatory spine is the load-bearing wall of the case. It is what makes the denial letter a factual representation rather than a litigation posture, and therefore what makes the negligent-misrepresentation count survive the inevitable "this was just opinion or aspirational language" defense. Section 2695.7(b) requires the carrier to accept or deny within forty days of receiving sufficient information to make a determination, which means a premature denial is itself a declaration by the carrier that the file is sufficient, a factual representation. Section 2695.7(d) requires that decisions be based on all information reasonably available, so a failure to obtain the BWC, 911, or CAD when they are obtainable by Pitchess, CPRA, or routine subpoena is per se a violation. Section 2695.7(g) bars the insurer from representing or implying that a claim will be denied without conducting and fully documenting a thorough, fair, and objective investigation; closing the file is itself an implied representation that such an investigation occurred. [10 CCR § 2695.5(b) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29) requires the carrier to respond to claimant communications within fifteen days, so ignoring a "Notice of Incomplete Investigation" letter compounds the violation. Each subsection is independently enforceable as evidence of unfair claims practices under [Zhang v. Superior Court (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/opinions-pdf/Demurrer-cal-4th-vol57-p364-Zhang-v-Superior-Court.pdf) and [Hughes v. Progressive Direct Ins. Co. (2024) 99 Cal.App.5th 1019 — external, Google Scholar search](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Hughes%22+%22Progressive+Direct%22+%2299+Cal.App.5th+1019%22). On top of that regulation sits [Insurance Code § 790.03(h)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/INS-790.03.md), where three subprongs are squarely violated by a BWC-less, 911-less denial: (h)(1) prohibits misrepresenting to claimants pertinent facts relating to coverage, and a "concluded investigation" representation is precisely a misrepresentation of fact about the scope and completeness of the investigation when key materials were never retrieved; (h)(3) prohibits failing to adopt and implement reasonable standards for the prompt investigation and processing of claims, and a standard that allows liability denials before BWC and 911 retrieval is per se unreasonable for any motor-vehicle or pedestrian claim; and (h)(5) prohibits failing to effectuate prompt, fair, and equitable settlements where liability has become reasonably clear, which it has the moment the BWC contains an independent eyewitness placing fault on the insured and the 911 audio contains a party-opponent admission. Stacked together, the doctrines ([Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083), [Wilson](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/439609/wilson-v-twenty-first-century-insurance-company/), [Egan](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/opinions-pdf/Egan-v-Mutual-of-Omaha-Ins-Co-24-Cal-3d-809.pdf), [Mariscal](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/42/1617.html), [Maslo](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2014/b249271.html), [Hamilton](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/27/718.html)) and the regulations ([10 CCR § 2695.7(b)(d)(g) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29) and [§ 790.03(h)(1)(3)(5)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/INS-790.03.md)) leave no layer the carrier can defend on; it must defeat all of them, which is impossible where the BWC and 911 in fact exist and were retrievable by routine subpoena. And the doctrinal-regulatory convergence is what converts the denial letter into a factual representation in the [Borba v. Thomas (1977) 70 Cal.App.3d 144 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/3d/70/144.html) / [Bily v. Arthur Young & Co. (1992) 3 Cal.4th 370 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/3/370.html) / [Quelimane Co. v. Stewart Title Guaranty Co. (1998) 19 Cal.4th 26 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/19/26.html) sense: the matter is peculiarly within the carrier's knowledge, the regulated communicative posture imposes a duty of accuracy, and any third-party claimant or evaluating counsel reasonably relies on a regulated insurer's "concluded" representation as factual. That is the doorway into negligent misrepresentation under [Civil Code § 1710(2)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-1710.md), and where the carrier knew the silent witnesses existed and were obtainable, into fraudulent misrepresentation under [§ 1710(1)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-1710.md) one tier up.

The four silent witnesses deserve to be named as a doctrine, because that is how they travel into a motion. The 911 audio is a party-opponent admission under [Evidence Code § 1220](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/EVID-1220.md) and [§ 1222 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1222.), capturing the operator's question and the insured's "yeah" response to "you hit a pedestrian?", a confession of liability admissible against the insured directly and against the carrier as principal under [§ 1222 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1222.). The body-worn camera footage is an independent percipient eyewitness statement (Rosemond stating on camera that the insured struck the pedestrian) plus scene observations, admissible as a spontaneous declaration or present sense impression under [Evidence Code § 1240 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1240.) and [§ 1241 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1241.) and not subject to any plaintiff-bias attack. The CAD log is a business record under [Evidence Code § 1271 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1271.) documenting the temporal sequence (911 received, units dispatched, scene work) before any later self-serving statements by the insured. The supplemental report is an official record under [Evidence Code § 1280 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1280.) supplying the officer's narrative consistent with both the BWC and the 911. Each of these is public, each is available, and each is reachable through [Penal Code § 832.18 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=832.18.), [Government Code § 7920 et seq. (CPRA) — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=7920.), [Penal Code § 832.7 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=832.7.) as amended by SB 1421 / SB 16 (officer-record disclosure), and [Evidence Code § 1560 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1560.) (subpoena duces tecum to records custodian). For [Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083)'s "reasonably available" prong, the carrier needed only a subpoena duces tecum to SFPD records custodian under [CCP § 1985 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1985.) and [Evidence Code § 1560 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1560.), a CPRA request under [Government Code § 7922.530 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=7922.530.), or a routine claims-handling protocol authorization letter from the insured. California carriers have routinely retrieved BWC and 911 in motor-vehicle claims since at least 2018; there is no industry-standard defense to non-retrieval. The only defenses are "we didn't know it existed," which is defeated by the police report itself listing BWC and 911, and "we didn't think we needed it," which is exactly the [Wilson](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/439609/wilson-v-twenty-first-century-insurance-company/) "selective evaluation" violation. Liability becomes reasonably clear within [§ 790.03(h)(5)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/INS-790.03.md) on the strength of any one silent witness; on the strength of all four it is dispositive. The carrier's "investigation concluded → denial supported" claim is therefore not a defensible position. It is a counter-factual one.

The strategic consequences cascade from there. A premature denial without BWC and 911 shifts the litigation from "is there coverage?" to "was the investigation reasonable?", a bad-faith merits posture the carrier cannot defeat without producing the silent witnesses it never retrieved. Ignoring a BWC containing a party admission and an independent eyewitness statement makes the denial not merely incorrect but demonstrably unreasonable once a single declaration of facts is filed showing the carrier could have obtained the materials by routine subpoena. Closing the file demonstrates intent to truncate the inquiry, which is the [Mock v. Michigan Millers Mut. Ins. Co. (1992) 4 Cal.App.4th 306 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/4/306.html) / [Civil Code § 3294](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md) territory of oppression and malice for punitive damages. And the carrier's later litigation conduct disclaiming the same materials, panel counsel saying "no idea where this came from," denying possession or knowledge, generates a fourth, devastating hook: the judicial-estoppel multiplier. Position A (the file was concluded) and Position B (no possession, no knowledge of the silent witnesses) cannot both be true; one of them is necessarily false. [Jackson v. County of Los Angeles (1997) 60 Cal.App.4th 171 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/60/171.html), [Aguilar v. Lerner (2004) 32 Cal.4th 974 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/32/974.html), and [MW Erectors v. Niederhauser (2005) 36 Cal.4th 412 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/4th/36/412.html) supply the doctrine that picks which falsity binds the carrier.

That fourth hook is also why this analysis is not really about a single tort, but about a willful-blindness theory that supplies the punitive-damages spine. California civil law parallels the criminal "ostrich" doctrine in [People v. Lara (1996) 44 Cal.App.4th 102 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/44/102.html) and the federal [Global-Tech Appliances, S.A. v. SEB S.A. (2011) 563 U.S. 754 — external](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/10pdf/10-6.pdf) framework: a party who deliberately avoids confirming a fact it suspects to be true is treated as having knowledge of that fact. [In re Marriage of Cloney (2001) 91 Cal.App.4th 429 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/91/429.html) charges a party with knowledge of all that a reasonable inquiry would reveal once that party has facts sufficient to put a reasonable person on inquiry notice; [Mock](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/4/306.html) makes plain that an insurer's "deliberate failure to investigate" is malice within [§ 3294](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md). What does the punitive work in this case is not the failure to retrieve the BWC standing alone, it is the affirmative act of closing the claim file while the BWC was retrievable. That act supplies volition (a discrete decision was made), awareness (the police report referenced the BWC and 911, so the carrier had actual or imputed knowledge), cost-benefit motive (closing avoids the indemnity payment the silent witnesses would compel), and pattern evidence (closing other files in the same way supports the "despicable conduct" finding under [§ 3294(c)(2)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md)). Each of the [§ 3294(c)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md) hooks then opens: oppression in the form of despicable conduct that subjects a person to cruel and unjust hardship in conscious disregard of that person's rights (closing a file containing the insured's confession is by definition in conscious disregard of the third-party claimant's rights); malice in the form of willful and conscious disregard, supplied by continued denial after notice; and fraud in the form of intentional concealment of a material fact, supplied by the "concluded investigation" representation made while the silent witnesses were never retrieved. Under [State Farm Mutual Auto. Ins. Co. v. Campbell (2003) 538 U.S. 408 — external](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/538/408/case.pdf), single-digit multipliers up to 9:1 are presumptively constitutional where the conduct is "particularly egregious," and a deliberate file closure to avoid an indemnity obligation in the presence of an insured's recorded confession is exactly that.

Restated as an operational [Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083) test the court can actually apply: was the evidence reasonably available (yes, by subpoena, by CPRA, or by routine claims-handling authorization to the insured); did the carrier obtain or attempt to obtain it (no, the claim file does not contain the BWC, 911, CAD, or supplemental report); and was the failure to obtain objectively reasonable (no, a reasonable carrier in the same position, faced with a police report referencing BWC activation and a 911 dispatch, would have obtained those materials, and carriers routinely do). The denial fails all three steps and the "concluded investigation" representation is therefore objectively false.

The tactical fulcrum that converts the carrier's posture from negligence to willfulness is a single notice letter, properly framed. A "Notice of Incomplete Investigation" operates simultaneously on three legal levels. Regulatorily, it triggers [§ 2695.5(b) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29)'s fifteen-day duty to respond. Substantively, it identifies the omitted silent witnesses with specificity, CAD number, BWC officer ID and DSN, 911 timestamp, supplemental report number, so that any further denial is knowingly false within the meaning of [Wilson](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/439609/wilson-v-twenty-first-century-insurance-company/) and [Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083). Punitively, it converts post-notice denial from negligence (where [§ 3294](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md) is not triggered) into willful, conscious disregard (where [§ 3294(c)(1)–(2)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md) is triggered), via [Mock](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/4th/4/306.html). The notice must contain five operative paragraphs: an identification of the omitted evidence with specifics, a statement of availability under [CCP § 1985 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1985.) / [Evidence Code § 1560 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EVID&sectionNum=1560.) or [Government Code § 7922.530 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=7922.530.), a citation to [Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083) and [10 CCR § 2695.7(d) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29)'s "all information reasonably available" floor, a demand to reopen with a fifteen-day deadline mirroring [§ 2695.5(b) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29), and a trap clause stating that continued denial after the date of the letter will be relied upon as evidence of willful, conscious disregard for purposes of [Civil Code § 3294](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md) and of the (h)(5) violation. Once that notice is sent and acknowledged (or treated as received under the mailbox rule), the carrier is confined to three branches, none of which is good for it: it can retrieve and reverse, in which case the denial is rescinded, the carrier has admitted the original investigation was incomplete, and the negligent-misrepresentation count survives because the original 2/25/21 letter was nevertheless false when made; it can retrieve and re-deny, in which case it must explain why the BWC's eyewitness statement and the 911 confession do not support liability, for which there is no good-faith answer; or it can refuse to retrieve and continue the denial, at which point it has refused to investigate after specific identification of available evidence, and pure willfulness ripens punitive exposure. The decision tree has no "win" branch; that is the structural reason this notice is so powerful.

For the litigation phase, the silent-witnesses theory generates a tightly focused discovery plan. The Requests for Admission lock down the false representation: admit that as of the denial date the claim file contained no copy of any SFPD body-worn camera footage from the incident; admit no recording of the 911 audio; admit no CAD log; admit no supplemental SFPD report; admit that as of the denial date the BWC was obtainable by subpoena duces tecum to the SFPD records custodian; admit that the 2/25/21 "concluded" letter was made without retrieval of those materials. Refusal to admit, where the materials in fact were never in the file, exposes the carrier to cost-of-proof sanctions under [CCP § 2033.420](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CCP-2033.420.md). Targeted Requests for Production confirm the void: the complete claim file in native format with metadata; all claims-handling guidelines in effect on the denial date governing motor-vehicle and pedestrian claims and BWC / 911 retrieval; all policies, training materials, and memoranda governing the use of CPRA requests and SDTs in claims handling; all written communications between claims handlers regarding the incident; and all reserves, indemnity-exposure, and settlement-authority analyses. The deposition spine for claims handler and supervisor follows three lines: knowledge of the police report's references to BWC and 911 (did the handler read the report, note the BWC reference, note the 911 dispatch reference); the decision to close the file without the silent witnesses (who authorized, on what basis, was a [10 CCR § 2695.7(d) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29) "reasonably available" analysis performed and documented); and subsequent ratification (once the lawsuit was filed and panel counsel disclaimed possession of the same materials in MIL Nos. 6 and 17, what did the carrier do to correct the record). That third line is the judicial-estoppel bridge to 0007.md: the carrier's claims-handling testimony and panel counsel's MIL representations cannot both be true. A [CCP § 2025.230 — external](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=2025.230.) designated topic on "the completeness, contents, and curation of the claim file" forces the carrier to produce a witness bound by [Maslo](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2014/b249271.html) and [Wilson](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/439609/wilson-v-twenty-first-century-insurance-company/) on the duty to maintain a complete file.

The cross-link to 0007.md is what gives the entire architecture its bite. This memo supplies the falsity spine that the judicial-estoppel motion needs. The doctrines nest: a premature denial without BWC and 911 is a factually false "concluded investigation" representation; that falsity is the necessary fact for negligent misrepresentation under [§ 1710(2)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-1710.md), which is Position A; Position B (panel counsel's "no possession / no knowledge" representations in MIL 6, MIL 17, and the 2/18/25 hearing) is mutually exclusive with Position A; judicial estoppel locks CSAA into Position A under 0007.md § 5; and the court is asked to deem established, against CSAA, that the "investigation concluded" representation was a representation of fact, that CSAA had possession and knowledge of the BWC, 911, CAD, and supplemental report at all material times, and that CSAA is barred from contesting authenticity, chain of custody, or "unknown provenance" of those materials. Without the doctrinal work in this memo, Position A reads as opinion or aspirational language and the judicial-estoppel motion is weaker. With it, Position A reads as a regulated factual representation under [10 CCR § 2695.7(b)(d)(g) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29), and the judicial-estoppel doctrine bites cleanly. The two memos are designed to be filed as a pair, or merged into a single bench-brief sub-section under II.0(B) of the existing 631802 packet, the recommended insertion point being a new sub-section II.0(B)(2) titled "The 'concluded investigation' representation is a regulated factual misrepresentation that fixes liability under both negligent misrepresentation and bad-faith standards," cross-referenced into BENCH-BRIEF-MFL-SAC-CGC-25-631802.tex, into CSAA-INVESTIGATION-FRAUD-COROLLARY.md § 1–4, into 0007.md § 5, and against the bench-brief exhibits A1–A7 (police report, BWC, 911 transcript, CAD log, supplemental report, denial letter, panel-counsel MIL excerpts) which already supply the evidentiary basis for every element.

The risk register, viewed from the carrier's side, is what a reasonable insurer reads before settling. Bad-faith tort liability brings compensatory damages, emotional-distress damages, and [Brandt v. Superior Court (1985) 37 Cal.3d 813 — external](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/37/813.html) attorney fees once a denial is made without thorough investigation. Punitive damages under [§ 3294](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-3294.md) reach a single-digit multiplier of compensatory once denial continues after notice. Negligent misrepresentation under [§ 1710(2)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/CIV-1710.md) brings third-party compensatory and reliance damages once the falsity of the "concluded" representation is established. Judicial estoppel under 0007.md essentially fixes liability by court order once panel counsel's contradictory MIL representations are placed in the record. Regulatory exposure includes CDI Market Conduct review and civil penalties for [10 CCR § 2695.7(d) — external, official CCR](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Index?transitionType=Default&contextData=%28sc.Default%29) and [§ 790.03(h)(1)(3)(5)](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/INS-790.03.md) violations. A spoliation-by-neglect inference under [Evidence Code § 413](CASE-LAW-AND-STATUTES/statutes-cal/EVID-413.md) attaches at trial to any material the carrier had the opportunity to retrieve and chose not to. Brandt fees ride along with any bad-faith finding. And statutory penalties cumulate across multiple regulations on the multi-count violations. A reasonable insurer settles. An unreasonable one continues the denial. [Bartel](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/published/2025-05-30/h052083) (2025) is the test for which one is in front of the court.
