**III. BASIS FOR THE DEMAND: LIABILITY**

This demand rests on the certified Reporter's Transcript, which constitutes an official record of the Superior Court and is subject to mandatory judicial notice under Evidence Code section 452, subdivision (d).

On April 16, 2025 -- Trial Day 7, at page 214, lines 13 through 15 -- CSAA's retained defense counsel, Priya D. Navaratnasingham, stated to the Court:

> "Sure. They were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure."

This statement is irreconcilable with the following representations made by the same counsel to the same Court earlier in the same proceeding:

On January 17, 2025, in the defense Motion in Limine No. 17, Navaratnasingham represented: "This alleged log and recording were not produced during discovery, rather produced at the eleventh hour prior to trial." She further represented: "Defendant has no idea where this 911 call log or audio recording came from and who created it." (MIL 17, p. 3, para. A; p. 4, para. A.)

On February 18, 2025, at the pretrial hearing, Navaratnasingham represented to Judge Wong: "So this is news to me. We didn't realize that prior to handling counsel may have received it." She further represented: "I am representing to this Court that we did not have those two items until the day of the MSC." (Hearing Tr. p. 141, ll. 4-11; pp. 163-165.)

These two sets of statements cannot coexist. The body-worn camera footage and 911 audio were produced together in the same discovery response. Jeremy Jessup of the Dolan Law Firm testified under oath that "body cam footage, about five videos, as well as the 911 audio, as well as the CAD log" were "produced to defense counsel" and "served on them in the production for discovery." (Hearing Tr. pp. 139-140.) Receipt of one establishes constructive receipt of all.

The Court relied on the false pre-trial representations to deny Plaintiff's motion to continue the trial (Hearing Tr. p. 181), to grant the exclusionary Motions in Limine, and to frame the evidentiary dispute as Plaintiff's procedural failure rather than defense suppression. Those rulings were procured by fraud upon the court. The resulting judgment is subject to equitable vacatur under *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471 and *Aldrich v. San Fernando Valley Lumber Co.* (1985) 170 Cal.App.3d 725, 735.

The nine categories of anticipated defenses have been analyzed and foreclosed. A detailed analysis accompanies this demand in the companion Notice of Intent to Impeach. In brief: the litigation privilege does not apply to fraud on the court (*Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1065); anti-SLAPP does not reach an independent equity action (*Finton Construction, Inc. v. Bidna & Keys APLC* (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 200, 213); the fraud was extrinsic, not intrinsic, because the false statements were made to the judge to induce procedural rulings, not to the jury for the jury to weigh (*Kulchar*, *supra*, at p. 471); and harmless error analysis does not apply to structural fraud that corrupted the decisional process itself.

**IV. BASIS FOR THE DEMAND: DAMAGES**

The demand of $75,000,000 represents 75% of the conservatively modeled aggregate trial exposure. The damages model is as follows:

**A. Economic Damages at Retrial.** Upon vacatur, the underlying personal injury action will be retried on a fair record. Plaintiff is a highly qualified senior AI Architect in the San Francisco tech market. Past lost earnings over six years at a median rate of $750,000 total $4,500,000. Provable business loss totals $5,000,000. Future earning capacity loss, given total occupational disability, projects at $10,000,000 to $15,000,000. Lifetime medical specials for catastrophic traumatic brain injury project at $3,000,000 to $5,000,000. The base economic subtotal is $22,500,000 to $29,500,000.

**B. Non-Economic Damages at Retrial.** In catastrophic TBI cases tried in San Francisco, jury awards for pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, emotional distress, and cognitive impairment routinely apply a multiplier to the economic baseline. A conservative 1.0x to 1.5x multiplier yields $22,500,000 to $44,250,000. Combined retrial exposure is $45,000,000 to $73,750,000.

**C. Ancillary Equitable Monetary Relief (CGC-25-631801).** Incident to vacatur, Plaintiff seeks restitutionary relief for out-of-pocket litigation costs, fraud-caused lost time and earnings, and incremental medical expenses during the fraud-driven period. Estimated at $2,000,000 to $5,000,000.

**D. Prentice Damages (CGC-25-631802).** Under *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618, CSAA is liable for the full segregable cost of the underlying multi-year litigation proximately caused by the denial-letter fraud.

**E. Punitive Damages (CGC-25-631802).** CSAA's conduct satisfies the statutory predicates of fraud, malice, and oppression under Civil Code section 3294, with corporate authorization under section 3294(b). Under *Neal v. Farmers Ins. Exchange* (1978) 21 Cal.3d 910, even a conservative 1x multiplier on compensatory damages adds $45,000,000 to $73,000,000.

**F. Aggregate Trial Exposure: In Excess of $100,000,000.**

The demand of $75,000,000 is structured as follows:

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Past lost earnings (6 years at $750,000/yr.) | $4,500,000 |
| Provable business loss | $5,000,000 |
| Future earning capacity (occupational disability) | $15,000,000 |
| Medical specials (lifetime TBI care) | $5,000,000 |

| Appellate exposure resolution (elimination of independent reversal risk on instructional error grounds; A173827 dismissal with prejudice) | $11,000,000 |
| **Total** | **$75,000,000** |

This demand excludes punitive damages entirely. It excludes UCL restitution. It excludes injunctive relief. It excludes prejudgment interest accruing under Civil Code section 3287(a). It assigns no value to the institutional-practice discovery exposure, the State Bar referral consequences, the reputational cost of a published judicial finding of fraud on the court, or the appellate costs CSAA has already incurred. The $11,000,000 appellate component reflects the value to CSAA of eliminating the independent risk that Appeal No. A173827 results in reversal on instructional error grounds (the jury requested the governing Vehicle Code duties during deliberations; the trial court withheld that law on the mistaken premise that statutes are "not evidence"; the verdict was 9-3). A reversal would independently yield retrial on a fair record.

Plaintiff is offering CSAA the opportunity to resolve the entire multi-front litigation at 75% of hard compensatory value, without any punitive premium, without institutional exposure, and without public findings.

CSAA owes a duty of good faith to its insured and to claimants to evaluate and accept reasonable settlement demands. An insurer that unreasonably refuses a demand within policy limits exposes itself to bad faith liability for any excess judgment. (*Crisci v. Security Ins. Co.* (1967) 66 Cal.2d 425, 429; *Hogan v. Midland National Ins. Co.* (1970) 3 Cal.3d 553, 564.) If CSAA refuses this demand and the eventual judgment exceeds policy limits, Mr. Abdelhalim may have a separate bad faith claim against CSAA for failing to accept a reasonable settlement that would have protected him from personal exposure.
