Under *Howard v. American National Fire Insurance Co.* (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 498, 527-528, an insurer that selects and retains counsel is vicariously liable for the conduct of that counsel in the defense of the action. *Qui facit per alium facit per se* -- he who acts through another acts himself. The MILs, hearing representations, and trial statements were all within the scope of PSA's retained defense work. CSAA cannot distance itself from a fraud it funded and from which it directly benefited.

This constitutes extrinsic fraud (*Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, 471; *Caldwell v. Taylor* (1933) 218 Cal. 471, 476), and the resulting judgment is subject to equitable vacatur from its inception because the Court's discretion was exercised on a false factual predicate supplied by an officer of the court.

The Court's rulings were made in direct reliance on these false statements. Plaintiff could not "cross-examine" the Court's reliance on defense counsel's representations. The fraud was embedded in the procedural framework -- not in the evidence the jury weighed, but in the corruption of the judge's decisional process through willful deceit. ## VI. THE QUESTION AND THE NINE FORECLOSED DEFENSES

The certified record forecloses every viable escape route. Below are the nine categories of defense theories that have been analyzed, and why each fails:

**1. "Good faith mistake / clerical error"** -- The April 16 admission destroys this. *Scientia utrimque par pares contrahentes facit* -- equal knowledge on both sides makes the parties equal. The dismissive phrasing "for whatever, sure" reveals settled, casual familiarity with the Dolan origin -- *scienter*, not ignorance. One does not casually name a specific law firm from years earlier as the source of evidence if one is genuinely ignorant. The certified transcript captures both statements; neither can be altered.

**2. "Litigation privilege (Civ. Code sec. 47(b))"** -- The California Supreme Court held in *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1065 that the litigation privilege does not apply to fraud on the court. This action seeks equitable vacatur, not tort damages for the statements themselves.

**3. "Anti-SLAPP (CCP sec. 425.16)"** -- *Finton Construction, Inc. v. Bidna & Keys APLC* (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 200, 213 directly holds that an independent equity action challenging a judgment procured by fraud does not "arise from" protected petitioning. The gravamen is the fraud, not the act of filing motions.

**4. "Intrinsic fraud, not extrinsic"** -- The distinction is dispositive. Intrinsic fraud is *dolus in re ipsa* -- fraud within the thing itself (e.g., perjury before the jury, which the adversary system is designed to expose through cross-examination). Extrinsic fraud is *dolus dans locum contractui* -- fraud that corrupts the very framework within which the proceeding operates. Here, the false statements were made to the judge to induce procedural and evidentiary rulings, not to the jury for the jury to weigh. Plaintiff could not cross-examine the Court's reliance on defense counsel's representations. The fraud was collateral to the merits and embedded in the Court's decisional machinery. Under *Kulchar* and *Caldwell*, this is extrinsic fraud, and the equity court is obligated to intervene.

**5. "Plaintiff had the evidence too / no prejudice"** -- This defense is itself a product of the fraud it seeks to excuse. The Court denied the continuance because it was told the defense was "not blocking" Plaintiff and the materials were "news to me." The Court's entire framework for evaluating Plaintiff's diligence was manufactured by the false representations. The principle *nemo ex suo delicto meliorem suam conditionem facere potest* -- no one may improve his position by his own wrongdoing -- bars the defense from relying on a narrative it fabricated. The duty of candor under Bus. & Prof. Code section 6068(d) and Rule 3.3 is a duty owed to the tribunal, not to the opposing party; counsel may not misdirect the Court regardless of what the opposing party knew or did.

**6. "Harmless error"** -- Structural fraud defies harmless error analysis. Where the process itself was corrupted, the standard is not the ordinary *Watson* test of "reasonable probability of a more favorable result" but the more exacting inquiry into whether the fraud so infected the proceedings that the judgment cannot be permitted to stand. (*Smith v. Superior Court* (1992) 10 Cal.App.4th 1033, 1042.) In any event, the *Watson* standard is also met: the 9-3 verdict -- the minimum civil majority under Code of Civil Procedure section 618 -- means a single juror changing sides would have altered the outcome. The suppressed evidence includes a party admission under Evidence Code section 1220 ("I barely hit him with anything"), authenticated by the declarant himself ("Yes. That is my voice" -- RT Day 8, l. 1979), eyewitness body cam footage, and CAD corroboration. Prejudice is not speculative; it is demonstrable *per se*.

**7. "CSAA did not direct the fraud / agency separation"** -- *Howard v. American National Fire Insurance Co.* (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 498, 527-528 and Civil Code section 2338 foreclose this. CSAA funded the defense, selected PSA, and benefited from the verdict. The February 1, 2024 Deposition Subpoena issued by Alberto Reyna (Compex Order CA1147102-021) to the San Francisco Police Department -- requesting the 911 call and body-worn camera footage by name -- proves CSAA's agents knew exactly what records existed and sought them. At trial, Reyna then claimed he was "not sure what recording" Plaintiff was referring to. (RT 7, ll. 2990-2994.)

**8. "The substitution gap / prior counsel's fault"** -- Even assuming file-indexing confusion at the May 4, 2023 substitution, *ignorantia facti non excusat* when the party had the means of knowledge and failed to exercise due diligence. More critically, it does not explain the April 16, 2025 admission. By that date, Navaratnasingham casually knew the materials were "provided by Dolan." Either she discovered the materials between May 2023 and April 2025 and never corrected her prior false statements (violating Rule 3.3's *continuing* duty to correct a false statement of material fact previously made to the tribunal), or she knew all along. Either path is fatal.

**9. "Judicial estoppel does not apply"** -- Position A ("news to me") was accepted by the Court and relied upon to deny continuances and grant MILs. That is sufficient under *Jackson v. County of Los Angeles* (1997) 60 Cal.App.4th 171, 183. Position B ("provided by Dolan") merely needs to be totally inconsistent with Position A. It is.

**The question is this:** Does CSAA Insurance Exchange, through you as its counsel, have any explanation, defense, or exit for the irreconcilable contradiction between Navaratnasingham's pre-trial representations and her April 16, 2025 admission on the certified Reporter's Transcript?

If CSAA has an explanation that reconciles "news to me" and "did not receive until MSC" with "Sure. They were provided by Dolan. He has had them for whatever, sure," Plaintiff asks that you provide it in writing so it can be evaluated before the matter proceeds further. **Plaintiff requests a written response within thirty (30) calendar days of the date of this letter.** If no response is received, Plaintiff will assume CSAA has no such explanation and will proceed accordingly.
