*1. Scope of the Apr. 30 tentative.*

The tentative denies, on two narrow grounds, only Plaintiff's April 16,
2026 Motion for Leave to File a Second Amended Complaint and the
accompanying request for judicial notice:

(a) *Procedural, CCP § 1005(b) notice.* The tentative finds Apr. 16 was not
16 court days before Apr. 30 (and adds the § 1010.6(a)(3)(B) two-court-day
extension for email service). That is a calendaring ruling.

(b) *"Amend around an anti-SLAPP."* The tentative cites *City of Colton v.
Singletary* (2012) 206 Cal.App.4th 751, *Law Offices of Andrew L. Ellis v.
Yang* (2009) 178 Cal.App.4th 869, *Sylmar Air Conditioning v. Pueblo
Contracting Services* (2004) 122 Cal.App.4th 1049, *Simmons v. Allstate
Ins. Co.* (2001) 92 Cal.App.4th 1068, *Jackson v. Mayweather* (2017) 10
Cal.App.5th 1240, *Hansen v. CDCR* (2008) 171 Cal.App.4th 1537, and *Contreras
v. Dowling* (2016) 4 Cal.App.5th 774. We agree those authorities accurately
state the rule that a plaintiff may not amend out of a pending special
motion to strike. That the SAC is a work around, was big claim when Tyler
has a problem, understanding the merit of the FAC to begin with, if you put
them next to each. other you will see that the SAC and TAC make no approach
towards protected communication.
How you're going to imagine folding communication into an action such as
failure to investigate is going to be an entertaining show in lack of
logic, the use of procedural warefare is distasteful , but since you can't
meet me on the Merits , as you already know, your position is weak, and
it's getting weaker by the day as we close in on the hearings where the
frivolous anti-slap, which doesn't even address a single one of the causes
of action will be found to be deficient on its face. As I warned you filing
superfluous motions in order to delay proceedings is going to bite you
back, remember it's a double edged sword what you did and it was a bad
mistake.

*2. The May 13 hearing on the FAC anti-SLAPP is the merits hearing, not
today.*

Three scenarios at May 13, all of which keep the SAC and TAC content in
play:

(a) *Threshold or prong-one denial of the anti-SLAPP* (under §
425.17(c)/(b) or under *Park*/*Cashman*). The "amend around" rule that
supplies the merits half of today's tentative dissolves by operation of
law. The lodged SAC and TAC re-enter on the demurrer/§ 472/§ 473(a)(1)
track on a properly noticed renewed leave motion with full 16 + 2 court-day
notice.

(b) *Partial grant of the anti-SLAPP* as to discrete allegations only.
*JKC3H8* and *Nguyen-Lam* authorize leave to amend the un-stricken
portions; the lodged SAC and TAC are the natural amendment vehicles.
Practically, that path lets the court adopt the SAC/TAC architecture
earlier rather than later. Refusing to engage with *JKC3H8* on reply
implicates CRPC 3.3(a)(2).

(c) *Full grant on a particular count.* That count exits with an
interlocutory appeal right under § 425.16(i); the surviving counts continue
and the leave-to-amend question for them is unchanged.

In every scenario, the lodged SAC and TAC remain in the record both as
proposed amendments and as evidentiary material relevant to the prong-two
minimum-merit showing under *Sweetwater Union High School Dist. v. Gilbane
Building Co.* (2019) 6 Cal.5th 931. The Apr. 30 tentative does not strike
that material from the case.
