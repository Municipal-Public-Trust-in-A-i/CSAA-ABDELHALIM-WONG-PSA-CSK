I acknowledge receipt of your email regarding alleged service issues and
your stated intent to attempt file a motion “to quash” by March 6, 2026 in
CGC-25-631801 and CGC-25-631802.

I would like to clarify a few things to ensure we are both ont he same page
before we take the next steps in this action.

   1.

   *Service on a corporation is not limited to the registered agent.*
   Your email suggests service is improper because it was not made on
   CSAA’s “proper agent for service of process.” Code of Civil Procedure
   section 416.10 authorizes service on a corporation by delivering the
   summons and complaint to, among others, the designated agent for service of
   process or specified corporate officers (and other persons identified in
   the statute). (CCP § 416.10.) Service is therefore not categorically
   defective solely because it was not made on the registered agent.
   2.

   *A motion to quash addresses service of summons, not “quashing the
   case.”*
   A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10 is a motion to
   quash service of summons (i.e., it challenges the sufficiency of
   service/jurisdictional service). (CCP § 418.10.) If service is found
   defective, the procedural result is that service is set aside and Plaintiff
   completes proper service; the action is not “quashed” on that basis.
   3.

   *Amended complaints have their own service/response clock.*
   The operative pleadings are the amended complaints filed February 25,
   2026. When a complaint is amended, the amended complaint must be served on
   affected defendants, and the responsive pleading deadline runs from service
   of the amended pleading. (CCP § 471.5(a).) Accordingly, regardless of any
   dispute about prior service attempts, I will serve the operative amended
   complaints so there is no dispute as to the response clock.
   4.

   *Time to complete service / effect of a service dispute.*
   California provides three years after commencement to serve the summons
   and complaint. (CCP § 583.210(a).) A dispute about a particular service
   attempt does not terminate the action; it means service must be completed,
   and the response deadline is then calculated from valid service.
   5.

   *Service on counsel / POS-015 / electronic service.*
   Your request to serve amended pleadings on your office by email with a
   Notice and Acknowledgment of Receipt (POS-015) is not a substitute for
   statutorily authorized service of summons/process unless your client (or
   authorized counsel) agrees in writing to accept service of process on
   CSAA’s behalf. POS-015 pertains to service by mail with notice and
   acknowledgment under CCP § 415.30. Electronic service is governed by CCP §
   1010.6 and Cal. Rules of Court, rule 2.251, and requires appropriate proof
   of electronic service. At this time, absent written confirmation that your
   office is authorized to accept service of summons on behalf of CSAA, I will
   serve CSAA using methods authorized by the Code of Civil Procedure
   (including service on CSAA’s agent for service of process and/or other
   persons authorized under CCP § 416.10).

I will proceed with service of the operative amended complaints in each
case by a statutorily authorized method and will file the corresponding
proof(s) of service when completed. Please calculate CSAA’s responsive
pleading deadline from the date of service as provided in CCP section
471.5(a).
