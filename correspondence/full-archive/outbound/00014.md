grounds of extrinsic fraud on the court. The action names CSAA Insurance

CGC-25-631802 is a related action against CSAA alone, arising from CSAA's

*II. WHY A DEMURRER WILL NOT DISPOSE OF THESE ACTIONS*

I anticipate that CSAA and its co-defendants may elect to challenge both
complaints by demurrer
under Code of Civil Procedure section 430.10 on the grounds that the
complaints fail to state a
cause of action. I want to be direct with you: the record in these actions
forecloses the standard
demurrer arguments.

*A. The Intrinsic/Extrinsic Fraud Distinction Is Established on the
Transcript*

The expected demurrer argument -- that this is intrinsic fraud that was or
could have been
addressed in the underlying trial -- fails because the complaint
specifically and directly addresses
the extrinsic/intrinsic distinction with record citations. The fraud
alleged here was not perjury
weighed by the jury. It was false representations made by defense counsel
to the trial judge that
shaped the evidentiary and procedural landscape of the trial itself:
specifically, the denial of
continuances, the granting of Motions in Limine Nos. 6 and 17, and the
exclusion of the
supplemental police report and the deferral of the 911 recording admission,
all as documented on
the certified Reporter's Transcript.

California law is settled: "Fraud is extrinsic when it deprives the
unsuccessful party of an
opportunity to present his case to the court." (Kulchar v. Kulchar (1969) 1
Cal.3d 467, 471.)
"Where the fraud goes to the regularity of the proceedings, rather than to
the merits, and prevents
the aggrieved party from having a fair hearing, the fraud is extrinsic."
(Caldwell v. Taylor (1933)
218 Cal. 471, 476.) A demurrer that characterizes this as intrinsic fraud
will fail because the
Complaint identifies the precise court rulings procured by the false
representations and traces the
causal chain from misrepresentation to ruling to evidentiary prejudice on
the face of the certified
transcript.

*B. The Diligence Defense Is Foreclosed by the Fraud Chronology*

The Complaint directly addresses plaintiff diligence. Plaintiff propounded
discovery requests
seeking the 911 recordings, body-worn camera footage, and CAD logs. The
defense, through
repeated representations -- "news to me," "did not receive until MSC," "not
produced in
discovery," "no idea where it came from" -- actively misled the Court about
defense-side
possession. Plaintiff reasonably relied on defense compliance with
discovery obligations and on
the Court to require verification once the conflict surfaced on the record.
The diligence analysis
is supported by the 972-day elapsed-time gap: the materials were served on
defense counsel on
or about August 18, 2022, and the "Dolan" admission did not come until
April 16, 2025. No
plaintiff can be charged with diligence failure when opposing counsel was
affirmatively
representing non-receipt over a three-year period.

*C. The Vicarious Liability Claim Against CSAA Is Independently Pleaded*

CSAA is named not merely as a funding party. The Complaint alleges, and the
law confirms, that
"[a]n insurer that selects and retains counsel to defend its insured is
vicariously liable for the
conduct of that counsel in the defense of the action." (Howard v. American
National Fire
Insurance Co. (2010) 187 Cal.App.4th 498, 527-528.) CSAA funded, directed,
and ratified
these litigation positions. It received the benefit of the verdict. It
cannot now disavow the conduct
of the agents it retained and controlled.

*III. WHY AN ANTI-SLAPP MOTION WILL NOT SUCCEED*

I further anticipate that the attorney-defendants -- and potentially CSAA
through their counsel --

425.16 (the "anti-SLAPP

This motion will face two insurmountable obstacles.

First, the litigation privilege under Civil Code section 47(b) does not
immunize fraud on the
court. The independent action to vacate a judgment for extrinsic fraud

protects the fraud and bars the
remedy would mean that no judgment procured by an attorney's false
representation to a judge
could ever be vacated by an independent action -- a result that is
irreconcilable with Kulchar, with
Code of Civil Procedure section 187, and with the inherent equitable power
of the Court. The
privilege does not swallow the remedy.

Second, the probability-of-prevailing prong of the anti-SLAPP test is
satisfied on the face of the
record. At the anti-SLAPP stage, Plaintiff need only make a prima facie
showing from admissible
evidence. Here, the evidence is not circumstantial. It consists entirely of
