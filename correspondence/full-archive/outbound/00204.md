CONTEST TENTATIVE RULING, JUNE 23, 2026 TENTATIVE (CONSOLIDATED JUNE 24,
2026 MOTIONS), SAN FRANCISCO LOCAL RULE 8.3(D)



*Verbal Argument Addition for June 24th:*

*May it please the Court, Your Honor.*

The February 25th, 2021 denial letter is not protected litigation. It is
routine, regulated commercial claim-handling. Before the Court even
considers an anti-SLAPP analysis under Code of Civil Procedure section
425.16, we must first look at the commercial speech exemption under section
425.17, subdivision c.

On the date of that denial, there was no lawsuit. I was an unrepresented
third-party claimant. CSAA was not petitioning a tribunal; they were simply
doing the everyday business of insurance. They were investigating a claim
and issuing a coverage decision exactly as governed by Insurance Code
section 790.03 and California's Fair Claims Settlement Practices
Regulations.

Section 425.17, subdivision c, explicitly excludes commercial
speakers—including insurers—when they make representations about their
business or services to customers in the ordinary course of delivering
those services. A denial letter is the moment the insurer delivers, or
refuses to deliver, the exact product the consumer purchased. As the
appellate court held in *Inland Oversight Committee*, the Court is required
to decide this commercial gateway *first*. If the commercial exemption
applies, the anti-SLAPP door never opens.

Furthermore, under the Supreme Court's ruling in *Park versus Board of
Trustees*, anti-SLAPP protection only applies when the protected activity
*itself* is the wrong complained of. The wrong complained of in the FAC
here is not that CSAA filed motions years later. The wrong is that they
certified a concluded investigation and denied the claim while suppressing
outcome-determinative evidence. We cited later litigation conduct in our
complaint merely as evidence of a pattern, not as the gravamen. If we treat
a standard insurance denial as "preparatory to litigation" simply because
the consumer is later forced to sue, then *every* bad-faith claim becomes
subject to anti-SLAPP. *Park* expressly forbids that result.

Defendant’s reliance on the litigation privilege under Civil Code section
47, subdivision b, also fails. The Supreme Court in *Action Apartment
Association* requires a good-faith, serious contemplation of *imminent*
litigation for pre-litigation speech to be privileged. Closing a claim file
with a routine denial to an unrepresented consumer does not meet that test.
And as the *Kimmel* court established, absolute immunity does not apply
where fraud is the gravamen.

*Your Honor, we respectfully submit that the tentative ruling mislabels
commercial activity as petitioning speech. We ask the Court to deny the
motion regarding the February 25th denial, or, at minimum, sever the claims
under Baral and apply the correct sequence.*

If this tentative ruling stands, it creates a highly dangerous permission
structure. It essentially tells insurers they can skip the
commercial-speaker gateway entirely. It allows an insurer to suppress
crucial evidence, like 911 materials, deny a claim on a supposedly
"concluded" investigation, and then hide behind the litigation privilege
and anti-SLAPP simply because the insured eventually sues.

Adopting this tentative means the Court would convert post-filing defense
statements into the core "wrong," allowing Defendant to strike the entire
complaint. It bypasses the minimal-merit screen required at prong two, and
it completely forecloses our rights to amend, conduct discovery, or seek
satellite relief.

In short, Your Honor, if adopted verbatim, this ruling tells insurers they
can bypass the Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations with complete
immunity. We urge the Court to apply the four-step sequence outlined in our
briefing, deny the motion, and hold the Defendant accountable to the merits
of this case.
