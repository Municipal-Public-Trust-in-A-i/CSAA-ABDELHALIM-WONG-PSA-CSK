*4. The Moradi-Shalal Exception and Common-Law Deceit*
Although the *California Supreme Court in Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund
Insurance Companies, 46 Cal. 3d 287 (1988)*, *barred direct third-party
statutory claims under the Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act, the
Court explicitly preserved the right of third-party claimants to bring
traditional, independent, common-law actions against insurers for fraud and
deceit.*

When an insurer deliberately misrepresents its investigative process to a
third-party claimant (who is a legally recognized participant in the
adjustment process) it steps outside its role as a defender of its insured
and acts as an independent tortfeasor. The plaintiff's cause of action does
not seek derivative damages for the underlying accident liability; rather,
it targets the insurer’s independent acts of intentional or reckless deceit
pursuant to California Civil Code sections 1709 and 1710.

*Key Elements of the Deceit Claim:*

   - Actionable Deceit (Civ. Code § 1710): The actionable deceit occurs
   when the insurer leverages its structural position to fabricate a false
   narrative of a comprehensive investigation, effectively lulling the
   claimant into a false sense of finality to induce the abandonment of
   legitimate rights. This conduct squarely violates California Civil Code
   section 1710, which defines deceit as the assertion of an untrue fact by
   one who has no reasonable ground for believing it true, or the suppression
   of a fact by one who is bound to disclose it. By issuing an immediate,
   baseless denial while falsely representing that a thorough investigation
   occurred, the insurer engages in an affirmative misrepresentation regarding
   its operational conduct, a distinct, non-derivative wrong entirely
   decoupled from the underlying tort claim.
   - Establishment of Scienter: To establish the requisite scienter for
   common-law deceit, the claimant must demonstrate that the insurer acted
   with reckless disregard for the truth. This element is established when the
   insurer practices willful blindness toward definitive, highly material, and
   readily accessible evidence of its own insured's liability. When an insurer
   issues an absolute denial while intentionally ignoring 911 audio
   recordings, police body-camera footage, or an express admission of fault by
   its own policyholder, the determination cannot be sanitized as an innocent
   administrative oversight. Under California common law, this deliberate
   evasion of reality satisfies the fraudulent intent requirement per se, as
   the insurer knowingly relies on a false premise to manipulate the claimant
   to their detriment.
   - Independent Proximate Damages (Civ. Code § 1709): Finally, the law
   recognizes this cause of action because the insurer’s fraudulent
   misrepresentations proximately cause independent, non-derivative damages
   separate and apart from the underlying physical or property injuries. As a
   direct consequence of the insurer's deceptive practices, the plaintiff is
   forced into avoidable, protracted litigation and must absorb ongoing
   economic harms, including the out-of-pocket costs of hiring experts and
   investigators to uncover a confession of fault that the insurer already
   possessed. These distinct financial burdens, alongside the attendant
   emotional distress, constitute actionable, independent damages under
   California Civil Code section 1709, anchoring direct tort liability for the
   insurer’s independent, reckless misconduct.
