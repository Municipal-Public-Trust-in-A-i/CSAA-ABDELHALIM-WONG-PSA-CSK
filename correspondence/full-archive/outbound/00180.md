<#ii-the-civil-harassment-label-is-legally-unavailable-on-these-facts>II.
The "civil harassment" label is legally unavailable on these facts

Code of Civil Procedure section 527.6(b)(1) defines "course of
conduct" and *expressly
excludes* "[c]onstitutionally protected activity." The conduct your email
targets, namely (a) filing pleadings as a self represented plaintiff, (b)
serving those pleadings on counsel of record as the law requires, and (c)
reporting suspected professional misconduct to the State Bar of California,
is core protected petitioning activity under the First Amendment of the
United States Constitution, Article I, sections 2 and 3 of the California
Constitution, and the *Noerr Pennington* doctrine. As a matter of statutory
text, that activity cannot supply the predicate for a civil harassment
claim.

Any petition predicated on those activities would also be vulnerable to a
special motion to strike under Code of Civil Procedure section 425.16. I
reserve all rights under section 425.16, including fees and costs.
------------------------------
<#iii-state-bar-communications-are-absolutely-privileged>III. State Bar
communications are absolutely privileged

Communications to the State Bar of California are absolutely privileged
under Civil Code section 47, subdivision (b). See *Chen v. Fleming* (1983)
147 Cal.App.3d 36; *Stanwyck v. Horne* (1983) 146 Cal.App.3d 450. They are
not actionable as harassment, as a tort, or as a basis for sanctions,
regardless of the role (attorney, paralegal, secretary) of the person whose
conduct is reported. Your demand that I "cease" reporting suspected
misconduct is therefore legally improper. I will continue to make any
reports I deem warranted, in accordance with the privilege the Legislature
has granted.

For the avoidance of doubt: my State Bar communications have addressed
conduct, not personalities, and have been made in good faith based on the
record in these matters.
------------------------------
<#iv-service-of-court-documents-is-required-by-statute-and-rule>IV. Service
of court documents is required by statute and rule

You and your firm are counsel of record. Under Code of Civil Procedure
sections 1010, 1010.6, and 1013, and California Rules of Court, rule 2.251,
I am required to serve every filing on every party of record. Your firm's
executed Consents to Electronic Service in CGC-25-631801 and CGC-25-631802
are on file. You are entitled to notice of my filings precisely because the
law requires it. Compliance with mandatory service obligations cannot be
repackaged as "harassment."

I will continue to serve every filing on Porter Scott in full compliance
with those provisions and with any electronic service order entered by the
Court. If there is a specific service issue you wish to raise (a wrong
address, a duplicate, a misdirected attachment), identify it and I will
address it. Vague demands that I "stop" are not a service objection.
------------------------------
<#v-your-firms-workload-and-the-courts-backlog-are-not-my-concern>V. Your
firm's workload and the court's backlog are not my concern

Whether your firm finds the volume of my filings inconvenient, and whether
the San Francisco Superior Court's docket is congested, are not matters
governed by your preferences. The pace, frequency, and content of my
filings are governed by the California Code of Civil Procedure, the
California Rules of Court, the San Francisco Superior Court's local rules,
and the orders of the Court in each case. The Legislature, not opposing
counsel, sets the rules of access to the courts.

If you contend that any specific filing is frivolous, the proper remedy is
a noticed motion in the proper court, in the proper case, under Code of
Civil Procedure section 128.5 or section 128.7, with the safe harbor
procedures the law requires. The proper remedy is not an unsworn email
demand that I unilaterally stop exercising rights of access. I expressly
invite you to use the statutory mechanisms if you believe they apply. You
have not done so, and your email is not a substitute.
------------------------------
<#vi-the-ai-created-label-is-not-a-substantive-objection>VI. The "AI
created" label is not a substantive objection

California has no rule of civil procedure or rule of court that prohibits a
self represented litigant from using research, drafting, or assistive tools
in the preparation of pleadings. Every document I file bears my signature.
I take full responsibility under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7 for
the factual and legal contentions in each filing. The provenance of my
drafting tools is not a permissible substantive objection and is not a
basis for any of the labels in your email ("frivolous," "harassment," "bad
faith"). It is a pejorative.
------------------------------
<#vii-your-email-is-a-cease-and-desist-directed-at-protected-activity>VII.
Your email is a cease and desist directed at protected activity

Stripped of adjectives, your email demands that I (1) stop filing
pleadings, (2) stop serving them on you, and (3) stop reporting suspected
misconduct to the State Bar. Each of those is constitutionally protected

(c), (f), and (g). I am preserving your email and this response for the
record.
