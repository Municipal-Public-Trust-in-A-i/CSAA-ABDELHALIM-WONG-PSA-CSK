Your firm appears to require a fundamental, remedial lesson in California
trial procedure. CACI instructions are merely standardized forms rather
than substantive restrictions on the law, whereas a trial judge maintains
an affirmative, mandatory legal duty to instruct the jury on all material
principles of law supported by the evidence. When a deliberating jury
explicitly requests guidance on applicable statutory standards, denying
that request based on procedural technicalities violates statutory
mandates, due process, and the fundamental constitutional right to a fair
trial. The reality of this record remains catastrophic for your side across
every phase of the proceedings. When Judge Wong entertained questions from

Your entire position rests upon a dangerous legal fallacy that distorts
basic trial procedure. The judge is not the jury and holds zero authority
to act as the arbiter of factual truth. Under Code of Civil Procedure
section 614, when a deliberating jury requests clarification on a point of
law, the court has an absolute, mandatory duty to provide that instruction.
A deliberating jury that identifies governing Vehicle Code sections by
number and explicitly asks for guidance has formally identified a point of
law that the court is legally bound to clarify. Furthermore, under Evidence
Code section 310, questions of law belong exclusively to the court, meaning
statutes are mandatory governing standards rather than physical exhibits
requiring formal evidentiary admission. Judge Wong's ruling telling the
jury it could not consider Vehicle Code sections 22107 and 21804(a) because
they were not part of the evidence represents a total, structural collapse
of basic jurisprudence. It improperly converted the court's initial
instructional omission into a permanent, unlawful prohibition, barring the
jury from applying the governing law to the facts before them.

convenient fairy tale, asserting that this appeal is a self-inflicted wound
born of waiver and invited error, but the trial record throws that fiction
right back in your face. Before jury instructions were settled, I
specifically requested coverage of Vehicle Code sections 22107 and
21804(a), which define the statutory turning and yielding duties of
ordinary care in a pedestrian collision. The court omitted those duties,
creating a glaring instructional vacuum. The defense then walked straight
into that trap by designating Defense Exhibit 500, my own complaint,
arguing from it during closing statements, and handing it directly to the
deliberating jury. That complaint explicitly pleaded those exact Vehicle
Code sections. You did not merely deny liability, you placed the very
document citing those statutory duties directly into the jury's hands. When
the jury predictably found the statutes where the defense left them and
asked whether to consider them in determining negligence, Judge Wong
declared those statutes off-limits. You brought this appellate reckoning
upon yourselves the moment you placed Exhibit 500 before the jury, and no
