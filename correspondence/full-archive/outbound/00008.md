*CGC-25-631801 (Second Amended Complaint – Equity / Fraud on Court)*

This case is an independent action in equity to set aside the judgment in
CGC-21-594102. We have one cause of action: extrinsic fraud on the court
(CCP § 187; *Kulchar v. Kulchar*).

   -

   *Theory:* The defense verdict was procured by fraud. Defense counsel
   made false or misleading representations to the court about whether they
   had received, possessed, or previously been given critical evidence (the
   911 call recording, body-worn camera footage, and CAD logs). The court
   relied on those representations and made procedural and evidentiary rulings
   that prevented me from fairly presenting that evidence at trial.
   -

   *Three-part mechanism:* (1) The defense had the evidence; prior counsel
   (Chambers / Carbone Smith & Koyama) had it in discovery, and the file was
   transferred to PSA. (2) PSA and attorneys Navaratnasingham and Reyna told
   the court the materials were "news to me" and had not been received until
   the eve of trial. (3) The court relied on those statements and denied
   continuances, excluded or deferred evidence, and limited how I could
   present it. That is process corruption, not simply a discovery dispute.
   -

   *Extrinsic vs. intrinsic fraud:* We allege extrinsic fraud: conduct that
   deprived me of a fair opportunity to present my case. The court’s rulings
   were induced by false or misleading information about receipt and
   possession of evidence. That is different from intrinsic fraud (e.g., trial
   perjury), which the law expects to be exposed at trial.
   -

   *Defendant roles:* PSA, Navaratnasingham, and Reyna are alleged to have
   made the false or misleading representations. Chambers and Carbone Smith &
   Koyama are alleged to have transferred the defense file without adequate
   documentation of what had been produced, which enabled successor counsel to
   make those representations. Abdelhalim and CSAA are alleged as principals
   and beneficiaries of the fraud.
   -

   *Meritorious underlying claim:* The 911 call contains an admission ("I
   barely hit him with anything"), and the bodycam and CAD evidence support
   liability. The jury split 9–3 on the negligence question. We allege that
   but for the fraud, a different outcome was reasonably probable.
   -

   *Relief sought:* Vacatur of the judgment, a retrial on a fair record,
   and ancillary equitable monetary relief tied to the fraud period (e.g.,
   time lost, emotional and physical toll, incremental medical/therapy costs,
   and out-of-pocket costs caused by the process corruption).

*CGC-25-631802 (First Amended Complaint – Fraud / UCL)*

This case is against CSAA only and is based on the February 25, 2021 denial

   burdens to undertake to pursue the claim), and that I was harmed by
   procurement and investigation costs, litigation costs forced by the
   misrepresentation, lost earnings and productive time, and incremental
   physical and psychological harm from the delay. We seek compensatory
   damages (including "fraud-delta" damages) and punitive damages according to
   proof.
   -

   *Second cause of action – UCL (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200):* We allege
   the same conduct as unlawful (common-law fraud). We allege it is fraudulent
   because a reasonable consumer would understand "investigation concluded" to
   mean the insurer had obtained and reviewed foundational liability evidence
   before issuing a categorical denial. We allege it is unfair because issuing
   such denials without that evidence shifts the cost of evidence gathering
   onto the claimant and creates delay and coercive pressure in a
   deny–delay–defend pattern. We allege standing based on lost money or
   property (e.g., out-of-pocket procurement and investigation costs).
   -

   *Narrow theory:* The theory is document-anchored: a pre-litigation
   misrepresentation in a business denial letter, used as institutional
   leverage to discourage or curtail pursuit of a valid claim, with harm
   flowing from that misrepresentation.

*CSAA Risk*

I’m setting out the legal framework more clearly to explain why the chain
of possession, receipt, and disclosure of the 911 recording is the central
risk driver in these cases, and why the defense has no position on that
issue that avoids severe consequences.

The documentary record establishes a provable timeline regarding when the
911 recording was obtained, transmitted, and maintained within the insurer
and defense-counsel chain. That record also establishes that the 911 audio
is material liability evidence. In practice, it is the type of
contemporaneous admission evidence that courts treat as foundational, and
juries treat as decisive. Because of that, the possession, receipt, and
disclosure history is not a peripheral discovery point. It goes to
finality, the integrity of the adjudicative process, and the enforceability
of the existing judgment.

   1.

   *If the defense acknowledges earlier possession, receipt, access, or
   inclusion in the defense file before trial*
   That concession eliminates finality because it confirms that the court’s
   trial-management decisions were made on an inaccurate factual basis
   concerning the defense’s possession and receipt of critical evidence. The
   court issued rulings on continuance, evidentiary sequencing, and the manner
   and timing of presentation while relying on representations about whether
   the defense had the materials, when they were received, and whether they
   were previously in the defense file.

Once earlier possession is confirmed, the elements supporting equitable
relief from the judgment on the ground of extrinsic fraud are satisfied in
the way California courts recognize: the challenged conduct operated to
deprive Plaintiff of a fair adversary hearing and a meaningful opportunity
to present material evidence, and the court’s process was affected by
misinformation bearing directly on the court’s procedural decisions. In
that posture, the appropriate remedy is vacatur of the judgment and retrial
on a fair record. Finality is lost.

Earlier possession also locks in CSAA’s exposure in the denial-letter case.
If foundational liability evidence was obtained or accessible within the
claim-handling period, then the denial letter representation that the
investigation was concluded and liability was resolved is not defensible as
an accurate statement of investigative finality. It becomes an actionable
misrepresentation that drove delay and caused economic and non-economic
harm, with punitive exposure according to proof.

   2.

   *If the defense denies earlier possession and maintains that the 911
   audio was not possessed until the eve of trial*
   That position is contradicted by the objective record, including
   production pathways, file-transfer continuity, and other documentary
   indicia of earlier receipt and control. When a denial conflicts with the
   documentary chain, it does not create a contained factual dispute. It
   creates a credibility collapse that materially worsens the defense posture.

In that posture, the possession issue becomes a court-integrity inquiry. It
triggers heightened exposure for sanctions, fee shifting, adverse
credibility findings, and collateral consequences that follow from
misstatements to the court about control and receipt of central evidence.
It also forces intrusive discovery into file-transfer practices, production
logs, internal coordination between insurer and counsel, and
witness-by-witness consistency. That is not litigation the defense can
control or cabin once the record is fixed.

   3.

   *Why either answer produces catastrophic defense exposure*
   Because the evidence fixes the timeline and because the 911 audio is
   central, there is no answer that preserves the protection of the existing
   judgment.

An admission of earlier possession confirms the factual mechanism that
supports equitable set-aside relief and a retrial, and it intensifies
CSAA’s fraud exposure tied to the denial letter.

A denial of earlier possession is refuted by the documentary record and
creates a worse outcome through credibility and sanctions dynamics, while
still leaving the defense facing the same end-state risk of loss of
finality and a reopened trial on the merits.

For those reasons, the 911 possession and receipt history is the trap door
for the defense. It removes the ability to treat these matters as ordinary
discovery disputes and forces the case into a finality and
process-integrity posture where the downside risk compounds quickly.
