- Although Plaintiff does not assert a private cause of action arising
   directly under the California Rules of Professional Conduct or Business and
   Professions Code section 6068(d), which do not, standing alone, create an
   independent civil claim enforceable by an opposing party (Stanley v.
   Richmond; Mirabito v. Liccardo),
   - *this action arises in equity for vacatur based on extrinsic fraud,
   and in tort for intentional misrepresentation, concealment, and
   constructive fraud under Civil Code sections 1709, 1710(1), 1710(3), and
   1573, as well as for statutory attorney deceit under Business and
   Professions Code section 6128(a).*
   - Specifically, by appearing of record in CGC-21-594102 and making
   affirmative, tribunal-directed representations, in Motions in Limine Nos. 6
   and 17, at the February 18, 2025 hearing, and at trial—that they had “no
   idea where” the silent-witness materials “came from,” Defendants made
   representations of material fact to the court. These statements are
   actionable not as routine litigation advocacy, but as alleged intentional
   or recklessly false representations made to the tribunal, on which the
   court and Plaintiff foreseeably relied.
   - *Liability is predicated on Defendants’ affirmative misrepresentations
   to the court, coupled with resulting reliance and prejudice that deprived
   Plaintiff of a fair adversarial hearing.* Under the framework
   articulated in Kulchar v. Kulchar and In re Marriage of Modnick, such
   conduct constitutes extrinsic fraud where it prevents a party from fully
   and fairly presenting his case.
   - The authorities cited herein, including Shafer v. Berger, Kahn, are
   therefore not invoked as independent sources of liability, but as
   establishing the governing standards of candor and truthfulness against
   which Defendants’ tribunal-directed representations must be evaluated.
   Those standards define the minimum level of honesty required of counsel
   acting as officers of the court.
   - *Liability arises because Defendants affirmatively represented to the
   trial court that they neither possessed nor had knowledge of the
   silent-witness materials when, in fact, they lacked any reasonable basis
   for such assertions, or made them with knowledge of their falsity or
   reckless disregard for the truth. *These misrepresentations foreseeably
   induced reliance by the court and impaired Plaintiff’s ability to litigate
   the matter on a complete and accurate evidentiary record.
   - CSAA Insurance Exchange is additionally liable to the extent those
   representations were made within the scope of counsel’s authority and were
   authorized or ratified by the client. See Civ. Code § 2330; Evid. Code §
   1222; Gulf Ins. Co. v. Berger, Kahn.
