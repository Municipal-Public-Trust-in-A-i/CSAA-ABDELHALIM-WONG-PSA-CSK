> <#m_4416183905336339549_procedural-posture-and-admission-consequence>Procedural
> Posture and Admission Consequence
>
> Defendants have filed an anti-SLAPP motion and a demurrer. By their chosen
> procedural vehicles, they have (a) moved on protected-activity and
> legal-sufficiency grounds, (b) declined to file an answer, and (c) declined
> to put the operative facts of the complaints in issue.
>
> A demurrer admits the truth of every well-pleaded fact for the limited
> purposes of the motion. (*Blank v. Kirwan* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.)
> The anti-SLAPP step-two minimal-merit standard accepts the plaintiff's
> evidence as true and disregards the defendants' contrary evidence. (*Soukup
> v. Law Offices of Herbert Hafif* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 260, 291.) Under *D'Amico
> v. Board of Medical Examiners* (1974) 11 Cal.3d 1, 21–22, sworn
> admissions are binding for these purposes; that authority is invoked here
> as governing the procedural-admission inference, not as a merits assertion.
>
> Therefore, by filing as they did and not answering, defendants have, for
> the limited purposes of these motions, admitted the truth of the
> well-pleaded facts that compose the complaint counts identified above.
> Those admitted facts include, in CGC-25-631801, the documented possession
> and concealment of the 911/BWC/CAD record and the seven consequential
> rulings (801 TAC ¶¶ 3, 5, 9, 48–50); and, in CGC-25-631802, CSAA's
> non-performance of the investigation required by 10 C.C.R. § 2695.7(b) (802
> TAC ¶¶ 1, 4, 7, 8, 46). Plaintiff is identifying that admission consequence
> on the record, not soliciting a stipulation as to merits.
