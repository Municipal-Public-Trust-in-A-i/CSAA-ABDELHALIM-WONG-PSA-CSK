You selected a characterization label for plaintiff's claims. You applied

before those labels could carry any legal weight. Conclusions dressed as
arguments are not a legal defense. They are pleading filler. Filler that is
billable to a sophisticated client does not become doctrinal analysis
merely because a licensed attorney signed it.

Nothing in your pleadings engaged, on the merits, with:

   1. The gravamen of the First Amended Complaint.
   2. The controlling post-*Park* Anti-SLAPP framework.
   3. The controlling *Moradi-Shalal* preservation of common law fraud

   4. The controlling *Zhang* reading of UCL channel separation when the

   5. The *Kimmel v. Goland* distinction between communicative and

   6. The *Baral v. Schnitt* step-two prima facie standard.
   7. The *Crowley v. Katleman* primary rights doctrine.

   9. The *Action Apartment* boundary on the litigation privilege.

   law.

A brief that does not engage these authorities, in a case that depends on
these authorities, is not an opposition to the FAC. It is an invitation for
the Court to bypass California law. The Court will decline that invitation.

<#iii.-the-specific-doctrinal-errors-you-committed>III. THE SPECIFIC
DOCTRINAL ERRORS YOU COMMITTED

Plaintiff sets the errors out below in the same order they appear in the
opposition briefing filed today. Each heading is a separate defect. Each
defect is independently fatal to the pleading in which it appears. The
cumulative picture is considerably worse.
<#a.-you-ignored-park-v.-board-of-trustees-of-california-state-university-(2017)-2-cal.5th-1057.>A.
You ignored *Park v. Board of Trustees of California State University* (2017)
2 Cal.5th 1057.

*Park* is the controlling Supreme Court authority on Anti-SLAPP step-one
gravamen analysis. *Park* does not appear in your Table of Authorities.
*Park* is not cited, distinguished, or acknowledged anywhere in the body of
your special motion memorandum. That omission is not a clerical error. Your
entire step-one argument is grounded in the exact reading of Code of Civil
Procedure section 425.16 that *Park* unanimously and emphatically rejected.
*Park* requires the trial court to isolate the specific act or omission
that supplies the elements of the claim and to decide whether that act, not
adjacent protected activity, is itself protected. Where the
injury-producing act is noncommunicative institutional conduct, the claim
does not arise from protected activity. Your motion treats a later
communicative act (the denial letter) and a later litigation posture (the
pending lawsuit) as if they convert noncommunicative upstream conduct (the
failure to investigate) into protected petitioning. *Park* forbids that
move.

*Consequence:* The step-one analysis in your special motion is
unsalvageable. No reply brief can cure the absence of the controlling
framework. The proper course is withdrawal.
<#b.-you-misused-martinez-v.-metabolife-international%2C-inc.-(2003)-113-cal.app.4th-181.>B.
You misused *Martinez v. Metabolife International, Inc.* (2003) 113
Cal.App.4th 181.

Your citation to *Martinez* for the "principal thrust" test is, with
respect, one of the most self-defeating citations in your motion. The
principal thrust of plaintiff's claims is the institutional failure to
investigate, not the communicative act of issuing a denial letter. That
principal thrust is noncommunicative. *Martinez*, read correctly,
forecloses the motion. Your willingness to cite *Martinez* without
apparently recognizing that it supplies the analytic test that defeats the
motion is a direct measure of the motion's rigor.
<#c.-you-refused-to-draw-the-communicative-versus-conduct-line-that-kimmel-v.-goland-(1990)-51-cal.3d-202-requires.>C.
You refused to draw the communicative-versus-conduct line that *Kimmel v.
Goland* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 202 requires.

*Kimmel* draws a bright line between communicative acts (which may qualify
for litigation privilege under Civil Code section 47(b)) and
noncommunicative institutional conduct (which does not). The failure to
retrieve a 911 recording, CAD logs, or body-worn camera footage is not a
communication. The institutional decision to process a liability claim
through a denial pipeline without an independent investigation is not a
communication. Those are acts and omissions. They are conduct. They fall
categorically outside Civil Code section 47(b) and Code of Civil Procedure
section 425.16. Your briefing attempts to treat the February 25, 2021
denial letter as both the gravamen of the action and as the vehicle for
absolute immunity. The two readings are mutually exclusive. You may not
have it both ways.
<#d.-you-applied-the-litigation-privilege-as-a-blob%2C-not-as-a-doctrine.>D.
You applied the litigation privilege as a blob, not as a doctrine.

Under *Action Apartment Assn., Inc. v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41
Cal.4th 1232, the litigation privilege applies to qualifying
communications; it does not immunize underlying conduct or omissions, and
it does not reach extrinsic fraud that operates before and outside the
proceeding itself. Plaintiff's pleaded theory is textbook extrinsic fraud:
the misrepresentation of investigative completeness occurred in February
2021, in the pre-litigation period, and deprived plaintiff of the ability
to preserve, authenticate, and subpoena foundational evidence at a time
when such steps were still operative. Your pleadings apply section 47(b) as
if it absorbs everything in the temporal vicinity of litigation. California
law has consistently refused that overreach. *Flatley v. Mauro* (2006) 39
Cal.4th 299 further excludes from Anti-SLAPP protection conduct that is
illegal as a matter of law. You cited *Flatley* for policy language on
finality and bypassed the operative exception. That is not careful
briefing. It is selective briefing.
<#e.-you-treated-your-own-labels-as-the-demurrer-record.>E. You treated
your own labels as the demurrer record.

Under *Serrano v. Priest* (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584, on demurrer the trial court
accepts the plaintiff's well-pleaded allegations as true and ignores the
defendant's legal conclusions and characterizations. Your demurrer ran the
reverse operation. You imported your own labels ("improper appeal,"
"collateral attack," "no channel to sue an insurer") and reasoned backward
from those labels to the conclusion that the FAC is facially defective.
That is not a demurrer. It is a rhetorical inversion of the demurrer
standard. It will not survive contact with the opposition briefing
plaintiff filed today.
<#f.-the-%22improper-appeal%22-ground-is-a-category-error.>F. The "improper
appeal" ground is a category error.

Code of Civil Procedure section 904.1 governs appeals. It is a narrow,
procedurally specific vehicle. An original civil action in the Superior
Court alleging independent torts by a different defendant on a different
liability theory and seeking different relief is not an appeal. Your
assertion that plaintiff's FAC is an "appeal" of the underlying negligence
judgment is not just incorrect. It is a category error that no amount of
supplemental briefing can rehabilitate. *Rochin v. Pat Johnson
Manufacturing Co.* (1998) 67 Cal.App.4th 1228, on which you relied, governs
attempts to relitigate or alter a judgment *within the same proceeding*. It
has no application to an independent fraud action filed against a new
defendant (the insurer) on new theories (fraud and UCL) seeking new damages
(the reliance-based economic harm).
<#g.-you-read-moradi-shalal-as-a-bar-when-it-is-a-preservation.>G. You read
*Moradi-Shalal* as a bar when it is a preservation.

*Moradi-Shalal v. Fireman's Fund Ins. Companies* (1988) 46 Cal.3d 287
eliminated private causes of action under the Unfair Insurance Practices
Act, Insurance Code section 790.03. In the same opinion, the Supreme Court
expressly preserved traditional common law remedies, including common law
fraud, against insurers. Your papers cited *Moradi-Shalal* repeatedly
without engaging with that preservation language. The effect of your
selective citation was to transform a preservation into a bar and to
convert a case that *saved* common law fraud claims into a case that
allegedly extinguishes them. The transformation is not supported by the
text, reasoning, or disposition of *Moradi-Shalal*. It is, candidly, the
kind of citation practice that invites a Court of Appeal to remind the bar
that California Rules of Professional Conduct, rule 3.3, prohibits
misstatement of controlling authority.
<#h.-you-read-zhang-v.-superior-court-(2013)-57-cal.4th-364-as-closing-a-channel-it-kept-open.>H.
You read *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364 as closing a
channel it kept open.

*Zhang* confirms that a UCL claim cannot simply repackage a forbidden
Insurance Code section 790.03 cause of action under a different label. That
limitation is real, and plaintiff has honored it explicitly. The Proposed
Second Amended Complaint tethers the UCL "unlawful" prong to common law
fraud under Civil Code sections 1709 and 1710, expressly disclaims any
private enforcement of Insurance Code section 790.03(h), and carries an
affirmative channel-separation disclaimer. *Zhang* simultaneously confirmed
that UCL claims grounded in independently actionable common law fraud
*survive* the channel-separation doctrine. Your motion asked this Court to
close a channel *Moradi-Shalal* and *Zhang* kept expressly open. That is
not a tenable position.
<#i.-you-imported-summary-judgment-evidentiary-burdens-into-anti-slapp-step-two.>I.
You imported summary judgment evidentiary burdens into Anti-SLAPP step two.

*Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376 sets the step-two standard: minimal
merit through a prima facie showing. The trial court does not weigh
evidence, resolve credibility disputes, or apply a preponderance standard
at the pleading stage. Your motion demands trial-level proof of each fraud
element at a stage where California law expressly forbids that demand. The
courts have a term for this: step-two creep. It will not work here. The
prima facie record (the 2021 denial letter representing investigative
completeness; the 2025 Jessup pretrial testimony; the April 16, 2025 trial
admission that materials had been provided by Dolan rather than retrieved
by CSAA; the documented contradiction sequence from 2021 through 2025) is
more than sufficient for minimal merit under *Baral*.
<#j.-your-collateral-estoppel-argument-fails-under-crowley-v.-katleman-(1994)-8-cal.4th-666.>J.
Your collateral estoppel argument fails under *Crowley v. Katleman* (1994)
8 Cal.4th 666.

California's primary rights doctrine requires identity of parties, primary
right, duty, and injury before a prior judgment has preclusive effect on a
subsequent action. The prior negligence action was against the insured
tortfeasor. This action is against the insurer. Different defendants.
Different duties. Different rights. Different injuries. Under *Crowley*,
there is no preclusion. Your resort to 19th-century federal equity
dicta in *United
States v. Throckmorton* (1878) 98 U.S. 61 did not help the position; it
underscored how far from California state law your preclusion argument had
to reach to find any support at all.
<#k.-your-statute-of-limitations-argument-ignored-the-face-of-the-pleading.>K.
Your statute of limitations argument ignored the face of the pleading.

Code of Civil Procedure section 338(d) and *Cansino v. Bank of America* (2014)
224 Cal.App.4th 1462 provide the rule: when the plaintiff pleads fraudulent
concealment, the limitations period does not start to run until the
plaintiff discovered or, with reasonable diligence, could have discovered
the facts underlying the fraud. Plaintiff's Proposed Second Amended
Complaint anchors discovery to January 17, 2025, when defense-side filings
in the underlying action first revealed that the 911 recordings, CAD logs,
and body-worn camera footage were of unknown provenance and had not been
produced by CSAA as its 2021 denial represented. On demurrer, that
allegation is taken as true. Your limitations argument demanded a clock
that never started on the representations you or your client could prove.

RECORD YOUR MOTIONS WILL NOT DISPLACE

Your pleadings worked hard to obscure the factual record. They will not

In February 2021, Franciscus Dylan Rosario, an unrepresented injured

The Jessup pretrial testimony on February 18, 2025 confirmed that the same
materials had been produced in discovery years earlier yet were
simultaneously "news to me" to defense counsel at the pretrial hearing. The
April 16, 2025 trial admission that the materials had been "provided by
Dolan" confirmed that CSAA never retrieved them. This is not a credibility
contest. This is a documented contradiction sequence that runs from 2021
through 2025.

The fraud caused independent economic injury. Plaintiff did not preserve,
authenticate, or subpoena the foundational materials at the pre-litigation
stage because CSAA represented it had already done so. Plaintiff was later

to permit an injured plaintiff to recover.

<#a.-expected-structural-elements-of-a-compliant-california-superior-court-answer>A.
Expected Structural Elements of a Compliant California Superior Court Answer

The Answer must, at a minimum, satisfy the following structural and
substantive elements under California law:

   1. *Caption and Pleading Paper.* Judicial Council caption block, 28
   numbered lines per page, 1 inch top and bottom margins, 1.5 inch left
   margin, 0.5 inch right margin, consistent with California Rules of Court,
   rule 2.104 through rule 2.108.
   2. *Identification of Pleading.* "ANSWER OF DEFENDANT CSAA INSURANCE
   EXCHANGE TO PLAINTIFF'S [FIRST AMENDED] [SECOND AMENDED] COMPLAINT" on the
   first page caption block, with the case number, department, judge, and
   hearing information consistent with Code of Civil Procedure section
   430.41(a)(3).
   3. *Paragraph by Paragraph Responses.* Either a general denial under
   Code of Civil Procedure section 431.30(d), if the complaint is unverified
   and the allegations qualify, or specific paragraph-by-paragraph admissions
   and denials under Code of Civil Procedure section 431.30(b), including
   denials on information and belief where appropriate under section 431.30(e).
   4. *Affirmative Defenses.* Each affirmative defense separately numbered
   and stated with the ultimate facts that support it, consistent with *FPI
   Development, Inc. v. Nakashima* (1991) 231 Cal.App.3d 367 and related
   authority; boilerplate defenses unsupported by pleaded facts are not
   compliant.
   5. *Prayer.* A prayer stating the relief defendant seeks, including
   attorney fees only where a statute, contract, or other legal basis permits
   a fee award and is identified.
   6. *Signature and Verification.* Attorney signature block consistent
   with Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7, verification by a party or
   authorized agent where the operative complaint is verified, consistent with
   Code of Civil Procedure section 446.
   7. *Proof of Service.* Proof of service consistent with Code of Civil
   Procedure section 1013a and Code of Civil Procedure section 1010.6 for
   electronic service, executed by a non-party.

Plaintiff does not intend this list as a brief on California Answer
practice. It is included because your office's filings to date have not
reflected a clean understanding of the distinction between a motion
challenging the pleading and a pleading that actually responds to the
complaint. Plaintiff will oppose any further evasive motion practice as
vigorously and as completely as it opposed the two pending motions.
