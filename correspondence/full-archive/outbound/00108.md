>
> *I. The Second Amended Complaint is Procedurally Proper and Conforms to
> the Evidentiary Record.*

As a threshold matter, the Court has broad discretion to grant leave to
> amend under Code of Civil Procedure sections 473(a)(1) and 576. While the
> defense may argue that *Simmons v. Allstate Ins. Co.* (2001) 92
> Cal.App.4th 1068 generally restricts amendments while an anti-SLAPP motion
> is pending, that rule is not absolute. Under the well-established exception
> in *Nguyen-Lam v. Cao (2009) 171 Cal.App.4th 858, 871*, where a
> plaintiff’s evidentiary showing demonstrates a probability of prevailing on
> the merits, the court may, and should, grant leave to amend to conform the
> pleadings to that evidence.

Plaintiff is not seeking to amend to dodge a valid anti-SLAPP motion; as
> demonstrated by the evidentiary spine already before this Court (Exhibits
> A1–A7 in the Bench Compendium), Plaintiff easily clears the “minimal merit”
> threshold required by *Navellier v. Sletten* (2002) 29 Cal.4th 82 and *Oasis
> West Realty, LLC v. Goldman* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 811. Granting leave to
> file the SAC is the proper procedural mechanism to align the pleadings with
> the irrefutable evidentiary record.



> *II. The Anti-SLAPP Statute Does Not Apply Because the Gravamen of the SAC
> is Unprotected Conduct, Not Petitioning Activity.*

The SAC clarifies precisely why the defense’s anticipated anti-SLAPP motion
> fails at Prong One as a matter of law. The defense attempts to shield its
> liability by pointing to a communicative artifact (a letter) and
> characterizing it as protected petitioning activity.  [a] As the California
> Supreme Court held in *Park v. Board of Trustees (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057,
> 1062–1064*, and reaffirmed in *Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (2021)
> 11 Cal.5th 995, 1010–1012, a claim is only subject to the anti-SLAPP
> statute if the protected communication itself is the injury-producing
> conduct.
> The true gravamen here is the defendant’s action, and lack of action,
> underlying that artifact: specifically, the substantive misrepresentation
> of facts and the failure to act in accordance with legal duties. The letter
> is merely an incidental artifact that transmitted or concealed the true
> tortious conduct.  [b] Following *Baral v. Schnitt (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376,
> 396* regarding “mixed-conduct” pleadings, the SAC surgically isolates the
> unprotected, injury-producing actions. Because the core injury arises from
> unprotected conduct rather than the exercise of free speech or petition
> rights, the anti-SLAPP statute does not apply. Section 425.16(f) preserves
> the defendants’ right to file a renewed special motion after the SAC is
> filed; thus, granting leave does not moot anti-SLAPP relief, it merely
> re-targets it.

*Note to Defense Counsel:*  *Defense will still have an opportunity to file
your special motion to strike within 60 days after leave to amend is
granted, provided the Defense can satisfy the anti-SLAPP gravamen analysis
by proving/identifying plaintiff causes of action fall within the scope of
protected activity. Otherwise, the Court retains the inherent authority to
permit leave to amend any complaint.*
