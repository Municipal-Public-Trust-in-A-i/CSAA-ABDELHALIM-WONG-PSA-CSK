Dear Dept. 301,

After carefully reviewing the Court’s tentative ruling, I am writing to
provide notice of the primary issues I will address during oral argument
tomorrow morning. My focus will be strictly narrowed to clarifying the
record on the classification of pre-litigation communications, the proper
application of the Park standard, and the factual scope of the underlying
adjudication.

*1. Classification of Pre-Litigation Commercial Speech (Item 2)*
I will respectfully challenge the classification of the February 25
communication as protected petitioning activity. The February 25 document
was a pre-litigation commercial communication certifying that a mandatory
investigation duty was completed. Extending anti-SLAPP protection to
routine, pre-litigation business communications carries severe policy
implications: it effectively grants insurers blanket immunity to issue
false representations regarding regulatory compliance, only to later hide
behind the anti-SLAPP statute to evade accountability.

*2. The Gravamen of the Claim and the Park Standard*
It is critical to clarify the timeline and the gravamen of the complaint to
avoid conflating the tort with the evidence of the tort. Plaintiff is not
suing CSAA for filing a document in court. CSAA had a mandatory duty to
investigate, which they certified as complete on February 25. When the
defense later revealed in court that they had not actually performed that
duty, that litigation conduct served simply as proof that the February 25
certification was false. I will direct the Court to Park v. Board of
Trustees of California State University, 2 Cal.5th 1057, 1060 (2017), which
explicitly holds that litigation statements may be used as evidence of a
prior tort; they do not constitute protected speech shielding that prior
tort.

*3. The Scope of the Underlying Adjudication*
Finally, I will address the tentative finding that the underlying case was
"fully adjudicated." I will direct the Court to the specific and actual
record from Judge Garrett Wong, who explicitly prohibited the jury from
adjudicating the legal issues in question.

Thank you for your time and attention to these matters.

Respectfully,
