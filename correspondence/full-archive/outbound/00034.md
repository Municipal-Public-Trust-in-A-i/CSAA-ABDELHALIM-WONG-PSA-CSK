Why the Defense Approach Is Weak

The weakness is not subtle. It is structural. The defense position suffers
from at least five fundamental deficiencies:

*1. Doctrinal Flattening / Failure to Engage the Actual Cause of Action*

The most glaring problem is that defense counsel *never acknowledges what
the complaints actually allege*. In the 801 letter, the complaint is an
independent action in equity for extrinsic fraud -- a recognized California
cause of action under *Kulchar v. Kulchar* (1969) 1 Cal.3d 467, *Olivera v.
Grace* (1942) 19 Cal.2d 570, and *Aldrich v. San Fernando Valley
Lumber Co.* (1985)
170 Cal.App.3d 725. The defense letter does not cite a single one of these
cases. It does not even mention the term "extrinsic fraud" in any
substantive analytical way. It simply relabels the equity action as an
"appeal" and argues the superior court cannot hear it.

This is misdirection by characterization. If you never name the doctrine
you are actually facing, you never have to explain why your defenses
overcome it.

*2. The Litigation Privilege Argument Ignores Controlling Exceptions*

The defense's heaviest weapon is the threat of anti-SLAPP and the
litigation privilege. But this argument has a fatal gap: *Rusheen v.
Cohen* (2006)
37 Cal.4th 1048, 1065 holds explicitly that the litigation privilege does
not apply to claims of fraud upon the court. *Finton Construction, Inc. v.
Bidna & Keys, APLC* (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 200, 213 holds that the gravamen
of a fraud-on-the-court claim is the fraud, not the exercise of the right
to petition.

The defense letter cites neither case. That is not an oversight -- those
cases are directly on point and well-known. The omission suggests counsel
either (a) knows the cases undermine the position and hopes the pro per
plaintiff will not find them, or (b) has not researched the issue with
sufficient care. Either way, it is a weakness.

*3. The Statute of Limitations / Section 473(b) Argument Applies the Wrong
Remedy*

For 801, the defense asserts that the "proper means" was a CCP sec. 473(b)
motion within six months. But an independent action in equity for extrinsic
fraud is a *separate* recognized remedy governed by equitable diligence,
not the six-month statutory cap. *Kulchar* is explicit on this.
*Aldrich* confirms
the timeliness standard is equitable reasonableness, not statutory. The
defense's argument would reduce the entire doctrine of extrinsic fraud to a
dead letter -- if every such claim had to go through 473(b), then the
independent equitable action recognized by the California Supreme Court
would have no function.

For 802, the defense asserts the three-year fraud and four-year UCL
statutes bar the claims but ignores the discovery rule allegations and the
delayed-discovery doctrine, which tolls the limitations period until the
plaintiff discovered or reasonably should have discovered the fraud.

*4. The Standing Argument Confuses Two Different Doctrines*

Citing *Shaolian v. Safeco Ins. Co.* (1999) 71 Cal.App.4th 268, the defense
argues the plaintiff has no standing because he is a third party to the
insurance relationship. But *Shaolian* addresses when a third-party
claimant can sue an insurer for insurance benefits. That is not what is
alleged. The injury here is the procurement of a judgment through fraud
upon the court. Standing derives from being the party deprived of a fair
hearing -- not from contractual privity with the insurer. The argument
answers the wrong question entirely.

*5. The "Another Action Pending" and Jurisdiction Arguments Rest on a False
Equivalence*

The defense claims the superior court has no jurisdiction because the
remedy is appeal (CCP sec. 904.1), and that the pending appeal bars this
action under CCP sec. 430.10(b). Both arguments require treating the equity
action as identical to an appeal. They are not the same thing. An appeal
asks a reviewing court whether the trial court committed reversible error.
An equity action asks the original superior court whether the judgment was
procured by extrinsic fraud. Different legal predicates, different
elements, different remedies. The defense never explains how they are
identical because they are not.

<#the-financial-intimidation-component>The Financial Intimidation Component

Both letters end with the same threat: withdraw your pleadings or face
attorneys' fees under the anti-SLAPP statute. This is a textbook pressure
tactic against a pro per plaintiff. The defense is leveraging the asymmetry

But anti-SLAPP fee-shifting is not automatic. Fees go to a *prevailing* movant.
If the anti-SLAPP motion fails -- as it should under *Finton* and
*Rusheen* when
the gravamen is fraud on the court -- no fees are awarded. Moreover, CCP

that ignores *Rusheen*, *Finton*, and the extrinsic-fraud line of authority
invites that inquiry.

The financial threat is therefore not a substantive legal argument. It is a
deterrent aimed at a pro per litigant, designed to exploit the fear of
financial exposure rather than address the merits.

*If the defense files and loses the anti-SLAPP motion:*

   *Plaintiff may recover attorneys' fees and costs.* CCP sec. 425.16(c)(1)

   that ignores *Rusheen v. Cohen* (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048, 1065 (litigation
   privilege does not apply to fraud on the court) and *Finton
   Construction, Inc. v. Bidna & Keys, APLC* (2015) 238 Cal.App.4th 200,

   merits.* A denied anti-SLAPP motion means the court has already found,
   at minimum, that the plaintiff showed a probability of prevailing (step
   two). That is an early judicial signal that the claim has merit. It does
   not decide the case, but it establishes a record-level credibility marker
   that the defense would prefer not to create.
   -

   *The motion triggers discovery consequences.* CCP sec. 425.16(g)
   provides that discovery is stayed while an anti-SLAPP motion is pending
   unless the court orders otherwise on noticed motion and a showing of good
   cause. If the anti-SLAPP motion is denied, the discovery stay lifts and
   Plaintiff proceeds to discovery -- the very thing the defense wants to
   avoid. The "Sure. They were provided by Dolan" contradiction becomes
   subject to formal deposition, document production, and interrogatory
   scrutiny. The defense has every incentive to delay that moment, and a
   failed anti-SLAPP accelerates it.
   -

   *The motion alerts the court to the defense's avoidance posture.* Judges
   are sophisticated consumers of motion practice. An anti-SLAPP motion that
   treats a recognized *Kulchar* equity action as a prohibited "appeal,"
   omits the Supreme Court authority, and relies on an intimidation-style fee
   threat tells the court something about how the defense views the strength
   of its own position. That impression persists through later stages of the
   case.

*Net consequence:* The anti-SLAPP motion is the single most dangerous move
for the defense. If it fails, the defense faces fee exposure, a judicial
finding of minimal merit on Plaintiff's side, the lifting of any discovery
stay, and a court that has now been educated on *Rusheen*, *Finton*, and
the extrinsic fraud line.

<#2.-demurrer-(ccp-sec.-430.10)----gives-the-court-a-chance-to-validate-the-theory>2.
Demurrer (CCP sec. 430.10) -- Gives the Court a Chance to Validate the
Theory

*If the demurrer is overruled:*

   -

   *The cause of action is confirmed as legally cognizable.* An overruled
   demurrer means the court has determined that the complaint, taken as true,
   states a viable claim for relief. For the 801 case, that means the court
   has recognized that an independent action in equity for extrinsic fraud is
   properly before it. For the 802 case, that means the court has recognized
   that the pre-litigation denial-letter misrepresentation theory states a
   cause of action for fraud and UCL violations. That is a significant early
   win because the defense's entire strategy depends on preventing the court
   from ever reaching the merits.
   -

   *The defense is forced to answer.* An overruled demurrer requires the
   defendant to file an answer. Once an answer is filed, the case enters
   discovery. The defense must respond to interrogatories, produce documents,
   sit for depositions, and defend their factual positions under oath. The
   central contradiction -- prior representations of "unknown provenance"
   versus "Sure. They were provided by Dolan" -- becomes the subject of formal
   record development. That is exactly the outcome the defense is trying to
   prevent.
   -

   *Leave to amend further strengthens the pleading.* Even if the demurrer
   is sustained in part, the standard remedy in California is leave to
amend. (*Blank
   v. Kirwan* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318; *Aubry v. Tri-City Hospital
Dist.* (1992)
   2 Cal.4th 962, 970.) You have already prepared proposed Second Amended
   Complaints for both 801 and 802 that narrow and sharpen the theories. A
   demurrer sustained with leave to amend lets you file those improved
   pleadings, which address every objection the defense has raised. The
   defense would then have to demur again to the new complaint, and its
   arguments would be weaker the second time because the amended complaint
   would have cured the very issues they identified.
   -

   *Demurrer sustained without leave to amend is extremely unlikely.*
California
   courts almost never deny leave to amend on a first or second demurrer
   unless it is clear that no amendment could cure the defect. (*Goodman v.
   Kennedy* (1976) 18 Cal.3d 335, 349.) The *Kulchar* doctrine is
   established. The record contradiction is specific. The proposed amendments
   are already drafted. There is no basis for concluding that amendment would
   be futile.

*Net consequence:* The demurrer either fails outright (validating the
claim) or results in leave to amend (letting you file a stronger pleading).
In neither scenario does the defense eliminate the case.

   *No fee consequence to the defense directly, but wasted judicial
   goodwill.* Unlike anti-SLAPP, there is no statutory fee-shifting on a
   failed motion to strike. But filing a motion to strike alongside a demurrer
   and an anti-SLAPP motion creates the appearance of motion practice by
   volume rather than by precision. Courts notice that.

*Net consequence:* The motion to strike is the lowest-risk move for the
defense, but it also accomplishes the least and reveals the most about
their defensive priorities.
------------------------------
<#4.-cumulative-strategic-consequences-of-filing-all-three>4. Cumulative
Strategic Consequences of Filing All Three

The defense has threatened a combined package: demurrer, motion to strike,
and anti-SLAPP. Filing all three simultaneously creates several compounding
risks:

   -

   *Inconsistent positions.* A demurrer argues the complaint fails to state
   facts sufficient to constitute a cause of action. An anti-SLAPP motion
   argues the complaint arises from protected activity and the plaintiff
   cannot show a probability of prevailing. A motion to strike argues specific
   allegations are irrelevant or improper. These positions are not inherently
   contradictory, but the anti-SLAPP analysis at step two requires the court
   to evaluate whether the plaintiff has minimal merit -- which directly
   undercuts the demurrer's premise that the complaint fails as a matter of
   law. If the court evaluates step two and finds minimal merit, the defense
   has inadvertently strengthened the plaintiff's position against the
   demurrer.
   -

   *Judicial education.* The combined motion practice forces the court to
   engage deeply with *Kulchar*, *Olivera*, *Aldrich*, *Baltins*, *Rusheen*
   , *Finton*, *Lazar*, and the rest of the authorities. The defense
   letters conspicuously omit these cases. Once the opposition brief puts them
   before the court, the judge will notice the omission. That notice carries
   over to every subsequent stage of the case.
   -

   *Cost and fee exposure compounds.* If the anti-SLAPP fails and the court
   finds it frivolous, the fee award could be measured not just against the
   anti-SLAPP motion itself but against the overall motion practice posture. A
   defense that files three simultaneous motions, omits controlling authority,
   and leads with financial threats to a pro per plaintiff invites the court's
   close attention to whether the motion practice was undertaken in good faith.
   -

   *The threshold motion (801) preempts the defense narrative.* Your 801
   filing asks the court to confirm the operative pleading and clarify that
   the case is an independent equity action, not an appeal. If the court
   grants that threshold order before or contemporaneously with the defense's
   motion package, the defense's entire "this is an appeal" narrative
   collapses before the demurrer hearing even begins. The demurrer then has to
   be rewritten to address the actual equitable theory, which the defense has
   so far refused to engage with.
   -

   *The 802 amendment motion further weakens the defense.* Your 802 filing
   seeks leave to file the proposed SAC that narrows the gravamen to the
   pre-litigation denial-letter misrepresentation. If leave is granted, the
   defense's anti-SLAPP argument (that the case arises from protected
   litigation activity) collapses because the amended complaint explicitly
   designates the denial letter -- a pre-litigation business communication --
   as the operative wrong. Under *Park v. Board of Trustees of California
   State University* (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057, 1062-1063, the gravamen
   analysis then points to pre-litigation conduct, not petitioning.

<#5.-the-deeper-consequence%3A-discovery>5. The Deeper Consequence:
Discovery

The real consequence the defense is trying to avoid is not the motion
outcome itself. It is what happens after the motions fail: *discovery*.

Once the case survives threshold motion practice, Plaintiff is entitled to:

   - *Depose defense counsel* about the representations made to the court
   concerning possession, provenance, and prior production of the challenged
   evidence.
   - *Depose Jeremy Jessup* about the August-September 2022 production and
   deposition use of the materials.
   - *Obtain the CSAA claims file* showing when the insurer had access to
   or knowledge of the 911 calls, bodycam footage, and CAD logs.
   - *Obtain the Carbone/Chambers defense file* to trace the chain of
   custody from prior counsel through substitution.
   - *Subpoena the "Dolan" production records* to establish when and how
   the materials were transferred.
   - *Obtain internal CSAA communications* about the denial letter's
   investigative basis (or lack thereof).

Every one of those discovery items bears directly on the central
contradiction. The defense knows this. That is why the motion practice is
front-loaded with threats: the objective is to kill the case before it
reaches discovery, because discovery is where the record-based
contradiction becomes provable with documents under oath rather than merely
alleged in a complaint.
