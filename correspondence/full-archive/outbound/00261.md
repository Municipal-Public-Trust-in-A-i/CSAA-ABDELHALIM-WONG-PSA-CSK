White v. Western Title Ins. Co. (1985) 40 Cal.3d 870, 885-886 matters to
CSAA because California law does not wall off your claim handling from your
litigation conduct. Under White, CSAA’s duty of good faith and fair dealing
continues after suit is filed. Post-filing litigation conduct may be
considered as evidence of CSAA’s earlier claim-handling intent and
objectives. That means the Court may treat what CSAA and its agents did
after litigation began as proof of whether CSAA was investigating fairly,
or whether CSAA had already moved into deny-delay-defend mode.


For CSAA, that collapses the separation you want between the February 2021
certified investigation and everything that followed. The empty-file
certification, the immediate adversarial posture, the failure to obtain
foundational evidence including the 911 audio, and the later campaign to
block merits adjudication are one continuum. Under White, that continuum
can be used against CSAA as evidence of litigation objectives and
claim-handling avoidance.

CSAA’s section 391 strategy seeks to keep the empty investigation file from
being tested, White tells the Court those facts are not insulated as
harmless “litigation strategy.” They are evidence of CSAA’s position on the
empty file itself. That is why 885-886 is not academic. It puts CSAA’s
post-filing course of conduct directly into the same evidentiary frame as
the certified investigation CSAA still has not defended on the merits.

or simply overconfident. Either way, the record reads the same.*

moved straight into an adversarial litigation posture without notifying the
claimant. CSAA’s own anti-SLAPP papers tell the Court it went adversarial
immediately, without collecting the insured’s material admission that would
have signaled liability. The empty file and the missing 911 audio are not a
paperwork quibble. They are the proof of the avoidance.

>>>> amended complaints, opposing motions, and asserting claims that arise when
>>>> they become legally ripe are ordinary litigation activities and are not,
>>>> standing alone, evidence of vexatious conduct.
>>>>

>>>> elected to file numerous motions attacking amended pleadings. My responsive
>>>> filings were made in direct response to those motions and were required to

>>>> evidence of vexatiousness is unsupported by the statute and contrary to the
>>>> purpose of the vexatious litigant laws.

>>>> statutory and common-law duties. They do not arise merely because CSAA
>>>> insured another party. Those claims concern CSAA's own conduct, its
>>>> investigation, its claims handling, and its compliance with California law.

>>>> litigation against a different defendant misstates both the facts and the
>>>> applicable law.
