Appellate Argument: Questions Presented and Summaries

Case Identifiers:

Appellate Record Origin: Superior Court of San Francisco, Dept 301 and 302 (Hon. Christine Van Aken)
Current Actions: CGC-25-631801 ("Case 801") and CGC-25-631802 ("Case 802") [Transaction: 79865405]
Underlying Action: CGC-21-594102

I. Abuse of Discretion Regarding Evidentiary Foundation (Prong 1)
Question Presented: Did the trial court abuse its discretion by relying on defense declarations to satisfy the defendant's threshold burden under Code of Civil Procedure § 425.16 (Prong 1), when those declarations were concurrently contradicted by the defense's own on-the-record admissions regarding the fabricated provenance of critical evidence?

Argument Summary: The defense bears the initial burden to prove their conduct arises from protected petitioning activity via admissible evidence. The trial court's tentative findings (see Proposed Order, Eighth Affirmative Defense: "The Provenance Paradox") explicitly acknowledge that the defense claimed the 911 audio was of "unknown provenance" during the underlying trial (CGC-21-594102), while concurrently admitting on Day 7 it was "provided by Dolan." Furthermore, the defense's own sworn declarations filed in support of their Anti-SLAPP motion (e.g., Decl. of Tyler J. O'Connell, filed April 23/24, 2026 [Transactions: 79212258 and 79304162]) are tainted by these admitted, concurrent contradictions, lacking foundation and credibility as a matter of law. By relying on fundamentally compromised evidence to grant the Anti-SLAPP, the trial court committed a reversible abuse of discretion.

II. Equitable Estoppel and Spoliation (Prong 2)
Question Presented: Is a defendant equitably estopped from claiming a plaintiff lacks a probability of prevailing on the merits (Prong 2) when the defendant concurrently admits to anticipating litigation—triggering a duty to preserve—yet intentionally failed to preserve or obtain the very foundational evidence required by the plaintiff?

Argument Summary: The trial court's findings (see Proposed Order, Ninth Affirmative Defense) establish that the defense anticipated litigation as of February 25, 2021, yet failed to preserve the 911 audio. Concurrently, the court found (Sixth Affirmative Defense) the defense concluded their investigation without this foundational data, a position advanced in their own moving papers (DEF802 Anti-SLAPP MPA, filed 04/09/2026 [Transaction: 79007560]). A party cannot intentionally spoliate foundational evidence and then use an Anti-SLAPP motion to demand the plaintiff meet an evidentiary burden they actively sabotaged. The trial court erred in allowing the defense to weaponize their own spoliation to secure an early dismissal.

III. The Inherent Authority to Sanction Fraud
Question Presented: Did the trial court err in failing to exercise its inherent authority to issue terminating sanctions against the defense for documented and admitted fraud on the court, thereby improperly allowing a fundamentally tainted Anti-SLAPP motion to proceed to judgment?

Argument Summary: The record conclusively demonstrates a calculated deception regarding the provenance and possession of the 911 audio and CAD data. The defense lied to the court in specific, identifiable pleadings:

  1.  The "Unknown Provenance" Deception: In the underlying trial, the defense filed Motion in Limine No. 6 (Transaction 80936030) and Motion in Limine No. 17 (Transaction 80936053), affirmatively stating they did not have the investigation and silent witness materials. This was directly contradicted by their own admission on the record on April 16, 2025 (Day 7 of trial), that the materials were "provided by Dolan."
  2.  The "Concluded Investigation" Fabrication: The defense's original February 25, 2021 Denial Letter (Pre-suit letter; filed as an exhibit, e.g., Ex. F in Transaction ID 79334688, and Ex. 2 in Defense Transaction ID 79007560) claimed the investigation was "concluded"—an impossibility given the intentional omission of the CAD data and 911 audio.
  3.  The Current Anti-SLAPP Fraud: In the current litigation, the defense's Anti-SLAPP moving papers (Transactions: 79007825 and 79007560) and the Porter Scott Demurrer filings (Transactions: 79038715 and 79038567) perpetuate this fraud by falsely claiming the defense performed their statutory duties.
Rather than address this established fraud via the plaintiff's Motion for Terminating Sanctions (Motions for Sanctions, filed 04/23/2026), the trial court finalized the Anti-SLAPP order following the June 24, 2026 hearing in Dept 301. The primary reversible error was the trial court's refusal to police its own jurisdiction. Had the court properly struck the defense's pleadings as a sanction for admitted deceit, the Anti-SLAPP motion would have been rendered moot.

IV. Misapplication of the Litigation Privilege (Civ. Code § 47)
Question Presented: Did the trial court err as a matter of law by applying the absolute litigation privilege (Civil Code § 47) to immunize the defense's non-communicative, tortious conduct (spoliation and concealment of evidence)?

Argument Summary: While communicative acts (such as issuing a denial letter) may fall under the litigation privilege, non-communicative conduct does not. The trial court conflated the two (see Proposed Order, Tenth Affirmative Defense), accepting arguments advanced in the defense's motion (DEF801/802 Anti-SLAPP MPAs, filed 04/09/2026 [Transactions: 79007825 and 79007560]). The defense's physical, intentional act of failing to gather, preserve, or produce the 911 audio/CAD data is non-communicative conduct. The litigation privilege cannot be expanded to shield the physical destruction or intentional concealment of evidence from liability under Rusheen v. Cohen (2006) 37 Cal.4th 1048.

V. Abuse of Discretion Regarding Judicial Notice
Question Presented: Did the trial court abuse its discretion by refusing to take mandatory judicial notice of the defense's specific, on-the-record admissions from the underlying trial transcript, thereby artificially blinding the court to conclusive proof of the defense's fraud?

Argument Summary: The definitive proof of the defense's perjury resides in the official reporter's transcript of the underlying trial (CGC-21-594102). By refusing to properly bridge this transcript into the current record via Plaintiff's Requests for Judicial Notice (filed 04/16/2026 and 04/21/2026), the trial court improperly shielded the defense's misconduct from review. An Anti-SLAPP ruling procured by a court artificially blinding itself to its own historical records cannot stand.

VI. Extrinsic Fraud Voiding Collateral Estoppel
Question Presented: Is the doctrine of collateral estoppel (res judicata) voided by extrinsic fraud when the defense engineered the underlying judgment by actively concealing foundational evidence, thereby preventing a fair adversarial hearing on the merits?

Argument Summary: The defense relies on the underlying trial verdict to claim the issues are "fully litigated" and "fully adjudicated" (DEF801 Demurrer MPA, filed 04/13/2026 [Transaction: 79038715], and Order of Hon. Christine Van Aken, filed May 12, 2026). However, collateral estoppel does not apply to judgments procured by extrinsic fraud. By concealing the 911 audio and CAD data, the defense kept the plaintiff in ignorance and prevented them from fully presenting their case in the underlying trial (CGC-21-594102). Because the underlying judgment is tainted by extrinsic fraud, it is void of any preclusive effect against the current claims.

VII. Reversible Procedural and Sequencing Errors
Question Presented: Did the trial court commit reversible procedural error by adjudicating a dispositive Anti-SLAPP motion while simultaneously adopting an improper denial of Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to Amend on procedural grounds, effectively locking the pleadings to artificially advantage the defense?

Argument Summary: The trial court permitted a structural sequencing error that prejudiced the Plaintiff. On April 30, 2026, the court denied Plaintiff’s Motion for Leave to File a Second Amended Complaint on purely procedural grounds (page limits), despite the fact that a plaintiff generally retains the right to cure defects when an Anti-SLAPP motion targets an initial pleading (see JKC3H8 v. Colton (2013) 221 Cal.App.4th 468, distinguishing the standard amendment freeze). The trial court then adopted the tentative ruling regarding the pleadings despite an on-the-record objection by the Plaintiff. By freezing the pleadings procedurally and subsequently evaluating the Anti-SLAPP motion on a record the plaintiff was barred from curing, the trial court bypassed the policy favoring resolution on the merits and artificially engineered a dispositive defense victory.

VIII. Direct Violation of Supreme Court Black Letter Law (Park and § 425.17)
Question Presented: Did the trial court violate California Supreme Court precedent and statutory exemptions by granting Anti-SLAPP protection to an ordinary commercial insurance claims denial?

Argument Summary: The trial court fundamentally misapplied the foundational rule of Park v. Bd. of Trustees (2017) 2 Cal.5th 1057, which holds that a claim may be struck under the Anti-SLAPP statute only if the protected speech or petitioning activity itself is the wrong complained of, rather than merely evidence of liability. The core wrong in this action is the issuance of the February 25, 2021 Denial Letter to an unrepresented third-party claimant, which falsely asserted the investigation was "concluded."

This act is highly regulated commercial claims-handling behavior under Insurance Code § 790.03, not a "communication intimately intertwined with" protected litigation just because the consumer later filed suit. As such, the conduct is entirely exempt from Anti-SLAPP protection under the commercial speech exemption in Code of Civil Procedure § 425.17(c). By allowing defense litigation-conduct framing to swallow an unprotected commercial transaction, the trial court ruled in direct defiance of both the Legislature’s explicit statutory carve-out and binding Supreme Court authority.
