  1.  Service on a corporation is not limited to the registered agent.
Your email suggests service is improper because it was not made on CSAA’s “proper agent for service of process.” Code of Civil Procedure section 416.10 authorizes service on a corporation by delivering the summons and complaint to, among others, the designated agent for service of process or specified corporate officers (and other persons identified in the statute). (CCP § 416.10.) Service is therefore not categorically defective solely because it was not made on the registered agent.
  2.  A motion to quash addresses service of summons, not “quashing the case.”
A motion under Code of Civil Procedure section 418.10 is a motion to quash service of summons (i.e., it challenges the sufficiency of service/jurisdictional service). (CCP § 418.10.) If service is found defective, the procedural result is that service is set aside and Plaintiff completes proper service; the action is not “quashed” on that basis.
  3.  Amended complaints have their own service/response clock.
The operative pleadings are the amended complaints filed February 25, 2026. When a complaint is amended, the amended complaint must be served on affected defendants, and the responsive pleading deadline runs from service of the amended pleading. (CCP § 471.5(a).) Accordingly, regardless of any dispute about prior service attempts, I will serve the operative amended complaints so there is no dispute as to the response clock.
  4.  Time to complete service / effect of a service dispute.
California provides three years after commencement to serve the summons and complaint. (CCP § 583.210(a).) A dispute about a particular service attempt does not terminate the action; it means service must be completed, and the response deadline is then calculated from valid service.
  5.  Service on counsel / POS-015 / electronic service.
Your request to serve amended pleadings on your office by email with a Notice and Acknowledgment of Receipt (POS-015) is not a substitute for statutorily authorized service of summons/process unless your client (or authorized counsel) agrees in writing to accept service of process on CSAA’s behalf.
