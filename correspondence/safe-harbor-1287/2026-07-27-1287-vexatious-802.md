## III. WHY THE SECTION 391 MOTION IS SUBJECT TO SAFE-HARBOR CHALLENGE

Without restating the full opposition, the Section 391 Motion is challenged as unwarranted under existing law and as presented for an improper purpose, including the following:

**A. Code of Civil Procedure section 391(b)(2) fails.** The personal-injury primary right ran against Subhi Abdelhalim, not CSAA. The primary rights in CGC-25-631802 concern carrier claims-handling (empty-file certification / concealment). Wrong primary right, wrong defendant, incomplete finality (appeal A173827; July 1, 2026 anti-SLAPP order is not claim-by-claim adjudication of empty-file institutional-omission theories). (*Crowley v. Katleman* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 666; *Mycogen Corp. v. Monsanto Co.* (2002) 28 Cal.4th 888; *Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376; *Bonni v. St. Joseph Health System* (2021) 11 Cal.5th 995.)

**B. Code of Civil Procedure section 391(b)(3) fails.** Docket and email volume are not *Morton* flagrant abuse. (*Morton v. Wagner* (2007) 156 Cal.App.4th 963, 971-972.) Much of the paper volume was created by Defendant's own multi-motion stack (anti-SLAPP, demurrer, Code of Civil Procedure section 436 strike, oppositions to leave) and Plaintiff's obligated responses.

**C. Code of Civil Procedure section 391.3 security fails.** Security requires both a vexatious finding and no reasonable probability of prevailing. (*Shalant v. Girardi* (2011) 51 Cal.4th 1164.) July 1, 2026 did not extinguish unadjudicated empty-file primary rights. Plaintiff placed Defendant on notice of that separation by letter dated July 6, 2026.

**D. The proposed order is overbroad under Code of Civil Procedure section 391.7.** Blank statewide bond and universal pre-service screening cannot be smuggled into a case-specific security motion. (*Shalant*, *supra*.)

**E. Facial candor defects** in the moving papers include, as detailed in Plaintiff's opposition: mis-citation of the July 1 order by Request for Judicial Notice exhibit letter; misstatement of the governing statutory subdivision for the vexatious definition; and notice/relief mismatch between the Notice of Motion and the extreme proposed order.

**F. Continuum evidence** (Plaintiff Exhibits F-N), including the April 30, 2026 express threat to seek vexatious-litigant designation (Exhibit K), shows the July 21 filing as the endgame of an access-blocking campaign, not a sudden discovery of statutory predicates.
