## I. PURPOSE AND AUTHORITY

This letter constitutes notice under Code of Civil Procedure section 128.7(c)(1) that Plaintiff Franciscus Dylan Rosario has identified specific factual contentions lacking evidentiary support and legal contentions not warranted by existing law, or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law, in Defendant CSAA Insurance Exchange's Anti-SLAPP Reply filed May 6, 2026 in CGC-25-631802.

CCP section 128.7(b) provides that by presenting a pleading, motion, or other paper to the Court, an attorney or unrepresented party certifies to the best of the person's knowledge, information, and belief, formed after an inquiry reasonable under the circumstances, that: (1) the paper is not being presented primarily for an improper purpose; (2) the claims, defenses, and other legal contentions therein are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law; and (3) the factual contentions have evidentiary support or, if specifically so identified, are likely to have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery.

CCP section 128.7(c)(1) requires that a motion for sanctions under section 128.7 "shall be served as provided in Section 1010, but shall not be filed with or presented to the court unless, within 21 days after service of the motion, or any other period as the court may prescribe, the challenged paper, claim, defense, contention, allegation, or denial is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected."

This letter is the section 128.7(c)(1) safe-harbor notice. It is served on defense counsel; it is not filed with the Court at this time.

## II. IDENTIFIED CONTENTIONS LACKING EVIDENTIARY SUPPORT OR LEGAL WARRANT

The following contentions in Defendant's Anti-SLAPP Reply (filed May 6, 2026) are identified as lacking evidentiary support or as not warranted by existing law or a nonfrivolous argument for its extension, modification, or reversal:

### A. STANDING ARGUMENT: IGNORING BUS. AND PROF. CODE SECTION 17204 AND *ZHANG V. SUPERIOR COURT*

Defendant's Reply asserts or implies that Plaintiff lacks standing to bring the UCL cause of action. This contention ignores the plain text of Business and Professions Code section 17204, which confers standing on "any person who has suffered injury in fact and has lost money or property as a result of the unfair competition." It further ignores *Zhang v. Superior Court* (2013) 57 Cal.4th 364, which expressly holds that Insurance Code section 790.03(h) violations are available as predicates for a UCL section 17200 claim. Plaintiff has pleaded injury in fact (loss of insurance policy benefits through premature denial; compelled litigation costs) and has standing under section 17204. The standing challenge in the Reply lacks evidentiary support and is not warranted by existing law.

### B. FEE PRAYER OF $10,218.00 FIRST RAISED ON REPLY

Defendant's Reply introduces for the first time a fee prayer of $10,218.00 in connection with the anti-SLAPP motion. This fee prayer was not included in the original moving papers filed April 9, 2026 (seqs 015 to 018). Raising a specific monetary demand for the first time on Reply violates CCP section 1005(b), which requires that the notice of motion state the nature of the order being sought; the opposing party is entitled to notice of the relief sought in the moving papers so that the opposition can address it. The fee prayer raised on Reply deprives Plaintiff of the opportunity to oppose it in the ordinary course. This contention is not warranted by existing law and is procedurally improper.

### C. CHARACTERIZATION OF FAC PARAGRAPH 22F AS GRAVAMEN

Defendant's Reply characterizes FAC paragraph 22F as the "gravamen" of Plaintiff's action. This characterization misapplies *Baral v. Schnitt* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 376. Under *Baral*, the anti-SLAPP analysis requires the court to determine whether any particular claim (or claim within a mixed cause of action) arises from protected activity. *Baral* does not authorize a party to select a single paragraph from a multi-paragraph cause of action and declare it the "gravamen" of the entire action while ignoring the remaining allegations. The FAC pleads insurer conduct spanning from the February 2021 claim denial through the present, with multiple independent bases for each cause of action. The characterization of paragraph 22F as the gravamen is a factual contention lacking evidentiary support and a legal contention not warranted by *Baral* or any other existing authority.
