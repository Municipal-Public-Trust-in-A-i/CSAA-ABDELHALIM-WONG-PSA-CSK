## II. Section 128.7 Standards

Section 128.7(b) provides that, by presenting a paper to the court, an attorney certifies that:

(1) the paper is not being presented for any improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in cost;
(2) the claims, defenses, and other legal contentions therein are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law;
(3) the allegations and other factual contentions have evidentiary support or, if specifically so identified, are likely to have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery; and
(4) the denials of factual contentions are warranted on the evidence or, if specifically so identified, are reasonably based on a lack of information or belief.

A certification under (b) is judged by an objective standard of "reasonableness under the circumstances" at the time of presentation. (*Bockrath v. Aldrich Chemical Co.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 71, 82; *Guillemin v. Stein* (2002) 104 Cal.App.4th 156, 166-167.)

The 21-day safe-harbor procedure under section 128.7(c)(1) is jurisdictional. (*Martorana v. Marlin & Saltzman* (2009) 175 Cal.App.4th 685, 698-699; *Galleria Plus, Inc. v. Hanmi Bank* (2009) 179 Cal.App.4th 535, 537-538.)

## III. Specific Bases for Withdrawal

### A. April 9, 2026 Anti-SLAPP Moving Papers (CGC-25-631801, register Nos. 017-020) Are Frivolous as to Step One

The moving papers contend that Plaintiff's independent equity action to vacate a judgment for extrinsic fraud "arises from" protected petitioning. Under controlling Supreme Court authority, an action's gravamen, not its evidentiary backdrop, governs step one. (*Park*, supra, 2 Cal.5th at 1062-1063.) An independent equity action does not arise from protected activity. (*Finton Construction*, supra, 238 Cal.App.4th at 211-213.) A position contrary to *Park* and *Finton Construction*, advanced without acknowledgment of those authorities, is objectively unreasonable under section 128.7(b)(2).

### B. May 5, 2026 Anti-SLAPP Reply (CGC-25-631801), Section II Is Improper-Purpose Procedural Weaponization

Section II of the Reply characterizes Plaintiff's opposition papers as "forty (40) pages" of "noncompliant" filings and seeks sanctions on a CRC 3.1113(d) basis without addressing CRC 3.1113(e) (over-length on good cause), CRC 3.1113(j) (incorporation by reference), or Plaintiff's status as a self-represented litigant facing a seven-doctrinal-framework opposition. The relief requested (sanctions to foreclose substantive consideration of section 425.17(b), *Flatley*, and step-two evidence) is the textbook "improper purpose" of obtaining advantage by procedural weaponization. (Section 128.7(b)(1).)

### C. May 5, 2026 Anti-SLAPP Reply (CGC-25-631801), Section V Misstates *Kulchar*/*Stevenot*

Section V of the Reply deploys *Kulchar* and *Stevenot* as a categorical bar on the ground that "Plaintiff actively participated" in the underlying trial. The cited authorities, properly read, distinguish intrinsic from extrinsic fraud and **do not** bar an extrinsic-fraud claim where conduct outside the adversarial test (concealment of file possession, misrepresentation to the court about provenance) prevents fair adjudication. (*Kulchar*, supra, 1 Cal.3d at 471-472; *Rappleyea v. Campbell* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 975, 981-982.) Defense counsel possessed objective evidence of file possession (Substitution of Attorney, Trans. ID 69953800, May 4, 2023; sworn Jessup testimony) and possessed Reyna's February 2024 written request for "all documents, records stored in any format." A flat *Kulchar* bar advanced against this record is not warranted by existing law.

### D. May 5, 2026 O'Connell Letter: Threats Lack Evidentiary or Legal Support

The May 5 letter telegraphs contempt sanctions for any in-person appearance at the May 12 hearing absent a "contest" deadline. As of the date of this letter, Plaintiff has not received the tentative for the May 12 hearing through any channel. (See companion Notice of Non-Receipt.) A contempt threat directed at an appearance at a noticed hearing, by a self-represented party who has not received the tentative, is unsupported by section 1209 et seq. or by CRC 3.1308, and is interposed for an improper purpose. (Section 128.7(b)(1), (b)(2); see Cal. Rules of Prof. Conduct, rule 3.10.)

### E. May 1 Supplementals: Position Now Contradicts Defense's Own Reply Characterization

Defense's May 5, 2026 Anti-SLAPP Reply (CGC-25-631801) characterizes the May 1 supplemental memoranda (filed by Defendants or otherwise referenced) as "late-filed, prejudicial, noncompliant with the Rules of Court, and merely repetitive." To the extent any defense paper similarly noncompliant remains in the record without leave, the certification under section 128.7(b)(2) is not warranted by existing law on the very ground Defendants themselves articulate.

### F. April 23-24, 2026 Oppositions to Leave to Amend (CGC-25-631801, register Nos. 052-053) Are Moot Yet Repackaged

The underlying motions for leave were adjudicated by Department 302 on April 30, 2026. Carrying these oppositions forward into the May 12 anti-SLAPP record (or into the May 22 demurrer record) without complying with CCP section 1008, where applicable, violates section 128.7(b)(2). (See *Le Francois v. Goel* (2005) 35 Cal.4th 1094, 1107-1109; *California Correctional Peace Officers Assn. v. Virga* (2010) 181 Cal.App.4th 30, 42-43.)
