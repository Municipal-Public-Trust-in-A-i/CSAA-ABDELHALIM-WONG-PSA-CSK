## I. STATUTORY FRAMEWORK

Code of Civil Procedure § 128.7(b) provides that by presenting a pleading, written motion, or other paper to the court, an attorney or unrepresented party is certifying that to the best of the person's knowledge, information, and belief, formed after an inquiry reasonable under the circumstances:

> "(1) It is not being presented primarily for an improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay or needless increase in the cost of litigation.
>
> "(2) The claims, defenses, and other legal contentions therein are warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for the extension, modification, or reversal of existing law or the establishment of new law.
>
> "(3) The allegations and other factual contentions have evidentiary support or, if specifically so identified, are likely to have evidentiary support after a reasonable opportunity for further investigation or discovery."

---

## II. DEFECTS IN THE MOTION TO STRIKE

Plaintiff contends that Defendant's Motion to Strike violates § 128.7(b) in at least the following particulars:

### Defect 1: CCP § 435(b)(1) Timing Violation

The motion to strike under CCP § 436 was not filed concurrently with or before Defendant's demurrer and anti-SLAPP motion. CCP § 435(b)(1) requires that a motion to strike "shall be made within the time allowed to respond to a pleading, however, if a demurrer is also filed, the motion to strike shall be made at the same time as the demurrer is filed and shall be noticed for hearing at the same time." Defendant's demurrer and anti-SLAPP were filed on a prior date. The MTS was filed April 22, 2026—late and waived as a matter of law.

### Defect 2: CCP § 435.5 Meet-and-Confer Deficiency

Defendant's MTS does not contain a § 435.5 declaration demonstrating that counsel "met and conferred" with Plaintiff in person, by telephone, or by video conference before filing the motion, as required by § 435.5(a). The statute requires actual oral communication—not email or letter—at least five days before the motion is filed. No such declaration is attached.

### Defect 3: Frivolous Pre-Litigation Privilege Argument

The MTS invokes Civil Code § 47(b) to bar claims arising from the February 25, 2021 denial letter—a pre-litigation communication issued 21 days after the collision, before any litigation was pending or contemplated. Under *Action Apartment Ass'n v. City of Santa Monica* (2007) 41 Cal.4th 1232, 1248–49, § 47(b) applies only to communications made in connection with judicial proceedings. The February 25, 2021 denial letter does not meet any of the three *Action Apartment* contemplation factors.

### Defect 4: Misapplication of *Trope v. Katz*

The MTS argues that Plaintiff cannot recover attorney's fees as a pro se litigant under *Trope v. Katz* (1995) 11 Cal.4th 274. However, the operative complaint does not seek attorney's fees for self-representation—it seeks *Prentice* tort damages under *Prentice v. North American Title Guaranty Corp.* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 618, which are recoverable as an element of fraud damages, not as a fee award. The MTS mischaracterizes the complaint's damages theory.

### Defect 5: Ignoring *Lazar* Particularity Compliance

The MTS argues fraud is not pleaded with particularity, yet ignores that the complaint specifically alleges WHO (Sarah Rash, Claims Representative), WHAT (false representation of investigative completeness), WHEN (February 25, 2021), WHERE (CSAA claims office to Plaintiff in San Francisco), HOW (written denial letter without investigation), and BY WHAT MEANS (U.S. Mail), as well as Claim No. 1004-09-1054. This satisfies *Lazar v. Superior Court* (1996) 12 Cal.4th 631.

### Defect 6: Improper Purpose — Harassment and Delay

The MTS was filed on the last possible day to gain maximum calendaring delay, notwithstanding that Defendant had already filed a demurrer and anti-SLAPP motion raising substantially overlapping arguments. This suggests an improper purpose to multiply litigation costs and delay resolution.
