## Procedural Posture and Admission Consequence

Defendants have filed an anti-SLAPP motion and a demurrer. By their chosen procedural vehicles, they have (a) moved on protected-activity and legal-sufficiency grounds, (b) declined to file an answer, and (c) declined to put the operative facts of the TACs in issue.

A demurrer admits the truth of every well-pleaded fact for the limited purposes of the motion. (*Blank v. Kirwan* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 311, 318.) The anti-SLAPP step-two minimal-merit standard accepts the plaintiff's evidence as true and disregards the defendants' contrary evidence. (*Soukup v. Law Offices of Herbert Hafif* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 260, 291.) Under *D'Amico v. Board of Medical Examiners* (1974) 11 Cal.3d 1, 21–22, sworn admissions are binding for these purposes; that authority is invoked here as governing the procedural-admission inference, not as a merits assertion.

Therefore, by filing as they did and not answering, defendants have, for the limited purposes of these motions, admitted the truth of the well-pleaded facts that compose the TAC counts identified above. Those admitted facts include, in CGC-25-631801, the documented possession and concealment of the 911/BWC/CAD record and the seven consequential rulings ([801 TAC ¶¶ 3, 5, 9, 48–50](../../801-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-801-06-TAC.pdf)); and, in CGC-25-631802, CSAA's non-performance of the investigation required by 10 C.C.R. § 2695.7(b) ([802 TAC ¶¶ 1, 4, 7, 8, 46](../../802-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-802-06-TAC.pdf)). Plaintiff is identifying that admission consequence on the record, not soliciting a stipulation as to merits.

# Implications of the 0003 Meet-and-Confer Letter

The transmission of this letter — to the Court, all defendants, the insurance regulator (CDI), and Tyler simultaneously — is structurally devastating to the defense's "no merits" posture. Here is what changes the moment this hits the multi-channel record.

---

## 1. The "No Merits" Argument Is Foreclosed As a Procedural Matter

The defense has been running a posture that the merits "have not been articulated." That posture survives only as long as no comprehensive, paragraph-cited statement of merits exists on a single record. This letter eliminates that condition.

Specifically, lines 14–18 of `TEMP/MERITS/0003.md` accomplish three things at once:

- **Single-record articulation.** Every cause of action in both TACs is listed with paragraph-level cites ([801 TAC ¶¶ 52–87; 802 TAC ¶¶ 5–69](../../801-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-801-06-TAC.pdf)). There is no longer a colorable "we don't know what the merits are" claim available to counsel.
- **Burden shift via written demand.** Counsel is required to identify, *in writing and before the meet-and-confer date*, **(a) the specific cause of action**, **(b) the specific element**, and **(c) the operative TAC paragraph(s)** they claim are deficient. Silence is explicitly defined as forfeiture: *"Plaintiff will proceed on the working understanding that the merits as stated above are not in further procedural dispute for meet-and-confer purposes, and will so represent on the record if the matter is heard."*
- **Public record of the demand.** Because the letter went to the Court, the defendants, the CDI, and Tyler, the demand cannot be ignored privately. Whatever the defense does or does not respond with is now visible to four independent audiences.

Practically: the defense now has to either (i) respond substantively — which itself concedes the merits are pleaded clearly enough to be addressed element-by-element — or (ii) stay silent and have the "no merits" rhetoric impeached the next time it appears in a motion.

The demurrer is on a *Blank v. Kirwan* track — it admits the truth of every well-pleaded fact for the limited purpose of the motion. The letter (lines 75–81, 112–116) drives three points home:

- **Recharacterization of the demurrer.** It expressly invites the Court to view the demurrer as testing factual disputes rather than legal sufficiency — invoking *Committee on Children's Television v. Step two of anti-SLAPP requires only *minimal merit*, with plaintiff's evidence taken as true and defense's contrary evidence disregarded (*Soukup v. Law Offices of Herbert Hafif* (2006) 39 Cal.4th 260, 291). The letter does two things to the anti-SLAPP posture:

- **Documentary minimal-merit showing.** Lines 85–105 stage the merit evidence as a foreclosed contradiction: the [**2021 Sarah Rash denial letter**](../../MIL6-MIL17-Denial-Letter-Exhibits/CSAA_Denial_Letter-Feb-25-2021.pdf) (representation that the investigation **was** performed) versus the **January 17, 2025 MIL Nos. [6](../../MIL6-MIL17-Denial-Letter-Exhibits/CGC-21-594102-CT1-MIL-No-6_Clerk-pp-149-151.pdf) and [17](../../MIL6-MIL17-Denial-Letter-Exhibits/CGC-21-594102-CT1-MIL-No-17_Clerk-pp-238-241.pdf) declarations signed by Priya under CCP § 2015.5** (representation that the investigative product **was not** in counsel's possession). On their face, both cannot be true. That alone clears a minimal-merit bar.
- **Anti-SLAPP scope disavowal preserved.** The 802 TAC's express anti-SLAPP disavowal at ¶ 52A — that the gravamen is *non-communicative pre-litigation claim handling*, not petitioning conduct — is reasserted in writing (line 58). This guards against a step-one "protected activity" win.

## 5. Multi-Recipient Transmission: Why Each Channel Matters

The defense is now boxed in across four parallel records that must all remain consistent.

### a. Insurance Investigation Board (CDI)

- The letter establishes a documented regulatory complaint with **named individual** (Sarah Rash) and **specific regulatory predicates**: 10 C.C.R. § 2695.7(b) (claim-investigation duty) and Insurance Code § 790.03(h)(5) (liability-reasonably-clear) — pleaded as UCL "unlawful" predicates at 802 TAC ¶ 47A.
- CDI has independent investigatory authority. A CDI market-conduct review can proceed in parallel and is not stayed by the civil litigation.
- Anything CSAA tells CDI to defend the Rash denial letter must be reconcilable with what its appointed counsel filed in MIL Nos. 6 and 17. The contradiction the letter identifies (line 103) was previously latent; it is now framed for a regulator who reads denial letters for a living.

### b. The Court

- This is now a **record-preserving statement** (line 129). If meet-and-confer breaks down or the defense claims they were never told the merits, the Court has the letter in front of it.
- It frames the demurrer for the Court before the defense's reply brief lands — the Court reads "merits articulated, paragraph by paragraph" before it reads "no merits."
- It also creates a predicate for CCP § 128.7 sanctions if the defense continues to file papers asserting "no merits" or asserting the MIL declarations as truthful.

### c. All Defendants (CSAA + Attorney Defendants)

- Each Attorney Defendant is now individually on notice of the § 6128(a) and officer-of-the-court fiduciary theories. Any continued assertion of the MIL declarations on the record is now done with documented knowledge of the contradiction.
- CSAA, as principal under Civil Code § 2338 ([802 TAC ¶ 6A](../../802-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-802-06-TAC.pdf)), is on notice that the funded, directed, and ratified conduct of its retained counsel is the operative tort theory — it cannot disclaim the attorneys' acts.
- Internal conflicts between CSAA and its appointed counsel are now foreseeable: CSAA may need to question whether its counsel's January 2025 declarations are defensible if the regulator finds the 2021 investigation was not performed.

### d. Tyler — CSAA's Counsel of Record in 801/802

Tyler's status as CSAA's currently appearing counsel converts the transmission into a series of personal and imputed legal events that did not exist five minutes before the letter hit his inbox:

- **Personal § 6128(a) notice.** Tyler now holds, in writing, the denial-letter-vs-MIL-declarations contradiction and the *Jessup* officer-of-the-court testimony that is already on the public record corroborating delivery of the investigation materials to CSAA's defense counsel. Any subsequent paper he signs that adopts, relies upon, or reasserts the MIL Nos. 6 and 17 positions is signed with documented knowledge of the inconsistency. That is the precise mens-rea posture B&P Code § 6128(a) is written to reach — *deceit "with intent to deceive the court or any party"* — and 801 TAC ¶¶ 61–63 already pleads it as to the Attorney Defendants.

- **CCP § 128.7 certification trap.** Every brief, declaration, or pleading Tyler signs after receipt carries a § 128.7(b) certification that factual contentions have evidentiary support and legal contentions are warranted. With the contradiction in his hands, a signed paper that asserts the 2021 investigation was performed (or that the MIL declarations were truthful) is no longer arguable advocacy — it is a § 128.7 sanctions predicate. The letter's record-preserving character (line 129) makes the prior-knowledge element trivially provable.

- **Imputed knowledge to CSAA.** Tyler's knowledge as appearing counsel is imputed to CSAA. CSAA cannot in any later proceeding (this case, the related case, the CDI investigation, or any reinsurance/coverage proceeding) claim it was unaware of the 2021/2025 contradiction. The Civil Code § 2338 ratification theory pleaded at 802 TAC ¶ 6A now runs through Tyler in real time: anything Tyler does that relies on the prior MIL declarations is fresh ratification of the underlying conduct by CSAA, *post-notice*.

- **Successor-counsel adoption doctrine.** Successor counsel who knowingly continues to assert a predecessor's misrepresentation does not inherit a defense — they incur their own. The MIL Nos. 6 and 17 declarations were signed by prior defense counsel under CCP § 2015.5, but the moment Tyler files a reply brief that depends on those declarations being truthful, he steps into the § 6128(a) and officer-of-the-court fiduciary theories in his own name. The letter freezes the moment that exposure begins: now.

- **Meet-and-confer obligation under CCP §§ 430.41 / 435.5.** Tyler is the addressee of the meet-and-confer demand at lines 120–129, including the written-element-identification requirement at lines 16–18. His response (or non-response) is now the operative meet-and-confer record. If he stays silent or refuses to identify the deficient element/cause/paragraph, the "no merits" rhetoric becomes affirmatively unavailable in his next filing — and the Court will have the letter to confirm he was given the opportunity.

- **Withdrawal-or-correct dilemma.** Tyler is now forced to choose, on a clock, among three doors:
  1. **Withdraw or correct** the MIL Nos. [6](../../MIL6-MIL17-Denial-Letter-Exhibits/CGC-21-594102-CT1-MIL-No-6_Clerk-pp-149-151.pdf) and [17](../../MIL6-MIL17-Denial-Letter-Exhibits/CGC-21-594102-CT1-MIL-No-17_Clerk-pp-238-241.pdf) declarations — which concedes the underlying 801 TAC misrepresentation theory.
  2. **Continue to defend** the declarations as truthful — which requires him to reconcile them with the Sarah Rash 2021 denial letter and with Jessup's public-record testimony, on pain of § 128.7 sanctions and personal § 6128(a) exposure.
  3. **Stay silent** — which the letter expressly converts into a procedural waiver of the "no merits" objection (lines 14–18) and which the CDI may read as institutional non-cooperation.
  Each door produces a recorded consequence; there is no costless fourth option.

- **Conflict-of-interest exposure.** The Attorney Defendants face individual § 6128(a) and officer-of-the-court fiduciary theories ([801 TAC ¶¶ 52–87](../../801-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-801-06-TAC.pdf)). CSAA faces aiding-and-abetting ([801 TAC ¶¶ 72–75](../../801-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-801-06-TAC.pdf)) and § 2338 ratification ([802 TAC ¶ 6A](../../802-MOTION-STRIKE-APR-22/TAC-RESPONSE/court-pdfs/TAC-802-06-TAC.pdf)). The Attorney Defendants' interest in defending their MIL declarations as truthful diverges from CSAA's interest in distancing itself from those declarations once the contradiction is on the regulator's desk. Tyler, as CSAA's counsel, is now navigating that divergence with documented notice — which is itself a Rules of Professional Conduct 1.7 issue if his representation extends to or coordinates with the Attorney Defendants.

- **Knowledge attribution forecloses the "good-faith successor" narrative.** Any future argument that CSAA's current defense team was unaware of the 2021 investigation history, the Jessup delivery testimony, or the MIL declaration contradiction is precluded by this letter. The narrative "we just stepped in and were briefed by the file" is foreclosed because the file now affirmatively contains the contradiction in Tyler's own correspondence.

- **CDI cross-channel risk.** The same letter that reaches Tyler reaches the Department of Insurance. Whatever Tyler tells this Court about the 2021 investigation must be reconcilable with whatever CSAA tells CDI about it. Inconsistent statements across the two channels are themselves a Title 10 § 2695.7 / Insurance Code § 790.03(h) compliance event, independent of the civil-litigation outcome.
