# Notice charge versus discretion: Reply No. 2 and section 614

**Case:** *Rosario v. Abdelhalim*, San Francisco Superior Court No. CGC-21-594102; appeal A173827; Supreme Court S299144. Hub: [S299144](../01-APPEAL/s299144-supreme-court/README.md). Registry: [CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md](CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md).

## The charge

**Actor.** The trial court.

**Act.** On Rosario's account, the court materially enhanced the response to Jury Request No. 2 (highway / street clarification) and transmitted the completed enhancement to the deliberating jury.

**Object.** Code of Civil Procedure section 614's presence-or-notice requirement for the information that actually entered the jury room.

Section 614 states that when a deliberating jury requests information on a point of law, the information "must be given in the presence of, or after notice to, the parties or counsel."

The charge is not that a judge may never clarify law. The charge is that a material modification must be presented through that procedure before delivery.

## Three answers that do not meet the charge

### 1. Discretion

At 11 RT 11:28-12:4, the court stated that the objections were noted and overruled and that the court believed the responses were within its discretion to clarify issues the jury raised.

Authority to issue a clarification is a different proposition from compliance with the delivery procedure for the completed text.

### 2. Correct answer

At 11 RT 8:13-16, after Rosario accused the court of enhancing and sending the highway/street language, the court answered by pointing to the code text: "Highway includes street."

Accuracy of the statutory quote does not establish that the parties received notice of the completed enhancement before the jury did.

### 3. No prejudice

A claim that the enhancement caused no harm addresses *Soule* prejudice or article VI, section 13. It does not identify the notice event section 614 requires for the information actually delivered.

## Object identity: Reply No. 1 is not Reply No. 2

At 11 RT 6:19-21, the court stated that it presented the response in Jury Request No. 1 to each party before delivering it to the jury.

That statement names Request No. 1. Rosario's enhancement accusation names Reply No. 2.

Proof that one response was circulated does not prove that the enhanced Reply No. 2 was circulated. The evidence offered for compliance must match the judicial act charged.

## Rosario's recorded charge on Reply No. 2

At 11 RT 8:7-12, Rosario stated:

> "Inaccurate, Your Honor. You enhanced it by adding that highways also indicates street. That is not in the actual black-letter law. You enhanced the statement and you sent that back. That is a direct influence over the Jury."

On his account, the completed modification reached the jurors before he and his assisting team received an opportunity to review and object.

He objected to the trial judge's failure to instruct the jury before deliberations, which, in his understanding, left jurors needing to seek legal guidance concerning the evidence. His objection to that resulting predicament is not an objection to supplying the instructions, and it is not consent to refusing them. Request No. 2 keeps its own cause: the court sent or enhanced Vehicle Code text. That sending is named here; it is not moved onto Refusal 1.

## Due process limit stated in the filed papers

The Fourteenth Amendment and California Constitution article I, section 7 protect due process, including a meaningful opportunity to be heard on a material change in the law given to a deliberating jury. A post-delivery objection does not recreate the pre-delivery notice moment section 614 describes.
