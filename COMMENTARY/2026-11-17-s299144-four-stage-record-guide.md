This guide maps four stages the filed corrected petition and the filed overlength reply organize from the April 23, 2025 reporter's record and the jury note already in the superior-court file: (1) forfeiture and invited error; (2) instructional error under Code of Civil Procedure section 614; (3) prejudice under *Soule*; (4) the disposition those papers request once prejudice is found. Every stage cites a filed paper on the hub, or a reporter / superior-court pin those papers already use. Diction: [Deny / Delay / Defend / Oppose](CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md). ## Stage 1. Forfeiture and invited error

**Actor.** The Court of Appeal Opinion and the Answer.

**Act.** Treat agreement and invited error as closing instructional and prejudice review. (Opinion pp. 2-4; Answer pp. 4, 8-9.)

**Object.** The completed refusal to instruct (Refusal 1), not a separate Form No. 2 sending.

The corrected petition and the filed reply assign distinct objects on the afternoon colloquy:

| Ground stated in the filed papers | Record pins those papers use |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| The sequence of acknowledgment and later answer is disputed | 11 RT 4:8-25; Answer p. 6 |
| The immediate qualification of the acknowledgment appears on the next lines | 11 RT 4:11-14 |
| The completed response contained a separate affirmative refusal to instruct | 11 RT 6:23-27 |
| The afternoon statements followed transmission of that response | 11 RT 3:5-22; 6:16-27 |
| The judge overruled objections to both completed responses before verdict | 11 RT 10:4-7; 11:28-12:4; 12:27-13:7 |

A statement made after a judicial response was transmitted cannot itself have induced that earlier response. The filed reply cites *Norgart v. Upjohn Co.* (1999) 21 Cal.4th 383, 403 (invited error requires inducing conduct) and *Mary M. v. City of Los Angeles* (1991) 54 Cal.3d 202, 212-213 (inducing error versus acquiescing after objection). Code of Civil Procedure section 647 addresses preservation of instructional refusals. The filed reply presents those authorities against the Opinion's forfeiture holding. (Reply §§ II, V.)

He objected to the trial judge's failure to instruct the jury before deliberations, which, in his understanding, left jurors needing to seek legal guidance concerning the evidence. His objection to that resulting predicament is not an objection to supplying the instructions, and it is not consent to refusing them.

The corrected petition disputes whether the completed refusal clause was included in any formulation reviewed in the earlier unreported consultation. The filed reply organizes that distinction and the judge's final rulings. (Reply § I.)

## Stage 2. Instructional error under section 614

**Actor.** The trial court.

**Act.** Complete a dual prohibition: jurors may not consider Vehicle Code sections 22107 and 21804(a), and the court will not instruct on them. (11 RT 6:23-27.)

**Object.** The void: failure to instruct before deliberations on the statutes the jury named. Registry: [jury-prohibition](CONSEQUENCE-REGISTRY.md#jury-prohibition).

### A. The jury's written request

The jury's complete question was:

> "In considering the question of whether the defendant was negligent should we consider the provisions of Vehicle Code Sections 22107 and 21804(a). If so could we be instructed on those provisions?" (Super. Ct. doc. 09577565, p. 2.)

The jury asked the court whether those sections applied and, if so, to supply instructions. The Opinion quoted the first sentence and omitted the second sentence requesting instruction. The filed reply identifies that omission. (Reply § III; Opinion p. 2.)

Code of Civil Procedure section 614 requires the court to provide necessary information concerning points of law raised by a deliberating jury, with appropriate notice to the parties. The statute requires an appropriate judicial response to a request concerning law arising in the case.

### B. Section 22107 and the admitted turn

Vehicle Code section 22107 requires a driver to exercise reasonable safety when turning or moving laterally upon a roadway. Respondent testified that he was executing a right turn at approximately five miles per hour. (8 RT 120:17-26.) The corrected petition and the filed reply treat section 22107 as applicable turning-duty law for that maneuver, citing *Butigan v. Yellow Cab Co.* (1958) 49 Cal.2d 652, 656. Section 21804(a) carries a separate predicate (entering or crossing a highway from property or an alley). The filed papers treat section 22107 as the primary statutory basis for this stage.

### C. The completed refusal

The completed response prohibited jurors from considering both statutes and declared that the court would not instruct on either provision. (11 RT 6:23-27.) Immediately afterward, the judge stated that the statutes had not been presented as evidence. (11 RT 7:4-9.) The filed papers assign the error as treating statutory law as factual evidence that must first be admitted as an exhibit, rather than as law the court determines and the jury applies to the facts.

### D. Authorities used in the filed papers

The filed reply and the corrected petition rely on decisions reversing where deliberating jurors were denied necessary guidance, including *Bartosh v. Banning* (1967) 251 Cal.App.2d 378, *Sesler v. Ghumman* (1990) 219 Cal.App.3d 218, and *Drury v. Ryan* (2025) 109 Cal.App.5th 1102. Those papers state that a deliberating jury's request can trigger a supplemental-instruction duty distinct from earlier proposed-instruction submissions, and that general negligence instructions do not erase a specific statutory question the jury itself raised.

The Answer argues that CACI No. 401 and related instructions already conveyed ordinary care. The filed reply's response is that the completed writing affirmatively prohibited consideration of the statute the jury named. (Reply § III; Answer pp. 7-9.)

## Stage 3. Prejudice under *Soule*

*Soule v. General Motors Corp.* (1994) 8 Cal.4th 548, 580-581, requires examination of the evidence, other instructions, counsel's arguments, and indications from the jury that it was misled. The standard is a reasonable probability of a more favorable result without the error. The filed reply organizes the record under those factors. (Reply § VI.)

**Evidence.** Respondent admitted the right turn at about five miles per hour and described his lookout; the evidence was disputed concerning contact, Petitioner's position, and whether the turn was executed with reasonable care. (8 RT 120:17-26, 133:6-21.)

**Other instructions.** The court gave general negligence guidance and supplied vehicle-definition material while refusing the turning statute the jury requested. (10 RT 16:1-18:13; 11 RT 6:23-8:6.)

**Counsel's arguments.** Defense counsel characterized Respondent's maneuver as a lawful right turn. (10 RT 54:16-24.) A legally permitted right turn is not the same inquiry as a turn executed with reasonable safety under section 22107.

**Jury indication.** Jurors named section 22107, connected it to determining Respondent's negligence, and asked whether they could be instructed on it. (Super. Ct. doc. 09577565, p. 2.) The judge rejected that request. The verdict found Respondent not negligent.

**Nine-to-three concurrence.** The jury returned a no-negligence verdict with nine concurring jurors. (11 RT 15:14-17, 16:1-7.) Nine votes were the minimum civil concurrence. The filed papers treat that numerical margin, together with the written question and the omitted statutory duty, as part of the *Soule* showing.

The cumulative record the filed papers assemble is: the defendant admitted the turning movement; the applicable turning-duty statute was absent from the charge; the jury specifically requested that statute to decide negligence; the completed response prohibited its consideration; defense counsel characterized the turn as lawful; and the jury returned a no-negligence verdict with nine concurring votes.

## Stage 4. Disposition requested in the filed papers

California Constitution, article VI, section 13 permits reversal where a miscarriage of justice is shown. The corrected petition and the filed reply request grant of review and transfer under rules 8.500(b)(4) and 8.528(d), with directions to vacate the Opinion, reconsider consent and invited error, settle the record as needed under rule 8.155(c)(2), and adjudicate the preserved section 614 claim and *Soule* prejudice; alternatively, retention of review under rule 8.500(b)(1). (Reply § IX; Petition § VI.C.)

The original verdict's first finding was that Respondent was not negligent. The jury did not reach affirmative findings on the later liability and damages elements. The disposition those papers request, on a prejudice finding as to that first finding, is reversal of the defense judgment and remand for a new trial under proper instructions. That is the disposition pattern in *Sesler*, *Bartosh*, and *Drury* after prejudicial instructional error.

## Controlling connection (as the filed papers present it)

1. **Forfeiture.** Respondent **Opposed** merits review by expanding colloquy into consent; the filed papers assign that expansion as legally ineffective consent to the completed refusal. ([DEFENSE.FAILURE.md](../DEFENSE.FAILURE.md))
2. **Instructional error.** The deliberating jury requested applicable turning-duty law; the court withheld it without an adequate substitute (the void).
3. **Prejudice.** The missing guidance concerned the defendant's admitted maneuver, the jury's written question, defense argument that the turn was lawful, and the nine-vote negligence finding.
4. **Disposition.** The filed papers request vacatur, reconsideration of the consent bar, and adjudication of section 614 and *Soule* prejudice, with remand for a new trial if prejudice is found.

The central record assignment is the jury's own request for governing statutory law during deliberations and the court's affirmative prohibition in reply. That assignment appears in the corrected petition and the filed overlength reply on the hub.
